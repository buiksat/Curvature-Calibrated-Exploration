\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:scope-limitations}

\paragraph{Statistical and computational premises.}
Optimism concerns mean rewards, not individual noisy rewards. The theory
requires reference confidence, bounded queries, measurability, pre-reward
timing, and valid matrix/solver certificates; geometry supplies no confidence
event. Finite enumeration supplies action-space measurability, not
certification. Standard-Borel graph measurability alone ensures neither
measurable suprema nor selectors (Appendix~A). Reference/current matrices and representation are
prefix predictable; certificates may use pre-reward randomness. Terminal
quantities need predictable prefix envelopes to enter earlier radii.
Every scored action needs an upper-valid width; only the played action needs
sharpness. Selection/checking use the same realized map. Changing a score width through
refinement requires rechecking selection or retaining the original score width. Randomized post-selection checks need their own
conditioning and failure allocation. On uncountable domains, pointwise
certificates cannot be union-bounded after adaptive selection without
separately conditioned verification.

\paragraph{What transport establishes.}
A width is geometric, not statistical information. The reference-width floor
fixes history, center, radius, bias and the full reference band; additional
mean restrictions or confidence information can change the admissible set.
Same-history width comparisons and comparable-order guarantees do not rank
adaptive regret or prove transport unnecessary. The exact actionwise ratio
already evaluates the reference width it recovers, so supplies no shortcut as
written; this is not a general computational lower bound. The abstract
counterexample is neither a realizable scaled-tanh learning trajectory nor an
observed failure or necessity instance. Its metric balls are not asserted
statistical confidence sets. Symmetric-factor sharpness concerns metric
factors, not a regret lower bound or every trajectory's worst case.

\paragraph{Certificate and model limits.}
Large path inflation, weak lower operator comparisons, loose solver widths or
persistent bias can make valid bounds vacuous; an upper comparison alone
cannot close the displayed regret bound. Endpoint distance need not bound the
interpolated path length, which can be positive for equal endpoints. Direct
SPD path length is factorization independent; factor-path envelopes depend on
factorization and interpolation. Rowwise interpolation need not be any common
model's GGN or its training path. Changing weights adds derivative obligations.
Vector-output Fisher/GGN geometry needs controlled factors and Jacobians;
singular square roots need not be differentiable, and a uniform positive
spectral floor is essential. It supplies no likelihood confidence sequence or
exponential-family regret theorem. Local and exponential comparisons use
different premises and do not uniformly dominate each other.

The corrected center needs a reference model fixed before collection,
zero-centered ridge in the stated coordinates, and supplied historical and
current Taylor and misspecification bounds. An arbitrary predictable optimizer
does not make these errors small. Persistent misspecification can give linear
bias and a vacuous radius: this is graceful degradation, not a robust
misspecified-bandit guarantee. $W$ is a near-linearity scale, not generic hidden
width or parameter count. Its horizon scaling is sufficient, not necessary,
under the stated other assumptions; it gives neither unrestricted neural
training guarantees nor sublinear regret for an arbitrary fixed nonlinear
model. Fixed-direction/category comparisons do not cover general rotating
gradients. Cheaper certificate maintenance leaves center and width solves;
horizon-independent endpoint control leaves historical/current error costs
and does not itself imply sublinear regret or better decisions. Geometry,
self-normalized regression, determinant-potential and spectral-sandwich
arguments are established tools. We give no generic innovation in these tools,
general neural-linear representation-learning analysis, or nonstationary
reward analysis.

\paragraph{Empirical scope and uncertainty.}
The study covers one synthetic scaled-tanh model and fixed teacher with shared
prediction-MSE optimizer selection and dense float64 Cholesky solves. That
selection is not policy-specific regret optimization. It evaluates no
approximate/sketched operator, iterative solver, large model, real data or
matrix-free scalability, and establishes no computational or performance
advantage over scalable neural-bandit methods, LO-FI, LMC-TS or fixed-feature
alternatives. Changing $W$ changes rewards as well as curvature; condition
comparisons do not isolate drift, and target labels are not measured endpoint
distances. Fixed $W$ at one horizon neither tests asymptotic scaling nor
establishes a theorem-assumption violation.

Matched exogenous streams preserve pairing, not adaptive actions or histories.
The comparisons concern complete policies, not isolated certificate changes or
a universal ordering. Paired bootstrap intervals are pointwise and unadjusted,
conditional on the fixed teacher, design and selected optimizer; they include
no environment-selection or tuning uncertainty or simultaneous coverage.
Rounds are dependent, and shared seeds across conditions do not make all runs
one independent population; development/tuning trajectories are not evaluation
replicates. Descriptive benchmarks do not estimate best contextual performance,
bound contextual benefit, establish matching actions/use of context or policy
equivalence. Recorded confidence and
optimism checks, including naive current, establish neither population
coverage, numerical theorem certification, necessity nor practical advantage.
Float64 solves, eigenvalues, quadrature, replay residuals and source hashes are
not rigorous numerical enclosures.

\paragraph{Reading bounds and exploratory diagnostics.}
Appendix~\ref{app:transport-followup} defines fixed-history rescoring, decimal
score-arithmetic checks and fixed-state kernel timings. Bounds and regret means
use each named policy's own histories. Logarithms of
mean RHS/cap are distinct from means of logarithms and individual-bound
checks; subtracting inflation does not reconstruct another policy's bound or
outcome. Post-outcome rescoring/replay holds recorded histories fixed: it is
exploratory, reveals no alternative adaptive trajectory or downstream action
effects, and does not identify an adaptive causal mechanism. Non-Hessian rows
evaluate Hessian's score on other behavior histories, not those policies' own
scores. Per-seed medians/ranges are descriptive, not confidence intervals.
The decimal calculation uses already rounded binary64 inputs/coefficient,
without recomputing upstream solves, exponentials or fitted parameters; early
ranking changes' downstream effects are unmeasured, and score equality (including initially zero centers) alone
does not prove corruption. Width overhead averages actionwise ratios, not
ratios of means; relative factors concern endpoints, not synthetic path length.
Fixed-state timings under parallel load exclude matrix construction,
confidence estimation and the policy loop, so do not establish end-to-end
speedup. Category methods still need collection weights and matrix solves.

\paragraph{Reproducibility and deployment.}
Portable per-seed exports support terminal means and paired intervals, not all
roundwise audits or curve intervals. Full raw trajectories are not included in the
compact supplement, and no durable public raw-bundle URL is provided; exploratory
recomputation needs those raw inputs, and hashes do not replace bytes.
 Deployment limits are stated in the Broader Impact Statement.
