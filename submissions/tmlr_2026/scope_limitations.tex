\subsection{Scope and limitations}\label{sec:scope-limitations}

The guarantees concern mean rewards, not individual noisy rewards. They require
a reference confidence event, bounded queries, measurability, pre-reward
timing and valid matrix and solver certificates; the geometric comparisons do
not supply a confidence event. A smaller inverse-metric width alone does not
establish a tighter confidence interval. Large path inflation, weak lower
operator comparisons, loose solver widths or a persistent error allowance can
make a valid bound vacuous.

Both studies cover synthetic scaled-tanh models with a fixed teacher, one
optimizer selected by prediction mean squared error, and dense float64
Cholesky solves. Selecting the optimizer this way is not policy-specific regret
optimization. Neither study evaluates an approximate or sketched operator, an
iterative solver, a large model, real data or matrix-free scalability.
Changing $W$ changes rewards as well as curvature; condition comparisons do not
isolate drift. A fixed $W$ at one horizon neither tests asymptotic scaling nor
shows that an assumption of a theorem fails. Matched exogenous streams preserve
pairing, not adaptive actions or histories. The comparisons concern complete
policies, not isolated certificate changes or a universal ordering. Paired
bootstrap intervals are pointwise and unadjusted, conditional on the fixed
teacher, design and selected optimizer; they include no environment-selection
or tuning uncertainty and have no simultaneous coverage. Recorded confidence and
optimism checks, including naive current, establish neither population
coverage, numerical theorem certification, necessity nor practical advantage.

The second study covers one six-dimensional model with 12 feature categories
and one optimizer, selected in the design cell and not retuned per cell. Its
only cell with a non-vacuous frozen bound is near-linear, so its design checks
and equivalence results test the setup rather than transport under curvature.
No cell of either study has both substantial curvature and a non-vacuous
frozen bound.
