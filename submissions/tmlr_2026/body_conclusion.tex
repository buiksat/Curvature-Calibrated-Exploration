\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Collection-time confidence can justify a score computed with current
relinearized curvature through explicit metric, operator, and solver
certificates. The corrected center supplies the reference-confidence event
without requiring nonlinear optimizer convergence, while retaining historical
error and current bias. The resulting theorem reduces to the usual frozen
linear-feature UCB argument for exact solves and unchanged features.

This construction also clarifies what transport alone cannot add. Its certified
width is at least the direct reference width on the same history, and exact
actionwise transport reproduces that width (\Cref{prop:reference-width-floor}).
This elementary comparison concerns the same information and center; it neither
rules out stronger confidence information nor orders the realized regret of
different policies. A cheaper way to compute a justified width could still be
useful, but its total cost must include the frozen estimator needed by the
corrected center.

The completed follow-up exposes a substantial operational cost of the evaluated
Hessian certificate. All nine pointwise paired regret comparisons favor its
comparators, and exploratory log analysis finds that its bonus usually
determines the selected action despite variation in the corrected centers.
The primary bound is dominated by path inflation in this study. This differs
from base-component dominance in the original near-linear grid; neither
decomposition establishes a causal effect of drift across reward tasks. The
frozen policy's own bounds remain vacuous, so tightening transport does not
remove all confidence and approximation-error costs. All evaluated policies,
including the uncertified naive control, retain the reported floating-point
confidence and optimism diagnostics in the follow-up. These observations do
not establish the empirical necessity of the transport correction.

For this model, a relative-weight comparison supplies a horizon-independent
endpoint certificate (\Cref{cor:tanh-relative-weights}). Its derivation exploits
scalar reweighting of fixed feature directions. Auditing that certificate on
existing histories evaluates its tightness and numerical behavior; it does not
measure a new policy's adaptive regret. Any such evaluation requires a
separately fixed protocol and fresh evaluation data.

\paragraph{Limitations and research questions.}
The abstract theorem requires measurable action and certificate events, finite
measurable suprema, a measurable approximate selector, valid pre-reward
confidence and metric comparisons, and the same realized solver-width map for
selection and sharpness. The squared-loss specialization requires a fixed
ex-ante reference model, a predictable parameter path in a certified region,
and supplied linearization and misspecification envelopes. Persistent errors
can produce linear or vacuous bounds. The horizon-independent relative-weight
certificate does not itself make the fixed-smoothness regret bound sublinear;
the separate confidence and bias terms remain.

The experiment is one finite-dimensional synthetic model with a fixed teacher,
shared prediction-based optimizer selection, dense matrices, and direct solves.
It evaluates neither approximate operators nor CG, and establishes no practical
advantage over scalable neural-bandit methods. Floating-point checks are
diagnostics, not verified numerical enclosures. The endpoint method is a dense
diagnostic comparator, and the naive policy remains uncertified despite its
observed optimism. The original study has unresolved raw-data and runtime
provenance gaps, distinguished from the retained follow-up evidence in the
reproducibility statement. The next statistical question is whether genuinely
stronger confidence information can justify useful current-geometry scores;
the computational question is whether their complete cost can improve on
direct reference scoring.
