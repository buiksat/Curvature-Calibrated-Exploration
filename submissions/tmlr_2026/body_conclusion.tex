\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Recomputing old gradients changes an uncertainty matrix without collecting new
rewards. A policy that uses this current curvature therefore needs a reason
why its score is still an upper confidence bound. We give that reason through
separate bounds on the change of matrix, its computational approximation, and
the numerical width used to select an action. For squared loss, the corrected
center supplies the reference confidence band along predictable parameter
paths in a certified region, with explicit approximation-error bounds and no
optimizer-convergence requirement.

The guarantee comes with a basic limitation. On the same history, with the same
center, radius and bias, transport cannot produce a smaller certified width
than direct reference scoring; exact actionwise transport recovers that width
(\Cref{prop:reference-width-floor}). The comparison does not order the realized
regret of policies that collect different data. A larger bonus can change
future actions beneficially or adversely.

In our prespecified study, the evaluated Hessian policy has higher mean regret
than every comparator: all nine pointwise paired regret comparisons favor its
comparators. Its regret
bound is dominated by transport inflation, and exploratory analysis shows that
its bonus usually determines its recommendation on the recorded states. Even
the reference policy's own bounds remain vacuous. Tightening the transport
factor alone therefore does not remove the confidence and approximation-error
costs. The original near-linear study has a different, base-dominated bound
decomposition; these studies do not isolate a causal effect of drift across
reward tasks.

The scaled-tanh model also permits a tighter certificate obtained by comparing
weights on fixed feature directions (\Cref{cor:tanh-relative-weights}). Its
audit measures width overhead on existing histories.
It leaves open how a policy using that certificate would behave as it collects
its own data. A horizon-independent comparison of matrices also leaves the
separate confidence and bias terms in the regret bound; it does not by itself
guarantee sublinear regret.

\paragraph{Scope and next questions.}
The theory is conditional on the confidence, measurability, matrix-comparison
and solver requirements in \Cref{asm:abstract-transport,thm:transported-ucb}.
The corrected-center construction additionally needs a reference model fixed
before data collection and supplied Taylor and misspecification error bounds.
The experiments cover one synthetic model, a fixed teacher, shared
prediction-based optimizer selection and dense direct solves. All evaluated
policies, including the uncertified control, pass the reported floating-point
optimism checks. Those diagnostics do not establish verified numerical
certification or the empirical necessity of transport. The original study's
missing raw data and runtime provenance gaps remain disclosed in the
reproducibility statement.

The next useful step is to identify stronger confidence information or a lower
complete computational cost that could justify using current curvature instead
of direct reference scoring. That cost must include the frozen estimator used by the
corrected center. A new adaptive-policy comparison should follow a specific
hypothesis, a separately fixed protocol and fresh evaluation data. The present
results establish no practical advantage over scalable neural-bandit methods.
