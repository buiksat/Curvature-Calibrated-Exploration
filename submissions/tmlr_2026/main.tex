\begin{abstract}
Relinearizing historical gradients changes a contextual-bandit uncertainty
matrix without adding observations. For squared loss, we construct a
corrected-center confidence band along predictable parameter paths in a
certified region, with supplied approximation-error bounds and no optimizer
convergence requirement. We establish a reference-width floor on fixed
histories and sharp symmetric exponential transport factors. For scaled tanh,
relative gradient weights give a horizon-independent endpoint certificate.
A transported-UCB theorem connects these results through explicit statistical,
matrix and solver conditions. A prespecified study compares four policies in
three conditions of a synthetic 29-parameter scaled-tanh model, with 50 paired
seeds per condition. Hessian transport has higher mean pseudo-regret than every
comparator; all nine pointwise paired 95\% intervals exclude zero. Its bonus
usually determines its action, and its bounds and the frozen policy's own
bounds exceed the deterministic cap. All four policies pass the recorded
confidence and optimism checks. A second prespecified study, at a scale
where the frozen bound is below the reward cap, finds endpoint transport
equivalent to frozen scoring within a prespecified margin, a regret cost of
16.8 units for Hessian transport, and 34.4 units less regret for a
generalized-linear UCB baseline, which has higher regret than linear UCB in
the most curved descriptive cell.
\end{abstract}
