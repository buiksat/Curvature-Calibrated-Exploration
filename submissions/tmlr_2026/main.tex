\begin{abstract}
Sequential confidence in a contextual bandit can use features frozen when
rewards are collected, while retraining changes the features used by current
curvature. We study the guarantees and limitations of transporting confidence
to this relinearized curvature. A conditional UCB theorem separates metric
transport, approximate operators, and solver widths, and bounds regret through
the frozen sequential information gain. For squared loss, a corrected
prediction center supplies reference confidence along predictable parameter
paths in a certified region, with explicit Taylor and misspecification error
envelopes and no nonlinear optimizer-convergence requirement. The certified
width cannot be smaller than the direct reference width on the same history;
exact actionwise transport reproduces that width. This comparison does not order the realized regret of policies
with different histories. We examine these distinctions in a 29-parameter
scaled-tanh model, for which a relative-weight comparison also supplies a
horizon-independent endpoint certificate. A prespecified follow-up evaluates
four policies across three conditions with 50 paired seeds per condition. The
Hessian-transport policy has higher mean pseudo-regret than every comparator
in every condition; all nine pointwise paired confidence intervals exclude
zero. Its regret bounds are vacuous and dominated by path inflation, while the
reference policy's own bounds are also vacuous. Floating-point confidence and
optimism diagnostics pass for all evaluated policies, including an uncertified
control. Exploratory log analyses distinguish conditional validity,
certificate usefulness, and observed policy behavior in this implementation.
\end{abstract}
