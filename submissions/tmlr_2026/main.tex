\begin{abstract}
Relinearizing historical gradients changes a contextual-bandit uncertainty
matrix without adding observations. We study how confidence from
collection-time gradients can support UCB scores from current curvature. For
squared loss, a frozen ridge correction yields a confidence band along
predictable parameter paths in a certified region, given Taylor-remainder and
misspecification bounds, without optimizer convergence. A transport theorem
combines this reference confidence with metric, operator and solver
certificates. With the history, center, confidence multiplier and error
allowance held fixed, preserving the full reference band cannot yield a
narrower certified width. We establish sharp symmetric exponential
metric-transport factors and endpoint certificates for scaled tanh on a
bounded parameter region; when its near-linearity scale $W\ge cT$, the uniform
endpoint factor is $1+O(1/(cT))$. Two synthetic studies use 50 paired seeds
per condition. Hessian-path transport has higher mean cumulative pseudo-regret
(mean regret) than the frozen-reference policy in both. In a near-linear
design cell where the frozen-reference policy's regret bound is below the
deterministic cumulative pseudo-regret cap, endpoint transport and the
frozen-reference policy have equivalent mean regret within a three-unit margin
fixed before evaluation, and a generalized-linear UCB baseline has lower mean
regret than both. In descriptive cells with stronger curvature, removing the
Taylor-error allowances lowers mean regret substantially.
\end{abstract}
