\section{What confidence transport did in two synthetic studies}
\label{sec:transport-followup}

Two synthetic studies run the policies of the theory on scaled-tanh models
with Gaussian rewards. Each compares mean cumulative pseudo-regret at the
horizon $T$, which we call \emph{mean regret}, over evaluation seeds that share
their context and potential-outcome streams across policies. The
\emph{deterministic cumulative pseudo-regret cap} $2T\min(1,\sqrt W)$ comes
from the range of the mean reward, whose width is at most $2\min(1,\sqrt W)$
per round; we call it the \emph{regret cap}, and a bound above it is
\emph{vacuous}. The first study fixed its protocol before evaluation and finds
every certified bound vacuous. The second chooses a scale at which the frozen
bound is below the regret cap and adds two uncertified baselines.
\Cref{tab:transport-study-design} lists the cells of both studies.

\paragraph{First study: design and policies.}
The first study fixed its protocol before evaluation: $T=1000$,
$W\in\{16,1,0.25\}$ and 50 paired seeds per policy--condition cell (600
trajectories) of a 29-parameter scaled-tanh model. A fixed teacher generates
Gaussian scaled-tanh rewards. The shared projected optimizer was selected by
prediction MSE before evaluation; the implementation uses dense float64
Cholesky solves. Appendix~\ref{app:transport-followup} gives the full model,
protocol, splits and checks.
All policies update their representations and use the corrected center and
confidence multiplier of \Cref{cor:corrected-center-new}.
\emph{Hessian-path transport} inflates the current width by the Hessian path
certificate $\bar D_t^Q$ of \Cref{cor:hessian-path-new}. \emph{Endpoint
transport} inflates it by the dense endpoint Thompson distance
$d_{\rm Th}(\bVbar_t,\bV_t)$; \emph{frozen reference} scores with the reference
width; \emph{naive current} scores with the current width without transport,
as an uncertified control.

\paragraph{Regret.}
\Cref{tab:transport-followup-outcomes} gives the numerical values. Each paired
difference is a comparator's cumulative pseudo-regret minus that of
Hessian-path transport on the same evaluation seed. All nine pointwise paired
95\% intervals exclude zero in the comparator's favor; they use 10,000 paired
seed-bootstrap resamples. Hessian-path transport's mean regret is close to the
context-free benchmark's 185.26, 183.06 and 176.67 at $W=16,1,0.25$. Endpoint
transport, frozen reference and naive current are not separated by this study:
their mean regrets are within about one unit in each condition, and no
equivalence test was performed. For descriptive context, the
best-fixed-action-in-hindsight means are 180.01, 177.97 and 171.84 on the same
streams as the context-free benchmark (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-followup},
which also plots these outcomes in \Cref{fig:transport-followup-outcomes}).

\paragraph{Bounds relative to the regret cap.}
The regret cap is 2000, 2000 and 1000 at $W=16,1,0.25$. Every Hessian-path
bound and all 150 frozen-reference own-history bounds exceed their caps.
The base-ten logarithms of the Hessian-path policy's mean terminal bound-to-cap
ratio are approximately 9.51, 37.19 and 80.91; the frozen-reference policy's
mean ratios are approximately 19, 60 and 231
(\Cref{fig:transport-followup-bound-scale}). Path inflation is the
Hessian-path policy's largest bound contribution in all three conditions.

Three terms set the scale of the frozen bound
$2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\gamma_T\sum_t\beta_t^2}+2\sum_tb_t(a_t)$ of
\Cref{prop:frozen-width-ucb}: the exploration term with $\beta_t$ reduced to
its stochastic and ridge parts; the remainder of the exploration term, due to
the historical Taylor part of $\beta_t$; and the allowance term. On the
frozen-reference policy's own histories their medians are 10,155, 26,898 and
758 at $W=16$; 10,122, 108,171 and 3,031 at $W=1$; and 10,001, 215,317 and
6,063 at $W=0.25$. The first term alone exceeds every cap: with $d=29$ and
$T=1000$ the realized $\gamma_T$ is about 151--153 (medians). The first term
contains no Taylor term, and on the same queries a linear model's reference
matrix dominates the scaled-tanh one because every collection weight satisfies
$\operatorname{sech}^4\le1$. On the 150 recorded context--action sequences,
an exactly linear model's frozen bound would therefore exceed its own cap
$2T=2000$ as well. The historical term grows as $1/\sqrt W$ and is the
largest term at every $W$; the allowance term is about $2T$ times the mean
Taylor envelope, which Appendix~\ref{app:transport-followup} reports.

\paragraph{Confidence and drift checks.}
In all 600 trajectories, no violation was observed in the recorded checks of
simultaneous reference confidence, policy optimism and the deterministic
diagnostics, including for the uncertified naive-current control. Each
policy--condition cell has 50 of 50 trajectories without a violation, with an
exact 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval of approximately $[92.9\%,100\%]$.
Hessian-path transport reaches endpoint distance above one in 16 of 50 seeds
at $W=0.25$; distances are at least one in 286 of 50,000 seed--round
observations (\Cref{fig:transport-followup-drift-bounds}).

\paragraph{Exploratory diagnostics.}
The diagnostics in this paragraph were specified after outcomes were observed
(Appendix~\ref{app:transport-followup}). Post-outcome rescoring and replay
hold recorded histories fixed: they are exploratory, reveal no alternative
adaptive trajectory or downstream action effects, and do not identify an
adaptive causal mechanism.
On the Hessian-path policy's own histories, the gap between the two largest
width bonuses exceeds the full range of the centers on 97.1\%, 99.4\% and
99.5\% of rounds at $W=16,1,0.25$; this gap is sufficient for the
largest-width action to maximize the score. Its action agrees with the
current-width argmax on 99.4\% of rounds at $W=16$ and on more than 99.9\% at
$W=1$ and $0.25$. The endpoint policy scores with the dense endpoint distance;
no policy uses the relative-weight certificate of
\Cref{cor:tanh-relative-weights}, which appears only in a retrospective audit.
After outcomes were observed, replay with the recorded actions and rewards
audits all 600 histories. On the Hessian-path policy's histories, the
category-specific outward factor has mean effective-width overheads
$0.28\%$--$12.68\%$ relative to the reference width, versus $0.16\%$--$12.13\%$
for the dense endpoint distance across conditions.

\subsection{A second study in which the frozen bound is non-vacuous}
\label{sec:transport-v3}

Every certified bound in the first study is vacuous, so it cannot show what
transport does where the frozen bound is non-vacuous. The second study chooses
a model and, in its design cell, a scale at which the frozen bound is below
the regret cap on every trajectory, and adds two baselines: UCB-GLM
\citep{li2017provably} and a linear UCB. Its scale and equivalence margin were
chosen on development runs and fixed before the held-out evaluation seeds were
run (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-v3}). Its three descriptive cells, with
stronger curvature, were chosen after development output was read and carry
no test; every certified bound in them is vacuous, and the intervals reported
for them are descriptive. No cell of either study has both substantial
curvature and a non-vacuous frozen bound.

\paragraph{Design.}
Contexts are drawn uniformly from the four vectors $(\pm1,\pm1)/\sqrt2$, there
are $K=3$ actions, and $\phi(x,a)=x\otimes e_a\in\R^6$, so $\|\phi\|_2=B_\phi=1$
and there are 12 feature categories. The teacher has unit norm; rewards are
Gaussian with scaled-tanh mean and $\sigma=0.25$; $\lambda=1$, $\delta=0.05$,
$R=1$ and $T=20{,}000$. The design cell sets $W=T$. There the a priori frozen
bound, from \eqref{eq:finite-d-gamma-new} and the envelopes of the first study,
is 37,195, which is 0.93 of the cap $2T\min(1,\sqrt W)=40{,}000$; but
curvature is negligible, since \Cref{cor:tanh-relative-weights} gives
$d_{\rm Th}(\bVbar_t,\bV_t)\le4\log\cosh(1/\sqrt W)\approx1.0\times10^{-4}$.
At these constants, the a priori frozen bound with $W=cT$ is below the regret
cap exactly when $c>0.709$ (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-v3}).
Three descriptive cells, $W=100,10,1$, have stronger curvature. Each cell has
50 paired evaluation seeds, and all policies in a cell share a seed's context
and potential-outcome streams. One optimizer, selected in the design cell by
prediction MSE on uniform-random histories, is used in every cell
(Appendix~\ref{app:transport-v3}).

\paragraph{Policies.}
The four corrected-center policies are those of the first study. UCB-GLM is
Algorithm~1 of \citet{li2017provably} with link $\mu(u)=\sqrt W\tanh(u/\sqrt W)$.
It plays uniformly random actions until the smallest eigenvalue of
$\sum_iX_iX_i^\top$ reaches one, then plays the maximizer of
$X^\top\hat\theta_t+\alpha\|X\|_{V_t^{-1}}$, where $\hat\theta_t$ solves the GLM
score equation $\sum_{i<t}(Y_i-\mu(X_i^\top\theta))X_i=0$,
$V_t=\sum_{i<t}X_iX_i^\top$, $\alpha=(\sigma/\kappa)\sqrt{(d/2)\log(1+2T/d)
+\log(1/\delta)}$ and $\kappa=\operatorname{sech}^2((R+1)/\sqrt W)$. The rule
that ends the random phase is ours: their theorem fixes its length only up to
a universal constant. Their analysis states rewards in $[0,1]$, so its regret
bound does not apply to these Gaussian rewards. Linear UCB fits ridge
regression to raw rewards with metric $\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\phi_s
\phi_s^\top$ and radius $\sqrt{\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta)}+\sqrt\lambda R$;
under scaled-tanh means it is uncertified. The descriptive cells add
envelope-free frozen and naive policies, which drop the \emph{Taylor-error
allowances}: the historical Taylor part of $\beta_t$ and the error allowance
($b_t=0$). They are uncertified ablations that show which terms make the
certified bounds vacuous.

\paragraph{Design-cell results.}
\Cref{tab:transport-v3-design} gives the design cell. Hessian-path transport's mean cumulative pseudo-regret exceeds
that of the frozen-reference policy by 16.8 units (paired 95\% interval
$[15.4, 18.0]$), and UCB-GLM's is 34.4 units lower ($[-36.6, -32.3]$).
Endpoint transport and naive current differ from the frozen-reference policy
by less than 0.2 units in mean regret. The decisions use paired $t$-tests fixed
before evaluation. Both primary comparisons, two-sided paired $t$-tests with
Holm over the two, reject (unadjusted $p$ shown): UCB-GLM minus frozen is
$-34.4$ ($p=5.7\times10^{-34}$) and Hessian-path minus frozen is $16.8$
($p=5.3\times10^{-30}$). Endpoint transport, frozen reference and naive current
have equivalent mean cumulative pseudo-regret within the margin $\Delta=3.0$
regret units: for each of the three pairs the two-sided 90\% paired-$t$
interval lies inside $(-1.0,1.0)$ (two one-sided tests, intersection--union).
The design checks hold: all six policies have lower mean regret than the
context-free benchmark (one-sided paired $t$-tests, Holm over six;
Appendix~\ref{app:transport-v3}), and all 50 frozen bounds are below the cap;
the largest is 0.761 of it. These results concern one near-linear cell, in
which the equivalence of the first three policies is expected by
construction because curvature is negligible.

\paragraph{Descriptive cells.}
\Cref{tab:transport-v3-descriptive} gives mean regret in the three curved
cells. As $W$
decreases, the certified policies' regret grows (frozen: 440.9, 1,004.4 and
1,902.1), while the envelope-free policies and linear UCB stay between 43.1
and 44.1. Dropping the Taylor-error allowances thus brings frozen and naive to
linear UCB's level; the contributions of the historical Taylor part of
$\beta_t$ and of the error allowance were not separated. Without the
Taylor-error allowances, naive minus frozen is $-0.6$ ($[-1.8, 0.5]$), $-0.1$
($[-1.0, 0.9]$) and $-0.3$ ($[-1.4, 0.8]$) at $W=100$, $10$ and $1$. Over every
action and round of every run that computes both widths, in all four cells,
the ratio $s_t(a)/\bar s_t(a)$ of current to reference width lies between
0.985 and 1.305, so this study does not test a current width much smaller than
the reference width. Among the reference, endpoint and naive widths the choice
matters less: at $W=100$ and $10$ both paired intervals against frozen include
zero, while at $W=1$ endpoint minus frozen is $-15.3$ ($[-20.5,-10.2]$) and
naive minus frozen is $-23.1$ ($[-28.3,-18.0]$), so the design cell's
equivalence does not carry over to $W=1$. The difference in mean cumulative
pseudo-regret between Hessian-path transport and the frozen-reference policy
grows to 2,396.8, 3,784.6 and 6,733.3. UCB-GLM has mean regret 33.4, 42.8 and
340.0. At $W=1$, where $\kappa=0.0707$ makes its radius $\alpha=19.19$
(against $1.36$ at $W=T$), it has about 7.7 times the regret of linear UCB
(44.1); whether the radius causes this was not tested.

\paragraph{Numerical checks.}
All 1,500 evaluation runs completed with no crash and no recorded failure.
UCB-GLM's Newton solves always converged, and its random phase lasted 12 to 34
rounds. In the 1,100 corrected-center runs, the category-count current matrix,
the reference matrix and the drift sum $Q_t$ of \Cref{cor:hessian-path-new} were
checked against dense replay at six rounds, 19,800 checks in all; the largest
discrepancy was 0.039 of its tolerance. No run that records the optimism
check failed it; UCB-GLM does not record it.

\paragraph{What the second study shows.}
Where the frozen bound is non-vacuous and curvature is negligible, endpoint
transport and naive current have mean cumulative pseudo-regret equivalent to
that of the frozen-reference policy within the margin, which is consistent with
the bound comparison after \Cref{cor:tanh-endpoint-regime} (that comparison
concerns bound expressions, not realized regret), and the Hessian path
certificate raises mean regret even there. UCB-GLM, whose regret theorem does
not cover Gaussian rewards, has the lowest mean regret in the design cell and
at $W=100$, about the same mean regret as linear UCB at $W=10$, and much
higher regret at $W=1$. In the curved cells, removing the Taylor-error
allowances lowers frozen's mean regret from 440.9--1,902.1 to 43.3--43.9,
while the reference, endpoint and naive widths differ in mean regret by at
most 23.1 units. These are results for one synthetic model and one selected
optimizer, and no cell has both substantial curvature and a non-vacuous
frozen bound.
