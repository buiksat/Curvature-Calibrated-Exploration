    35	\begin{abstract}
    36	Model updates change the sensitivities used for allocation, while
    37	reward confidence may rely on sensitivities fixed when observations
    38	were collected. We give a finite-action confidence-transfer result
    39	under explicit pre-reward metric and solver certificates, yielding
    40	reward optimism and a regret bound on their joint validity event.
    41	Under fixed-reference and approximation-envelope assumptions, a
    42	corrected center supplies confidence without nonlinear optimizer
    43	convergence. A controlled nearly linear, 29-parameter simulation
    44	records simultaneous float64 confidence and optimism diagnostics,
    45	yet the evaluated regret bound is vacuous. Within each primary-horizon
    46	condition, enabling the analytic transport multiplier increases sample
    47	mean pseudo-regret relative to omitting it in an otherwise matched policy.
    48	\end{abstract}
    49	\vspace{-0.4em}

    51	\section{Adaptive model updates and confidence transport}
    52	An allocation policy might retrain an outcome model in batches, then use predicted rewards and uncertainty to choose an ad or ranking action. This illustration differs from our synthetic study. Learned reward models also appear in neural contextual bandits \cite{zhou2020neural,riquelme2018deep}. Recomputing past sensitivities changes the Gram matrix. Collection-time confidence alone gives no upper bound using the recomputed matrix: an inverse-metric comparison for the current query is missing.
    53	
    54	At round $t\le T$, observe $x_t$, choose $a_t$ from a fixed finite
    55	nonempty set $\mathcal A$, then observe $r_t$. Our confidence target
    56	is the conditional mean reward $\mu_t^*(a)$ used for allocation,
    57	not a treatment-effect estimand. Let
    58	$\mathcal H_t^-$ precede fresh round-$t$ randomization $\Omega_t$ and
    59	$\mathcal H_t=\mathcal H_t^-\vee\sigma(\Omega_t)$ contain all
    60	pre-reward information. Queries $q_t(a)\in\R^d$ use fixed coordinates
    61	and dimension. The reference and exact current metrics,
    62	$\beta_t\ge0$, and $\theta_t$ when used are
    63	$\mathcal H_t^-$-measurable (predictable). All remaining score inputs
    64	and the selected action are finite and $\mathcal H_t$-measurable,
    65	actionwise; each selected query is fixed before its reward.
    66	All validity events are measurable, and any claimed certificate
    67	probability is a supplied simultaneous bound.
    68	
    69	For $\lambda,\sigma>0$, with $I$ the $d$-dimensional identity, define reference geometry and widths by
    70	\begin{equation}
    71	\Vbar_1=\lambda I,\qquad
    72	\Vbar_{t+1}=\Vbar_t+\sigma^{-2}q_t(a_t)q_t(a_t)^\top,
    73	\qquad
    74	\bar s_t(a)^2=q_t(a)^\top\Vbar_t^{-1}q_t(a).
    75	\label{eq:reference}
    76	\end{equation}
    77	Define cumulative pseudo-regret and frozen information gain, with $\gamma_0=0$, as
    78	\begin{equation}
    79	R_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\left[\max_{a\in\mathcal A}\mu_t^*(a)-\mu_t^*(a_t)\right],\qquad
    80	\gamma_T=\log\frac{\det\Vbar_{T+1}}{\det\Vbar_1}.
    81	\label{eq:regret-gain}
    82	\end{equation}
    83	Let $V_t$ be the exact current metric and $C_t$ the algorithmic operator, both $d\times d$ symmetric positive-definite (SPD). Set $s_t(a)^2=q_t(a)^\top V_t^{-1}q_t(a)$ and $s_{C,t}(a)^2=q_t(a)^\top C_t^{-1}q_t(a)$.
    84	
    85	\begin{theorem}[Finite-action confidence transfer]\label{thm:transfer}
    86	Under this protocol, suppose $\|q_t(a)\|_2\le G<\infty$.
    87	Define $E_{\rm conf}$ as the event that, simultaneously for
    88	$t\le T$ and $a\in\mathcal A$,
    89	\begin{equation}
    90	|\mu_t^*(a)-m_t(a)|
    91	\le\beta_t\bar s_t(a)+b_t(a),\qquad b_t(a)\ge0.
    92	\label{eq:refconf}
    93	\end{equation}
    94	Let $\Dbar_t\ge0$,
    95	$0<\kappa_{-,t}\le\kappa_{+,t}<\infty$, and
    96	$1\le\alpha_t<\infty$ be finite pre-reward factors.
    97	Define $E_{\rm cert}$ as simultaneous validity, for every $t\le T$,
    98	of the Loewner comparisons
    99	\begin{equation}
   100	e^{-\Dbar_t}\Vbar_t\preceq V_t\preceq e^{\Dbar_t}\Vbar_t,\qquad
   101	\kappa_{-,t}V_t\preceq C_t\preceq\kappa_{+,t}V_t,
   102	\label{eq:certificates}
   103	\end{equation}
   104	and, for a nonnegative pre-reward width map,
   105	\begin{equation}
   106	s_{C,t}(a)\le\shat_t(a)\quad(a\in\mathcal A),\qquad
   107	\shat_t(a_t)\le\alpha_t s_{C,t}(a_t).
   108	\label{eq:solver}
   109	\end{equation}
   110	Use this same realized width map in the score and selector:
   111	\begin{equation}
   112	U_t(a)=m_t(a)+\beta_t e^{\Dbar_t/2}
   113	\sqrt{\kappa_{+,t}}\,\shat_t(a)+b_t(a),\qquad
   114	U_t(a_t)\ge\max_{a\in\mathcal A}U_t(a)-\xi_t,\quad\xi_t\ge0.
   115	\label{eq:score}
   116	\end{equation}
   117	Changing a scored width after selection requires rechecking the
   118	selector inequality. On $E_{\rm conf}\cap E_{\rm cert}$,
   119	$U_t(a)\ge\mu_t^*(a)$ for every action and round, and pathwise
   120	\begin{equation}
   121	\begin{aligned}
   122	R_T\le{}&
   123	\sqrt{\left(\sigma^2+\frac{G^2}{\lambda}\right)\gamma_T
   124	\sum_{t=1}^T\beta_t^2
   125	\left[1+\alpha_t e^{\Dbar_t}
   126	\sqrt{\frac{\kappa_{+,t}}{\kappa_{-,t}}}\right]^2}\\
   127	&+2\sum_{t=1}^T b_t(a_t)+\sum_{t=1}^T\xi_t.
   128	\end{aligned}
   129	\label{eq:regret}
   130	\end{equation}
   131	If $\Pr(E_{\rm conf})\ge1-\delta$ and
   132	$\Pr(E_{\rm cert})\ge1-\delta_{\rm cert}$, these conclusions hold
   133	with probability at least $1-\delta-\delta_{\rm cert}$;
   134	independence is unnecessary.
   135	\end{theorem}
   136	Theorem~\ref{thm:transfer} states the certificate composition directly.
   137	Classical Thompson geometry supplies endpoint and path-length
   138	constructions of its first metric comparison
   139	\cite{nussbaum1994finsler}. The corrected-center construction below
   140	supplies reference confidence without nonlinear optimizer convergence.

   142	\section{A corrected center without optimizer convergence}
   143	Use a differentiable model mean $\mu_\theta(x,a)$ in fixed parameter coordinates $\theta\in\Theta\subseteq\R^d$, with $\Theta$ a convex smoothness region. For $z=(x,a)$, define $q_\theta(z)=\nabla_\theta\mu_\theta(z)$, $q_t(a)=q_{\theta_t}(x_t,a)$, $z_s=(x_s,a_s)$, and $q_s:=q_{\theta_s}(x_s,a_s)$. The representation path $\theta_t\in\Theta$ is predictable. Fix before data collection $\theta^\circ\in\Theta$, $\|\theta^\circ\|_2\le S$, with $\mu_t^*(a)=\mu^*(x_t,a)$ and $\mu^*(z)=\mu_{\theta^\circ}(z)+m^\circ(z)$. Observed rewards obey $r_t=\mu_t^*(a_t)+\eta_t$ and $\mathbb E[e^{u\eta_t}\mid\mathcal H_t]\le e^{u^2\sigma^2/2}$ for every real $u$.
   144	
   145	Supply nonnegative pre-reward bounds $\epsilon_{\rm lin}(s)$ on the absolute Taylor remainder of $\mu_{\theta^\circ}(z_s)$ about $\theta_s$, and $\epsilon_{\rm mis}(s)$ for $|m^\circ(z_s)|$. Define the current actionwise envelopes $\epsilon_{{\rm lin},t}(a),\epsilon_{{\rm mis},t}(a)$ analogously about $\theta_t$; the past sum below is known before selection. Containment alone does not make these errors small. With access to the exact auxiliary frozen ridge estimator, set
   146	\begin{align}
   147	y_s&=r_s-\mu_{\theta_s}(z_s)+q_s^\top\theta_s, &
   148	\widehat\theta_t^{\rm lin}&=\Vbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}q_sy_s, \\
   149	m_t^{\rm corr}(a)&=\mu_{\theta_t}(x_t,a)+q_t(a)^\top(\widehat\theta_t^{\rm lin}-\theta_t).&&
   150	\label{eq:center}
   151	\end{align}
   152	For $\delta\in(0,1)$, self-normalization \cite{abbasi2011improved} and the Taylor envelopes give \eqref{eq:refconf} with probability at least $1-\delta$, simultaneously over rounds and actions, using $m_t=m_t^{\rm corr}$,
   153	\begin{equation}
   154	\beta_t^{\rm corr}=\sqrt{\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta)}+\sqrt{\lambda}S
   155	+\frac1\sigma\sqrt{\sum_{s<t}[\epsilon_{\rm lin}(s)+\epsilon_{\rm mis}(s)]^2},
   156	\quad b_t(a)=\epsilon_{{\rm lin},t}(a)+\epsilon_{{\rm mis},t}(a).
   157	\label{eq:beta}
   158	\end{equation}
   159	No nonlinear optimizer-convergence assumption is used. The exact current squared-loss generalized Gauss-Newton (GGN) metric is $V_t=\lambda I+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}q_{\theta_t}(z_s)q_{\theta_t}(z_s)^\top$.

   161	\section{Controlled audit}
   162	We ran four policies in a nearly linear scaled-tanh bandit with five actions and 29 parameters. For normalized features $\|\phi(z)\|_2\le1$, the mean is $\mu_{\theta,W}(z)=\sqrt W\tanh(\phi(z)^\top\theta/\sqrt W)$. Exact realizability holds with $\|\theta^\circ\|_2\le R=1$; parameters are projected to radius $R$, $\lambda=1$, and Gaussian noise has $\sigma=0.25$. For each horizon $T\in\{250,500,1000\}$ and target $D_{\rm target}\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$, the predeclared rule sets
   163	\begin{equation}
   164	W=\left[\frac{4c_hR\sqrt{T-1}}{\sigma\sqrt\lambda D_{\rm target}}\right]^2,
   165	\qquad c_h=\frac4{3\sqrt3},\qquad L_g=\frac{c_h}{\sqrt W},\qquad
   166	Q_t=\sum_{s<t}\|\theta_t-\theta_s\|_2^2,\quad D_{Q,t}=\frac{2L_g\sqrt{Q_t}}{\sigma\sqrt\lambda}.
   167	\label{eq:experiment-scale}
   168	\end{equation}
   169	At $T=1000$, the chosen scales make the model uniformly close to
   170	its linear counterpart on the stated domain. For
   171	$u=\phi(z)^\top\theta$, $|u|\le1$, and
   172	$f_W(u)=\sqrt W\tanh(u/\sqrt W)$,
   173	\[
   174	0\le1-\frac{f_W(u)}u
   175	\le\frac{u^2}{3W}\le\frac1{3W},
   176	\]
   177	with $f_W(u)/u=1$ by continuity at zero. The four conditionwise
   178	uniform upper bounds range from $1.375\times10^{-7}$ to
   179	$8.798\times10^{-6}$. This quantifies the study's near-linearity;
   180	it is not floating-point exactness or a restriction on what
   181	other transport certificates can achieve.
   182	Here $L_g$ bounds the operator norm of the mean Hessian along replay segments. The target is a worst-case trust-region label, not realized path length or parameter displacement; $W$ is a smoothness scale. Separate runs at each horizon and 50 evaluation seeds give 2,400 trajectories. Ten disjoint tuning seeds selected shared optimizer settings by mean squared prediction error. Policies share context and potential-noise streams but have their own histories, parameters, centers, radii, and metrics. All use dense float64 Cholesky solves and finite score maximization, with failure level $\delta=0.05$. The exact-arithmetic primary instantiation has $C_t=V_t$, $\kappa_{-,t}=\kappa_{+,t}=\alpha_t=1$, and $\xi_t=0$.
   183	
   184	The transport-endpoint policy is a dense endpoint-certificate
   185	comparator. It substitutes
   186	$d_{{\rm Th},t}=\max_i|\log\lambda_i(V_t,\Vbar_t)|$, where
   187	$V_tv_i=\lambda_i\Vbar_tv_i$. This is the smallest nonnegative
   188	common exponent for the symmetric all-direction sandwich in
   189	\eqref{eq:certificates} \cite{nussbaum1994finsler}, not an optimum
   190	over action-specific or statistical certificates. Both matrices
   191	are learner-known before reward observation. The certificate is
   192	valid in exact arithmetic; its float64 evaluation is not a verified
   193	enclosure. Every method's runner computes these eigenvalues, so
   194	reported runtime does not isolate the endpoint policy's incremental
   195	cost. Each policy scores
   196	$m_t^{\rm corr}(a)+\beta_t^{\rm corr}w_t(a)+b_t(a)$ on its own history:
   197	\[
   198	\begin{array}{ll@{\qquad}ll}
   199	\text{transport Hessian:}&w_t=e^{D_{Q,t}/2}s_t,&
   200	\text{transport endpoint:}&w_t=e^{d_{{\rm Th},t}/2}s_t,\\
   201	\text{frozen reference:}&w_t=\bar s_t,&
   202	\text{naive current:}&w_t=s_t.
   203	\end{array}
   204	\]
   205	The primary policy uses $\Dbar_t=D_{Q,t}$; ``Hessian'' refers to
   206	the envelope argument, not its GGN width. Frozen reference applies
   207	the current query to the collection-time Gram; naive current omits
   208	transport. Let $\mathcal D_{{\rm selected},t}$ be the exact
   209	Thompson--Finsler length of the SPD path obtained by interpolating
   210	each replay parameter from $\theta_s$ to $\theta_t$. Analytically,
   211	$d_{{\rm Th},t}\le\mathcal D_{{\rm selected},t}\le D_{Q,t}$.
   212	$\mathcal D_{{\rm quad},t}$ estimates this integral using 32-point
   213	Gauss--Legendre quadrature at the declared checkpoints; unenclosed
   214	quadrature cannot replace the exact integral in that inequality.

   236	Within each fixed $T=1000$ condition, enabling the analytic
   237	transport multiplier in the otherwise matched current-width
   238	policy increased sample mean cumulative pseudo-regret relative
   239	to omitting it (Table~\ref{tab:audit}). The policies use the same
   240	state-to-score and update rules apart from this multiplier, with
   241	common context and potential-noise streams; their subsequent
   242	histories may diverge. This descriptive total-policy contrast
   243	does not identify a fixed-history mechanism, per-seed dominance,
   244	or a cross-condition dose response, since $D_{\rm target}$ also
   245	changes $W$. In each of the 12 horizon-target cells, 50/50 primary trajectories passed both all-action, all-round reference-confidence and optimism diagnostics, with cellwise exact 95\% Clopper-Pearson interval $[92.9\%,100\%]$. Base seeds are reused across cells; no deterministic audit failed. These tolerance-based float64 checks and quadrature values are diagnostics, not verified enclosures. Across the 12 primary cells, medians of per-trajectory ratios of the numerical right-hand side (RHS) of \eqref{eq:regret} to pseudo-regret range from $126.7$ to $155.7$. Every primary trajectory's terminal RHS exceeds $2T$, the pseudo-regret cap from $|\mu^*(z)|\le1$, despite unbounded Gaussian observations.
   246	
   247	\section{Implications for adaptive experimentation}
   248	The theorem supplies reward optimism and a regret bound on the joint
   249	confidence and certificate event; certificate validity alone does
   250	not ensure useful allocation. In this nearly linear simulation,
   251	the analytic multiplier increased sample mean pseudo-regret relative
   252	to its omission within each fixed condition. This does not establish
   253	a uniform policy ordering, a cross-condition dose response, or
   254	treatment-effect inference after adaptive assignment.
