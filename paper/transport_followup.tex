\section{What confidence transport did in the nonlinear study}
\label{sec:transport-followup}

Post-outcome score diagnostics show that the Hessian policy's bonus determines
its action by current width alone on almost all rounds: the gap between the
two largest width bonuses exceeds the full center range on 97.104\%,
99.372\% and 99.508\% of rounds at $W=16,1,0.25$. Its action agrees with the
current-width argmax on 99.382\%, 99.962\% and 99.982\% of rounds.
Its mean pseudo-regret is close to the context-free benchmark's 185.26,
183.06 and 176.67. Endpoint, frozen reference and naive current are not
separated by this study: their mean regrets are within about one unit in
each condition (\Cref{tab:transport-followup-outcomes}). No equivalence test
was performed. All nine pointwise paired comparisons favor these comparators
over Hessian. Both Hessian's bounds and the frozen policy's own bounds exceed
the deterministic cap, while every recorded confidence and optimism check passes.
\Cref{sec:transport-v3} reports a second prespecified study in which the
frozen bound is informative and two generalized-linear baselines are added.

\paragraph{Protocol and policies.}
The prespecified study fixes $T=1000$, $W\in\{16,1,0.25\}$ and 50 paired
seeds per policy--condition cell (600 trajectories). A fixed teacher generates
Gaussian scaled-tanh rewards. The shared projected optimizer was selected by
prediction MSE before evaluation; the implementation uses dense float64
Cholesky solves. Appendix~\ref{app:transport-followup} gives the full model,
protocol, splits and checks.

All policies update their representations and use the corrected center and
radius of \Cref{cor:corrected-center-new}. Hessian inflates the current width
by the Hessian path certificate $\bar D_t^Q$ of
\Cref{cor:hessian-path-new}. Endpoint uses the dense endpoint Thompson
 distance; frozen reference uses the reference width directly; naive current
uses the current width without transport as an uncertified control.

\paragraph{Regret outcomes.}
\Cref{fig:transport-followup-outcomes} plots the policy outcomes;
\Cref{tab:transport-followup-outcomes} gives the numerical values. Each paired
 difference is a comparator's cumulative pseudo-regret minus Hessian's on the
same evaluation seed. All nine 95\% intervals exclude zero in the
comparator's favor; they use 10,000 paired seed-bootstrap resamples.
For descriptive context, the best-fixed-action-in-hindsight means are 180.01,
177.97 and 171.84 at $W=16,1,0.25$, using the same streams as the context-free
benchmark defined in the appendix.

\paragraph{Bounds relative to the reward cap.}
The reward range gives the deterministic cap $2T\min(1,\sqrt W)$, equal to
2000, 2000 and 1000. A bound above it is \emph{vacuous}. Every Hessian bound
and all 150 frozen-policy own-history bounds exceed their caps.
The base-ten logarithms of Hessian's mean terminal RHS/cap are approximately
9.51, 37.19 and 80.91; the frozen policy's mean ratios are approximately
19, 60 and 231 (\Cref{fig:transport-followup-bound-scale}). Path inflation
is Hessian's largest bound contribution in all three conditions.

Every policy uses the Taylor envelope at the current parameter norm,
$\epslin=\tfrac12(c_h/\sqrt W)(R+\|\btheta_t\|_2)^2$ with $c_h=4/(3\sqrt3)$
and $R=B_\phi=1$, both for the bias $b_t$ and for the historical terms of
$\beta_t$. At $\|\btheta_t\|_2=R$ it equals the constant $2c_hR^2/\sqrt W$ of
\Cref{cor:global-near-linear-new}: 0.385, 1.540 and 3.079 at $W=16,1,0.25$.
The per-run mean envelope is 98.3\%--98.5\% of that constant (median over
seeds, for every policy and condition).
Three terms set the scale of the frozen bound
$2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\gamma_T\sum_t\beta_t^2}+2\sum_tb_t(a_t)$ of
\Cref{prop:frozen-width-ucb}: the exploration term with $\beta_t$ reduced to
its stochastic and ridge parts; the remainder of the exploration term, due to
the historical Taylor part of $\beta_t$; and the bias term. On the frozen
policy's own histories their medians are 10,155, 26,898 and 758 at $W=16$;
10,122, 108,171 and 3,031 at $W=1$; and 10,001, 215,317 and 6,063 at
$W=0.25$. The first term alone exceeds every cap: with $d=29$ and $T=1000$
the realized $\gamma_T$ is about 151--153 (medians). It contains no Taylor
term, and on the same queries a linear model's frozen matrix dominates the
scaled-tanh one because every collection weight satisfies
$\operatorname{sech}^4\le1$. On the 150 recorded context--action sequences,
an exactly linear model's frozen bound would therefore exceed its own cap
$2T=2000$ as well. The historical term grows as $1/\sqrt W$ and is the
largest term at every $W$; the bias term is about $2T$ times the mean
envelope.





\paragraph{Confidence and drift checks.}
All 600 trajectories, including naive current, pass simultaneous reference
confidence, method optimism and deterministic diagnostics: each policy--condition
cell has 50/50 successes, with an exact 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval of
approximately $[92.9\%,100\%]$. Hessian reaches endpoint distance above one
in 16/50 seeds at $W=0.25$; distances are at least one in 286/50,000
seed--round observations (\Cref{fig:transport-followup-drift-bounds}).

\paragraph{Exploratory decisions on recorded histories.}
Appendix~\ref{app:transport-followup} expands the post-outcome width diagnostics
above: it evaluates the same Hessian score on all four behavior histories,
compares fixed-history recommendations, and checks sensitivity to early
numerical rankings. The bonus-gap condition above is sufficient for the
largest-width action to maximize the score.

\paragraph{Exploratory endpoint audit.}
The relative-weight certificate of \Cref{cor:tanh-relative-weights} compares
collection-time and current squared gradient multipliers within fixed feature
categories. After outcomes were observed, replay with the recorded actions and
rewards audits all 600 histories. On Hessian's histories, the category-specific
outward factor has mean effective-width overheads $0.28\%$--$12.68\%$
relative to the reference width, versus $0.16\%$--$12.13\%$ for dense endpoint
across conditions (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-followup}).

\subsection{A second study in which the frozen bound is informative}
\label{sec:transport-v3}

Every certified bound in the first study is vacuous, so it cannot show what
transport does where the theory is informative. A second prespecified study
chooses a model and, in its design cell, a scale at which the frozen bound is
below the cap on every trajectory, and adds two baselines: UCB-GLM \citep{li2017provably} and a
linear UCB. In its design cell, endpoint transport and naive current are
equivalent to frozen scoring within the prespecified margin, the Hessian
width costs 16.8 regret units relative to frozen, and UCB-GLM has 34.4 units
less regret than frozen. In three descriptive cells with stronger curvature
every certified bound is vacuous, and at $W=1$ UCB-GLM has about 7.7 times
the regret of linear UCB.

\paragraph{Design.}
Contexts are drawn uniformly from the four vectors $(\pm1,\pm1)/\sqrt2$, there
are $K=3$ actions, and $\phi(x,a)=x\otimes e_a\in\R^6$, so $\|\phi\|_2=B_\phi=1$
and there are 12 feature categories. The teacher has unit norm; rewards are
Gaussian with scaled-tanh mean and $\sigma=0.25$; $\lambda=1$, $\delta=0.05$,
$R=1$ and $T=20{,}000$. The design cell sets $W=T$. There the a priori frozen
bound, from \eqref{eq:finite-d-gamma-new} and the envelopes of the first study,
is 37,195, which is 0.93 of the cap $2T\min(1,\sqrt W)=40{,}000$; but
curvature is negligible, since \Cref{cor:tanh-relative-weights} gives
$d_{\rm Th}(\bVbar_t,\bV_t)\le4\log\cosh(1/\sqrt W)\approx1.0\times10^{-4}$.
Three descriptive cells, $W=100,10,1$, have stronger curvature and vacuous
certified bounds; they were chosen after development output was read and
carry no test. Each cell has 50 paired evaluation seeds, and all policies in a
cell share a seed's context and potential-outcome streams. One optimizer,
selected in the design cell by prediction MSE on uniform-random histories, is
used in every cell (Appendix~\ref{app:transport-v3}).

\paragraph{Policies.}
The four corrected-center policies are those of the first study. UCB-GLM is
Algorithm~1 of \citet{li2017provably} with link $\mu(u)=\sqrt W\tanh(u/\sqrt W)$.
It plays uniformly random actions until the smallest eigenvalue of
$\sum_iX_iX_i^\top$ reaches one, then plays the maximizer of
$X^\top\hat\theta_t+\alpha\|X\|_{V_t^{-1}}$, where $\hat\theta_t$ solves the GLM
score equation $\sum_{i<t}(Y_i-\mu(X_i^\top\theta))X_i=0$,
$V_t=\sum_{i<t}X_iX_i^\top$, $\alpha=(\sigma/\kappa)\sqrt{(d/2)\log(1+2T/d)
+\log(1/\delta)}$ and $\kappa=\operatorname{sech}^2((R+1)/\sqrt W)$. The rule
that ends the random phase is ours: their theorem fixes its length only up to
a universal constant. Their analysis states rewards in $[0,1]$, so its regret
bound does not apply to these Gaussian rewards. Linear UCB fits ridge
regression to raw rewards with metric $\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\phi_s
\phi_s^\top$ and radius $\sqrt{\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta)}+\sqrt\lambda R$;
under scaled-tanh means it is uncertified. The descriptive cells add
envelope-free frozen and naive policies, which drop the historical Taylor
part of $\beta_t$ and the bias ($b_t=0$). They are uncertified ablations that
show which terms make the certified bounds vacuous.

\paragraph{Design checks and primary comparisons.}
\Cref{tab:transport-v3-design} gives the design cell. All six policies have
lower mean regret than the context-free benchmark (one-sided paired $t$-tests,
Holm over six, every $p\le1.2\times10^{-108}$). Frozen reference, endpoint and
naive current are equivalent within the prespecified margin $\Delta=3.0$
regret units: for each of the three pairs the two-sided 90\% paired-$t$
interval lies inside $(-1.0,1.0)$ (two one-sided tests, intersection--union).
All 50 frozen bounds are below the cap; the largest is 0.761 of it. Both primary
comparisons, two-sided paired $t$-tests with Holm over the two, reject:
UCB-GLM minus frozen is $-34.4$ ($p=5.7\times10^{-34}$) and Hessian minus
frozen is $16.8$ ($p=5.3\times10^{-30}$). These checks and comparisons concern
one near-linear cell, in which equivalence of the first three policies is
expected by construction.

\paragraph{Numerical checks.}
All 1,500 evaluation runs completed with no crash and no recorded failure.
UCB-GLM's Newton solves always converged, and its random phase lasted 12 to 34
rounds. In the 1,100 corrected-center runs, the category-count current matrix,
the frozen matrix and the drift sum $Q_t$ of \Cref{cor:hessian-path-new} were
checked against dense replay at six rounds, 19,800 checks in all; the largest
discrepancy was 0.039 of its tolerance. No run that records the optimism
check failed it; UCB-GLM does not record it.

\paragraph{What the second study shows.}
Where the frozen bound is informative and curvature is negligible, endpoint
transport and naive current match frozen scoring within the margin, which is
consistent with the bound comparison after \Cref{cor:tanh-endpoint-regime}
(that comparison concerns bound expressions, not realized regret), and the
Hessian path certificate costs regret even there. UCB-GLM, whose regret
theorem does not cover Gaussian rewards, has the lowest mean regret in the
design cell and at $W=100$ and $10$, but not at $W=1$. In the curved cells,
removing the envelope terms lowers frozen's mean regret from 440.9--1,902.1 to
43.3--43.9, while the frozen, endpoint and naive widths differ in mean regret
by at most 23.1 units. These are results for one synthetic model and one selected optimizer.
