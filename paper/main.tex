% --- abstract -------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Contextual-bandit confidence is naturally built from gradient features fixed
when rewards are collected, whereas a retrained model may score actions with a
current relinearized generalized Gauss--Newton or Fisher metric.  We study when
the former can justify the latter.  A Thompson--Finsler path certificate
transports frozen confidence to current curvature; two-sided operator bounds and
a certified solver width then connect that metric to the numerical score used
for action selection.  The resulting conditional UCB theorem closes regret
through frozen sequential information gain and allows measurable approximate
selection on standard-Borel action spaces.  For differentiable squared-loss
models, a corrected center establishes the reference-confidence event for any
predictable parameter path in a certified region, without a nonlinear optimizer
convergence assumption.  Historical and current approximation errors remain
explicit in the bound.  A locked 29-parameter scaled-tanh audit records
simultaneous float64 confidence and optimism in all 600 transport-Hessian runs
and no deterministic audit failure.  It also finds that the analytic path
certificate is highly conservative, every reported numerical regret bound is
vacuous relative to the deterministic cap, and all three comparison policies
have lower sample mean regret at the primary horizon.  The theory is conditional
on valid pre-reward confidence, path, operator, and solver certificates; the
experiment is a controlled audit rather than evidence of a general curvature
advantage.
\end{abstract}
