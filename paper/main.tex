\title{Curvature-Calibrated Exploration for Foundation-Model Bandits}
\author{Anonymous Author(s)}

\begin{document}

% --- abstract -------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
We study contextual bandits where expected reward is parameterized by a
differentiable parametric model, with foundation-model deployments as
one motivating example.  Efficient exploration requires calibrated epistemic
uncertainty over model predictions, yet standard neural bandit methods
approximate the loss curvature with diagonal or block-diagonal matrices,
distorting exploration bonuses and degrading regret.  We propose
\emph{Curvature-Calibrated UCB} (CC-UCB), which computes the
Laplace-approximate predictive variance induced by the \emph{full}
Gauss--Newton (GGN) curvature matrix via conjugate-gradient solves that
access curvature only through curvature--vector products---without
materializing or compressing the curvature matrix.
For the Gaussian/squared-loss instantiation, we provide a conditional
regret decomposition for exact-curvature CC-UCB under high-level
local-linearization, prediction-stability, and feature-drift
assumptions.  The result isolates the regret cost of CG truncation and
spectral curvature distortion, but it is potentially nonvacuous mainly
in frozen-backbone, near-NTK, or otherwise near-stationary fine-tuning
regimes; we do not establish these conditions for unrestricted
end-to-end optimization.
A spectral-sandwich corollary quantifies how replacing the full
curvature with a spectral approximation inflates the exploration term.
The same matrix-free CG/CVP machinery extends algorithmically to
exponential-family likelihoods via the Fisher/GGN curvature, but no
regret guarantee beyond Gaussian squared loss is claimed in this draft.
\end{abstract}

% --- body -----------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Contextual bandits provide a principled framework for sequential
decision-making under partial feedback.  When the reward predictor is a deep
network---a setting addressed by \emph{neural bandits}
\citep{zhou2020neural,riquelme2018deep}---the central algorithmic challenge is
constructing an exploration bonus that faithfully reflects epistemic
uncertainty over predictions.

Large differentiable parametric models---including pretrained networks
for text, vision, or multimodal domains---are increasingly deployed as
reward predictors in recommendation, content selection, and scientific
design loops.  The theory developed here applies to generic
differentiable parametric models; foundation models are a motivating
application class, not a requirement.
In the \emph{white-box} setting, where gradient and
curvature--vector product access to the model is available, one can in
principle compute the
Laplace-approximate predictive variance:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance-intro}
  s_t^2(a) \;=\; \bg_t(a)^\top \bC_t^{-1}\,\bg_t(a),
\end{equation}
where $\bg_t(a) = \nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$ is the gradient
feature (Jacobian of the mean reward) and $\bC_t$ is the positive-definite
curvature matrix---the damped Gauss--Newton (GGN)---at the
current parameter estimate~$\btheta_t$.

Existing neural bandit algorithms sidestep the cost of~\eqref{eq:variance-intro}
by replacing $\bC_t$ with a tractable surrogate: a diagonal approximation, a
block-diagonal or Kronecker-factored estimate, or a last-layer restriction
that discards backbone curvature entirely
\citep{zhou2020neural,riquelme2018deep,ritter2018scalable}.
These approximations change the
\emph{spectrum} of the curvature matrix, which in turn distorts the variance
proxy~$s_t^2(a)$---over-exploring along directions where curvature is
underestimated and under-exploring where it is overestimated.

We argue that recent advances in scalable curvature--vector product
computation---enabled by automatic differentiation and sharded parameter
layouts (e.g., FSDP) \citep{granziol2026hessian}---make it practical to solve
$\bC_t\,\bu = \bg_t(a)$ by conjugate gradients (CG) \emph{without
forming}~$\bC_t$; per-sample gradient features are not stored---only $O(d)$
working vectors for CG are maintained, with CVPs computed on the fly.
Because the damped GGN is symmetric positive definite by
construction, CG convergence is guaranteed.  Building on this observation, we
propose \textbf{Curvature-Calibrated UCB (CC-UCB)}, a neural bandit
algorithm that uses the full curvature matrix---accessed through implicit CG
solves---to produce exploration bonuses consistent with the uncompressed
Laplace posterior.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1)}~We formalize the curvature-calibrated exploration framework for
contextual bandits with differentiable parametric reward predictors
(motivated by foundation-model deployments), introducing both
a relinearized algorithmic curvature~$\bC_t$ and a frozen-feature Gram
matrix~$\bCbar_t$ that satisfies the predictability and sequential-update
properties required for rigorous self-normalized analysis
(\Cref{sec:problem,sec:method}).
\textbf{(2)}~We provide a modular conditional regret decomposition under
explicit assumptions on local linearization, prediction stability, and
feature drift.  The result converts externally supplied bounds on these
quantities into regret; it is not a full nonlinear fine-tuning theorem
(\Cref{sec:theory}).
\textbf{(3)}~We outline an evaluation protocol designed to test when
full-curvature exploration matters relative to last-layer and diagonal
surrogates (deferred to \Cref{sec:experiments} in the appendix; no
empirical results are included in this draft).
The regret analysis is carried out for the Gaussian/squared-loss
instantiation; the underlying CG/CVP machinery applies algorithmically
to exponential-family likelihoods via the Fisher/GGN curvature, but we
do not prove a regret theorem beyond the Gaussian case
(\Cref{sec:expfam-ext}).

\section{Problem Setup}\label{sec:problem}

We present the problem setup for the \emph{Gaussian/squared-loss}
instantiation, which is the setting analyzed in \Cref{thm:regret}.
An extension to exponential-family likelihoods is discussed in
\Cref{sec:expfam-ext}.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
At each round $t=1,\dots,T$ the learner observes a context
$\bx_t\in\mathcal{X}$ and a finite action set~$\cA_t$.  It selects
$a_t\in\cA_t$ and observes a stochastic reward
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reward}
  r_t \;=\; \mu^*(\bx_t, a_t) + \eta_t, \qquad
  \eta_t \text{ is } \sigma\text{-sub-Gaussian},
\end{equation}
where $\mu^*:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}\to\R$ is the unknown mean reward.
The goal is to minimize cumulative regret
$R_T = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl[\mu^*(\bx_t,a_t^*) - \mu^*(\bx_t,a_t)\bigr]$,
where $a_t^* = \argmax_{a\in\cA_t}\mu^*(\bx_t,a)$.
We assume $|\cA_t|\le A_{\max}$ deterministically for all~$t$.

\paragraph{Filtration.}
Define $\cF_0 = \sigma(\btheta_1)$ and, for $t\ge 1$,
\[
  \cF_t \;:=\;
  \sigma\!\bigl(\btheta_1,\,\bx_1,a_1,r_1,\,\ldots,\,\bx_t,a_t,r_t\bigr).
\]
At round~$t$: the context $\bx_t$ is revealed; the agent selects $a_t$
(measurable with respect to $\cF_{t-1}\cup\sigma(\bx_t)$); then the
reward $r_t = \mu^*(\bx_t,a_t)+\eta_t$ is observed.
Since $\btheta_t$ is determined by $\cF_{t-1}$, the realized design
vector $\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)$ is measurable with respect to the pre-reward
sigma-algebra $\cG_t=\cF_{t-1}\cup\sigma(\bx_t,a_t)$.
Candidate-action features $\bg_t(\bx_t,a)$ are measurable after $\bx_t$
and the action set are observed.
We write $\cG_t := \cF_{t-1}\cup\sigma(\bx_t,a_t)$ for the sigma-algebra
available immediately before the reward is revealed.

\paragraph{Reward model.}
We maintain a parametric model $\mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a)$ with
$\btheta\in\R^d$, implemented by a differentiable parametric model
(e.g.\ a pretrained network composed with a reward head).
Define the regularized loss at round~$t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erm}
  \cL_t(\btheta)
  \;:=\;
    \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
      \bigl(r_s - \mu_{\btheta}(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)^2
    \;+\; \frac{\lambda}{2}\|\btheta\|^2.
\end{equation}
We write $\btheta_t$ for the parameter returned by the warm-started
optimizer on~$\cL_t$.
In convex settings (e.g.\ frozen backbone with a linear head) this
coincides with the global minimizer; in nonconvex settings $\btheta_t$
may be a local minimizer or approximate stationary point.
The gap between $\btheta_t$ and an idealized global minimizer is absorbed
by the prediction-stability assumption (\Cref{asm:optim}).

\paragraph{Gradient features and curvature matrices.}
Define the \emph{gradient feature} (Jacobian of the mean reward) at the
current parameter estimate:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gfeat}
  \bg_t(\bx,a) \;:=\; \nabla_{\btheta}\,\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx,a)
  \;\in\;\R^d.
\end{equation}
The algorithm uses the \emph{relinearized curvature matrix}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature}
  \bC_t
  \;=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\,\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^{\!\top},
\end{equation}
the \emph{damped GGN curvature} for the Gaussian/squared-loss model
at the current parameter~$\btheta_t$.
(For this likelihood, the GGN coincides with the true Fisher
information; see the remark below.)
While $\bC_t$ is the natural object for
computing predictive variances at $\btheta_t$, it
re-evaluates all past Jacobians at~$\btheta_t$ and therefore does
\emph{not} admit a sequential rank-one update---all summands change
when $\btheta_t$ changes.

For the regret analysis we introduce the
\emph{frozen-feature Gram matrix}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature-bar}
  \bCbar_t
  \;:=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)\,\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^{\!\top},
\end{equation}
where each $\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx_s,a_s)$
is evaluated at the parameter~$\btheta_s$ that was current when the sample
was collected.  This matrix satisfies the recursive update
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bar-update}
  \bCbar_{t+1}=\bCbar_t+\sigma^{-2}\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)^\top,
\end{equation}
and, critically, each realized design vector
$\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$ is measurable with respect to the pre-reward
sigma-algebra $\cG_s=\cF_{s-1}\cup\sigma(\bx_s,a_s)$, which is the
filtration used in the self-normalized bound.
Both the additive update~\eqref{eq:bar-update} and
this measurability are structural requirements of the
self-normalized martingale bound and the elliptic potential lemma invoked
in~\Cref{sec:theory}.
The algorithm computes UCB widths using~$\bC_t$ (via CG), but the regret
analysis is conducted in terms of~$\bCbar_t$;
\Cref{asm:drift} below provides the bridge.

\paragraph{Auxiliary linearized ridge estimator.}
For the analysis we define a \emph{linearized ridge estimator} based on
frozen features.  Set the predictable intercept
$b_s := \mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx_s,a_s) - \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\btheta_s$
and the pseudo-response $y_s := r_s - b_s$.
Under \Cref{asm:linear} and realizability ($\mu^*=\mu_{\btheta^*}$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pseudo-response}
  y_s
  \;=\;
  \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\btheta^*
  \;+\;
  \rho_s(\bx_s,a_s,\btheta^*)
  \;+\;
  \eta_s,
\end{equation}
where $|\rho_s|\le\epslin(s)$.
The linearized estimator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thetahat-lin}
  \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
  \;:=\;
  \argmin_{\btheta}\,\Biggl[\,
    \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
      \bigl(y_s - \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\btheta\bigr)^2
    \;+\;\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\btheta\|^2
  \Biggr]
  \;=\;
  \bCbar_t^{-1}\,\frac{1}{\sigma^2}
    \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)\,y_s.
\end{equation}
This is a standard linear ridge regression with predictable design
vectors~$\bg_s/\sigma$ and Gram matrix~$\bCbar_t$, so the
self-normalized martingale bound
\citep{abbasi2011improved} applies to it directly
(\Cref{lem:confidence}).

\paragraph{GGN approximation quality.}
For squared loss~\eqref{eq:erm} the full Hessian of the data-fit term is
\[
  \bH_t \;=\; \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
  \Bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top
  \;-\; \bigl(r_s - \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)
        \nabla^2\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)\Bigr]
  \;+\;\lambda\bI.
\]
The curvature matrix $\bC_t$ drops the residual-times-second-derivative
term, retaining only the outer-product part that is guaranteed PSD.
The dropped residual-times-second-derivative term
$D_t=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}(r_s{-}\mu_{\btheta_t})\nabla^2\mu_{\btheta_t}$
has operator norm at most
$(t{-}1)\epsilon_r H/\sigma^2$, where $\epsilon_r$ bounds the residuals and
$H$ the Hessian norms.
When this quantity is small relative to~$\lambda$, $\bC_t$ approximates
the full Hessian in the Loewner sense (a formal statement with proof is
given in \Cref{app:ggn-approx}).
This comparison is \emph{not} invoked in the regret analysis
(\Cref{thm:regret} uses $\bC_t$ directly as the exploration metric);
it serves only to contextualize $\bC_t$ as a proxy for the full
Hessian.  Outside the small-residual regime, we treat $\bC_t$ as a PSD
exploration metric rather than the exact Laplace covariance.

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Remark (Fisher, empirical Fisher, and GGN).}
Three curvature objects appear in the literature and must be
distinguished.
The \emph{empirical Fisher} is the realized score outer product
$\sum_s\nabla_{\btheta}\log p(r_s\!\mid\!\bx_s,a_s;\btheta)\,
 \nabla_{\btheta}\log p(r_s\!\mid\!\bx_s,a_s;\btheta)^\top$;
it depends on observed rewards and is \emph{not} the operator used by
CC-UCB.
The \emph{true (model) Fisher} is the model expectation of the score
outer product,
$F(\btheta)=\mathbb{E}_{r\sim p(\cdot\mid\bx,a;\btheta)}
 [\nabla_{\btheta}\log p\;\nabla_{\btheta}\log p^\top]$.
The \emph{generalized Gauss--Newton} (GGN) drops the
residual-times-Hessian term from the full Hessian, retaining only the
outer-product Jacobian part that is guaranteed PSD
\citep{martens2020new,schraudolph2002fast}.
For Gaussian regression with fixed variance (the squared-loss setting
of this paper), the true Fisher and GGN are identical, and both
reduce to the outer-product curvature $\sigma^{-2}\sum_s\bg_s\bg_s^\top$
used in~\eqref{eq:curvature}.
More generally, for exponential-family negative log-likelihoods
parameterized by natural parameters $z_{\btheta}(\bx,a)$, the true
Fisher equals the GGN:
$J_{\btheta}^\top\nabla^2 A(z)\,J_{\btheta}$,
where $J_{\btheta}=\nabla_{\btheta}z_{\btheta}$ and
$\nabla^2 A$ is the Hessian of the log-partition function
\citep{martens2020new,pascanu2014revisiting}.
Bernoulli rewards yield scalar weight~$p(1{-}p)$;
categorical rewards yield $\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbf{p})-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^\top$.
See \Cref{sec:expfam-ext} for the extension.
\end{quote}

We access $\bC_t$ only through curvature--vector products
$\bv\mapsto\bC_t\,\bv$, computed via forward-mode Jacobian--vector
products composed with reverse-mode vector--Jacobian products
\citep{pearlmutter1994fast,schraudolph2002fast}.

\paragraph{Extension to exponential-family likelihoods.}%
\label{sec:expfam-ext}
The same matrix-free CG/CVP machinery extends algorithmically to
exponential-family likelihoods (e.g.\ Bernoulli or categorical rewards)
via the Fisher/GGN curvature $J_t^\top(\nabla^2 A)\,J_t$, but no regret
guarantee beyond Gaussian squared loss is claimed in this draft.
Details of the curvature definitions are in \Cref{app:expfam}.

\subsection{Assumptions}\label{sec:assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[Sub-Gaussian noise]\label{asm:noise}
The noise $\eta_t$ is conditionally $\sigma$-sub-Gaussian given the
context and action:
$\mathbb{E}[\exp(s\eta_t)\mid\cG_t]\le\exp(s^2\sigma^2/2)$
for all~$s\in\R$, where
$\cG_t := \cF_{t-1}\cup\sigma(\bx_t,a_t)$ is the sigma-algebra
available immediately before the reward is revealed
(see the filtration defined above).
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded gradient features]\label{asm:bounded}
For a constant $G>0$,
\[
  \sup_{t\le T}\;\sup_{s\le t}\;\sup_{a}
  \|\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a)\| \;\le\; G.
\]
This bounds both the current-round features
$\bg_t(\bx_t,a)$ and the replayed features
$\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)$ appearing in the curvature
matrix~\eqref{eq:curvature}, as well as the frozen features
$\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$ in~\eqref{eq:curvature-bar}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Local linearization]\label{asm:linear}
For each $(\bx,a)$, the reward model admits the expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearization}
  \mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a) \;=\;
    \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx,a)
    + \bg_t(\bx,a)^\top(\btheta - \btheta_t)
    + \rho_t(\bx,a,\btheta),
\end{equation}
where the remainder satisfies
$|\rho_t(\bx,a,\btheta)| \le \tfrac{L}{2}\|\btheta - \btheta_t\|^2$
for a constant~$L\ge 0$.
Evaluating at $\btheta=\btheta^*$ gives a time-varying linearization error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epslin-t}
  \epslin(t)
  \;:=\;
  \sup_{a\in\cA_t}|\rho_t(\bx_t,a,\btheta^*)|
  \;\le\;
  \frac{L}{2}\|\btheta^* - \btheta_t\|^2.
\end{equation}
For total regret to be sublinear, we require the cumulative linearization
error $E_T:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\epslin(t) = o(T)$; see \Cref{thm:regret}
for the precise dependence.  Concretely, $E_T=O(1)$ when
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(1/t)$ (since
$\epslin(t)=O(1/t^2)$ and $\sum t^{-2}<\infty$;
achievable in convex or strongly convex regimes),
and $E_T=O(\sqrt{T})$ when
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(t^{-1/4})$.
In practice this is enforced by restricting fine-tuning depth,
using trust-region updates, or freezing the backbone (which renders the
last-layer model convex or near-linear with small~$L$).
These rate examples are illustrative only.  The present paper does not
prove convergence of the nonconvex optimization path
$\{\btheta_t\}$ to~$\btheta^*$; sublinear regret therefore requires
this term to be controlled by an external stability or convergence
argument.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Prediction stability]\label{asm:optim}
The nonlinear MAP estimate~$\btheta_t$ and the auxiliary linearized ridge
estimator~$\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$ from~\eqref{eq:thetahat-lin}
satisfy, for all actions $a\in\cA_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pred-stab}
  \bigl|\bg_t(\bx_t,a)^\top
    \bigl(\btheta_t - \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}\bigr)\bigr|
  \;\le\;
  \eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}},
\end{equation}
for a known tolerance sequence $\{\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}\}$.
This tolerance absorbs three sources of discrepancy:
(i)~optimization error (if $\btheta_t$ is only an approximate
stationary point of~$\cL_t$, the gradient residual
$\|\nabla\cL_t(\btheta_t)\|\le\eta_t^{\mathrm{opt}}$ contributes
at most $\eta_t^{\mathrm{opt}}\cdot G/\lambda$ under
$\nabla^2\cL_t\succeq\lambda\bI$);
(ii)~feature drift between~$\bg_s$ (used by~$\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$)
and~$\bg_t$ (used by~$\btheta_t$); and
(iii)~higher-order nonlinear terms.
In the convex-in-parameters regime (frozen backbone with a linear reward
head), $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}=\btheta_t$ and
$\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}=0$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[Status of Assumption~\ref{asm:optim}]
At present we do not provide a primitive sufficient condition implying
$\sum_{t=1}^T \eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}} = o(T)$ outside the convex
last-layer regime.  Accordingly, \Cref{thm:regret} should be read as a
conditional reduction, not as a full regret theorem for unrestricted
nonlinear fine-tuning.
Assumption~\ref{asm:optim} is the main unresolved nonlinear term in the
present draft: outside convex last-layer or similarly near-linear
regimes, we do not know a primitive sufficient condition implying
$\sum_{t=1}^T \eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}} = o(T)$.
\end{remark}

\begin{assumption}[Feature drift bound]\label{asm:drift}
There exists $\epsdrift \in [0,1)$ such that, for all $t \le T$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift-sandwich}
  (1 - \epsdrift)\,\bCbar_t
  \;\preceq\;
  \bC_t
  \;\preceq\;
  (1 + \epsdrift)\,\bCbar_t.
\end{equation}
This holds trivially with $\epsdrift=0$ when features do not drift
(frozen backbone, or the NTK regime where
$\bg_t\approx\bg_s$ for all $s,t$).
A uniform bound $\epsdrift<1$ is plausible whenever feature drift
is controlled relative to the regularization level (e.g.\ frozen
backbone plus trainable head, or small learning-rate fine-tuning); we
do not claim it holds generically for unrestricted deep networks.
The algorithm requires a known upper bound~$\epsdriftbar\in[\epsdrift,1)$
as input (\Cref{alg:ccucb}); the strict inequality $\epsdriftbar<1$ is
needed for $\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}$ to be finite.
\end{assumption}

The following lemma gives a concrete sufficient condition for
\Cref{asm:drift} in terms of Jacobian Lipschitz continuity and
parameter stability.

\begin{lemma}[Sufficient condition for feature-drift bound]\label{lem:drift-sufficient}
Suppose $\btheta\mapsto\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a)$ is
$L_g$-Lipschitz for all $(\bx,a)$, and
$\|\btheta_t - \btheta_s\| \le \Delta$ for all $s \le t \le T$.
Then for all $t$,
\[
  \|\bC_t - \bCbar_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  \;\le\;
  \frac{(t{-}1)}{\sigma^2}\,(2GL_g\Delta + L_g^2\Delta^2).
\]
In particular, if
$(t{-}1)(2GL_g\Delta + L_g^2\Delta^2)/(\sigma^2\lambda) \le \epsdrift$
for all $t \le T$, then \Cref{asm:drift} holds.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $\bg_t^{(s)} := \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)$ and
$\bg_s := \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$.
Then $\bC_t - \bCbar_t
= \sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}(\bg_t^{(s)}{\bg_t^{(s)}}^\top
  - \bg_s\bg_s^\top)$.
For each summand, write $\delta_s := \bg_t^{(s)} - \bg_s$ with
$\|\delta_s\| \le L_g\Delta$.
Then
$\bg_t^{(s)}{\bg_t^{(s)}}^\top - \bg_s\bg_s^\top
= \delta_s\delta_s^\top + \delta_s\bg_s^\top + \bg_s\delta_s^\top$,
so
$\|\bg_t^{(s)}{\bg_t^{(s)}}^\top - \bg_s\bg_s^\top\|
\le \|\delta_s\|^2 + 2\|\bg_s\|\|\delta_s\|
\le L_g^2\Delta^2 + 2GL_g\Delta$.
Summing over $s<t$ gives the bound.
Since $\bCbar_t \succeq \lambda\bI$, the additive bound
$\|\bC_t-\bCbar_t\| \le \epsdrift\lambda$ implies the
multiplicative sandwich~\eqref{eq:drift-sandwich} by the same
standard Loewner argument (used also in the GGN approximation bound;
see \Cref{app:ggn-approx}).
\end{proof}

Concretely, in the frozen-backbone regime ($\Delta=0$) or the NTK regime
($L_g\Delta \ll G$), $\epsdrift$ is small.
Under last-$k$-layer fine-tuning with SGD step size~$\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}$,
$\Delta = O(\eta_{\mathrm{lr}}\sum \|\nabla\cL_s\|)$; bounding the
gradient norms over training gives an explicit~$\epsdrift$ as a function
of the learning rate and training budget.

\begin{remark}[Implication of the drift sufficient condition]
In the small-drift regime $L_g\Delta \ll G$,
\Cref{lem:drift-sufficient} scales as
$\epsdrift \gtrsim 2(t{-}1)GL_g\Delta/(\sigma^2\lambda)$.
Thus a horizon-uniform bound $\epsdrift<1$ over $t\le T$ requires
$\Delta = O(\sigma^2\lambda/(TGL_g))$ up to constants.
This makes the lemma most plausible in frozen-backbone, NTK, or
otherwise near-stationary regimes rather than unrestricted end-to-end
fine-tuning.
\end{remark}

A heuristic route toward bounding $\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$ under local
strong convexity is sketched in the remark after \Cref{lem:discrepancy}:
if $\nabla^2\cL_t \succeq \lambda\bI$ and the optimization residual satisfies
$\|\nabla\cL_t(\btheta_t)\| \le \eta_t^{\mathrm{opt}}$, one expects
$\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}} \lesssim G\cdot[\eta_t^{\mathrm{opt}}/\lambda
+ O(L_g\Delta/\lambda)]$.
This is an informal decomposition, not a proved sufficient condition;
deriving a formal sufficient condition for \Cref{asm:optim} from
primitive assumptions remains future work.

Positive definiteness of both~$\bC_t$ and~$\bCbar_t$ follows from
$\lambda>0$ and the outer-product form; it is not an independent assumption.
All theoretical results hold under these explicit assumptions; we do
\emph{not} claim a regret guarantee for the full nonlinear model outside
the local-linearization regime.

\section{Method: Curvature-Calibrated Exploration}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{CC-UCB Algorithm}

\Cref{alg:ccucb} summarizes our approach.  At each round the agent
computes, for every candidate action~$a$, the gradient feature
$\bg_t(a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$ and the
\emph{curvature-calibrated variance proxy}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
  s_t^2(a)
  \;=\;
  \bg_t(a)^\top\bC_t^{-1}\,\bg_t(a).
\end{equation}
Under the Laplace approximation with covariance $\bC_t^{-1}$, the quantity
$s_t^2(a)$ is the predictive variance of the linearized reward at
$(\bx_t,a)$.

Because $\bC_t\succ 0$ by construction (see \S\ref{sec:problem}),
conjugate gradients (CG) is guaranteed to converge on the system
$\bC_t\bu=\bg_t(a)$.  In practice we run CG for a budget of~$I$
iterations, yielding an approximate solve~$\tilde\bu$ and the computable
proxy $\tilde{s}_t^2(a) = \bg_t(a)^\top\tilde\bu$.
\Cref{lem:cg} shows $\tilde{s}_t^2(a)$ approximates $s_t^2(a)$ within a
multiplicative $(1\pm\varepsilon_I)$ factor.  To ensure the UCB bonus
\emph{upper-bounds} $\beta_t\,s_t(a)$ despite the approximation, the
algorithm inflates the width by $(1-\bar\varepsilon)^{-1/2}$
(line~\ref{line:ucb}), where $\bar\varepsilon$ is a known tolerance
satisfying $\varepsilon_I \le \bar\varepsilon < 1$.

\paragraph{CG tolerance parameter~$\bar\varepsilon$.}
The algorithm uses a \emph{known} tolerance $\bar\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ in
the UCB bonus (line~\ref{line:ucb}), not the true energy-norm error
$\varepsilon_I$ (which is unobservable without the exact solve).
It suffices that $\varepsilon_I \le \bar\varepsilon$ at every round; the
bonus is then conservative.  Two strategies ensure this:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Adaptive residual stopping (recommended).}
  Monitor the computable Euclidean relative residual
  $\|r_I\|/\|\bg\|$ at each CG step.
  Since
  $\varepsilon_I \le \sqrt{\bar\kappa}\cdot\|r_I\|/\|\bg\|$
  (standard CG bound), stopping when
  $\|r_I\|/\|\bg\| \le \bar\varepsilon/\sqrt{\bar\kappa}$
  guarantees $\varepsilon_I \le \bar\varepsilon$.
  This is conservative by the factor~$\sqrt{\bar\kappa}$ but requires
  no access to $\hat\bC_t^{-1}$ and adapts automatically to the
  per-round condition number.
\item \emph{Fixed budget (requires a uniform condition-number bound).}
  If an a~priori bound
  $\bar\kappa \le \kappa_{\max}$ holds for \emph{all rounds}~$t\le T$,
  choose $I$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:cg-budget} at $\kappa_{\max}$ and
  target~$\bar\varepsilon$.
  This is directly feasible when the curvature operator uses a fixed
  buffer of size~$n$ (giving
  $\bar\kappa\le 1+nG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ independent of~$T$).
  For the full-history operator ($W_t=t{-}1$), the condition number
  grows with~$t$, so a fixed budget does \emph{not} guarantee
  $\varepsilon_I\le\bar\varepsilon$ uniformly; adaptive stopping or an
  increasing budget $I_t$ is required in that case (see \Cref{app:cg}).
\end{enumerate}
In the regret bound (\Cref{thm:regret}), $\alpha_I$ is evaluated at
$\bar\varepsilon$:
$\alpha_I = \sqrt{(1{+}\bar\varepsilon)/(1{-}\bar\varepsilon)}$.

\paragraph{Action screening (practical heuristic).}
When the action set $\cA_t$ is large, computing the CG solve for every
action is prohibitive ($|\cA_t|\times I$ CG steps per round).
A practical heuristic is to screen the top-$K$ actions by mean prediction
$m_t(a)$ (forward passes only), then compute UCB widths for the $K$
candidates.  This reduces cost to $K\times I$ CG solves.
\emph{The regret guarantee in \Cref{thm:regret} analyzes the
full-enumeration case ($K=|\cA_t|$); screening can drop the optimal arm
and is not covered by the theory unless one adds an assumption that
$a_t^*\in\cA_t^K$ for all~$t$.}
An alternative is CC-TS (see below), which requires only \emph{one}
parameter sample per round, avoiding the per-action loop entirely.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Curvature-Calibrated UCB (CC-UCB)}\label{alg:ccucb}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Reward predictor $\mu_{\btheta}$, damping $\lambda>0$,
         noise proxy $\sigma$,
         predictable confidence upper-bound schedule $\{\bar\beta_t\}$
         (e.g.\ from an external predictable bound $\bar F_t \ge F_t$;
         see \Cref{cor:beta-constructive}),
         CG budget $I$ (or target $\bar\varepsilon$),
         drift bound $\epsdriftbar\in[0,1)$
\STATE Initialize $\btheta_1$ (pretrained weights), dataset $\mathcal{D}_0=\emptyset$
\FOR{$t = 1, 2, \ldots, T$}
  \STATE Observe context $\bx_t$ and action set $\cA_t$
  \STATE Form curvature operator
         $\hat\bC_t\bv = \lambda\bv
         + \sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}\!w_s\,\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\bv\bigr]$
         \hfill\COMMENT{weights $w_s$ per \S\ref{sec:curvop}}
  \FOR{each $a \in \cA_t$}
    \STATE $m_t(a) \gets \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t, a)$
        \hfill\COMMENT{mean prediction}
    \STATE $\bg_t(a) \gets \nabla_{\btheta}\,\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t, a)$
        \hfill\COMMENT{gradient feature}
    \STATE $\tilde\bu \gets \mathrm{CG}(\hat\bC_t,\;\bg_t(a),\;I)$;
           stop early if $\|r_I\|/\|\bg\| \le \bar\varepsilon/\sqrt{\bar\kappa}$
        \hfill\COMMENT{CG solve; Euclidean proxy for $\varepsilon_I\!\le\!\bar\varepsilon$} \label{line:cg}
    \STATE $\tilde{s}_t^2(a) \gets \bg_t(a)^\top \tilde\bu$
        \hfill\COMMENT{approx.\ predictive variance}
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE $a_t \gets \argmax_{a\in\cA_t}\Bigl[\,m_t(a)
    + \dfrac{\sqrt{1+\epsdriftbar}\;\bar\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\varepsilon}}\,
      \sqrt{\tilde{s}_t^2(a)}\,\Bigr]$
      \hfill\COMMENT{$\bar\beta_t\!\ge\!\beta_t$; $\bar\varepsilon$: known CG tolerance}
      \label{line:ucb}
  \STATE Play $a_t$, observe $r_t$
  \STATE $\mathcal{D}_t \gets \mathcal{D}_{t-1} \cup \{(\bx_t, a_t, r_t)\}$
  \STATE Update $\btheta_{t+1}$ via warm-started optimization on $\mathcal{D}_t$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\paragraph{Thompson-sampling variant (CC-TS).}
An alternative draws a parameter perturbation
$\delta\btheta\sim\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0},\bC_t^{-1})$ by sampling in a
Krylov subspace via the Lanczos algorithm, then selects the action maximizing
$\mu_{\btheta_t+\delta\btheta}(\bx_t,a)$.
CC-TS requires only \emph{one} sample per round regardless of $|\cA_t|$,
making it attractive when action sets are large.
We focus on CC-UCB for its deterministic analysis, but CC-TS is a natural
companion whose sampling cost scales as $O(I)$ CG-equivalent iterations
per round rather than $O(|\cA_t|\times I)$.

\subsection{Novelty Relative to Prior Neural Bandits}\label{sec:novelty}

Prior neural bandit algorithms share a two-part recipe: (i)~linearize
the network around a reference parameter (the NTK or last-layer regime) and
(ii)~approximate the resulting curvature matrix---typically with a diagonal,
block-diagonal, or Kronecker-factored estimate---to make the variance
computation tractable
\citep{zhou2020neural,riquelme2018deep,zhang2021neural}.
CC-UCB retains the linearization for analysis (\Cref{asm:linear}), but
replaces the curvature approximation with the \emph{full} GGN
accessed through implicit CG solves.

The main algorithmic contribution is a matrix-free implementation recipe
for full-curvature exploration via curvature--vector products and
conjugate gradients.
The main theoretical contribution in this draft is a modular reduction,
not a new structural regret theorem: standard self-normalized
linear-bandit, CG-accuracy, and spectral-sandwich ingredients are
assembled to isolate the costs of CG truncation, feature drift, and
curvature approximation.
An efficient UCB estimator for nonlinear contextual bandits was
proposed by~\citet{kassraie2021rofu} using a different uncertainty
quantification route (randomized OFU);
we compare approaches in \Cref{sec:related}.

\subsection{Computable Curvature Operator}\label{sec:curvop}

Applying $\bC_t\bv = \lambda\bv + \sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\bv\bigr]$
\emph{naively} requires one curvature--vector product (CVP) per historical
sample per CG step---cost $O(It)$ CVPs per round.
Each CVP evaluates one Jacobian--vector product (forward mode) followed by
one vector--Jacobian product (reverse mode) through the network, at roughly
twice the cost of a gradient evaluation; we do not store
per-sample gradient features~$\bg_s$, only $O(d)$ working vectors
for CG, with CVPs computed on the fly.
We describe three strategies that limit the per-step cost to $|\mathcal{S}_t|$
CVPs:

\paragraph{(i) Subsampled GGN (worst-case spectral baseline).}
At each round draw a uniform subsample $\mathcal{S}_t\subset\{1,\dots,t{-}1\}$
of size~$n$ (without replacement) and set $w_s=(t{-}1)/n$ to obtain the
rescaled operator.
The following lemma provides the spectral guarantee.

\begin{lemma}[Subsampled GGN spectral sandwich]\label{lem:subsample}
Fix round~$t$ and set $m=t{-}1$.
Let $Y_s = \sigma^{-2}\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$ for
$s=1,\dots,m$, so that $\bC_t = \lambda\bI + V$ with $V=\sum_{s=1}^m Y_s$.
Each $Y_s$ is PSD with $\|Y_s\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le G^2/\sigma^2 =: B$.
Draw $\mathcal{S}_t$ of size~$n$ uniformly without replacement from
$\{1,\dots,m\}$ and define $\hat V = \tfrac{m}{n}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}Y_s$
and $\hat\bC_t = \lambda\bI + \hat V$.
Write $L:=\log(2d/\delta)$.

\emph{(i) Deviation bound.}
With probability $\ge 1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subsample-dev}
  \|\hat V - V\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  \;\le\;
  mB\sqrt{\frac{2L}{n}}
  \;+\;
  \frac{4\,mB\,L}{3\,n}.
\end{equation}

\emph{(ii) Sufficient sample size.}
For any $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subsample-n}
  n \;\ge\; 8L\,\biggl[\,
    \frac{m^2\,B^2}{\varepsilon^2\,\lambda^2}
    \;+\;
    \frac{m\,B}{3\,\varepsilon\,\lambda}
  \biggr],
\end{equation}
then $\Pr[\|\hat V - V\|_{\mathrm{op}} > \varepsilon\lambda] \le \delta$
and consequently
$(1{-}\varepsilon)\bC_t\preceq\hat\bC_t\preceq(1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The $\{Y_s\}_{s=1}^m$ are fixed PSD self-adjoint matrices (deterministic
conditional on the history $\cF_{t-1}$ and the current
parameter~$\btheta_t$).
The displayed proof is for \emph{with-replacement} (i.i.d.) sampling.
The without-replacement version follows because sampling without
replacement from a finite population of bounded self-adjoint matrices
satisfies the same matrix Bernstein tail as i.i.d.\ sampling with
identical (or better) constants.
The precise reference is
\citet[Thm.~4.22]{tropp2015introduction}, which shows that the
Laplace-transform moment-generating function for a sum drawn without
replacement is dominated term-by-term by that of the i.i.d.\ sum,
and therefore every tail bound derived from the matrix
Laplace-transform method (including matrix Bernstein) carries over.
In our setting the $\{Y_s\}$ are \emph{fixed} PSD matrices
(deterministic conditional on $\cF_{t-1}$ and~$\btheta_t$) and only
the index set $\mathcal{S}_t$ is random, so the hypotheses of
Thm.~4.22 are satisfied directly.
We therefore state and prove the bound for i.i.d.\ draws
$s_1,\dots,s_n$ uniform on~$\{1,\dots,m\}$; the
without-replacement guarantee is at least as strong.
Define the centered random matrices
$Z_k = (m/n)Y_{s_k} - V/n$ (mean zero, independent).
The range satisfies
$\|Z_k\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le (m/n)B + (1/n)\|V\| \le 2mB/n =: R$
(using $\|V\|\le mB$).
For the variance parameter, since $Y_s\preceq B\bI$,
\[
  \mathbb{E}[Z_k Z_k^\top]
  \;\preceq\; \tfrac{m^2}{n^2}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s_1}^2]
  \;=\; \tfrac{m}{n^2}\sum_{s=1}^m Y_s^2
  \;\preceq\; \tfrac{m}{n^2}B\,V
  \;\preceq\; \tfrac{m^2B^2}{n^2}\bI,
\]
so $v :=
\bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbb{E}[Z_kZ_k^\top]\bigr\|\le m^2B^2/n$.
By the matrix Bernstein inequality
\citep[Thm.~6.1.1]{tropp2015introduction},
$\Pr[\|\hat V{-}V\| \ge t]
\le 2d\exp\!\bigl(-t^2/(2v + 2Rt/3)\bigr)$.
Setting the right-hand side $\le\delta$ and solving for~$t$
(quadratic in~$t$) gives
$t \le \sqrt{2vL}+\tfrac{2}{3}RL
= mB\sqrt{2L/n}+\tfrac{4mBL}{3n}$,
yielding~\eqref{eq:subsample-dev}.
For~\eqref{eq:subsample-n}, require each summand
in~\eqref{eq:subsample-dev} to be $\le\varepsilon\lambda/2$:
$mB\sqrt{2L/n}\le\varepsilon\lambda/2$ gives
$n\ge 8m^2B^2L/(\varepsilon^2\lambda^2)$, and
$4mBL/(3n)\le\varepsilon\lambda/2$ gives
$n\ge 8mBL/(3\varepsilon\lambda)$.
Summing the two conditions
yields~\eqref{eq:subsample-n}; this is a conservative sufficient
condition (using $a+b$ rather than $\max\{a,b\}$), not a sharp threshold.
The multiplicative sandwich follows:
$\|\hat V{-}V\|\le\varepsilon\lambda$ gives
$\bC_t - \varepsilon\lambda\bI \preceq \hat\bC_t \preceq
\bC_t + \varepsilon\lambda\bI$.
Since $\bC_t\succeq\lambda\bI$, we have
$\varepsilon\lambda\bI \preceq \varepsilon\bC_t$, so
$\bC_t - \varepsilon\lambda\bI \succeq (1{-}\varepsilon)\bC_t$ and
$\bC_t + \varepsilon\lambda\bI \preceq (1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$.
\end{proof}

With $B=G^2/\sigma^2$, the sample complexity~\eqref{eq:subsample-n} is
$n=\Theta\!\bigl(L\cdot[m^2G^4/(\varepsilon^2\lambda^2\sigma^4)
  + mG^2/(\varepsilon\lambda\sigma^2)]\bigr)$.
The first (variance) term dominates unless
$\varepsilon\lambda\sigma^2\gtrsim mG^2$, so in general $n$~scales
\emph{quadratically} in~$m=t{-}1$.

\emph{This is a worst-case baseline guarantee, too conservative for
practical use at large~$t$.}
In particular, the bound above does not by itself justify computational
savings at large $t$; it should be read as a worst-case spectral
guarantee rather than as a practical scaling result.
Subsampling reduces computation ($n\ll m$) only when $m$ is moderate or
$\varepsilon\lambda$ is large relative to~$mB$; for large~$t$ the full
history may be needed under the uniform Bernstein bound.
Under additional structure---bounded effective rank
$d_{\mathrm{eff}}=\tr(\bC_t{-}\lambda\bI)/\|\bC_t{-}\lambda\bI\|$
or fast spectral decay---leverage-score sampling or Frequent Directions
sketching~\citep{kuzborskij2019efficient} can achieve
$n=\widetilde{O}(d_{\mathrm{eff}}/\varepsilon^2)$ independent of~$m$,
at the cost of more complex per-round bookkeeping.
We leave such structure-aware sampling as future work and evaluate the
effective spectral gap empirically (\Cref{sec:experiments}).
To obtain a spectral sandwich that holds simultaneously for all
$t\le T$, union-bound over $t$ (replacing $\delta$ by $\delta/T$).
\Cref{cor:approx} then translates
the spectral guarantee into a regret inflation factor.

\paragraph{(ii) Windowed curvature (heuristic).}
Maintain a sliding window of the most recent~$n$ interactions ($w_s=1$).
No unconditional spectral sandwich between the windowed operator and the
full~$\bCbar_t$ holds without distributional assumptions.
Under a stability model (bounded feature drift and
mixing context distribution), one can bound the spectral gap between
$\bCbar_t$ and its windowed counterpart; we treat this as a practical
heuristic and evaluate the effective spectral gap~$\varepsilon$ empirically.
The regret theory of \Cref{thm:regret} does \emph{not} cover windowed
curvature unless an explicit sandwich of the
form~\eqref{eq:drift-sandwich} is verified.

\paragraph{(iii) Periodic refresh (heuristic).}
Recompute the curvature operator ($w_s=1$) every~$M$ rounds on a cached
buffer of size~$n$, reusing the same operator for~$M$ consecutive actions.
As with windowing, no unconditional spectral sandwich holds; the stale
curvature can both over- and under-estimate $\bCbar_t$ depending on how
the data distribution evolves.
We evaluate the spectral gap empirically; the regret theory applies
only when the sandwich~\eqref{eq:drift-sandwich} is verified.

\subsection{Systems Feasibility}\label{sec:systems}

\paragraph{Primitive assumptions.}
CC-UCB requires a single computational primitive: the
\emph{curvature--vector product} (CVP) $\bv\mapsto\bC_t\bv$, which
decomposes into per-sample operations
$\bv\mapsto\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\bv\bigr]$.
Each such operation consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a Jacobian--vector product (JVP) $\bv\mapsto\bg_t^\top\bv$
  (forward-mode AD, cost $\approx 1\times$ forward pass);
\item a vector--Jacobian product (VJP) $\alpha\mapsto\alpha\,\bg_t$
  (reverse-mode AD, cost $\approx 1\times$ backward pass).
\end{enumerate}
Under FSDP or similar sharded-parameter layouts, each per-sample CVP has
the same communication pattern as a gradient step, so the CG loop
parallelizes over samples in the buffer.
A full round of CC-UCB with action screening costs $K\times I$ CG
iterations, each involving $|\mathcal{S}_t|$ forward--backward passes.
Memory overhead is $O(d)$ for CG working vectors
($\tilde\bu$, residual, search direction); no $d\times d$ matrix is formed
or stored.

Recent work on Krylov methods for Hessian spectral analysis
\citep{granziol2026hessian} demonstrates that these CVP primitives scale to
networks with $>10^8$ parameters under standard distributed-training
infrastructure, suggesting that the underlying CVP primitive may scale
to large models.
Warm-starting CG from the previous round's solution can further reduce the
iteration count in practice.

\section{Theoretical Analysis}\label{sec:theory}

We state a regret bound for CC-UCB (\Cref{alg:ccucb}, with
full action enumeration $K{=}|\cA_t|$) under the
Gaussian/squared-loss setup of \Cref{sec:problem} and
\Cref{asm:noise,asm:bounded,asm:linear,asm:optim,asm:drift}.
The analysis does not yet constitute a full exponential-family or
generalized linear bandit proof; the extension in
\Cref{sec:expfam-ext} is a curvature-definition extension only.
The analysis is conducted in terms of the frozen-feature Gram
matrix~$\bCbar_t$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:curvature-bar}), which has the sequential
update and predictability properties required by the self-normalized
martingale bound.
The feature-drift sandwich (\Cref{asm:drift}) bridges the gap between
$\bCbar_t$ and the algorithmic curvature~$\bC_t$; the drift-inflation factor
$\sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}$ in the UCB bonus
(line~\ref{line:ucb} of \Cref{alg:ccucb}) ensures UCB dominance
up to the additive $\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$ and $\epslin(t)$ terms.
The CG approximation enters as a multiplicative constant, not an
additive $O(T)$ term.

The theorem below is conditional and exact-curvature: it analyzes the
case $\hat{\bC}_t=\bC_t$ with full action enumeration, and it does not
by itself establish sublinear~$E_T$, $P_T$, or a horizon-uniform drift
sandwich for unrestricted deep fine-tuning.
It should be read as a \emph{reduction}: once bounds on
linearization error, prediction stability, and feature drift are
supplied, the regret follows with the stated multiplicative and
additive factors; the paper does not prove those input bounds from
primitive network assumptions.

\begin{quote}\small
\textbf{Objects and roles.}
\emph{Curvature matrices:}
$\bCbar_t$ = frozen-feature Gram (predictable, admits rank-one updates;
used in self-normalized proof);
$\bC_t$ = relinearized curvature at $\btheta_t$ (target of CG);
$\hat\bC_t$ = buffer/subsample approximation to~$\bC_t$.
\emph{Variances:}
$\bar s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t}$ (theoretical width);
$s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t^\top\bC_t^{-1}\bg_t}$ (width under relinearized curvature);
$\hat s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t^\top\hat\bC_t^{-1}\bg_t}$ (width under buffer approximation);
$\tilde s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t^\top\tilde\bu}$ (CG approximation to~$\hat s_t$).
\emph{Which theorem covers which substitution:}
\Cref{asm:drift}:~$\bC_t\leftrightarrow\bCbar_t$;\;
\Cref{lem:cg}:~$\hat\bC_t\to\tilde s_t$;\;
\Cref{cor:approx}:~$\bCbar_t\to\hat\bC_t$.
\Cref{thm:regret} analyzes the exact-curvature case $\hat\bC_t{=}\bC_t$.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{Information gain.}
Define the \emph{information gain} along the realized trajectory after $T$~rounds as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infogain}
  \gamma_T
  \;:=\;
  \log\frac{\det(\bCbar_{T+1})}{\det(\lambda\,\bI)}
  \;=\;
  \sum_{t=1}^{T}
    \log\!\Bigl(1+\sigma^{-2}\bg_t(a_t)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a_t)\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where $\bCbar_{T+1}=\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s=1}^T\bg_s\bg_s^\top$
is the Gram matrix after all $T$~rounds.
The second equality is the determinant telescoping identity
$\det(\bCbar_{t+1})/\det(\bCbar_t)=1+\sigma^{-2}\bar s_t^2(a_t)$
applied to~\eqref{eq:bar-update}.
Since $\bCbar_{T+1}\succeq\lambda\bI$, $\gamma_T\ge 0$.
A standard dimension bound gives
$\gamma_T \le d\,\log\!\bigl(1+TG^2/(d\,\lambda\sigma^2)\bigr)$,
connecting our bound to the effective-dimension formulation of
kernelized bandits \citep{valko2013finite} and NeuralUCB
\citep{zhou2020neural}.
(An effective-dimension refinement replacing~$d$ with a smaller
data-dependent quantity is possible under additional spectral-decay
assumptions on~$\bCbar_{T+1}$, but we do not state it here.)

\subsection{CG Approximation Lemma}

\begin{lemma}[CG multiplicative accuracy]\label{lem:cg}
Let $\bu = \hat\bC_t^{-1}\bg$ and let $\tilde\bu$ be the iterate after
$I$~steps of CG on the SPD system $\hat\bC_t\bu=\bg$ starting from
$\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf{0}$.
Define the relative energy-norm error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-rate}
  \varepsilon_I
  \;:=\;
  \frac{\|\bu-\tilde\bu\|_{\hat\bC_t}}{\|\bu\|_{\hat\bC_t}}
  \;\le\;
  2\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\bar\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\bar\kappa}+1}\right)^{\!I},
\end{equation}
where $\bar\kappa := \lambda_{\max}(\hat\bC_t)/\lambda
\le 1 + W_tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ is an upper bound on the condition
number $\kappa=\lambda_{\max}(\hat\bC_t)/\lambda_{\min}(\hat\bC_t)$
(since $\lambda_{\min}\ge\lambda$);
$W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}\!w_s$ with all weights $w_s\ge 0$
(so $\hat\bC_t\succeq\lambda\bI\succ 0$, ensuring CG is well-posed);
$W_t=n$ for windowed/refresh, giving $\bar\kappa$ and $\alpha_I$
independent of~$T$; $W_t=t{-}1$ for full history.
The eigenvalue bound uses $\|\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\|\le G$
from \Cref{asm:bounded} (which quantifies over all replayed samples).
Write $\tilde{s}_t^2(a) = \bg^\top\tilde\bu$ and
$\hat{s}_t^2(a)=\bg^\top\hat\bC_t^{-1}\bg$.

\textbf{Assume $\varepsilon_I < 1$}
(see \Cref{app:cg} for the minimum~$I$ ensuring this as a function
of~$\bar\kappa$;
\Cref{alg:ccucb} uses a known tolerance~$\bar\varepsilon$ in the bonus,
and adaptive residual stopping or a fixed budget under a uniform
condition-number bound ensures
$\varepsilon_I \le \bar\varepsilon < 1$; see \S\ref{sec:method}).
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-quad}
  (1-\varepsilon_I)\,\hat{s}_t^2(a)
  \;\le\;
  \tilde{s}_t^2(a)
  \;\le\;
  (1+\varepsilon_I)\,\hat{s}_t^2(a).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\tilde{s}_t^2 - \hat{s}_t^2
= \bg^\top(\tilde\bu-\bu)
= (\hat\bC_t\bu)^\top(\tilde\bu-\bu)
= \bu^\top\hat\bC_t(\tilde\bu-\bu)$.
By Cauchy--Schwarz in the $\hat\bC_t$-norm,
$|\bu^\top\hat\bC_t(\tilde\bu-\bu)|
\le\|\bu\|_{\hat\bC_t}\|\tilde\bu-\bu\|_{\hat\bC_t}
\le\varepsilon_I\|\bu\|_{\hat\bC_t}^2 = \varepsilon_I\,\hat{s}_t^2$.
The condition number bound follows from
$\lambda_{\max}(\hat\bC_t)\le\lambda+\sigma^{-2}\sum w_s\|\bg_s\|^2
\le\lambda+W_tG^2/\sigma^2$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequence for the UCB width.}
The drift-inflated bonus from \Cref{alg:ccucb} is
\[
  w_t(a)
  \;:=\;
  \frac{\sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\;\bar\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\varepsilon}}\,
  \sqrt{\tilde{s}_t^2(a)},
\]
where $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$ is the predictable confidence upper bound
and $\bar\varepsilon\ge\varepsilon_I$ is the known CG tolerance.
From the lower bound $\tilde{s}_t^2\ge(1{-}\varepsilon_I)\hat{s}_t^2
\ge (1{-}\bar\varepsilon)\hat{s}_t^2$:
$w_t(a) \ge \sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\;\bar\beta_t\,\hat{s}_t(a)
\ge \sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\;\beta_t\,\hat{s}_t(a)$.
From the upper bound $\tilde{s}_t^2\le(1{+}\varepsilon_I)\hat{s}_t^2
\le (1{+}\bar\varepsilon)\hat{s}_t^2$:
$w_t(a) \le \sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\;\alpha_I\,\bar\beta_t\,\hat{s}_t(a)$,
where $\alpha_I := \sqrt{(1{+}\bar\varepsilon)/(1{-}\bar\varepsilon)}$
(a known constant).

\subsection{Confidence and Discrepancy Lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Confidence for the linearized ridge estimator]%
\label{lem:confidence}
Suppose \Cref{asm:noise,asm:bounded,asm:linear} hold and
$\mu^*=\mu_{\btheta^*}$ with $\|\btheta^*\|\le S$.
Assume $|\cA_t|\le A_{\max}$ for all~$t$.
Define the linearized ridge estimator~$\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$
by~\eqref{eq:thetahat-lin}, the frozen-feature variance
$\bar s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)}$, and
the cumulative squared linearization error
$F_t := \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\epslin(s)^2$.
Then with probability at least $1-\delta$, for all $t\ge 1$ and all
$a\in\cA_t$ simultaneously,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf-lin}
  \bigl|\bg_t(a)^\top
    \bigl(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}-\btheta^*\bigr)\bigr|
  \;\le\;
  \bar s_t(a)\,\beta_t,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
  \beta_t
  \;:=\;
  \sqrt{\gamma_{t-1} + 2\log\!\bigl(2\,A_{\max}\,t^2/\delta\bigr)}
  \;+\; \sqrt{\lambda}\,S
  \;+\; \frac{1}{\sigma}\sqrt{F_t}.
\end{equation}
(Here $\gamma_{t-1}=\log\det(\bCbar_t)/\det(\lambda\bI)$ is the
information gain through round $t{-}1$.)

The quantity $\beta_t$ in~\eqref{eq:beta} is a theoretical radius.
The algorithm may use any predictable upper bound
$\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$; all results below remain valid with
$\bar\beta_t$ in place of~$\beta_t$.
This is necessary because $\beta_t$ depends on the unknown cumulative
linearization term~$F_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
From~\eqref{eq:pseudo-response},
$y_s = \bg_s^\top\btheta^* + \rho_s + \eta_s$ with
$|\rho_s|\le\epslin(s)$.
The closed form of $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$ gives
\[
  \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}} - \btheta^*
  = -\lambda\bCbar_t^{-1}\btheta^*
    + \bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s<t}\bg_s\rho_s
    + \bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s<t}\bg_s\eta_s.
\]
Taking the inner product with~$\bg_t(a)$ and bounding in absolute value:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item\emph{Prior bias.}\;
$|\lambda\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\btheta^*|
\le \lambda\,\bar s_t(a)\,\|\btheta^*\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
\le \bar s_t(a)\sqrt{\lambda}\,S$.
(Cauchy--Schwarz in the $\bCbar_t$-norm;
$\lambda\|\btheta^*\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
\le \lambda\|\btheta^*\|/\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\bCbar_t)}
\le \sqrt{\lambda}\,S$
since $\bCbar_t\succeq\lambda\bI$.)
\item\emph{Linearization bias.}\;
Define $\Phi=[\bg_1/\sigma,\dots,\bg_{t-1}/\sigma]$ (the $d\times(t{-}1)$
design matrix) and $\hat\rho=(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_{t-1})^\top$.
Then $\sigma^{-2}\sum\bg_s\rho_s = \sigma^{-1}\Phi\hat\rho$.
Since $\bCbar_t \succeq \Phi\Phi^\top$, we have
$\Phi^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\Phi \preceq \bI$, so
$\|\Phi\hat\rho\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
= \|\hat\rho\|_{\Phi^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\Phi}
\le \|\hat\rho\| = \sqrt{F_t}$.
Therefore
$|\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-1}\Phi\hat\rho|
\le \bar s_t(a)\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{F_t}$.
\item\emph{Noise.}\;
We apply the self-normalized martingale bound
\citep{abbasi2011improved} with filtration $\{\cG_t\}_{t\ge 1}$
(the pre-reward sigma-algebra defined in \S\ref{sec:problem}):
the design vector $\bg_s/\sigma$ is $\cG_s$-measurable
(since $\btheta_s,\bx_s,a_s$ are all determined before the reward),
and $\xi_s=\eta_s/\sigma$ is conditionally $1$-sub-Gaussian
given~$\cG_s$ (\Cref{asm:noise}).
The bound gives, at confidence level $\delta_{t,a}$,
$|\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-2}\sum\bg_s\eta_s|
\le \bar s_t(a)\sqrt{\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta_{t,a})}$.
A union bound over all $a\in\cA_t$ ($\le A_{\max}$ actions) and
$t\ge 1$ with $\delta_{t,a} = \delta/(2\,A_{\max}\,t^2)$
yields the simultaneous bound:
$\sum_{t\ge 1}A_{\max}\delta_{t,a}
= \tfrac{\delta}{2}\sum_{t\ge 1}t^{-2}
< \tfrac{\delta}{2}\cdot 2 = \delta$
(using $\sum_{t=1}^\infty t^{-2} < 2$), and
$\log(1/\delta_{t,a})=\log(2\,A_{\max}\,t^2/\delta)$.
\end{enumerate}
Summing gives~\eqref{eq:conf-lin}--\eqref{eq:beta}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Implementable confidence schedule under a known linearization bound]\label{cor:beta-constructive}
Suppose a deterministic predictable sequence $\{\bar F_t\}_{t\ge 1}$
satisfies $F_t \le \bar F_t$ for all~$t$.
Then \Cref{alg:ccucb} may set
\[
  \bar\beta_t \;:=\;
  \sqrt{\gamma_{t-1} + 2\log(2A_{\max}t^2/\delta)}
  \;+\; \sqrt{\lambda}\,S
  \;+\; \sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\bar F_t},
\]
which is predictable and satisfies $\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$\bar F_t \ge F_t$ term-by-term implies each summand in~\eqref{eq:beta}
is dominated, so $\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$.
Predictability holds because $\gamma_{t-1}$, $S$, $\bar F_t$, $\lambda$,
$\sigma$, $A_{\max}$, $\delta$ are all determined before round~$t$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Prediction discrepancy]\label{lem:discrepancy}
Under \Cref{asm:optim},
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t - \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|\le\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$
for all $a\in\cA_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from~\eqref{eq:pred-stab}.
\end{proof}

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Remark (informal decomposition of $\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$).}
Under the additional (informal) conditions that
$\nabla^2\cL_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ (local strong convexity of the loss)
and the optimization residual satisfies
$\|\nabla\cL_t(\btheta_t)\|\le\eta_t^{\mathrm{opt}}$,
one expects
$\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}} \lesssim G\cdot[\eta_t^{\mathrm{opt}}/\lambda
+ O(L_g\Delta/\lambda)]$,
where the second term captures feature drift between~$\bg_s$
and~$\bg_t$ (see \Cref{lem:drift-sufficient} for explicit drift bounds).
This decomposition is heuristic: the optimization-error contribution
follows from strong convexity via
$\|\btheta_t - \bar\btheta_t\| \le \eta_t^{\mathrm{opt}}/\lambda$
(where $\bar\btheta_t$ is a global minimizer of~$\cL_t$
at round~$t$, not to be confused with the realizability
parameter~$\btheta^*$), while the drift
contribution depends on problem-specific structure.  The formal
regret bound (\Cref{thm:regret}) uses $\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$
directly without decomposing it.
Accordingly, Theorem~\ref{thm:regret} should be interpreted strictly as
a regret transfer lemma from externally controlled prediction
discrepancy to cumulative regret.
\end{quote}
In the convex last-layer regime, $\btheta_t = \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$
and $\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}=0$.
The full prediction error at $(\bx_t,a)$ is then bounded by a triangle
inequality:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pred-error}
  |\mu_{\btheta^*}(\bx_t,a) - \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)|
  \;\le\;
  \bar s_t(a)\,\beta_t
  \;+\;
  \eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}
  \;+\;
  \epslin(t),
\end{equation}
using \eqref{eq:conf-lin}, \Cref{lem:discrepancy}, and the per-round
linearization remainder $|\rho_t|\le\epslin(t)$.

\subsection{Regret Bound}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional regret bound for exact-curvature CC-UCB (Gaussian/squared-loss)]\label{thm:regret}
Suppose \Cref{asm:noise,asm:bounded,asm:linear,asm:optim,asm:drift} hold,
the model is realizable in the linearized sense
($\mu^*(\bx,a) = \mu_{\btheta^*}(\bx,a)$ for some~$\btheta^*$ with
$\|\btheta^*\|\le S$), $|\cA_t|\le A_{\max}$ for all~$t$, and
$\epsdriftbar\in[\epsdrift,1)$.
Assume the \textbf{full-history curvature operator}:
$\mathcal{S}_t = \{1,\ldots,t{-}1\}$, $w_s=1$, so $\hat\bC_t = \bC_t$.
Assume full action enumeration ($K{=}|\cA_t|$, no screening) and
that each round's CG solve satisfies
$\varepsilon_I \le \bar\varepsilon$ for a known $\bar\varepsilon < 1$
(achievable via adaptive residual stopping, or via a fixed budget under
a uniform condition-number bound; see \S\ref{sec:method}).
The algorithm is run with any predictable schedule
$\{\bar\beta_t\}_{t\ge 1}$ satisfying $\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$ for
all~$t$ (see the remark after \Cref{lem:confidence}).
Let
$E_T = \sum_{t=1}^T \epslin(t)$,\;
$P_T = \sum_{t=1}^T \eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$,\;
$\bar\beta_{\max,T} := \max_{1\le t\le T}\bar\beta_t$,
and define $\gamma_T$, $\beta_t$ as
in~\eqref{eq:infogain}--\eqref{eq:beta}.

Then with probability at least $1-\delta$, CC-UCB achieves cumulative regret
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret-bound}
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  \underbrace{2\,\alpha_I\,\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\,\bar\beta_{\max,T}\,
    \sqrt{(\sigma^2 + G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T\vphantom{T^2}}}_{\text{exploration cost}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{2\,E_T}_{\text{linearization remainder}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{2\,P_T}_{\text{prediction stability}},
\end{equation}
where
\begin{align}
  \alpha_I &\;:=\; \sqrt{\frac{1+\bar\varepsilon}{1-\bar\varepsilon}}
    \qquad\text{(CG truncation; determined by the known
    tolerance~$\bar\varepsilon$)}, \label{eq:alpha-I}\\[4pt]
  \alpha_{\mathrm{drift}} &\;:=\;
    \sqrt{\frac{1+\epsdriftbar}{1-\epsdriftbar}}
    \qquad\text{(feature-drift inflation; depends on the
    \emph{known input}~$\epsdriftbar$)}. \label{eq:alpha-drift}
\end{align}
The exploration-cost term uses
$\sqrt{\sigma^2 + G^2/\lambda}$ in place of~$\sigma$; the condition
$G^2\le\lambda\sigma^2$ (which would simplify this to
$\sigma\sqrt{2}$) is \emph{not} required.
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Discussion of terms.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Exploration term.}
Sublinear in~$T$ whenever $\gamma_T = o(T)$, which holds for finite
effective dimension.
$\alpha_I$ is constant for any fixed CG tolerance;
$\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}$ is constant for any
fixed~$\epsdriftbar<1$ (e.g.\ frozen backbone gives
$\epsdriftbar=0$ and $\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}=1$).
By \Cref{lem:drift-sufficient}, a horizon-uniform drift bound over
$t\le T$ requires $\Delta = O(\sigma^2\lambda/(TGL_g))$ in the
small-drift regime, so the present guarantee should be read mainly as a
near-stationarity result.
The factor $\sqrt{\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda}$ arises from the elliptic
potential step; when $G^2 \le \lambda\sigma^2$ (well-regularized regime)
it is at most $\sigma\sqrt{2}$, recovering the simpler form.
\item \emph{Linearization remainder.}
The bound involves $E_T=\sum_t\epslin(t)$, not $T\epslin$.
Total regret is sublinear when $E_T=o(T)$.
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(1/t)$ gives $E_T=O(1)$;
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(t^{-1/4})$ gives $E_T=O(\sqrt{T})$.
The squared-error sum $F_T=\sum\epslin(t)^2$ also enters through~$\bar\beta_{\max,T}$
(via the definition of~$\beta_t$).
\item \emph{Prediction stability.}
$P_T=\sum_t\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$
vanishes when $\btheta_t = \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$
(convex last-layer models with exact optimization).
\item \emph{Regret improvement over diagonal/last-layer surrogates.}
The bound itself does not prove that full curvature yields strictly
lower regret than diagonal or last-layer methods in any specific regime;
it provides an upper bound with explicit constants.
\emph{Demonstrating regret improvement is an empirical hypothesis}
(\Cref{sec:experiments}); the theory shows that the regret cost of
curvature distortion is captured by the spectral-sandwich
factor~$\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}$ and \Cref{cor:approx}, providing a
diagnostic rather than a comparative guarantee.
\item \emph{Scope.}
The bound is proved for Gaussian/squared-loss rewards.
Extending it to exponential-family likelihoods (Bernoulli, categorical)
would require a weighted frozen-feature analysis accounting for the
data-dependent Fisher weights~$\nabla^2 A(z)$ and is left as future work
(\Cref{sec:expfam-ext}).
\end{itemize}

\begin{corollary}[Frozen-backbone / linear-head regime]\label{cor:frozen}
If the backbone is frozen and the reward head is linear in its
parameters, then $\epslin(t)=0$ for all~$t$ (the model is linear in
the trainable parameters, so $L=0$ in \Cref{asm:linear}),
$\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}=0$ for all~$t$
(the MAP and linearized estimators coincide, so \Cref{asm:optim}
holds with equality), and $\epsdrift=0$
(features do not change, so \Cref{asm:drift} holds trivially).
Setting $\epsdriftbar=0$, \Cref{thm:regret} gives
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_I\,\bar\beta_{\max,T}\,
    \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T},
\]
where $\alpha_I=\sqrt{(1{+}\bar\varepsilon)/(1{-}\bar\varepsilon)}$
is the only inflation factor, and $E_T=P_T=0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $\epsdriftbar=\epsdrift=0$,
$\epslin(t)=\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}=0$ into~\eqref{eq:regret-bound}.
Then $\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}=1$, $E_T=0$, $P_T=0$, and $F_T=0$
(so $\beta_t$ simplifies to
$\sqrt{\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(2\,A_{\max}t^2/\delta)}+\sqrt{\lambda}\,S$).
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation.}
\Cref{cor:frozen} is a sanity check, not the main target regime for
full-curvature exploration.  When only a modest-dimensional head is
trained, explicit linear-bandit updates may be simpler than CG on the
full network.  The main open question is whether similarly nonvacuous
guarantees can be obtained when the backbone adapts enough for full
curvature to matter.

\paragraph{Proof sketch.}
The full proof (\Cref{app:proof}) proceeds in four steps.
\emph{Step~1 (confidence + discrepancy):}
\Cref{lem:confidence,lem:discrepancy} yield the prediction-error
bound~\eqref{eq:pred-error}:
$|\mu_{\btheta^*}(a){-}\mu_{\btheta_t}(a)|
\le \bar s_t(a)\beta_t + \eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}} + \epslin(t)$.
\emph{Step~2 (drift sandwich + bonus bounds):}
Inverting~\eqref{eq:drift-sandwich},
$s_t(a)\ge(1{+}\epsdrift)^{-1/2}\bar s_t(a)$.
Since $\hat\bC_t = \bC_t$ (full-history instantiation) and
$\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$, the
drift-inflated bonus satisfies the lower bound
$w_t(a)\ge\sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\,\bar\beta_t\, s_t(a)
\ge \beta_t\bar s_t(a)$
(UCB dominance of the $\bar s_t\beta_t$ term; the additive
$\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$ and $\epslin(t)$ terms are handled in Step~3).
For the upper bound on~$w_t$:
$w_t(a) \le \sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\,\alpha_I\,\bar\beta_t\,s_t(a)
\le \alpha_I\,\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\,\bar\beta_t\,\bar s_t(a)$,
since $s_t(a) \le (1{-}\epsdrift)^{-1/2}\bar s_t(a)
\le (1{-}\epsdriftbar)^{-1/2}\bar s_t(a)$
(using $\epsdriftbar\ge\epsdrift$).
\emph{Step~3 (per-round regret):}
From Step~1,
$\mu^*(a_t^*)\le m_t(a_t^*)+\bar s_t(a_t^*)\beta_t
+\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+\epslin(t)
\le m_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)+\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+\epslin(t)$
(using $w_t\ge\beta_t\bar s_t$ from Step~2).
The UCB selection rule and a second application of the prediction-error
bound at~$a_t$ yield
$\mu^*(a_t^*)-\mu^*(a_t)\le
2\alpha_I\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\bar\beta_t\bar s_t(a_t)
+2\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+2\epslin(t)$.
\emph{Step~4 (elliptic potential):}
Define $c_t := \sigma^{-2}\bar s_t^2(a_t)$ and
$c_{\max} := \max_t c_t \le G^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$.
Using $\log(1{+}x)\ge x/(1{+}x) \ge x/(1{+}c_{\max})$ for $x\in[0,c_{\max}]$:
$\sum_t c_t \le (1{+}c_{\max})\gamma_T$, hence
$\sum_t \bar s_t^2(a_t) \le (\sigma^2 + G^2/\lambda)\gamma_T$.
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$\sum_t\bar s_t(a_t)\le\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T}$.
Collecting terms yields~\eqref{eq:regret-bound}.

\subsection{Cost of Approximate Curvature}

\begin{corollary}[Spectral-sandwich regret inflation]\label{cor:approx}
Assume an algorithm replaces $\bCbar_t$ with $\hat{\bC}_t$ satisfying, for
some $\varepsilon\in[0,1)$ and \textbf{all $t\le T$ simultaneously}
(with probability at least $1-\delta'$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-sandwich}
  (1-\varepsilon)\,\bCbar_t \;\preceq\; \hat{\bC}_t \;\preceq\; (1+\varepsilon)\,\bCbar_t.
\end{equation}
Then the variance proxy is distorted as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sandwich}
  \frac{1}{1+\varepsilon}\,\bar s_t^2(a)
  \;\le\;
  \hat{s}_t^2(a)
  \;\le\;
  \frac{1}{1-\varepsilon}\,\bar s_t^2(a),
\end{equation}
and the exploration term
in~\eqref{eq:regret-bound} inflates by at most
$\sqrt{(1{+}\varepsilon)/(1{-}\varepsilon)}$.
Since the sandwich is stated directly relative to~$\bCbar_t$,
\emph{no separate $\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}$ factor appears};
the drift is already absorbed.
With CG truncation, the total multiplicative factor on the exploration
term is
$\alpha_I\cdot\sqrt{(1{+}\varepsilon)/(1{-}\varepsilon)}$.
The confidence guarantee holds with probability at least
$1-\delta-\delta'$ by a union bound over the main-theorem event
(probability $\ge 1-\delta$) and the spectral-sandwich event
(probability $\ge 1-\delta'$).
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Composing subsampling with feature drift.}
\Cref{lem:subsample} guarantees a sandwich for the \emph{relinearized}
$\bC_t$:
$(1{-}\varepsilon)\bC_t\preceq\hat\bC_t\preceq(1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$.
To apply \Cref{cor:approx} (which requires a sandwich for~$\bCbar_t$),
compose with the feature-drift bound (\Cref{asm:drift}):
\[
  (1{-}\varepsilon)(1{-}\epsdrift)\,\bCbar_t
  \;\preceq\; \hat\bC_t
  \;\preceq\; (1{+}\varepsilon)(1{+}\epsdrift)\,\bCbar_t.
\]
The resulting sandwich is \emph{asymmetric}. Define the upper and lower
distortion parameters:
\begin{align*}
  \varepsilon_+ &\;:=\; (1{+}\varepsilon)(1{+}\epsdrift) - 1
    \;=\; \varepsilon + \epsdrift + \varepsilon\epsdrift, \\
  \varepsilon_- &\;:=\; 1 - (1{-}\varepsilon)(1{-}\epsdrift)
    \;=\; \varepsilon + \epsdrift - \varepsilon\epsdrift.
\end{align*}
Note $\varepsilon_+ > \varepsilon_-$ whenever $\varepsilon\epsdrift>0$.
Inverting the composed sandwich gives
\[
  (1{+}\varepsilon_+)^{-1}\,\bCbar_t^{-1}
  \;\preceq\; \hat\bC_t^{-1}
  \;\preceq\; (1{-}\varepsilon_-)^{-1}\,\bCbar_t^{-1},
\]
which requires $\varepsilon_- < 1$ (equivalently,
$(1{-}\varepsilon)(1{-}\epsdrift) > 0$, which holds
since $\varepsilon,\epsdrift \in [0,1)$).
The resulting exploration inflation factor in the composed
subsampling-plus-drift case is the asymmetric quantity
$\sqrt{(1{+}\varepsilon_+)/(1{-}\varepsilon_-)}$,
applied on top of~$\alpha_I$ only---the drift is already folded into
$\varepsilon_\pm$, so no separate $\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}$ is
multiplied.
When $\varepsilon=0$ (exact $\bC_t$, drift only), this recovers
$\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}$; when $\epsdrift=0$ (subsampling, no drift),
it recovers the symmetric factor
$\sqrt{(1{+}\varepsilon)/(1{-}\varepsilon)}$, matching
\Cref{cor:approx} and \Cref{thm:regret} as special cases.
A union bound over $t\le T$ (replacing $\delta'$ by $\delta'/T$ in
\Cref{lem:subsample}) gives the time-uniform guarantee.

For the \emph{windowed} and \emph{periodic-refresh} operators
(\S\ref{sec:curvop}(ii)--(iii)), no unconditional spectral sandwich holds
without a distributional stability assumption; the corollary does
\emph{not} provide a regret guarantee for those operators unless
an explicit sandwich is verified.
We treat windowed and refresh operators as practical heuristics and
evaluate their effective spectral gap empirically.

We omit empirical results in this draft; a detailed experimental
protocol---covering benchmarks, baselines, ablations, and
metrics---is provided in \Cref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Neural and linear contextual bandits.}
LinUCB \citep{abbasi2011improved} and Thompson sampling
\citep{thompson1933likelihood,agrawal2013thompson} provide the analytical
backbone for contextual bandit exploration.  Neural extensions---NeuralUCB
\citep{zhou2020neural}, NeuralTS \citep{zhang2021neural}, neural-linear
models \citep{riquelme2018deep}---typically linearize the network (via NTK or
last-layer features) and maintain a tractable covariance over the resulting
feature space.  Our work keeps the linearization for analysis but replaces
the compressed curvature with a full implicit solve via CG.

\paragraph{Online Bayesian and EKF-style neural bandits.}
\citet{duranmartin2022efficient} apply extended Kalman filter (EKF)
updates for online Bayesian inference in neural bandits, avoiding
explicit covariance inversion by maintaining a factored posterior.
Their approach shares the goal of tractable full-parameter uncertainty,
but relies on a diagonal-plus-low-rank posterior approximation rather than
exact curvature--vector products.
CC-UCB differs by using CG with the full GGN operator and providing a
regret analysis that explicitly quantifies the CG truncation error.

\paragraph{Last-layer Laplace for large-scale bandits.}
Last-layer Laplace methods
\citep{ritter2018scalable,daxberger2021laplace} restrict the posterior
approximation to the final layer of a deep network, arguing that
full-parameter covariance is computationally intractable for large models.
While last-layer methods are practical and well-understood, they discard
backbone curvature, which the experimental protocol in \Cref{sec:experiments}
is intended to test for miscalibration under partial fine-tuning.
CC-UCB targets exactly this gap: accessing full-parameter curvature
without materializing the covariance matrix.

\paragraph{Curvature-aware bandit exploration.}
\citet{li2024curvature} analyze bandits with curvature-weighted Gram
matrices and derive regret bounds tied to curvature constants.
Their theoretical contribution is a deeper structural analysis of how
curvature enters the regret, whereas our contribution is a concrete matrix-free route to
full-curvature exploration together with a conditional regret
decomposition; this draft does not empirically demonstrate
end-to-end scalability.

\paragraph{Efficient nonlinear UCB.}
\citet{kassraie2021rofu} propose ROFU (Randomized Optimistic Follow the
Leader), an efficient UCB estimator for nonlinear contextual bandits
that avoids explicit covariance inversion via randomized perturbation.
Their uncertainty quantification route differs from ours (randomized
OFU vs.\ Laplace/CG), and they work with the exact NTK Gram matrix
rather than the GGN.
CC-UCB targets the regime where the practitioner has a specific
pretrained model and wants to use its curvature as-is, via
curvature--vector products, rather than constructing a surrogate
NTK-based estimator.

\paragraph{Spectral sketching for linear bandits.}
\citet{kuzborskij2019efficient} analyze linear bandits where the Gram
matrix is replaced by a Frequent Directions sketch, deriving regret
bounds that depend on the spectral approximation quality.
\citet{wen2024pitfalls} catalog failure modes of sketch-based
bandits, showing that naive sketching can break optimism.
Our spectral-sandwich corollary (\Cref{cor:approx}) instantiates the
same conceptual lever---quantifying regret inflation via spectral
distortion---in the matrix-free GGN/CVP setting.
We do not claim novelty for the sandwich-to-regret reduction itself;
the contribution is adapting it to the CG/GGN context with explicit
CG truncation and feature-drift factors.

\paragraph{Laplace approximation in deep learning.}
The Laplace approximation \citep{mackay1992practical,ritter2018scalable}
provides a Gaussian posterior centered at a MAP estimate with covariance
given by the inverse curvature.  Scalable variants use diagonal, KFAC, or
subnetwork approximations.  CC-UCB sidesteps these compressions by accessing
the GGN curvature only through matrix--vector products.

\paragraph{GGN--vector products, Hessian spectral analysis, and influence
functions.}
Pearlmutter's algorithm \citep{pearlmutter1994fast} computes exact
Hessian--vector products; the analogous GGN--vector product has the same
Jacobian-transpose-times-Jacobian-times-vector structure
\citep{schraudolph2002fast}.
Recent work on Krylov methods for Hessian spectral analysis
\citep{granziol2026hessian} demonstrates that these primitives scale to
$>10^8$-parameter networks under distributed training,
directly supporting the systems feasibility of CC-UCB.
Influence functions
\citep{koh2017understanding} use inverse-curvature--vector products to
attribute predictions to training points; the CG-based implicit solve
we employ is the same computational primitive.

\paragraph{Optimal experimental design.}
Information-theoretic exploration objectives---such as maximizing
$\log\det(\bCbar_t)$ or minimizing the trace of~$\bCbar_t^{-1}$---connect
bandit exploration to the classical D-optimal and A-optimal design
criteria \citep{valko2013finite}.  CC-UCB's use of the full curvature
enables stochastic estimates of these quantities via Hutchinson-type trace
estimators \citep{ubaru2017fast}, without forming the matrix.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We introduced Curvature-Calibrated UCB, a contextual bandit algorithm that
computes exploration bonuses by solving for inverse-GGN--vector products via
conjugate gradients, avoiding the spectral distortion inherent in diagonal
or block-diagonal curvature approximations.  The regret analysis is
conducted in terms of a frozen-feature Gram matrix~$\bCbar_t$ that satisfies
the predictability and sequential-update properties required by
self-normalized martingale arguments; the proof introduces an auxiliary
linearized ridge estimator for which the self-normalized bound applies
directly, and a triangle inequality with a prediction-stability assumption
bridges to the nonlinear MAP estimate.
A feature-drift spectral sandwich
connects $\bCbar_t$ to the relinearized curvature~$\bC_t$ used by the
algorithm.
Under these assumptions, with a drift-inflation factor
$\sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}$ in the UCB bonus ensuring UCB dominance up to
additive prediction-stability and linearization terms,
CC-UCB achieves
$\widetilde{\cO}\bigl(\alpha_I\,\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\,
\bar\beta_{\max,T}
\sqrt{(\sigma^2{+}G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T}\bigr)
+ 2E_T + 2P_T$
regret (under full action enumeration, exact curvature
$\hat\bC_t{=}\bC_t$, and a predictable confidence schedule
$\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$), where $\gamma_T$ is the
information gain, $\alpha_I$ the CG truncation factor,
$\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}$ the feature-drift cost (depending only on
the known input~$\epsdriftbar$),
$\bar\beta_{\max,T}=\max_t\bar\beta_t$ the worst-case confidence width,
$E_T$ the cumulative linearization error, and $P_T$ the
prediction-stability cost.
Action screening is a practical heuristic not covered by the theory
without an additional assumption that the optimal arm survives
screening.
A spectral-sandwich corollary quantifies the cost of approximate curvature
operators; we provide a formal matrix Bernstein guarantee (with explicit
$(t{-}1)^2$-dependence in sample complexity for the variance term) for
subsampled GGN and clearly delineate windowed/refresh operators as
heuristics not covered by the theory without additional stability
assumptions.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The theoretical guarantee relies on a local linearization approximation
whose quality degrades for highly nonlinear models or large parameter
drift; the uniform feature-drift bound~$\epsdrift<1$ is plausible in
frozen-backbone or NTK regimes but may not hold for unrestricted
deep-network fine-tuning.
The prediction-stability assumption (\Cref{asm:optim}) is a
high-level condition; we sketch an informal decomposition under local
strong convexity (see the remark after \Cref{lem:discrepancy}), but
deriving a formal sufficient condition from primitive assumptions
in the general nonconvex case remains open.
The per-round cost scales linearly in the CG budget~$I$ and buffer
size~$n$; preconditioning and warm-starting CG are important practical
considerations not analyzed theoretically here.
The current regret proof applies to the Gaussian/squared-loss
instantiation; a full exponential-family Fisher/GGN regret analysis---requiring
weighted frozen-feature arguments and non-Gaussian noise
treatment---is future work (\Cref{sec:expfam-ext}).
Extending the analysis beyond the linearized regime---covering the
relinearized GGN directly, without the frozen-feature bridge---remains
an open direction that would require a non-standard potential argument
for non-monotone design matrices.
The main open problem left by this draft is to derive primitive
sufficient conditions for small prediction discrepancy and
horizon-uniform feature drift in a nontrivial partially adapting
backbone regime.

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:regret}}\label{app:proof}

We recall the key objects:
$\bg_s(\bx,a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx,a)$ (frozen features),
$\bCbar_t=\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$
(frozen-feature Gram matrix),
$\bar s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)}$ (frozen-feature
variance),
$\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$~(linearized ridge estimator,
Eq.~\ref{eq:thetahat-lin}),
and the information gain
$\gamma_T=\log\det(\bCbar_{T+1})/\det(\lambda\bI)$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:infogain}).

\paragraph{Step 1: Confidence, discrepancy, and prediction-error bound.}
By \Cref{lem:confidence}, with probability $\ge 1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf-app}
  \bigl|\bg_t(a)^\top(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}-\btheta^*)\bigr|
  \;\le\; \bar s_t(a)\,\beta_t,
  \qquad\forall\,t,\,a,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_t$ is as in~\eqref{eq:beta}.
The self-normalized martingale inequality
\citep{abbasi2011improved} is applied to the $1$-sub-Gaussian sequence
$\xi_s=\eta_s/\sigma$ with the predictable design $\bg_s/\sigma$
and Gram matrix~$\bCbar_t$.
Each realized design vector~$\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$ is
$\cG_s$-measurable (where $\cG_s=\cF_{s-1}\cup\sigma(\bx_s,a_s)$ is
the pre-reward sigma-algebra), and the sequential
update~\eqref{eq:bar-update} ensures the hypotheses
of the self-normalized bound are satisfied.
By \Cref{lem:discrepancy},
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t{-}\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|\le
\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$.
Combined with $|\rho_t|\le\epslin(t)$, the full prediction-error
bound~\eqref{eq:pred-error} gives
\[
  |\mu_{\btheta^*}(\bx_t,a)-\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)|
  \;\le\;
  \bar s_t(a)\,\beta_t + \eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}} + \epslin(t).
\]

\paragraph{Step 2: Feature-drift sandwich and bonus bounds.}
From \Cref{asm:drift},
$(1{-}\epsdrift)\bCbar_t \preceq \bC_t \preceq (1{+}\epsdrift)\bCbar_t$.
Inverting the Loewner ordering:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:inv-drift}
  (1{+}\epsdrift)^{-1}\bCbar_t^{-1}
  \;\preceq\; \bC_t^{-1}
  \;\preceq\; (1{-}\epsdrift)^{-1}\bCbar_t^{-1}.
\end{equation}
Taking quadratic forms $\bg_t(a)^\top(\cdot)\bg_t(a)$:
\begin{align}
  s_t^2(a) &\;\ge\; (1{+}\epsdrift)^{-1}\bar s_t^2(a),
    \quad\text{hence }
    s_t(a) \ge (1{+}\epsdrift)^{-1/2}\bar s_t(a), \label{eq:drift-lower}\\
  s_t^2(a) &\;\le\; (1{-}\epsdrift)^{-1}\bar s_t^2(a),
    \quad\text{hence }
    s_t(a) \le (1{-}\epsdrift)^{-1/2}\bar s_t(a). \label{eq:drift-upper}
\end{align}

\emph{Lower bound on $w_t$ (UCB dominance of the $\bar s_t\beta_t$ term).}
The algorithm's bonus (line~\ref{line:ucb}) is
$w_t(a) = \frac{\sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\,\bar\beta_t}{\sqrt{1{-}\bar\varepsilon}}\,
\sqrt{\tilde s_t^2(a)}$,
where $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$ is the predictable confidence upper bound.
By~\eqref{eq:cg-quad} and the theorem hypothesis
$\varepsilon_I\le\bar\varepsilon$,
$\tilde s_t^2(a)\ge(1{-}\varepsilon_I)\,s_t^2(a)
\ge(1{-}\bar\varepsilon)\,s_t^2(a)$,
so $\sqrt{\tilde s_t^2(a)}\ge\sqrt{1{-}\bar\varepsilon}\,s_t(a)$ and
\[
  w_t(a)
  \;\ge\; \sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\,\bar\beta_t\,s_t(a)
  \;\ge\; \sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\,\beta_t\,s_t(a)
  \;\ge\; \beta_t\,\bar s_t(a),
\]
where the second step uses $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$ and the last uses
$\epsdriftbar\ge\epsdrift$.
Combined with the prediction-error bound from Step~1, this gives
$\mu^*(a) \le m_t(a) + w_t(a) + \eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}} + \epslin(t)$
for all~$a$; the UCB covers~$\mu^*$ up to the additive
$\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}$ and $\epslin(t)$ model-mismatch terms.

\emph{Upper bound on $w_t$.}
By~\eqref{eq:cg-quad} and $\varepsilon_I\le\bar\varepsilon$,
$\tilde s_t^2(a)\le(1{+}\bar\varepsilon)\,s_t^2(a)$, so
$w_t(a)\le\frac{\sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\,\bar\beta_t}{\sqrt{1{-}\bar\varepsilon}}\,
\sqrt{1{+}\bar\varepsilon}\,s_t(a)
=\sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\,\alpha_I\,\bar\beta_t\,s_t(a)$.
Combined with~\eqref{eq:drift-upper}:
\[
  w_t(a)
  \;\le\; \sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\,\alpha_I\,\bar\beta_t\,s_t(a)
  \;\le\; \sqrt{1{+}\epsdriftbar}\,\alpha_I\,\bar\beta_t\,
          (1{-}\epsdrift)^{-1/2}\,\bar s_t(a)
  \;\le\; \alpha_I\,\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\,\bar\beta_t\,\bar s_t(a),
\]
where the last step uses $\epsdriftbar\ge\epsdrift$ to bound
$(1{-}\epsdrift)^{-1} \le (1{-}\epsdriftbar)^{-1}$, giving
$\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}} :=
\sqrt{(1{+}\epsdriftbar)/(1{-}\epsdriftbar)}$.
Note that $\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}$ depends only on the
\emph{known input}~$\epsdriftbar$, not on the true~$\epsdrift$.
When $\epsdriftbar = \epsdrift$ (exact knowledge), this is tight.

\paragraph{Step 3: Instantaneous regret decomposition.}
From the UCB selection rule,
$m_t(a_t)+w_t(a_t)\ge m_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)$.
Since $w_t(a^*)\ge\beta_t\bar s_t(a^*)$ (Step~2, using $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$) and
$|\mu^*(a^*)-m_t(a^*)|\le\bar s_t(a^*)\beta_t+\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+\epslin(t)$
(Step~1), we have
$\mu^*(a_t^*)\le m_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)+\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+\epslin(t)$.
Therefore
\begin{align*}
  \mu^*(a_t^*)-\mu^*(a_t)
  &\;\le\;
  [m_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)+\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+\epslin(t)]
  - \mu^*(a_t) \\
  &\;\le\;
  [m_t(a_t)+w_t(a_t)+\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+\epslin(t)]
  - \mu^*(a_t)
  \quad\text{(UCB selection)} \\
  &\;=\;
  w_t(a_t) + [m_t(a_t)-\mu^*(a_t)]
  + \eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+\epslin(t) \\
  &\;\le\;
  w_t(a_t) + \bar s_t(a_t)\beta_t
  + 2\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+2\epslin(t)
  \quad\text{(prediction error~\eqref{eq:pred-error})} \\
  &\;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_I\,\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\,\bar\beta_t\,\bar s_t(a_t)
  + 2\eta_t^{\mathrm{pred}}+2\epslin(t),
\end{align*}
using $w_t(a_t)\le\alpha_I\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\bar\beta_t\bar s_t(a_t)$
(Step~2) and
$\bar s_t(a_t)\beta_t\le\alpha_I\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\bar\beta_t\bar s_t(a_t)$
(since $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$ and $\alpha_I\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\ge 1$).

\paragraph{Step 4: Elliptic potential and summation.}
By definition~\eqref{eq:infogain},
\[
  \gamma_T
  \;=\;
  \sum_{t=1}^T\log\bigl(1+\sigma^{-2}\bar s_t^2(a_t)\bigr).
\]
Define $c_t := \sigma^{-2}\bar s_t^2(a_t)$ and
$c_{\max} := \max_{t\le T} c_t$.
Since $\bar s_t^2(a) = \bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)
\le \|\bg_t(a)\|^2/\lambda \le G^2/\lambda$
(\Cref{asm:bounded}), we have $c_{\max}\le G^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$.
Using $\log(1{+}x)\ge x/(1{+}x) \ge x/(1{+}c_{\max})$ for
$x\in[0,c_{\max}]$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:potential}
  \sum_{t=1}^T c_t
  \;\le\; (1{+}c_{\max})\,\gamma_T,
  \qquad\text{i.e.,}\quad
  \sum_{t=1}^T \bar s_t^2(a_t)
  \;\le\; \bigl(\sigma^2 + G^2/\lambda\bigr)\,\gamma_T.
\end{equation}
(When $G^2\le\lambda\sigma^2$, we recover
$\sum\bar s_t^2 \le 2\sigma^2\gamma_T$ since $1+c_{\max}\le 2$.)
By Cauchy--Schwarz,
$\sum_{t=1}^T\bar s_t(a_t)
\le\sqrt{T\sum_t\bar s_t^2(a_t)}
\le\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T}$.

\paragraph{Collecting terms.}
Summing Step~3 over $t=1,\dots,T$ and using
$\bar\beta_t\le\bar\beta_{\max,T}$:
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_I\,\alpha_{\mathrm{drift}}\,\bar\beta_{\max,T}\,
    \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T}
  \;+\; 2\,E_T \;+\; 2\,P_T.
  \qed
\]

\section{CG Convergence Analysis}\label{app:cg}

After $I$~iterations of CG on the SPD system $\hat\bC_t\bu=\bg$ (starting
from $\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf{0}$), the standard energy-norm bound gives
\[
  \frac{\|\tilde\bu_I - \hat\bC_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\hat\bC_t}}
       {\|\hat\bC_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\hat\bC_t}}
  \;\le\;
  2\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa}+1}\right)^{\!I},
\]
where $\kappa=\lambda_{\max}(\hat\bC_t)/\lambda_{\min}(\hat\bC_t)$
is the true condition number.
Since $\lambda_{\min}(\hat\bC_t)\ge\lambda$ and
$\lambda_{\max}(\hat\bC_t)\le\lambda+W_tG^2/\sigma^2$
(where $W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s$), we define the upper bound
$\bar\kappa := \lambda_{\max}(\hat\bC_t)/\lambda
\le 1+W_tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2) \ge \kappa$.

Setting $\varepsilon_I = 2[(\sqrt{\bar\kappa}{-}1)/(\sqrt{\bar\kappa}{+}1)]^I$ and
requiring $\varepsilon_I\le\bar\varepsilon < 1$ gives the budget
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-budget}
  I_{\min}(\bar\kappa,\bar\varepsilon)
  \;=\;
  \left\lceil\frac{\log(2/\bar\varepsilon)}
  {\log\!\bigl((\sqrt{\bar\kappa}+1)/(\sqrt{\bar\kappa}-1)\bigr)}
  \right\rceil
  \;=\; O\!\bigl(\sqrt{\bar\kappa}\,\log(1/\bar\varepsilon)\bigr).
\end{equation}
For $W_t=n$ (fixed buffer size),
$\bar\kappa\le 1+nG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ is bounded independently of~$T$,
so $I_{\min}$ is constant.
For full history ($W_t=t{-}1$),
$\bar\kappa=O(tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2))$ grows with~$t$, and maintaining
$\varepsilon_I\le\bar\varepsilon$ requires
$I=\Omega\!\bigl(G\sqrt{t/(\lambda\sigma^2)}\,\log(1/\bar\varepsilon)\bigr)$
--- growing as $\sqrt{t}$.
In practice, the spectrum of~$\hat\bC_t$ is concentrated
\citep{granziol2026hessian} and CG converges in far fewer iterations
than the worst-case bound suggests; we measure this empirically in
\Cref{sec:experiments}.

\section{Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:approx}}\label{app:cor}

Assume the spectral sandwich~\eqref{eq:spectral-sandwich} holds for all
$t\le T$ simultaneously (with probability $\ge 1-\delta'$).
Inverting the Loewner ordering:
\[
(1{+}\varepsilon)^{-1}\bCbar_t^{-1}
\;\preceq\;\hat\bC_t^{-1}
\;\preceq\;(1{-}\varepsilon)^{-1}\bCbar_t^{-1}.
\]
Taking the quadratic form $\bg^\top(\cdot)\bg$ yields~\eqref{eq:sandwich}.

\paragraph{Effect on the exploration term.}
\emph{Confidence set validity:}
From~\eqref{eq:sandwich},
$\hat{s}_t(a)\ge(1{+}\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\bar s_t(a)$, so using
$\hat s_t$ in place of~$\bar s_t$ requires inflating
$\beta_t$ by $(1{+}\varepsilon)^{1/2}$.

\emph{Sum of bonus widths:}
From the upper bound in~\eqref{eq:sandwich},
$\hat{s}_t(a)\le(1{-}\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\bar s_t(a)$, so the elliptic
potential sum
$\sum_t\hat{s}_t(a_t)\le(1{-}\varepsilon)^{-1/2}\sum_t \bar s_t(a_t)$.

\emph{Combined factor:}
$(1{+}\varepsilon)^{1/2}\cdot(1{-}\varepsilon)^{-1/2}
= \sqrt{(1{+}\varepsilon)/(1{-}\varepsilon)}$,
applied to the exploration term of~\eqref{eq:regret-bound}.

The combined regret guarantee holds with probability at least
$1-\delta-\delta'$ (union bound over the main-theorem confidence
event and the spectral-sandwich event).
The time-uniform requirement is essential: a sandwich that holds only at a
single~$t$ does not suffice for the regret bound, which sums over all
rounds.
For the subsampled GGN (\Cref{lem:subsample}), a further union bound
over $t\le T$ (replacing $\delta'$ by $\delta'/T$ per round) yields the
time-uniform sandwich with
the sample size in~\eqref{eq:subsample-n} evaluated at the worst-case
$m=T{-}1$.\qed

\section{GGN Approximation Error}\label{app:ggn-approx}

\begin{lemma}[GGN approximation error]\label{lem:ggn-approx}
Suppose $\mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a)$ is twice differentiable in~$\btheta$.
Suppose $|r_s - \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)| \le \epsilon_r$ and
$\|\nabla^2 \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le H$
for all $s < t$.
Define $\zeta := (t{-}1)\,\epsilon_r\, H / (\sigma^2 \lambda)$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item $\|\bH_t - \bC_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le (t{-}1)\,\epsilon_r\, H / \sigma^2 = \zeta\lambda$.
\item If $\zeta < 1$, then
$(1{-}\zeta)\bC_t \preceq \bH_t \preceq (1{+}\zeta)\bC_t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The dropped term is
$D_t = \sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}(r_s{-}\mu_{\btheta_t})\nabla^2\mu_{\btheta_t}$.
By the triangle inequality,
$\|D_t\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\epsilon_r H
= \zeta\lambda$, proving~(i).
For~(ii): since $-\|D_t\|\bI \preceq -D_t \preceq \|D_t\|\bI$,
we have $\bC_t - \zeta\lambda\bI \preceq \bH_t \preceq \bC_t + \zeta\lambda\bI$.
Because $\bC_t \succeq \lambda\bI$, the lower bound gives
$\bC_t - \zeta\lambda\bI \succeq \bC_t - \zeta\bC_t = (1{-}\zeta)\bC_t$
(using $\zeta\lambda\bI \preceq \zeta\bC_t$), and similarly
$\bC_t + \zeta\lambda\bI \preceq \bC_t + \zeta\bC_t = (1{+}\zeta)\bC_t$.
\end{proof}

\section{Exponential-Family Curvature Definitions}\label{app:expfam}

For a generic negative log-likelihood $\ell\bigl(r,\,z_{\btheta}(\bx,a)\bigr)$
with natural parameter $z_{\btheta}\in\R^{k}$ (scalar for Bernoulli,
$k$-dimensional for categorical), the GGN/Fisher curvature matrices are
\begin{align}
  \bC_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
  &\;=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    J_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\,\nabla^2 A\bigl(z_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)\,
    J_t(\bx_s,a_s),
  \label{eq:curvature-exp}\\[4pt]
  \bCbar_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
  &\;=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    J_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\,\nabla^2 A\bigl(z_s(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)\,
    J_s(\bx_s,a_s),
  \label{eq:curvature-bar-exp}
\end{align}
where $J_t(\bx,a)=\nabla_{\btheta}z_{\btheta_t}(\bx,a)\in\R^{k\times d}$
is the Jacobian of the natural parameter and
$\nabla^2 A(z)$ is the output-space Hessian of the log-partition
function.
For Bernoulli rewards $\nabla^2 A(z)=p(1{-}p)$ (scalar);
for categorical rewards $\nabla^2 A(z)=\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbf{p})
-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^\top$.
Setting $k=1$ and $\nabla^2 A=\sigma^{-2}$ recovers the
Gaussian curvature~\eqref{eq:curvature}--\eqref{eq:curvature-bar}.

\section{Experimental Protocol}\label{sec:experiments}

We outline an experimental protocol for a future empirical section.
No experimental results are included in this draft; the purpose of
this section is only to make the intended evaluation concrete,
reproducible, and aligned with the theoretical claims.
The protocol focuses on (i)~whether full-curvature
exploration produces measurable regret improvements over diagonal and
last-layer surrogates, (ii)~whether the variance-calibration diagnostic
predicts failure modes, and (iii)~the computational cost of CG-based
exploration at foundation-model scale.

\subsection{Benchmarks}

\paragraph{Synthetic squared-loss bandits.}
To match the squared-loss regression framework analyzed in \Cref{sec:theory},
we construct a synthetic benchmark with ground-truth reward
$\mu^*(\bx,a)=\mu_{\btheta^*}(\bx,a)$ where $\mu_{\btheta}$ is a two-layer
MLP with $d\in\{50,200,1000\}$ parameters.
Contexts $\bx\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\bI_p)$, rewards
$r=\mu^*(\bx,a)+\eta$ with $\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$, and
$|\cA|=10$ actions.
This benchmark provides exact knowledge of~$\mu^*$, enabling
precise evaluation of calibration and the linearization error
$\epslin(t)$.
Two regimes: (a)~last-layer only (convex, $\epslin\equiv 0$) to validate
the theory in the clean regime, and (b)~full-network fine-tuning to
measure the impact of nonzero linearization error.

\paragraph{Foundation-model contextual bandit (Bernoulli/categorical).}
A pretrained encoder backbone is composed with a learned reward head;
multi-class datasets (CIFAR-100, AG News) are converted to bandit
problems by treating classes as arms with binary click/no-click reward
$r_t=\mathbf{1}[a_t=y_t]\in\{0,1\}$.
Unlike the synthetic benchmark above, this experiment uses a
\emph{Bernoulli likelihood} with the Fisher/GGN curvature from
\Cref{sec:expfam-ext}, testing the exponential-family extension in
practice (the regret theory does not cover this setting).
We use a \emph{scaling ladder} of three model sizes:
(i)~a 2-layer MLP (${\sim}10^4$ params, sanity check),
(ii)~a ViT-Small or DistilBERT backbone (${\sim}20$--$60$M params),
(iii)~a ViT-B/16 or GPT-2-124M backbone (${\sim}90$--$124$M params).
Two fine-tuning regimes:
(a)~frozen backbone with a trainable reward head (convex regime where
$\epslin\approx 0$ and $\epsdrift\approx 0$), and
(b)~last-$k$-layer fine-tuning plus head (nonconvex regime where
the feature drift~$\epsdrift$ and linearization error~$E_T$ are
empirically quantified).

\paragraph{Offline-to-online bandit conversion.}
A supervised classification dataset (UCI covertype) is converted into
bandit feedback by simulating a logging policy that reveals the label only
for the selected arm.  The agent must improve upon the logging policy
using partial labels.

\subsection{Baselines}

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{NeuralUCB / Neural-Linear UCB} \citep{zhou2020neural}:
  last-layer features with diagonal or full last-layer covariance.
\item \textbf{NeuralTS} \citep{zhang2021neural}: Thompson sampling with
  diagonal approximation of the full-parameter GGN curvature.
\item \textbf{Last-layer Laplace UCB}
  \citep{ritter2018scalable,daxberger2021laplace}:
  exact Laplace covariance restricted to the last layer, discarding
  backbone curvature.  This baseline isolates whether the ``full curvature''
  component of CC-UCB contributes beyond last-layer methods.
\item \textbf{$\varepsilon$-greedy}: exploration by uniform random action
  with probability~$\varepsilon_t = c / \sqrt{t}$.
\item \textbf{Bootstrapped ensemble}: $M=10$ bootstrap heads
  \citep{osband2016deep} with shared backbone.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Key Ablations}

Each ablation targets a specific claim tied to the novelty of CC-UCB.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Curvature fidelity (core novelty):} full GGN via CG
  vs.\ diagonal vs.\ block-diagonal vs.\ low-rank Lanczos ($k$-rank
  approximation of $\bC_t^{-1}$) vs.\ last-layer-only Laplace.
  This ablation directly tests whether diagonal/low-rank curvature
  miscalibrates exploration where full curvature succeeds.
  Metrics: cumulative regret and calibration error.
\item \textbf{CG budget:} vary the iteration budget
  $I\in\{5,10,25,50,100\}$ and the adaptive stopping target
  $\bar\varepsilon\in\{0.01,0.1,0.3,0.5\}$;
  report regret (i.e.\ effective $\alpha_I$), the fraction of rounds where
  $\varepsilon_I\ge 1$ (if using a fixed budget), and
  wall-clock time per round.
\item \textbf{Curvature-operator strategy (\S\ref{sec:curvop}):}
  compare subsampled vs.\ windowed vs.\ periodic refresh; sweep the buffer
  size $n\in\{64,256,1024,\text{all}\}$ and the refresh period
  $M\in\{1,5,20\}$.  For subsampled curvature, measure the empirical
  spectral gap $\varepsilon$ to validate the matrix Bernstein prediction
  (\Cref{lem:subsample}).
  For windowed/refresh, measure the same gap to quantify the heuristic
  approximation quality.
\item \textbf{Damping sweep:} $\lambda\in\{10^{-4},10^{-3},\dots,10^{1}\}$
  to quantify sensitivity of exploration to prior strength, CG
  conditioning, and the $\sqrt{\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda}$ factor
  in the regret bound.
\item \textbf{Fine-tuning depth:} frozen backbone (reward head only) vs.\
  last-$k$-layers vs.\ full fine-tuning, measuring how much backbone
  curvature contributes to exploration quality across the scaling ladder,
  and empirically tracking the feature drift~$\epsdrift$ and cumulative
  linearization error~$E_T$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Metrics}

\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Cumulative regret} $R_T$ (lower is better).
\item \textbf{Variance-calibration ECE} (adapted to regression):
  bin rounds by the predicted standard deviation
  $\tilde{s}_t(a_t)$ into $J$~equal-mass quantile bins, compute the
  root-mean-square prediction error (RMSE) within each bin, and report
  the weighted average gap
  $\mathrm{ECE}_{\mathrm{var}}=\sum_{j=1}^{J}(n_j/T)\,\bigl|\mathrm{RMSE}_j - \bar{s}_j\bigr|$
  where $\bar{s}_j$ is the mean predicted std in bin~$j$.  We also plot
  the reliability diagram ($\bar{s}_j$ vs.\ $\mathrm{RMSE}_j$).
  The diagnostic should correlate with regret: methods with lower
  $\mathrm{ECE}_{\mathrm{var}}$ should achieve lower cumulative regret.
\item \textbf{Wall-clock overhead:} seconds per bandit round, broken down
  into forward pass, gradient, CVP/CG, and action selection, reported
  across the scaling ladder.
\item \textbf{Feature drift and linearization error:}
  empirical estimates of $\epsdrift$ (via eigenvalue comparison of
  $\bC_t$ and $\bCbar_t$ on a subsample) and $E_T$ (via direct evaluation
  of the linearization remainder), reported over time.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Expected Outcomes and Hypotheses}

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item In the frozen-backbone regime ($\epsdrift\approx 0$, convex last
  layer), CC-UCB should match or closely track the theoretical regret
  bound, and all curvature methods should perform similarly since
  the last-layer curvature is low-dimensional.
\item In the partial fine-tuning regime, diagonal and last-layer curvature
  should demonstrably miscalibrate exploration (high
  $\mathrm{ECE}_{\mathrm{var}}$), leading to higher regret, while full-GGN
  CC-UCB maintains calibration.
\item The CG budget ablation should exhibit a phase transition: below a
  critical~$I$, $\varepsilon_I$ exceeds~1 and regret degrades sharply;
  above it, regret is insensitive to~$I$ (as the theory predicts via the
  constant~$\alpha_I$).
\item Wall-clock cost should scale linearly in~$I$ and~$n$, with the
  per-CVP cost matching a single gradient evaluation.
\end{enumerate}
