% --- abstract -------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Exploration bonuses for differentiable-model bandits depend on an inverse
curvature that is usually replaced by a diagonal or last-layer surrogate.  We
introduce a matrix-free full-GGN/Fisher reference, using separate conjugate-gradient
(CG) solves and curvature--vector products without materializing a $d\times d$
matrix.  For Gaussian rewards and squared loss, a conditional regret theorem
decomposes the cost of linearization, centering, feature and operator transfer, CG
error, and realized dynamic width complexity.  A bounded linear audit checks in
float64 the dynamic determinant identity, one-sided transfer, CG width sandwich,
width-sum inequality, and realized regret implication.  Under controlled nonlinear
adaptation, the predetermined confidence and transfer schedules fail and optimism
failures are nonmonotone; those nonlinear quantities are therefore post-hoc audits,
not predictable certificates.  Operator studies position full curvature as a
reference rather than a uniformly preferred exploration rule, and the theorem does
not certify unrestricted fine-tuning.
\end{abstract}

% --- body -----------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Contextual bandits are a standard framework for sequential
decision-making under partial feedback, and increasingly the reward
predictor is a large differentiable parametric model---a pretrained
network for text, vision, or multimodal data composed with a reward head
\citep{zhou2020neural,riquelme2018deep}.  The central algorithmic
question is how to build an exploration bonus that faithfully reflects
epistemic uncertainty over such a model's predictions.  Our theory
applies to generic differentiable parametric models; foundation models
are a motivating application, not a requirement.

In the \emph{white-box} setting---gradient and curvature--vector-product
access to the model---the canonical bonus is the GGN/Fisher-linearized
predictive variance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance-intro}
  s_t^2(a) \;=\; \bg_t(a)^\top \bC_t^{-1}\,\bg_t(a),
\end{equation}
where $\bg_t(a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$ is the
gradient feature (Jacobian of the mean reward) and $\bC_t$ is the
damped Gauss--Newton (GGN) curvature at the current
estimate~$\btheta_t$.  Computing~\eqref{eq:variance-intro} with the
full curvature is widely viewed as intractable at scale.  Some analyses
retain the full gradient-outer-product Gram (e.g.\ NeuralUCB
\citep{zhou2020neural}, whose theoretical algorithm uses the full neural-gradient
Gram), but scalable implementations commonly approximate $\bC_t$ with a diagonal,
block-diagonal, Kronecker-factored, or last-layer surrogate
\citep{riquelme2018deep,ritter2018scalable}, and full-Gram methods typically form
or store the matrix rather than accessing it matrix-free.  Each
surrogate changes the \emph{spectrum} of~$\bC_t$ and hence the value
of~$s_t^2(a)$.  The spectral change does not by itself imply a change
in the selected action, and therefore in regret; whether it does is an
empirical question, which we make measurable and study directly.

\textbf{We make this width distortion measurable and compute the
full-curvature bonus directly---no surrogate---without materializing the
matrix.}  This matrix-free reference exposes surrogate-induced changes in the
predictive widths under a common operator interface; it does \emph{not} isolate a
causal effect of curvature fidelity on regret, since damping, empirical bonus
calibration, adaptive data paths, interpolation, and selection also differ.  Scalable
curvature--vector products (CVPs), enabled by automatic differentiation
and sharded layouts such as FSDP \citep{granziol2026hessian}, let us
solve $\bC_t\,\bu = \bg_t(a)$ by conjugate gradients (CG)
\emph{without forming}~$\bC_t$: only $O(d)$ \emph{additional curvature-state}
vectors (a constant number of CG vectors) are maintained and no $d\times d$
curvature matrix or per-sample gradient vector is stored---though exact
full-history curvature--vector products still require retaining or replaying the
historical raw examples to recompute their Jacobian products, and model
parameters, optimizer state, activations, replay data, and any frozen-feature
parameter checkpoints are not counted in this $O(d)$ curvature state.  Because
the damped GGN is symmetric positive definite, CG is guaranteed to
converge.  Building on this, we propose \textbf{Curvature-Calibrated
UCB (CC-UCB)}, a bandit algorithm whose exploration bonus is the
predictive variance under the \emph{uncompressed} GGN/Fisher metric,
accessed entirely through implicit CG solves.

\textbf{Our analysis prices this exactly.}  For the
Gaussian/squared-loss instantiation we prove a \emph{conditional regret
transfer theorem} (\Cref{thm:regret}) for a \emph{general predictable SPD
algorithmic curvature sequence}: optimism is transferred by a single
\emph{one-sided} factor $u_t(a)$ (\Cref{asm:transfer}), the CG truncation
enters through a per-round factor $\alpha_t$, and the realized width complexity
$\Lamalg_T$ of the nonmonotone curvature sequence admits a \emph{pathwise
decomposition} (\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential}) into an endpoint log-determinant
and a determinant-variation term.  This decomposition generalizes the standard
elliptic-potential/monotone-Gram argument to relinearized, subsampled, windowed,
refreshed, or sketched operators, but does not by itself bound $\Lamalg_T$, which
remains an assumed or observed trajectory quantity.  The time-varying factors
enter through a single sum of
squares, not horizon maxima; the analysis uses no horizon-uniform
\emph{lower} feature-drift sandwich and no additive stability term.  The
result is modular: it prices the cost of each approximation separately.
We do \emph{not} prove these input quantities are small for unrestricted
fine-tuning; they vanish in the exactly linear frozen-feature regime and may be
small under a separately justified near-NTK or near-stationary condition, and are
otherwise reduced to primitive diagnostic targets---optimizer residuals
and whitened replayed-Jacobian drift---computable only with additional
frozen-feature replay, checkpoints, a frozen-feature matrix--vector oracle, or a
separately validated estimator
(\Cref{lem:primitive-pred,lem:whitened}).

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1) Matrix-free full-curvature reference algorithm.}~CC-UCB computes the
\emph{full} GGN/Fisher predictive-variance bonus through a fixed curvature operator
accessed by per-action CG/CVP solves---forming no $d\times d$ matrix and storing no
per-sample gradient vectors---and separates the predictable frozen-feature Gram
$\bCbar_t$ from a general predictable SPD algorithmic curvature $\Calg_t$
(\Cref{sec:problem,sec:method}).
\textbf{(2) Conditional one-sided dynamic-transfer theory.}~For the
Gaussian/squared-loss setting we prove a conditional regret reduction
(\Cref{thm:regret}) that converts a self-normalized confidence bound, a one-sided
optimism-transfer factor (\Cref{lem:whitened}), per-round CG accuracy, and a
centering/linearization certificate into regret controlled by the realized width
complexity $\Lamalg_T$, whose nonmonotonicity is priced by a pathwise determinant
decomposition (\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential}); the certificate inputs are not
established for unrestricted fine-tuning and reduce to primitive diagnostic targets.
\textbf{(3) Theory-aligned empirical audit.}~A bounded-linear audit exercises the
theorem events through float64 checks; executed nonlinear policies then expose
failures of predetermined confidence and transfer schedules under controlled drift.  Operator and CG studies
and a separate common-trajectory width diagnostic distinguish online outcomes from
post-hoc decomposition checks (\Cref{sec:experiments}).  The earlier oracle-interpolated diagnostics remain
secondary evidence in \Cref{app:legacy-diagnostics}.  The regret analysis is for
Gaussian squared loss; the CG/CVP machinery extends algorithmically to other
likelihoods, but we prove no regret theorem beyond the Gaussian case
(\Cref{app:expfam}).

\section{Problem Setup}\label{sec:problem}

We present the problem setup for the \emph{Gaussian/squared-loss}
instantiation, which is the setting analyzed in \Cref{thm:regret}.
An extension to exponential-family likelihoods is discussed in
\Cref{app:expfam}.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
At each round $t=1,\dots,T$ the learner observes a context
$\bx_t\in\mathcal{X}$ and a finite action set~$\cA_t$.  It selects
$a_t\in\cA_t$ and observes a stochastic reward
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reward}
  r_t \;=\; \mu^*(\bx_t, a_t) + \eta_t, \qquad
  \eta_t \text{ is } \sigma\text{-sub-Gaussian},
\end{equation}
where $\mu^*:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}\to\R$ is the unknown mean reward.
The goal is to minimize cumulative regret
$R_T = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl[\mu^*(\bx_t,a_t^*) - \mu^*(\bx_t,a_t)\bigr]$,
where $a_t^* = \argmax_{a\in\cA_t}\mu^*(\bx_t,a)$.
We assume $|\cA_t|\le A_{\max}$ deterministically for all~$t$.

% [filtration construction -> app:deferred]
\paragraph{Reward model and parameterization convention.}
We fix a pretrained initialization $\btheta_0\in\R^d$ and parameterize the model
by the \emph{displacement} $\btheta\in\R^d$ from it: the deployed predictor is
$\mu_{\btheta_0+\btheta}(\bx,a)$, and we abbreviate
$\mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a):=\mu_{\btheta_0+\btheta}(\bx,a)$ throughout.  The initial
displacement is $\btheta_1=\mathbf 0$ (the deployed model starts at the pretrained
weights $\btheta_0$).  With this convention the ridge penalty below is a penalty
on the \emph{displacement from the pretrained weights}, not a zero-centered prior
on the raw weights, and the realizability radius $S$ bounds
$\|\btheta^\star\|=\|\btheta^\star_{\mathrm{orig}}-\btheta_0\|$, the distance of
the true parameter from the pretrained initialization.
Define the regularized loss at round~$t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erm}
  \cL_t(\btheta)
  \;:=\;
    \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
      \bigl(r_s - \mu_{\btheta}(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)^2
    \;+\; \frac{\lambda}{2}\|\btheta\|^2 .
\end{equation}
We write $\btheta_t$ for the parameter returned by the warm-started
optimizer on~$\cL_t$.
In convex settings (e.g.\ a frozen backbone with a linear head), under exact
optimization this coincides with the global minimizer; in nonconvex settings $\btheta_t$
may be a local minimizer or approximate stationary point.
The gap between $\btheta_t$ and an idealized global minimizer is absorbed
by the action-scaled centering certificate (\Cref{asm:optim}).

\paragraph{Gradient features and curvature matrices.}
Define the \emph{gradient feature} (Jacobian of the mean reward) at the
current parameter estimate:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gfeat}
  \bg_t(\bx,a) \;:=\; \nabla_{\btheta}\,\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx,a)
  \;\in\;\R^d.
\end{equation}
The algorithm uses the \emph{relinearized curvature matrix}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature}
  \bC_t
  \;=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\,\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^{\!\top},
\end{equation}
the \emph{damped GGN curvature} for the Gaussian/squared-loss model
at the current parameter~$\btheta_t$.
(For this likelihood, the GGN coincides with the true Fisher
information; see the remark below.)
While $\bC_t$ is the natural object for
computing predictive variances at $\btheta_t$, it
re-evaluates all past Jacobians at~$\btheta_t$ and therefore does
\emph{not} admit a sequential rank-one update---all summands change
when $\btheta_t$ changes.

For the regret analysis we introduce the
\emph{frozen-feature Gram matrix}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature-bar}
  \bCbar_t
  \;:=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)\,\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^{\!\top},
\end{equation}
where each $\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx_s,a_s)$
is evaluated at the parameter~$\btheta_s$ that was current when the sample
was collected.  This matrix satisfies the recursive update
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bar-update}
  \bCbar_{t+1}=\bCbar_t+\sigma^{-2}\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)^\top,
\end{equation}
and, critically, each realized design vector
$\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$ is measurable with respect to the pre-reward
sigma-algebra $\cG_s=\cHist_s\vee\sigma(a_s)$ of~\eqref{eq:filtration},
which is the filtration used in the self-normalized bound.
Both the additive update~\eqref{eq:bar-update} and
this measurability are structural requirements of the
self-normalized martingale bound and the elliptic potential lemma invoked
in~\Cref{sec:theory}.
The algorithm computes UCB widths using the algorithmic curvature~$\Calg_t$
(via CG; the exact case is $\Calg_t=\bC_t$), but the confidence analysis is
conducted in terms of~$\bCbar_t$; the one-sided optimism-transfer factor
of \Cref{asm:transfer}, certified by \Cref{lem:whitened}, provides the
bridge.

\section{Method: Curvature-Calibrated Exploration}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{CC-UCB Algorithm}

For a predictable SPD oracle $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$, set
$\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$ for the original nonlinear center.
\Cref{alg:ccucb} is the analyzed full-enumeration policy.  The same fixed
curvature operator is reused across separate per-action CG solves; a solution
for one action is not reused as the solution for another action.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Curvature-Calibrated UCB (CC-UCB)}\label{alg:ccucb}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Observe context $\bx_t$ and finite action set $\cA_t$.
\STATE Construct a fixed predictable SPD oracle $\bv\mapsto\Calg_t\bv$ with $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$.
\FOR{each $a\in\cA_t$}
  \STATE Compute $m_t(a)=\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$ and $\bg_t(a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$.
  \STATE If $\bg_t(a)\ne\mathbf0$, solve $\Calg_t\bu=\bg_t(a)$ separately by CG until the certified tolerance holds; otherwise set $\tilde\bu=\mathbf0$. \label{line:cg}
  \STATE Set $\tilde s_t^2(a)=\bg_t(a)^\top\tilde\bu$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Select and play $a_t\in\argmax_{a\in\cA_t}\{m_t(a)+\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}\sqrt{\tilde s_t^2(a)}\}$. \label{line:ucb}
\STATE Observe $r_t$, append $(\bx_t,a_t,r_t)$, and update $\btheta_{t+1}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The oracle is accessed only through matrix--vector products.  At each round the
agent computes,
for every candidate action~$a$, the gradient feature
$\bg_t(a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$ and the
\emph{curvature-calibrated variance proxy}, the generic algorithmic width,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
  s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)
  \;=\;
  \bg_t(a)^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\,\bg_t(a).
\end{equation}
Under the GGN/Fisher-linearized Gaussian approximation with covariance
$\Calg_t^{-1}$, the quantity $s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$ is the metric-dependent
predictive variance of the linearized reward at $(\bx_t,a)$.
The \emph{exact} full-history instantiation of CC-UCB takes $\Calg_t=\bC_t$, the
relinearized curvature~\eqref{eq:curvature}; weighted replay, subsampling,
sliding windows, periodic refreshes, and randomized sketches are special
implementations of $\Calg_t$ (\S\ref{sec:curvop}), covered by the theory only
when the corresponding one-sided transfer factor is certified.

Because $\Calg_t\succ 0$ by construction, conjugate gradients (CG) is guaranteed
to converge on the system $\Calg_t\bu=\bg_t(a)$, provided the operator is held
\emph{fixed} across all iterations of the solve (the randomized sketch/subsample
is drawn into $\Omega_t$ before action selection; \S\ref{sec:problem}).  For each
action with $\bg_t(a)\ne\mathbf0$ we run CG to the per-action relative
energy-norm error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-err-eta}
  \varepsilon_{t,a}
  \;:=\;
  \frac{\|\bu_{t,a}-\tilde\bu_{t,a}\|_{\Calg_t}}{\|\bu_{t,a}\|_{\Calg_t}},
  \qquad
  \bu_{t,a}:=\Calg_t^{-1}\bg_t(a),
\end{equation}
yielding the computable proxy $\tilde{s}_t^2(a)=\bg_t(a)^\top\tilde\bu_{t,a}$;
if $\bg_t(a)=\mathbf0$ we set $\tilde{s}_t^2(a)=0$ and skip the solve (leaving the
residual ratio $\|r_I\|/\|\bg\|$ undefined), and by convention put
$\varepsilon_{t,a}:=0$ in this case, so that $\varepsilon_{t,a}$ is defined for
every $a\in\cA_t$ and~\eqref{eq:cg-quad} holds trivially there.
\Cref{lem:cg} shows $\tilde{s}_t^2(a)$ approximates $s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$ within a
multiplicative $(1\pm\varepsilon_{t,a})$ factor.  To ensure the UCB bonus
\emph{upper-bounds} the confidence width $\omega_t\,\bar s_t(a)$ despite the
approximation, the algorithm inflates the width by
$\sqrt{u_t(a)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$ (line~\ref{line:ucb}), where
$\bar\varepsilon_t\in(0,1)$ is a \emph{per-round} known tolerance enforced
uniformly over all actions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-tol-uniform}
  \max_{a\in\cA_t}\varepsilon_{t,a}
  \;\le\;\bar\varepsilon_t\;<\;1,
\end{equation}
and $u_t(a)\ge1$ is the one-sided transfer factor (\Cref{asm:transfer}).

\section{Theoretical Analysis}\label{sec:theory}

We state a regret bound for CC-UCB (\Cref{alg:ccucb}, with full action
enumeration $K{=}|\cA_t|$) under the Gaussian/squared-loss setup of
\Cref{sec:problem} and
\Cref{asm:noise,asm:bounded,asm:linear,asm:optim,asm:transfer}.
The analysis does not constitute a full exponential-family or generalized
linear bandit proof; the extension in \Cref{app:expfam} is a
curvature-definition extension only.
The analysis handles a \emph{general predictable SPD algorithmic curvature
sequence} $\Calg_t$~\eqref{eq:calg-props}.  Confidence is proved for the
frozen-feature Gram $\bCbar_t$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:curvature-bar}), which has the
sequential-update and predictability properties required by the
self-normalized martingale bound; the \emph{one-sided} optimism-transfer
factor~$u_t(a)$ (\Cref{asm:transfer}) bridges from $\bCbar_t$ to $\Calg_t$
in the exploration direction only (no lower drift sandwich); the CG
approximation enters through the per-round factor $\alpha_t$; and the
nonmonotonicity of $\Calg_t$ is priced pathwise by the dynamic
log-determinant potential (\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential}).

The theorem is a conditional transfer result: once the linearization error
$E_T$, the centering certificate $\bar\psi_t$, and the one-sided transfer
factor $u_t$ are supplied, the regret follows with the stated per-round
factors and the realized dynamic complexity $\Lamalg_T$; the paper does not
prove those input quantities from primitive network assumptions, and does
not claim sublinear regret for unrestricted fine-tuning.

\paragraph{Core conditions and links.}
Assume conditionally $\sigma$-sub-Gaussian noise, $\|\bg_t(\bx_s,a)\|\le G$,
realizability by $\|\btheta^*\|\le S$, and a local expansion with round-$t$
remainder at most $\epslin(t)$.  For the original center assume the
action-scaled discrepancy is at most $\bar\psi_t\bar s_t(a)$.  The operator
$\Calg_t$ is predictable, SPD, satisfies $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ and
$\Calg_1=\lambda\bI$, and remains fixed throughout every CG solve.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-widths}
\begin{aligned}
 \bar s_t^2(a)&=\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a),&
 s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)&=\bg_t(a)^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bg_t(a),\\
 \tilde s_t^2(a)&=\bg_t(a)^\top\tilde\bu_t(a).&&
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The analysis requires only the one-sided transfer and uniform CG relations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-transfer-cg}
\begin{aligned}
 \bar s_t^2(a)&\le u_t(a)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a),\\
 (1-\bar\varepsilon_t)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)&\le\tilde s_t^2(a)
 \le(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
for every candidate action, where $u_t(a)\ge1$, $\bar\varepsilon_t<1$, and
$\alpha_t=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$.  Sufficient
feature-drift and approximate-operator constructions are given in
\Cref{lem:whitened,thm:spectral-distortion}; no lower spectral transfer is
needed.

\paragraph{Dynamic width complexity.}
The realized quantity used below is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-lambda}
 \Lamalg_T:=\sum_{t=1}^T\log\!\left(1+\sigma^{-2}s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\right).
\end{equation}
Let $\Calg_t^+=\Calg_t+\sigma^{-2}\bg_t(a_t)\bg_t(a_t)^\top$ and
$\Xi_t=(\Calg_t^+)^{-1/2}(\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^+)(\Calg_t^+)^{-1/2}$.
The pathwise determinant identity and its width consequence are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-dynamic}
\begin{aligned}
 \Lamalg_T&=\log\frac{\det(\Calg_{T+1})}{\det(\lambda\bI)}
 -\sum_{t=1}^T\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t),\\
 \sum_{t=1}^T s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)
 &\le(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\Lamalg_T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Thus relinearization, windowing, and refreshes are charged through the actual
widths and determinant-destroying operator changes; the identity does not imply
that this complexity is small.  Complete statements and proofs are in
\Cref{sec:dynamic-potential,app:proof}.

\subsection{Regret Bound}

Let $\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}$ denote the simultaneous self-normalized confidence
event and let $\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$ collect the predictable linearization,
centering, transfer, condition-number, CG, and randomized-operator certificates.
The appendix defines each component and its failure allocation; write their
total failure probability as $\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.

\begin{theorem}[Dynamic transfer regret bound for CC-UCB (Gaussian/squared-loss)]\label{thm:regret}
Under the conditions above and \Cref{asm:noise,asm:bounded,asm:linear,asm:optim,asm:transfer},
suppose $|\cA_t|\le A_{\max}$, the optimal action is present, and
\Cref{alg:ccucb} enumerates all actions using a predictable schedule
$\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$ and uniform per-round CG tolerance.  Let
$E_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\epslin(t)$, $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$,
$\alpha_t=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$, and
$S_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t^2u_t(a_t)\omega_t^2$.  On
$\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}\cap\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret-bound}
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sqrt{(\sigma^2 + G^2/\lambda)\;\Lamalg_T\;S_T}
  \;+\; 2\,E_T,
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-\delta-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.  Deterministic or
almost-sure certificates contribute zero to $\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.  For the
corrected center, the same statement holds without \Cref{asm:optim} after
replacing $\omega_t$ by $\bar\beta_t$.
\end{theorem}

In particular, the bound is sublinear whenever
$\Lamalg_T S_T=o(T^2)$ and $E_T=o(T)$.

The exact-curvature instantiation is $\Calg_t=\bC_t$ with
$u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ from \Cref{lem:whitened}; approximate operators are
covered through \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}.  The proof is in
\Cref{app:proof}.

\paragraph{Proof sketch.}
Self-normalized confidence plus the centering and linearization terms gives
$|\mu^*(a)-\mu_{\btheta_t}(a)|\le\omega_t\bar s_t(a)+\epslin(t)$.
The one-sided relation and CG lower bound make the computed bonus optimistic,
so instantaneous regret is at most $2w_t(a_t)+2\epslin(t)$.  The CG upper
bound gives $w_t(a_t)\le\alpha_t\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a_t)}s_{\Calg,t}(a_t)$.
Summing, applying Cauchy--Schwarz only once, and using \eqref{eq:main-dynamic}
yields \eqref{eq:regret-bound}; hence time-varying factors remain inside $S_T$
rather than being replaced by horizon maxima.

The corrected center replaces $\omega_t$ by $\bar\beta_t$ and removes the
centering certificate, but requires exact or certified frozen-feature access;
the formal statement and implementation discussion are in
\Cref{cor:corrected-center}.

\section{Experiments}\label{sec:experiments}

All primary outcomes below come from separately executed online policies.  Known
synthetic means are used only for evaluation.  Exact replay quantities are labelled
post hoc and are not supplied to nonlinear policies.  The common-trajectory study
holds history, parameters, actions, damping, and bonus fixed to isolate width
changes, but makes no causal claim about online regret.  The legacy interpolated
diagnostics are confined to \Cref{app:legacy-diagnostics}.

\subsection{Theory-factorial audit}\label{sec:factorial-audit}

\paragraph{Linear theorem-event audit.}
Normalized Rademacher contexts have $p=8$ and five actions, with bounded feature
$\phi(x,a)=[x,e_a,x\otimes e_a]\in\R^{53}$ and a fixed parameter for which the
optimal arm varies with context.  Rewards have Gaussian noise $\sigma=0.25$.
We enumerate every action and run to $T=1000$, reporting checkpoints at
$T\in\{250,500,1000\}$.  Ten tuning seeds select only from the prespecified
$(\lambda,\cbonus)$ grid, after which 20 disjoint evaluation seeds execute fresh
policies with common random numbers.  We report both a fixed-reference comparison
(common damping and bonus schedule) and validation-tuned operators.  In both,
$\cbonus\ge1$ multiplies the already valid deterministic base radius
$\beta_t^{\rm base}$ to produce $\bar\beta_t$; it never
replaces the theoretical radius (full policy formulas are in
\Cref{app:new-experiments}).

The seven linear policies are audited against dense float64 matrices; here
$E_T=F_T=\psi_t=\chi_t=0$ and $\bC_t=\bCbar_t$ numerically.  Diagonal,
rescaled-subsample, and Lanczos generalized-eigenvalue estimates and the full-CG
condition estimate lack verified upper enclosures, so those four policies are
theorem-event audits, not certified executions.  All runs compute dense pre-action
transfer and condition-number oracles available only at this audit scale; they test
theorem semantics, not scalable certification.  Every trace passes the recorded
float64 determinant-identity, width-sum, CG-sandwich, transfer, and regret-implication
checks.  For dense full Gram, mean
$R_T/T$ decreases from $0.163$ to $0.080$
and RHS$/T$ from $58.8$ to $37.1$ between $T=250$ and 1000, whereas window 64
stays near $R_T/T=0.298$ while RHS$/T$ increases from $75.2$ to $77.6$.  This is a
finite-horizon trajectory observation, not evidence of asymptotic sublinearity.
The reported RHS values are numerically loose: dense-full RHS$/R_T$ rises from
$361.5$ to $461.7$, and every reported RHS exceeds the exact support-wise maximum
possible cumulative pseudo-regret (all ratios are in \Cref{app:new-experiments}).

\paragraph{Controlled smooth feature drift.}
The nonlinear task is a realizable 45-parameter tanh network with $p=4$, five
context-dependent actions, and a known teacher.  At $T=100$ we compare an exact
frozen-head solve with mild, medium, and aggressive capped full-network updates,
executing both the original and exact corrected centers on 20 evaluation seeds.
The time-only policy schedules are fixed before execution.  Teacher quantities,
replayed Jacobians, dense float64 generalized eigenvalues, and reference CG errors
are used only after action selection; these runs are therefore executed-policy
post-hoc audits, not certified nonlinear executions.

The frozen-head regime recovers the linear invariants (all of $E_T,\psi_t,\chi_t$
are numerical zero).  Moving away from it raises linearization error and dynamic
complexity, but not monotonically every factor: for the corrected center, mean
$E_T$ rises from zero to $0.39,1.32,2.55$, while $\Lamalg_T$ rises from $151.4$ to

every nonlinear round, and the supplied transfer schedule fails on most adapting
rounds.  We therefore call its numerical RHS a \emph{post-hoc decomposition score},
not a valid regret certificate or theorem bound for these policies.

\subsection{Operator and CG trade-offs}\label{sec:operator-cg-audit}

On the linear task we vary window and subsample sizes, Lanczos rank, and stale
refresh period.  Dense float64 generalized-eigenvalue calculations give the global
factor $\kappa_{+,t}$; all-action widths give a separate action-set factor.  A logged
common trajectory evaluates every operator on identical data and checkpoints,
whereas online regret always comes from independently executed policies.  As
predicted, unrescaled windows satisfy $\kappa_{+,t}\le1$ numerically; diagonal,
Lanczos, rescaled-subsample, and stale operators exhibit different transfer and
dynamic-width trade-offs rather than an ordering by curvature error alone.  Under
the common fixed schedule, full and frozen Gram average regret $55.37$ and
$\Lamalg_T=258.3$; diagonal averages $32.57$ and $\Lamalg_T=176.3$ despite a large
global factor, whereas window 16 has the favorable factor one but regret $163.49$
and $\Lamalg_T=1725.6$.  The paired full-minus-diagonal regret difference is
$22.80$ $[20.42,25.17]$.  These are online comparisons with different adaptive
paths, not causal effects of the isolated operator change.  The full-Gram value
$55.37$ is from this $T=500$, seeds 120--139 operator protocol; Table~1 uses the
$T=1000$, seeds 100--119 linear audit (whose corresponding $T=500$ value is
$55.66$), so the values do not estimate one common configuration.

The same 11-operator factorial on the medium-drift corrected-center task uses
20 fresh online policies per operator plus separately logged common trajectories.
Full relinearized curvature has regret $13.23$ $[12.64,13.82]$, optimism-violation
rate $3.21\%$, and $\Lamalg_T=168.0$; diagonal has $6.74$ $[6.07,7.40]$,
$10.75\%$, and $102.7$, despite mean maximum global factor $964$.  Window 16
has global factor one but regret $35.00$ and $\Lamalg_T=484.9$.  The paired
diagonal-minus-full regret difference is $-6.50$ $[-7.37,-5.62]$.  These are
executed policies with dense float64 post-hoc transfer audits, not certified
nonlinear executions; the common-trajectory widths are reported separately from
regret.

The dense-reference CG audit spans condition numbers $10,100,1000$, tolerances
$\{0.5,0.25,0.1,0.05,0.01\}$, zero and warm starts, and no versus Jacobi
preconditioning.  Across 600,000 action solves on ten evaluation seeds, every
recorded residual bound, requested energy-error target, and multiplicative width
sandwich passes its dense-reference float64 check; no inflated-width undercoverage
is observed.  A separate
$T{=}200$ executed-policy sweep covers all 20 tolerance/start/preconditioner cells
on seeds 130--139; all 200 executions pass the recorded CG-error checks with zero
observed optimism violations.
For zero-start Jacobi CG, tolerance $0.5$ gives regret $42.20$ $[40.52,43.89]$,
$6.95$ iterations/action, and $0.358$ seconds/run, versus $36.74$
$[35.25,38.23]$, $17.58$, and $0.483$ at tolerance $0.01$; the paired regret
difference is $5.46$ $[3.65,7.28]$.  Warm starts begin with mean relative energy
error about $1.74$ (versus one at zero start), use more iterations in every cell,
and show no resolved regret difference, so they are not automatically beneficial.
A CPU scaling audit covers $d\in\{32,64,128\}$,
$n\in\{32,128,512\}$, $K\in\{5,10\}$, and CG budgets $I\in\{5,15,30\}$; it is a
measured small-scale study, not an extrapolation to foundation models.

\paragraph{Real-data baseline check.}
The Covertype study (\Cref{sec:covertype-rerun}) fails its basic baseline check:
at every horizon, deployable UCB1 and Beta--Bernoulli Thompson sampling beat every
contextual method.  It is not external validation of curvature-aware exploration.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

CC-UCB supplies a matrix-free full-GGN/Fisher reference and a conditional theory
that separates confidence, centering, feature drift, operator transfer, CG error,
and realized dynamic width complexity.  The linear audit exercises the stated
event logic and passes its recorded float64 checks, while controlled nonlinear runs
show that predetermined confidence and transfer schedules can fail and optimism
failures need not grow monotonically with drift.  The Covertype audit fails a basic
noncontextual baseline check and is therefore not used as evidence about curvature
choice.
Full curvature is therefore a reference operator, not a universally preferred
exploration method.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The theorem is restricted to Gaussian rewards, squared loss, full action
enumeration, and predictable valid certificates.  It does not establish the
linearization, centering, transfer, or dynamic-complexity schedules for unrestricted
fine-tuning; the nonlinear and Covertype runs are not certified by the theorem.
Corrected centering requires frozen-feature access, and warm starts or action
screening need additional certificates.  The finite-horizon RHS values are
numerically vacuous; unenclosed spectra also make four linear policies post-hoc audits.
The experiments use small float64 models on one CPU host, so measured operation,
runtime, and memory results do not establish
large-model feasibility.  Synthetic center and operator comparisons follow
different adaptive trajectories unless explicitly labelled common-trajectory, and
none identifies a causal mechanism.  The real-data horizon and seed count remain
limited, and every contextual method trails both deployable non-contextual
baselines.  Establishing predictable certificates and measuring full-network GGN-CG
at realistic scale remain open.

\section{Deferred main-text derivations}\label{app:deferred}
This appendix collects the formal statements, proofs, and derivations deferred
from the compact main text; all cross-referenced results are stated here.

For the sequential frozen-feature Gram, the usual information gain is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infogain}
\gamma_T:=\log\frac{\det(\bCbar_{T+1})}{\det(\lambda\bI)}
=\sum_{t=1}^T\log\!\left(1+\sigma^{-2}\bar s_t^2(a_t)\right).
\end{equation}

The dynamic-potential and CG-observable consequences of \Cref{thm:regret} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret-bound-dyn}
\begin{aligned}
R_T&\le2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\Gamdyn_T S_T}+2E_T,\\
R_T&\le2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\widehat{\Lamalg}_T S_T}+2E_T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Corrected-center variant, no centering certificate]\label{cor:corrected-center}
Replace the predictive center in \Cref{alg:ccucb} by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-center}
m_t^{\mathrm{corr}}(a):=\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)
+\bg_t(a)^\top(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}-\btheta_t)
\end{equation}
and use $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$.  Under the hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:regret} except \Cref{asm:optim}, the bounds
\eqref{eq:regret-bound}--\eqref{eq:regret-bound-dyn} hold.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Local linearization gives
$\mu^*(\bx_t,a)-m_t^{\mathrm{corr}}(a)
=\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta^*-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})+\rho_t$.
\Cref{lem:confidence} bounds the first term by
$\beta_t\bar s_t(a)$ and $|\rho_t|\le\epslin(t)$, which is the first step
of the regret proof with $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$; all later steps are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cost of the corrected center]
Exact evaluation requires a solve against $\bCbar_t$.  A constant number of
$O(d)$ CG vectors is insufficient by itself: one additionally needs stored
historical frozen gradients, raw replay data with the historical parameter
checkpoints needed to reconstruct them, a frozen-feature matrix--vector oracle,
an explicit low-dimensional representation, or a certified sketch.  Maintaining
$\bCbar_t$ explicitly costs $O(d^2)$ memory.  Approximate frozen-feature access
introduces an error that must be returned to the centering certificate unless
separately controlled.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Assumptions}\label{sec:assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[Sub-Gaussian noise]\label{asm:noise}
The noise $\eta_t$ is conditionally $\sigma$-sub-Gaussian given the
context and action:
$\mathbb{E}[\exp(s\eta_t)\mid\cG_t]\le\exp(s^2\sigma^2/2)$
for all~$s\in\R$, where
$\cG_t := \cHist_t\vee\sigma(a_t)$ is the pre-reward sigma-algebra
of~\eqref{eq:filtration}, available immediately after the action is
selected and before the reward is revealed.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded gradient features]\label{asm:bounded}
For a constant $G>0$,
\[
  \sup_{t\le T}\;\sup_{s\le t}\;\sup_{a}
  \|\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a)\| \;\le\; G.
\]
This bounds both the current-round features
$\bg_t(\bx_t,a)$ and the replayed features
$\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)$ appearing in the curvature
matrix~\eqref{eq:curvature}, as well as the frozen features
$\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$ in~\eqref{eq:curvature-bar}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Local linearization]\label{asm:linear}
For each $(\bx,a)$, the reward model admits the expansion
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearization}
  \mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a) \;=\;
    \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx,a)
    + \bg_t(\bx,a)^\top(\btheta - \btheta_t)
    + \rho_t(\bx,a,\btheta),
\end{equation}
where the remainder satisfies
$|\rho_t(\bx,a,\btheta)| \le \tfrac{L}{2}\|\btheta - \btheta_t\|^2$
for a constant~$L\ge 0$.
Evaluating at $\btheta=\btheta^*$ gives a time-varying linearization error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epslin-t}
  \epslin(t)
  \;:=\;
  \sup_{a\in\cA_t}|\rho_t(\bx_t,a,\btheta^*)|
  \;\le\;
  \frac{L}{2}\|\btheta^* - \btheta_t\|^2.
\end{equation}
For total regret to be sublinear, we require the cumulative linearization
error $E_T:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\epslin(t) = o(T)$; see \Cref{thm:regret}
for the precise dependence.  Concretely, $E_T=O(1)$ when
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(1/t)$ (since
$\epslin(t)=O(1/t^2)$ and $\sum t^{-2}<\infty$;
achievable in convex or strongly convex regimes),
and $E_T=O(\sqrt{T})$ when
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(t^{-1/4})$.
In practice this is enforced by restricting fine-tuning depth,
using trust-region updates, or freezing the backbone (which renders the
last-layer model convex or near-linear with small~$L$).
These rate examples are illustrative only.  The present paper does not
prove convergence of the nonconvex optimization path
$\{\btheta_t\}$ to~$\btheta^*$; sublinear regret therefore requires
this term to be controlled by an external stability or convergence
argument.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Action-scaled centering certificate]\label{asm:optim}
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred} below proves the geometric bound
$\bigl|\bg_t(\bx_t,a)^\top(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})\bigr|
\le\bar s_t(a)\,\psi_t$ for every $a\in\cA_t$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi-def}
  \psi_t \;:=\; \frac{\zeta_t}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
  \;+\; \|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}},
\end{equation}
$\zeta_t$ is the optimizer gradient-residual bound
($\|\nabla\cL_t(\btheta_t)\|_2\le\zeta_t$) and $M_t$ is the gradient
mismatch vector defined there.
We assume there is an $\cHist_t$-measurable upper bound
$\bar\psi_t\ge\psi_t$ (the \emph{centering certificate}) such that,
for every action $a\in\cA_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pred-stab}
  \bigl|\bg_t(\bx_t,a)^\top
    \bigl(\btheta_t - \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}\bigr)\bigr|
  \;\le\;
  \bar\psi_t\,\bar s_t(a).
\end{equation}
The discrepancy is thus \emph{action-scaled}: it multiplies the
confidence width $\bar s_t(a)$ rather than entering additively.  The
certificate absorbs the same three sources as before---optimizer residual
$\zeta_t$, replayed-Jacobian drift inside $M_t$, and higher-order nonlinear
terms---but now inside a single per-round scalar $\bar\psi_t$.
In the convex-in-parameters regime (frozen backbone with a linear reward
head), under exact optimization $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}=\btheta_t$ and
$\bar\psi_t=0$.
The corrected-center variant of \Cref{cor:corrected-center} removes
$\bar\psi_t$ entirely, at the cost of an explicit frozen-feature solve.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[Status of Assumption~\ref{asm:optim}]
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred} reduces $\psi_t$ to optimizer residuals and
replayed-Jacobian drift, but $\bar\psi_t$ remains an externally supplied or
separately certified quantity unless the implementation computes a valid
upper bound; we do not prove $\bar\psi_t$ is small outside the convex
last-layer regime.  \Cref{thm:regret} should therefore be read as a
conditional transfer result: $\bar\psi_t$ enters the exploration term
through $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$
(\Cref{eq:omega-def}), so its certification is one of the explicit
open certificate requirements listed in the Limitations, not an
additive $O(T)$ term.
\end{remark}

The next lemma gives the primitive geometric bound underlying
\Cref{asm:optim}, expressed through the certifiable quantity $\psi_t$.

\begin{lemma}[Primitive upper bound for prediction discrepancy]\label{lem:primitive-pred}
Let $z_s=(\bx_s,a_s)$,
$\bg_s=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(z_s)$,
$\bg_{s,t}=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(z_s)$, and
$\delta_{s,t}=\bg_{s,t}-\bg_s$.  Define
\[
  \bar\mu_{s,t}
  :=
  \mu_{\btheta_s}(z_s)+\bg_s^\top(\btheta_t-\btheta_s),
  \qquad
  e_{s,t}:=\mu_{\btheta_t}(z_s)-\bar\mu_{s,t},
  \qquad
  \bar r_{s,t}:=\bar\mu_{s,t}-r_s .
\]
Let $\bar{\cL}_t$ be the frozen-feature linearized ridge objective whose
minimizer is $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$, and define
\[
  M_t
  :=
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}
  \sum_{s<t}
  \left[
    \bar r_{s,t}\delta_{s,t}
    +
    e_{s,t}\bg_s
    +
    e_{s,t}\delta_{s,t}
  \right].
\]
If the nonlinear optimizer satisfies
$\|\nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)\|_2\le\zeta_t$, then for every candidate
action $a\in\cA_t$,
\[
  \left|
  \bg_t(a)^\top
  (\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})
  \right|
  \;\le\;
  \bar s_t(a)
  \left(
    \frac{\zeta_t}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
    +
    \|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
  \right),
  \qquad
  \bar s_t(a):=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)} .
\]
The left-hand side is exactly the geometric bound
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|\le\bar s_t(a)\,\psi_t$
of \Cref{asm:optim} with $\psi_t=\zeta_t/\sqrt\lambda+\|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}$
(\Cref{eq:psi-def}).  Consequently, since
$\|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}\le\|M_t\|_2/\sqrt{\lambda}$, a valid centering
certificate is
\[
  \bar\psi_t
  \;:=\;
  \frac{\zeta_t+\|M_t\|_2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
  \;\ge\;
  \psi_t .
\]
If, in addition, on a fixed event,
$\btheta\mapsto\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta}(z)$ is $L_g$-Lipschitz,
$|\bar r_{s,t}|\le B_{t,\delta}$ for all $s<t$, and
$\Delta_{s,t}:=\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|$, then
\[
  \|M_t\|_2
  \;\le\;
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}
  \sum_{s<t}
  \left[
    B_{t,\delta} L_g\Delta_{s,t}
    +
    \frac{G L_g}{2}\Delta_{s,t}^2
    +
    \frac{L_g^2}{2}\Delta_{s,t}^3
  \right].
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The linearized ridge objective satisfies
$\nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})=0$ and, by its
quadratic form,
\[
  \nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)
  =
  \bCbar_t(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}).
\]
Thus
$\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
=\bCbar_t^{-1}\nabla\bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)$.
Using
$\mu_{\btheta_t}(z_s)-r_s=\bar r_{s,t}+e_{s,t}$ and
$\bg_{s,t}=\bg_s+\delta_{s,t}$, the gradients of the nonlinear loss
and the frozen-feature linearized loss satisfy
\[
  \nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)
  =
  \nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)+M_t,
  \qquad
  \text{so}\qquad
  \nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)=\nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)-M_t .
\]
Therefore, by Cauchy--Schwarz in the $\bCbar_t$-norm,
\[
  |\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|
  \le
  \bar s_t(a)
  \|\nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
  \le
  \bar s_t(a)
  \left(
    \|\nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
    +
    \|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
  \right).
\]
Since $\bCbar_t\succeq\lambda\bI$,
$\|\nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}\le \zeta_t/\sqrt{\lambda}$,
which gives the geometric inequality
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|
\le\bar s_t(a)\bigl(\zeta_t/\sqrt{\lambda}+\|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}\bigr)
=\bar s_t(a)\,\psi_t$, i.e.\ the bound of \eqref{eq:psi-def}.
For the certificate, $\bCbar_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ also gives
$\|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}\le\|M_t\|_2/\sqrt{\lambda}$, so
$\psi_t\le(\zeta_t+\|M_t\|_2)/\sqrt{\lambda}=:\bar\psi_t\ge\psi_t$, the proposed
certificate.
For the final display, Lipschitzness gives
$\|\delta_{s,t}\|\le L_g\Delta_{s,t}$ and the Taylor remainder bound
$|e_{s,t}|\le (L_g/2)\Delta_{s,t}^2$; applying these together with
$\|\bg_s\|\le G$ (\Cref{asm:bounded}) and $|\bar r_{s,t}|\le B_{t,\delta}$
term by term in the definition of $M_t$ gives the claimed bound on $\|M_t\|_2$.
\end{proof}

In frozen linear-head models, $M_t=0$ and, under exact optimization,
$\zeta_t=0$, hence $\psi_t=0$ and the certificate $\bar\psi_t=0$.  In
near-NTK or small-update regimes, the displayed bound shows that the
relevant quantity is accumulated replayed-Jacobian drift, such as
$\sum_{s<t}\Delta_{s,t}$ and its higher-order analogues.  It converts the
centering certificate of \Cref{asm:optim} into primitive diagnostic terms that must
be verified or bounded by a separate optimization argument (they are not directly
observable using only the $O(d)$ additional curvature-state memory of the
implementation).  Any valid
$\cHist_t$-measurable upper bound on the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:psi-def}
serves as $\bar\psi_t$.
The residual envelope $B_{t,\delta}$ should be read as a conditional
high-probability event or as a predictable truncation/diagnostic
quantity; it is not an additional bounded-noise assumption in the main
regret theorem.

\paragraph{Generic algorithmic curvature.}
The analysis is conducted for a \emph{generic predictable SPD algorithmic
curvature sequence} $\{\Calg_t\}_{t\ge1}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calg-props}
  \Calg_t\ \text{is symmetric},\qquad
  \Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI\succ 0,\qquad
  \Calg_t\ \text{is}\ \cHist_t\text{-measurable}.
\end{equation}
This is the operator whose inverse the algorithm actually applies (via CG)
to form widths.  The exact full-history result is the special case
$\Calg_t=\bC_t$ (relinearized curvature~\eqref{eq:curvature}); approximate,
subsampled, windowed, refreshed, or sketched operators $\hat\bC_t$ are also
represented by $\Calg_t$, but are covered only when the required transfer
certificate below is available.  Write the algorithmic width
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalg-def}
  s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)\;:=\;\bg_t(a)^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bg_t(a).
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[One-sided optimism transfer]\label{asm:transfer}
For every round $t\le T$ there is an $\cHist_t$-measurable factor
$u_t(a)\ge 1$ such that, for every candidate action $a\in\cA_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer}
  \bar s_t^2(a)\;\le\;u_t(a)\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a).
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

This is the \emph{only} spectral relation the main theorem needs, and it is
one-sided: it dominates the predictable frozen-feature width $\bar s_t(a)$
(the object of the self-normalized confidence bound) by an inflated
algorithmic width.  No \emph{lower} Loewner factor on $\Calg_t$ is required
for optimism.  The factor may be action-dependent; when a uniform per-round
$u_t$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:transfer} for every action we write $u_t$ and
state the action-dependent theorem with $u_t(a)$.
For the exact-curvature instantiation $\Calg_t=\bC_t$, the whitened
relative-drift lemma below certifies~\eqref{eq:transfer} with the uniform
factor $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$; for an approximate operator, the composition
of \Cref{lem:whitened} with a one-sided spectral bound
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\kappa_{+,t}\bC_t$ gives
$u_t=\kappa_{+,t}(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ (\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}).

The following lemma supplies the exact-curvature transfer factor through a
\emph{whitened, relative} feature-drift bound: the drift is measured in the
$\bCbar_t^{-1}$ metric and accumulated in $\ell_2$, and the resulting
one-sided bound needs \emph{no} smallness condition.

\begin{lemma}[Whitened relative feature-drift bound]\label{lem:whitened}
Let $\bg_s := \nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx_s,a_s)$,
$\bg_{s,t}:=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)$,
$\delta_{s,t}:=\bg_{s,t}-\bg_s$, and
$\Delta_{s,t}:=\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|$.
Using the symmetric (principal) square root $\bCbar_t^{1/2}$, define the
$d\times(t{-}1)$ whitened column matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whitened-cols}
  \Gmat_t := \Bigl[\,\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bg_s/\sigma\,\Bigr]_{s<t},
  \qquad
  \Dmat_t := \Bigl[\,\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\delta_{s,t}/\sigma\,\Bigr]_{s<t},
  \qquad
  \chi_t := \|\Dmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}} .
\end{equation}
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.6em]
\item $\Gmat_t\Gmat_t^\top = \bI - \lambda\,\bCbar_t^{-1} \preceq \bI$,
  hence $\|\Gmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le 1$.
\item $\bCbar_t^{-1/2}(\bC_t-\bCbar_t)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}
  = \Gmat_t\Dmat_t^\top + \Dmat_t\Gmat_t^\top + \Dmat_t\Dmat_t^\top$,
  so $\bigl\|\bCbar_t^{-1/2}(\bC_t-\bCbar_t)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  \le 2\chi_t + \chi_t^2$.
\item \textbf{(One-sided, no smallness condition.)} For every $\chi_t\ge0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:onesided-C}
    \bC_t \;\preceq\; (1+\chi_t)^2\,\bCbar_t,
    \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
    \bar s_t^2(a)\;\le\;(1+\chi_t)^2\,s_t^2(a)\ \ \forall a.
  \end{equation}
  A predictable certificate $\bar\chi_t\ge\chi_t$ therefore satisfies
  \Cref{asm:transfer} in the exact case $\Calg_t=\bC_t$ with the uniform
  factor $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$.
\item \textbf{(Whitened $\ell_2$ primitive bound.)}
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:chi-primitive}
    \chi_t \;\le\; \|\Dmat_t\|_F
    = \Biggl[\,\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\|\delta_{s,t}\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}^2\,\Biggr]^{1/2}
    \;\le\;
    \frac{L_g}{\sigma\sqrt{\lambda}}
    \Biggl[\,\sum_{s<t}\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|^2\,\Biggr]^{1/2},
  \end{equation}
  the last step under the $L_g$-Lipschitz-Jacobian assumption
  $\|\delta_{s,t}\|\le L_g\Delta_{s,t}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(a)}
$\Gmat_t\Gmat_t^\top
=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bg_s\bg_s^\top\bCbar_t^{-1/2}
=\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bigl(\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bg_s\bg_s^\top\bigr)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}
=\bCbar_t^{-1/2}(\bCbar_t-\lambda\bI)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}
=\bI-\lambda\bCbar_t^{-1}$.
Since $\lambda\bCbar_t^{-1}\succeq0$, $\Gmat_t\Gmat_t^\top\preceq\bI$, and
$\|\Gmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2=\|\Gmat_t\Gmat_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1$.

\emph{(b)}
From $\bg_{s,t}=\bg_s+\delta_{s,t}$,
$\bg_{s,t}\bg_{s,t}^\top-\bg_s\bg_s^\top
=\bg_s\delta_{s,t}^\top+\delta_{s,t}\bg_s^\top+\delta_{s,t}\delta_{s,t}^\top$.
Conjugating each summand by $\bCbar_t^{-1/2}$ and weighting by
$\sigma^{-2}$, the three groups sum exactly to
$\Gmat_t\Dmat_t^\top$, $\Dmat_t\Gmat_t^\top$, and $\Dmat_t\Dmat_t^\top$.
By the triangle inequality and $\|\Gmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1$,
$\|\Gmat_t\Dmat_t^\top+\Dmat_t\Gmat_t^\top+\Dmat_t\Dmat_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le 2\|\Gmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\Dmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|\Dmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
\le 2\chi_t+\chi_t^2$.

\emph{(c)}
The matrix $W_t:=\bCbar_t^{-1/2}(\bC_t-\bCbar_t)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}$ is
symmetric with $\|W_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\chi_t+\chi_t^2$, hence
$W_t\preceq(2\chi_t+\chi_t^2)\bI$.  Congruence by $\bCbar_t^{1/2}$ gives
$\bC_t-\bCbar_t\preceq(2\chi_t+\chi_t^2)\bCbar_t$, i.e.
$\bC_t\preceq(1+\chi_t)^2\bCbar_t$; no bound on $\chi_t$ is used.
Both $\bC_t,\bCbar_t\succ0$, so inversion reverses the order:
$\bC_t^{-1}\succeq(1+\chi_t)^{-2}\bCbar_t^{-1}$.  The quadratic form at
$\bg_t(a)$ gives $s_t^2(a)\ge(1+\chi_t)^{-2}\bar s_t^2(a)$, i.e.
$\bar s_t^2(a)\le(1+\chi_t)^2 s_t^2(a)$.  Since $x\mapsto(1+x)^2$ is
increasing on $[0,\infty)$, replacing $\chi_t$ by any $\bar\chi_t\ge\chi_t$
preserves the inequality, giving $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$.

\emph{(d)}
$\chi_t=\|\Dmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|\Dmat_t\|_F$, and
$\|\Dmat_t\|_F^2=\sum_{s<t}\|\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\delta_{s,t}/\sigma\|^2
=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\delta_{s,t}^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\delta_{s,t}
=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\|\delta_{s,t}\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}^2$.
Since $\bCbar_t^{-1}\preceq\lambda^{-1}\bI$,
$\|\delta_{s,t}\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}^2\le\|\delta_{s,t}\|^2/\lambda
\le L_g^2\Delta_{s,t}^2/\lambda$; summing and taking square roots
gives~\eqref{eq:chi-primitive}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Two-sided sandwich (legacy)]\label{cor:drift-sandwich}
If additionally $\varepsilon_t:=2\chi_t+\chi_t^2<1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift-sandwich}
  (1-\varepsilon_t)\,\bCbar_t
  \;\preceq\;
  \bC_t
  \;\preceq\;
  (1+\varepsilon_t)\,\bCbar_t .
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
From part~(b), $W_t\succeq-(2\chi_t+\chi_t^2)\bI=-\varepsilon_t\bI$;
congruence by $\bCbar_t^{1/2}$ gives $\bC_t\succeq(1-\varepsilon_t)\bCbar_t$,
and the upper bound is~\eqref{eq:onesided-C} with
$(1+\chi_t)^2\le1+\varepsilon_t$ when $\varepsilon_t<1$
(indeed $(1+\chi_t)^2=1+\varepsilon_t$ exactly).
\end{proof}

The main theorem uses only the one-sided bound~\eqref{eq:onesided-C}, which
holds for \emph{any} $\chi_t\ge0$; the smallness condition
$\varepsilon_t<1$ is needed only for the legacy two-sided
sandwich~\eqref{eq:drift-sandwich}, which in turn is used only by the legacy
two-sided spectral corollary (\Cref{app:cor}) and by any optional analysis that
explicitly assumes a two-sided sandwich.  An unrescaled current-parameter window is
handled by \Cref{prop:window} through a one-sided relation and does not require this
condition.
The binding quantity is the \emph{whitened $\ell_2$ accumulation}
$\chi_t\le\|\Dmat_t\|_F$ of replayed-Jacobian drift, measured in the
$\bCbar_t^{-1}$ metric---a relative, whitened $\ell_2$ bound that can be sharper
than an $\ell_1$ sum normalized only by $\lambda$ when the drift occurs in
well-observed directions (it credits directions the design has already
explored), though it does not dominate uniformly.  Together,
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred,lem:whitened} reduce the two nonlinear inputs
(centering certificate $\bar\psi_t$, transfer factor $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$)
to optimizer residuals and whitened replayed-Jacobian drift.  In the stated
implementation with only $O(d)$ additional curvature-state memory these are
\emph{not} directly observable: they
are primitive diagnostic targets, computable only with additional frozen-feature
replay, checkpoints, applications of $\bCbar_t^{-1}$ or $\bCbar_t^{-1/2}$, or a
separately validated estimator, and remain strong requirements; controlling
$\bar\chi_t$ (equivalently $u_t$)
for a genuinely adapting backbone is one of the open certificate problems
listed in the Limitations.

Positive definiteness of both~$\bC_t$ and~$\bCbar_t$ follows from
$\lambda>0$ and the outer-product form; it is not an independent assumption.
All theoretical results hold under these explicit assumptions; we do
\emph{not} claim a regret guarantee for the full nonlinear model outside
the local-linearization regime.



\subsection{Novelty Relative to Prior Neural Bandits}\label{sec:novelty}

Prior neural bandit algorithms linearize the network around a reference
parameter---the NTK or last-layer regime.  Their uncertainty metric varies:
NeuralUCB and NeuralTS use \emph{full} neural-gradient Gram matrices in their
stated theoretical algorithms \citep{zhou2020neural,zhang2021neural}; practical
implementations may diagonalize these matrices.  NeuralLinear and last-layer
Laplace instead restrict uncertainty to a learned representation or final layer
\citep{riquelme2018deep,ritter2018scalable}.  We do \emph{not} claim prior work
uniformly replaces the full metric.
Against this backdrop CC-UCB's contribution is narrower and specific:
(i)~it uses the \emph{current-parameter relinearized} GGN/Fisher as the
algorithmic reference operator (rather than an initialization-anchored NTK Gram
or a last-layer restriction); (ii)~it accesses inverse-curvature--vector products
matrix-free via CG, forming and storing no $d\times d$ matrix and no per-sample
gradient features; (iii)~it separates the predictable frozen-feature confidence
metric $\bCbar_t$ from the algorithmic curvature $\Calg_t$; and (iv)~it prices,
through a conditional transfer analysis, the costs of CG truncation, centering,
feature drift, and a changing (nonmonotone) operator.

The main algorithmic contribution is a matrix-free implementation recipe
for full-GGN/Fisher exploration via curvature--vector products and
conjugate gradients.
The main theoretical contribution is twofold.  First, the regret bound is a
\emph{realized-width-complexity reduction}: it controls regret by the realized
complexity $\Lamalg_T=\sum_t\log(1+\sigma^{-2}s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t))$ of the widths
actually played, whose width-sum inequality follows directly from the definition
and the bounded-width cap.  Second, a \emph{pathwise decomposition for that
complexity} (\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential}) writes $\Lamalg_T$ as an endpoint
log-determinant minus a determinant-variation sum, generalizing the standard
monotone-Gram/elliptic-potential argument to a nonmonotone predictable SPD
sequence.  It does \emph{not} by itself show $\Lamalg_T$ is small: the resulting
complexity remains an assumed or observed trajectory quantity, and bounding its
endpoint and variation terms under primitive assumptions is left open.  These are
paired with a one-sided optimism-transfer reduction: rather than a two-sided
spectral sandwich, a single whitened, relative feature-drift factor
(\Cref{lem:whitened}) transfers optimism, and the CG-accuracy and transfer costs
enter through a sum of squares.
Efficient estimators for nonlinear contextual bandits were proposed
by~\citet{kassraie2022neural} using an NTK-based uncertainty route;
we compare approaches in \Cref{sec:related}.



\subsection{Computable Curvature Operator}\label{sec:curvop}

Applying $\bC_t\bv = \lambda\bv + \sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\bv\bigr]$
\emph{naively} requires one curvature--vector product (CVP) per historical
sample per CG step---cost $O(It)$ CVPs per round.
Each CVP evaluates one Jacobian--vector product (forward mode) followed by
one vector--Jacobian product (reverse mode) through the network, at roughly
the cost of one gradient evaluation (one JVP plus one VJP)\footnote{Implementations based on double-backward Hessian--vector
products cost closer to two gradient-equivalents per CVP; the constant
does not affect the $O(\cdot)$ accounting.}; we do not store
per-sample gradient features~$\bg_s$, only a constant number of $O(d)$ CG vectors
for CG, with CVPs computed on the fly.
We describe three strategies that limit the per-step cost to $|\mathcal{S}_t|$
CVPs:

\paragraph{(i) Subsampled GGN (worst-case spectral baseline).}
At each round draw a uniform subsample $\mathcal{S}_t\subset\{1,\dots,t{-}1\}$
of size~$n$ (without replacement) and set $w_s=(t{-}1)/n$ to obtain the
rescaled operator.
The following lemma is a worst-case uniform spectral guarantee.

\begin{lemma}[Worst-case uniform spectral guarantee for subsampled GGN]\label{lem:subsample}
Fix round~$t$ and set $m=t{-}1$.
Let $Y_s = \sigma^{-2}\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$ for
$s=1,\dots,m$, so that $\bC_t = \lambda\bI + V$ with $V=\sum_{s=1}^m Y_s$.
Each $Y_s$ is PSD with $\|Y_s\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le G^2/\sigma^2 =: B$.
Draw $\mathcal{S}_t$ of size~$n$ uniformly without replacement from
$\{1,\dots,m\}$ and define $\hat V = \tfrac{m}{n}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}Y_s$
and $\hat\bC_t = \lambda\bI + \hat V$.
Write $L:=\log(2d/\delta)$.

\emph{(i) Deviation bound.}
With probability $\ge 1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subsample-dev}
  \|\hat V - V\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  \;\le\;
  mB\sqrt{\frac{2L}{n}}
  \;+\;
  \frac{4\,mB\,L}{3\,n}.
\end{equation}

\emph{(ii) Sufficient sample size.}
For any $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subsample-n}
  n \;\ge\; 8L\,\biggl[\,
    \frac{m^2\,B^2}{\varepsilon^2\,\lambda^2}
    \;+\;
    \frac{m\,B}{3\,\varepsilon\,\lambda}
  \biggr],
\end{equation}
then $\Pr[\|\hat V - V\|_{\mathrm{op}} > \varepsilon\lambda] \le \delta$
and consequently
$(1{-}\varepsilon)\bC_t\preceq\hat\bC_t\preceq(1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The $\{Y_s\}_{s=1}^m$ are fixed PSD self-adjoint matrices (deterministic
conditional on the history $\cF_{t-1}$ and the current
parameter~$\btheta_t$).
The displayed proof is for \emph{with-replacement} (i.i.d.) sampling.
The without-replacement version follows from the domination argument of
\citet{gross2010note}, building on Hoeffding's reduction
\citep{hoeffding1963probability}: the matrix moment-generating function
of a sum sampled without replacement from a fixed finite collection of
self-adjoint matrices is dominated by that of the corresponding
i.i.d.\ sum, so every tail bound derived from the matrix
Laplace-transform method, including the matrix Bernstein inequality of
\citet[Thm.~6.1.1]{tropp2015introduction}, carries over with the same
constants.
In our setting the $\{Y_s\}$ are \emph{fixed} PSD matrices
(deterministic conditional on $\cF_{t-1}$ and~$\btheta_t$) and only
the index set $\mathcal{S}_t$ is random, so the domination argument
applies directly.
We therefore state and prove the bound for i.i.d.\ draws
$s_1,\dots,s_n$ uniform on~$\{1,\dots,m\}$; the
without-replacement guarantee is at least as strong.
Define the centered random matrices
$Z_k = (m/n)Y_{s_k} - V/n$ (mean zero, independent).
The range satisfies
$\|Z_k\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le (m/n)B + (1/n)\|V\| \le 2mB/n =: R$
(using $\|V\|\le mB$).
For the variance parameter, since $Y_s\preceq B\bI$,
\[
  \mathbb{E}[Z_k Z_k^\top]
  \;\preceq\; \tfrac{m^2}{n^2}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s_1}^2]
  \;=\; \tfrac{m}{n^2}\sum_{s=1}^m Y_s^2
  \;\preceq\; \tfrac{m}{n^2}B\,V
  \;\preceq\; \tfrac{m^2B^2}{n^2}\bI,
\]
so $v :=
\bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbb{E}[Z_kZ_k^\top]\bigr\|\le m^2B^2/n$.
By the matrix Bernstein inequality
\citep[Thm.~6.1.1]{tropp2015introduction},
$\Pr[\|\hat V{-}V\| \ge t]
\le 2d\exp\!\bigl(-t^2/(2v + 2Rt/3)\bigr)$.
Choosing
$t_0=\sqrt{2vL}+\tfrac{2}{3}RL$
makes the matrix-Bernstein tail probability at most~$\delta$.  Substituting
$v\le m^2B^2/n$ and $R=2mB/n$ gives
$t_0\le mB\sqrt{2L/n}+\tfrac{4mBL}{3n}$,
yielding~\eqref{eq:subsample-dev}.
For~\eqref{eq:subsample-n}, require each summand
in~\eqref{eq:subsample-dev} to be $\le\varepsilon\lambda/2$:
$mB\sqrt{2L/n}\le\varepsilon\lambda/2$ gives
$n\ge 8m^2B^2L/(\varepsilon^2\lambda^2)$, and
$4mBL/(3n)\le\varepsilon\lambda/2$ gives
$n\ge 8mBL/(3\varepsilon\lambda)$.
Summing the two conditions
yields~\eqref{eq:subsample-n}; this is a conservative sufficient
condition (using $a+b$ rather than $\max\{a,b\}$), not a sharp threshold.
The multiplicative sandwich follows:
$\|\hat V{-}V\|\le\varepsilon\lambda$ gives
$\bC_t - \varepsilon\lambda\bI \preceq \hat\bC_t \preceq
\bC_t + \varepsilon\lambda\bI$.
Since $\bC_t\succeq\lambda\bI$, we have
$\varepsilon\lambda\bI \preceq \varepsilon\bC_t$, so
$\bC_t - \varepsilon\lambda\bI \succeq (1{-}\varepsilon)\bC_t$ and
$\bC_t + \varepsilon\lambda\bI \preceq (1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$.
\end{proof}

With $B=G^2/\sigma^2$, the right-hand side of the sufficient
condition~\eqref{eq:subsample-n} is
$O\!\bigl(L\cdot[m^2G^4/(\varepsilon^2\lambda^2\sigma^4)
  + mG^2/(\varepsilon\lambda\sigma^2)]\bigr)$.
The first (variance) term dominates unless
$\varepsilon\lambda\sigma^2\gtrsim mG^2$, so this worst-case sufficient threshold has
a \emph{quadratic leading dependence} on~$m=t{-}1$ (it is a sufficient upper bound on
the required sample size, not an exact or necessary sample complexity).
The condition~\eqref{eq:subsample-n} is conservative and sufficient, not
necessary, with worst-case leading dependence quadratic in $m$.  If its
threshold exceeds the population size $m=t{-}1$, it provides no nontrivial
subsampling guarantee for $n<m$.  Taking $n=m$ recovers the full population
exactly for the separate reason that every summand is included.

\emph{This is a worst-case baseline guarantee, too conservative for
practical use at large~$t$.}
In particular, the bound above does not by itself justify computational
savings at large $t$; it should be read as a worst-case spectral
guarantee rather than as a practical scaling result.
Subsampling reduces computation ($n\ll m$) only when $m$ is moderate or
$\varepsilon\lambda$ is large relative to~$mB$; for large~$t$ the full
history may be needed under the uniform Bernstein bound.
Under additional structure---bounded effective rank
$d_{\mathrm{eff}}=\tr(\bC_t{-}\lambda\bI)/\|\bC_t{-}\lambda\bI\|$
or fast spectral decay---leverage-score sampling or Frequent Directions
sketching~\citep{kuzborskij2019efficient} can achieve
$n=\widetilde{O}(d_{\mathrm{eff}}/\varepsilon^2)$ independent of~$m$,
at the cost of more complex per-round bookkeeping.
The Bernstein guarantee is not the mechanism by which we expect
practical savings at scale.  Practical savings require small buffers
treated heuristically, spectral decay/effective dimension,
ridge-leverage sampling, Frequent Directions, or empirical verification
of the spectral sandwich.  We leave structure-aware sampling and empirical
spectral certification as future work.
To obtain a spectral sandwich that holds simultaneously for all
$t\le T$, union-bound over $t$ (replacing $\delta$ by $\delta/T$).
\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} then translates
the spectral guarantee into a regret inflation factor.

\paragraph{(ii) Windowed curvature (unrescaled, current parameter).}
Maintain a sliding window $\mathcal{S}_t\subseteq\{1,\dots,t{-}1\}$ of the most
recent~$n$ interactions with unit weights ($w_s{=}1$), \emph{re-evaluating the
Jacobians at the current parameter}~$\btheta_t$.  This is a nonnegative-weighted
\emph{subset} of the same current-parameter outer products, so
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\bC_t$ (\Cref{prop:window}); \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} then
applies with the \emph{deterministic} approximate-to-full factor $\kappa_{+,t}{=}1$
and the full-to-frozen factor $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$---no two-sided sandwich is
required, though the overall guarantee remains conditional on that transfer
certificate.  The cost of the
smaller operator is not hidden: it surfaces as larger realized widths
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)$ and hence a larger dynamic width complexity~$\Lamalg_T$.  The
same holds for any current-parameter downweighted subset with $w_s\in[0,1]$
(including an unrescaled subsample).  A \emph{stale} window that reuses Jacobians
from an older parameter is \emph{not} covered; see~(iii).

\paragraph{(iii) Periodic refresh (heuristic; stale operator).}
Recompute the curvature operator every~$M$ rounds on a cached buffer and reuse it
for~$M$ consecutive actions.  Because the reused operator evaluates Jacobians at
an \emph{older} parameter, it is no longer a subset of the current-parameter
outer products, so the $\hat\bC_t\preceq\bC_t$ argument of \Cref{prop:window} does
not apply and the stale operator can both over- and under-estimate $\bC_t$.  This
remains a heuristic: \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} covers it only when a
one-sided factor $\kappa_{+,t}$ (an explicit bound
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\kappa_{+,t}\bC_t$) is separately verified, which we evaluate
empirically.  The rescaled subsample of~(i), with weights $w_s=(t{-}1)/n>1$, is
likewise not covered by \Cref{prop:window}; it instead uses the two-sided matrix
Bernstein bound of \Cref{lem:subsample}.

\subsection{Computational Cost Model}\label{sec:systems}

\paragraph{Primitive assumptions.}
CC-UCB requires a single computational primitive: the
\emph{curvature--vector product} (CVP) $\bv\mapsto\bC_t\bv$, which
decomposes into per-sample operations
$\bv\mapsto\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\bv\bigr]$.
Each such operation consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a Jacobian--vector product (JVP) $\bv\mapsto\bg_t^\top\bv$
  (forward-mode AD, cost $\approx 1\times$ forward pass);
\item a vector--Jacobian product (VJP) $\alpha\mapsto\alpha\,\bg_t$
  (reverse-mode AD, cost $\approx 1\times$ backward pass).
\end{enumerate}
Under FSDP or similar sharded-parameter layouts, each per-sample CVP has
the same communication pattern as a gradient step, so the CG loop
parallelizes over samples in the buffer.
A full round of CC-UCB with action screening costs $K\times I$ CG
iterations, each involving $|\mathcal{S}_t|$ forward--backward passes.
Additional curvature-state memory is $O(d)$ for a constant number of CG vectors
($\tilde\bu$, residual, search direction); no $d\times d$ matrix is formed
or stored.

Krylov methods for Hessian spectral analysis have been run on networks with
$>10^8$ parameters under distributed training \citep{granziol2026hessian}, which
indicates that curvature--vector primitives of this general form are implementable
at scale.  We do \emph{not} claim foundation-model feasibility from this: that
work times Hessian--vector products, not the exact per-example GGN JVP/VJP used
here, and we report no wall-clock benchmark of our primitive.
Warm-starting CG from the previous round's solution can reduce the
iteration count in practice.

\subsection{Dynamic Log-Determinant Potential}\label{sec:dynamic-potential}

The regret bound is driven not by $\gamma_T$ but by the realized
\emph{algorithmic information complexity} of the widths the algorithm
actually uses,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lam-alg}
  \Lamalg_T \;:=\; \sum_{t=1}^T
  \log\!\bigl(1+\sigma^{-2}\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Unlike $\bCbar_t$, the algorithmic curvature $\Calg_t$ need \emph{not} be
monotone in~$t$: relinearization, subsampling, windowing, and periodic
refresh can all \emph{shrink} it, so the elementary determinant
telescoping of~\eqref{eq:infogain} no longer applies.  The following lemma
prices this nonmonotonicity exactly, through a pathwise log-determinant
identity.  This \emph{pathwise determinant identity} (a dynamic potential
decomposition) is what lets the analysis handle a general predictable SPD
curvature sequence rather than a monotone Gram matrix.

\begin{lemma}[Dynamic log-determinant potential]\label{lem:dynamic-potential}
Let $\{\Calg_t\}_{t\ge1}$ satisfy~\eqref{eq:calg-props} with
$\Calg_1=\lambda\bI$.  For the played feature
$\bh_t:=\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)$ define the rank-one update
$\Calg_t^{+}:=\Calg_t+\sigma^{-2}\bh_t\bh_t^\top$ and, using the symmetric
(principal) root $(\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}$, the normalized perturbation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-def}
  \Xi_t \;:=\; (\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}\,(\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^{+})\,
  (\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2},
\end{equation}
so that $\bI+\Xi_t=(\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}\Calg_{t+1}(\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}$ is SPD;
equivalently, every eigenvalue of $\Xi_t$ exceeds $-1$ and every eigenvalue of
$\bI+\Xi_t$ is positive (with no smallness condition on the jump
$\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^{+}$).  Fix any \emph{end-of-round-$T$} terminal operator
$\Calg_{T+1}$ constructed only from data and randomness available through
round~$T$ ($\cF_T$-measurable, hence not a function of $\bx_{T+1}$, $\cA_{T+1}$,
or future rewards; e.g.\ the canonical rank-one update $\Calg_{T+1}=\Calg_T^{+}$,
or the operator the algorithm commits for round $T{+}1$).  The identity below is
then an a~posteriori statement about the completed sequence
$\Calg_1,\dots,\Calg_{T+1}$; the quantities $\Xi_T$, $\Valg_T$, and $\Gamdyn_T$
are defined relative to this choice, and the bound $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$ holds
for \emph{every} such valid choice.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lam-identity}
  \Lamalg_T
  \;=\;
  \log\frac{\det(\Calg_{T+1})}{\det(\lambda\bI)}
  \;-\;
  \sum_{t=1}^T \log\det(\bI+\Xi_t).
\end{equation}
With the determinant-destroying variation charge and the dynamic potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vdyn-def}
  \Valg_T := \sum_{t=1}^T \bigl[-\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)\bigr]_+,
  \qquad
  \Gamdyn_T := \log\frac{\det(\Calg_{T+1})}{\det(\lambda\bI)} + \Valg_T,
\end{equation}
we have $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$, and, using
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\le G^2/\lambda$ and $\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:width-sum-dyn}
  \sum_{t=1}^T s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)
  \;\le\;
  (\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T
  \;\le\;
  (\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Gamdyn_T .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the matrix determinant lemma applied to
$\Calg_t^{+}=\Calg_t+\sigma^{-2}\bh_t\bh_t^\top$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:detratio-plus}
  \frac{\det(\Calg_t^{+})}{\det(\Calg_t)}
  = 1+\sigma^{-2}\bh_t^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bh_t
  = 1+\sigma^{-2}s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t).
\end{equation}
Writing $B_t:=(\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}$, we have
$\bI+\Xi_t=B_t\Calg_t^{+}B_t+B_t(\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^{+})B_t
=B_t\Calg_{t+1}B_t$, a congruence of the SPD matrix $\Calg_{t+1}$ by the
invertible symmetric $B_t$, hence SPD (in particular its eigenvalues exceed
$-1$ with no smallness condition on the jump $\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^{+}$), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:detratio-xi}
  \frac{\det(\Calg_{t+1})}{\det(\Calg_t^{+})}
  = \det\!\bigl(B_t^{-1}(\bI+\Xi_t)B_t^{-1}\bigr)\big/\det(\Calg_t^{+})
  = \det(\bI+\Xi_t),
\end{equation}
using $\det(\Calg_{t+1})=\det(\Calg_t^{+})\det(\bI+\Xi_t)$.
Taking logs of~\eqref{eq:detratio-plus}--\eqref{eq:detratio-xi} and adding,
\[
  \log\!\bigl(1+\sigma^{-2}s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\bigr)
  = \log\det(\Calg_{t+1}) - \log\det(\Calg_t) - \log\det(\bI+\Xi_t).
\]
Summing over $t=1,\dots,T$ telescopes the first difference to
$\log\det(\Calg_{T+1})-\log\det(\Calg_1)$; with $\Calg_1=\lambda\bI$ this is
$\log[\det(\Calg_{T+1})/\det(\lambda\bI)]$, proving~\eqref{eq:lam-identity}.
Since $-\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)\le[-\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)]_+$ term by term,
$-\sum_t\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)\le\Valg_T$, so $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$.
For~\eqref{eq:width-sum-dyn}: $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ gives
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)=\bh_t^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bh_t\le\|\bh_t\|^2/\lambda\le
G^2/\lambda=:c_{\max}\sigma^2$.  With $x_t:=\sigma^{-2}s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\in
[0,c_{\max}]$ and $\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)\ge x/(1+c_{\max})$,
$\Lamalg_T\ge(1+c_{\max})^{-1}\sum_t x_t$, i.e.
$\sum_t s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\le(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\Lamalg_T$; the second
inequality is $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation and scope of the dynamic potential]
Identity~\eqref{eq:lam-identity} is \emph{pathwise}: it uses no martingale
or probabilistic argument.  $\Lamalg_T$ is the exact accumulated
information complexity of the widths actually played.  $\Valg_T$ charges
only \emph{determinant-destroying} changes in the nonmonotone curvature
sequence---rounds where $\det(\bI+\Xi_t)<1$, i.e.\ where $\Calg_{t+1}$ loses
volume relative to the rank-one-updated $\Calg_t^{+}$; changes that
\emph{increase} determinant volume contribute zero.  If $\Calg_1\ne\lambda\bI$
but still $\Calg_1\succeq\lambda\bI$, the denominator in
\eqref{eq:lam-identity}--\eqref{eq:vdyn-def} is $\det(\Calg_1)$ rather than
$\det(\lambda\bI)$.
The charge $\Valg_T$ can be large for unstable curvature sequences: we do
\emph{not} claim the identity itself shows the dynamic complexity is small, nor
that the dynamic theorem automatically yields sublinear regret.  Note that
$\Valg_T$ alone does not control $\Lamalg_T$ or $\Gamdyn_T$: by
\eqref{eq:vdyn-def} the potential $\Gamdyn_T=\log\det(\Calg_{T+1}/\lambda\bI)
+\Valg_T$ also carries the endpoint log-determinant term.  Bounding \emph{both}
the variation charge $\Valg_T$ and the endpoint $\log\det(\Calg_{T+1}/\lambda\bI)$
is \emph{one sufficient route} to controlling $\Lamalg_T$, through
$\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$; it is not equivalent to it, since the exact identity
\eqref{eq:lam-identity} can contain cancellation that makes $\Lamalg_T$ small even
when $\Gamdyn_T$ is not.  Controlling $\Lamalg_T$ for a genuinely adapting
backbone is one of the open certificate problems.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Observable CG upper bound on the width complexity]\label{cor:lam-observable}
If the played-action CG solve satisfies $\varepsilon_{t,a_t}\le\bar\varepsilon_t<1$
so that $\tilde s_t^2(a_t)\ge(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)$
(\Cref{lem:cg}), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lam-observable}
  \Lamalg_T
  \;\le\;
  \widehat{\Lamalg}_T
  \;:=\;
  \sum_{t=1}^T
  \log\!\Bigl(1+\frac{\tilde s_t^2(a_t)}{\sigma^2\,(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\le\tilde s_t^2(a_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)$ and
$x\mapsto\log(1+\sigma^{-2}x)$ is increasing, so each summand
of~$\Lamalg_T$ is dominated term by term.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This gives an observable upper bound on the dynamic width-complexity term,
conditional on the certified CG tolerances; $\Valg_T$ itself is \emph{not}
observable in general (it requires determinant estimation of the curvature
sequence).



\subsection{CG Approximation Lemma}

\begin{lemma}[CG multiplicative accuracy]\label{lem:cg}
Fix a round~$t$ and an action~$a$ with right-hand side
$\bg:=\bg_t(a)\ne\mathbf0$.  Let $\bu = \Calg_t^{-1}\bg$ and let $\tilde\bu$ be
the iterate after $I_{t,a}$ steps of CG on the SPD system $\Calg_t\bu=\bg$
starting from $\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf{0}$, with $\Calg_t$ held \emph{fixed} across
all iterations.  The relative energy-norm error is the per-action quantity
$\varepsilon_{t,a}$ of~\eqref{eq:cg-err-eta}, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-rate}
  \varepsilon_{t,a}
  \;=\;
  \frac{\|\bu-\tilde\bu\|_{\Calg_t}}{\|\bu\|_{\Calg_t}}
  \;\le\;
  2\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}-1}{\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}+1}\right)^{\!I_{t,a}},
\end{equation}
where
$\kappa_t:=\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)/\lambda_{\min}(\Calg_t)$ is the true
condition number and $\bar\kappa_t\ge\kappa_t$ is a certified
$\cHist_t$-measurable upper bound.  The convergence rate in~\eqref{eq:cg-rate} is right-hand-side
independent, so it holds for every action with the same $\bar\kappa_t$.
For a \emph{nonnegative weighted outer-product} operator
$\Calg_t=\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s\,
\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$ ($w_s\ge0$, so
$\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI\succ0$ and CG is well-posed),
\[
\kappa_t\le\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)}{\lambda}
\le1+\frac{W_tG^2}{\lambda\sigma^2}=: \bar\kappa_t,
\]
with $W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s$ and
$\|\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\|\le G$ from \Cref{asm:bounded}; here $W_t=n$ for unrescaled
windowed/refresh buffers, giving $\bar\kappa_t$ and $\alpha_t$ independent
of~$T$, and $W_t=t{-}1$ for full history and for rescaled uniform subsampling
with $w_s=(t{-}1)/n$.  For a general SPD operator that is not such an outer
product, $\bar\kappa_t$ must be certified separately.
Write $\tilde{s}_t^2(a) = \bg^\top\tilde\bu$ and
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)=\bg^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bg$.

\textbf{Assume $\varepsilon_{t,a} < 1$}
(see \Cref{app:cg} for the minimum~$I_{t,a}$ ensuring this as a function
of~$\bar\kappa_t$;
\Cref{alg:ccucb} enforces a known \emph{per-round} tolerance~$\bar\varepsilon_t$
uniformly over the scored actions, $\max_a\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t<1$,
via adaptive residual stopping or a fixed budget under a certified
condition-number bound; see \S\ref{sec:method}).
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-quad}
  (1-\varepsilon_{t,a})\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)
  \;\le\;
  \tilde{s}_t^2(a)
  \;\le\;
  (1+\varepsilon_{t,a})\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a).
\end{equation}
For $\bg_t(a)=\mathbf0$ the algorithm sets
$\tilde{s}_t^2(a)=0=s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$ and~\eqref{eq:cg-quad} holds trivially.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
$\tilde{s}_t^2(a) - s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)
= \bg^\top(\tilde\bu-\bu)
= (\Calg_t\bu)^\top(\tilde\bu-\bu)
= \bu^\top\Calg_t(\tilde\bu-\bu)$.
By Cauchy--Schwarz in the $\Calg_t$-norm,
$|\bu^\top\Calg_t(\tilde\bu-\bu)|
\le\|\bu\|_{\Calg_t}\|\tilde\bu-\bu\|_{\Calg_t}
\le\varepsilon_{t,a}\|\bu\|_{\Calg_t}^2 = \varepsilon_{t,a}\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$.
For the outer-product special case the condition-number bound follows from
$\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)\le\lambda+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t} w_s\|\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\|^2
\le\lambda+W_tG^2/\sigma^2$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The algorithmic bonus.}
With the \emph{per-round} certified tolerance
$\bar\varepsilon_t\ge\max_a\varepsilon_{t,a}$ and the one-sided transfer factor
$u_t(a)\ge1$ of \Cref{asm:transfer}, \Cref{alg:ccucb} plays the bonus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bonus}
  w_t(a)
  \;:=\;
  \omega_t\,
  \sqrt{\frac{u_t(a)}{1-\bar\varepsilon_t}}\,
  \sqrt{\tilde{s}_t^2(a)},
\end{equation}
where $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$ is the predictable confidence upper bound,
$\bar\psi_t$ the centering certificate (\Cref{asm:optim}), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega-def}
  \omega_t := \bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t
\end{equation}
is the combined confidence-plus-centering width factor.  Define the
\emph{per-round} CG inflation factor
$\alpha_t:=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$.

\begin{lemma}[Bonus sandwiching]\label{lem:bonus}
Under \Cref{asm:transfer} and $\max_{a\in\cA_t}\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t<1$,
the bonus~\eqref{eq:bonus} satisfies, for every $a\in\cA_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bonus-sandwich}
  \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a)
  \;\le\;
  w_t(a)
  \;\le\;
  \alpha_t\,\omega_t\,\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\bg_t(a)=\mathbf0$ all three terms vanish and the bound is trivial.
Otherwise \Cref{lem:cg} with $\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t$ gives
$(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)\le\tilde s_t^2(a)\le
(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$.  The lower bound and~$\omega_t\ge0$
give $\sqrt{\tilde s_t^2(a)}\ge\sqrt{1-\bar\varepsilon_t}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)$,
so $w_t(a)\ge\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)$; then~\eqref{eq:transfer}
gives $\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)\ge\bar s_t(a)$, i.e.\
$w_t(a)\ge\omega_t\bar s_t(a)$.  The upper bound gives
$\sqrt{\tilde s_t^2(a)}\le\sqrt{1+\bar\varepsilon_t}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)$, whence
$w_t(a)\le\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}\sqrt{1+\bar\varepsilon_t}\,
s_{\Calg,t}(a)=\alpha_t\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)$.
Both bounds are per-round; no horizon maximum is taken before summing.
\end{proof}



\subsection{Confidence and Discrepancy Lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Confidence for the linearized ridge estimator]%
\label{lem:confidence}
Suppose \Cref{asm:noise,asm:bounded,asm:linear} hold and
$\mu^*=\mu_{\btheta^*}$ with $\|\btheta^*\|\le S$.
Define the linearized ridge estimator~$\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$
by~\eqref{eq:thetahat-lin}, the frozen-feature variance
$\bar s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)}$, and
the cumulative squared linearization error
$F_t := \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\epslin(s)^2$.
Then with probability at least $1-\delta$, for all $t\ge 1$ and all
$a\in\cA_t$ simultaneously,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf-lin}
  \bigl|\bg_t(a)^\top
    \bigl(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}-\btheta^*\bigr)\bigr|
  \;\le\;
  \bar s_t(a)\,\beta_t,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
  \beta_t
  \;:=\;
  \sqrt{\gamma_{t-1} + 2\log(1/\delta)}
  \;+\; \sqrt{\lambda}\,S
  \;+\; \frac{1}{\sigma}\sqrt{F_t}.
\end{equation}
(Here $\gamma_{t-1}=\log\det(\bCbar_t)/\det(\lambda\bI)$ is the
information gain through round $t{-}1$.)

The quantity $\beta_t$ in~\eqref{eq:beta} is a theoretical radius.
The algorithm may use any predictable upper bound
$\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$; all results below remain valid with
$\bar\beta_t$ in place of~$\beta_t$.
This is necessary because $\beta_t$ depends on the unknown cumulative
linearization term~$F_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
From~\eqref{eq:pseudo-response},
$y_s = \bg_s^\top\btheta^* + \rho_s + \eta_s$ with
$|\rho_s|\le\epslin(s)$.
The closed form of $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$ gives
\[
  \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}} - \btheta^*
  = -\lambda\bCbar_t^{-1}\btheta^*
    + \bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s<t}\bg_s\rho_s
    + \bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s<t}\bg_s\eta_s.
\]
Taking the inner product with~$\bg_t(a)$ and bounding in absolute value:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item\emph{Prior bias.}\;
$|\lambda\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\btheta^*|
\le \lambda\,\bar s_t(a)\,\|\btheta^*\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
\le \bar s_t(a)\sqrt{\lambda}\,S$.
(Cauchy--Schwarz in the $\bCbar_t$-norm;
$\lambda\|\btheta^*\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
\le \lambda\|\btheta^*\|/\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\bCbar_t)}
\le \sqrt{\lambda}\,S$
since $\bCbar_t\succeq\lambda\bI$.)
\item\emph{Linearization bias.}\;
Define $\Phi=[\bg_1/\sigma,\dots,\bg_{t-1}/\sigma]$ (the $d\times(t{-}1)$
design matrix) and $\hat\rho=(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_{t-1})^\top$.
Then $\sigma^{-2}\sum\bg_s\rho_s = \sigma^{-1}\Phi\hat\rho$.
Since $\bCbar_t \succeq \Phi\Phi^\top$, we have
$\Phi^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\Phi \preceq \bI$, so
$\|\Phi\hat\rho\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
= \|\hat\rho\|_{\Phi^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\Phi}
\le \|\hat\rho\| = \sqrt{F_t}$.
Therefore
$|\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-1}\Phi\hat\rho|
\le \bar s_t(a)\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{F_t}$.
\item\emph{Noise.}\;
\[
  \left\|
  \sum_{s<t}\frac{\bg_s}{\sigma}\frac{\eta_s}{\sigma}
  \right\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
  \le
  \sqrt{\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta)}
\]
holds for all $t\ge 1$ with probability at least $1-\delta$ by the
time-uniform vector self-normalized martingale bound
\citep{abbasi2011improved}.  Here $x_s=\bg_s/\sigma$ is predictable
with respect to the pre-reward sigma-algebra $\cG_s$, and
$\xi_s=\eta_s/\sigma$ is conditionally $1$-sub-Gaussian given
$\cG_s$.  For any candidate action $a$, Cauchy--Schwarz in the
$\bCbar_t$-norm gives
\[
  \left|
  \bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}
  \sum_{s<t}\frac{\bg_s\eta_s}{\sigma^2}
  \right|
  \le
  \bar s_t(a)\sqrt{\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta)} .
\]

\end{enumerate}
Summing gives~\eqref{eq:conf-lin}--\eqref{eq:beta}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Predictable confidence schedule under an externally certified linearization bound]\label{cor:beta-constructive}
Suppose a deterministic predictable sequence $\{\bar F_t\}_{t\ge 1}$
satisfies $F_t \le \bar F_t$ for all~$t$.
Then \Cref{alg:ccucb} may set
\[
  \bar\beta_t \;:=\;
  \sqrt{\gamma_{t-1} + 2\log(1/\delta)}
  \;+\; \sqrt{\lambda}\,S
  \;+\; \sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\bar F_t},
\]
which is predictable and satisfies $\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$\bar F_t \ge F_t$ term-by-term implies each summand in~\eqref{eq:beta}
is dominated, so $\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$.
Predictability holds because $\gamma_{t-1}$, $S$, $\bar F_t$, $\lambda$,
$\sigma$, and $\delta$ are all determined before round~$t$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This schedule is predictable but \emph{not} unconditionally computable:
it presupposes an externally certified bound $\bar F_t\ge F_t$ on the
cumulative squared linearization error, which the algorithm cannot form
from data alone.  It is operational only when such a certificate is
supplied.

\begin{corollary}[Observable information-gain surrogate via the one-sided transfer factor]\label{cor:gammahat}
Run \Cref{alg:ccucb} with a predictable algorithmic curvature
sequence~$\Calg_s$, per-round CG tolerance with
$\varepsilon_{s,a_s}\le\bar\varepsilon_s<1$ for the played action, and an
$\cHist_s$-measurable one-sided optimism-transfer factor $u_s(a)\ge1$ satisfying
$\bar s_s^2(a)\le u_s(a)\,s_{\Calg,s}^2(a)$ (\Cref{asm:transfer}).
Define the observable information-gain surrogate
\[
  \hat\gamma_{t-1} \;:=\;
  \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\log\!\Bigl(1+\sigma^{-2}\,
  \frac{u_s(a_s)}{1-\bar\varepsilon_s}\,
  \tilde s_s^2(a_s)\Bigr),
\]
where $\tilde s_s^2(a_s)=\bg_s(a_s)^\top\tilde\bu_s$ is the CG variance
proxy computed at round~$s$ for the played action.
Then $\hat\gamma_{t-1}$ is $\cHist_t$-measurable and
$\hat\gamma_{t-1}\ge\gamma_{t-1}$, so
\[
  \hat\beta_t \;:=\;
  \sqrt{\hat\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta)}
  \;+\;\sqrt{\lambda}\,S
  \;+\;\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\bar F_t}
\]
is a predictable schedule with $\hat\beta_t\ge\beta_t$, and
\Cref{thm:regret} holds with $\bar\beta_t=\hat\beta_t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The transfer factor gives
$\bar s_s^2(a_s)\le u_s(a_s)\,s_{\Calg,s}^2(a_s)$, and \Cref{lem:cg}
with $\varepsilon_{s,a_s}\le\bar\varepsilon_s$ gives
$s_{\Calg,s}^2(a_s)\le\tilde s_s^2(a_s)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_s)$;
hence
$\bar s_s^2(a_s)\le u_s(a_s)\,\tilde s_s^2(a_s)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_s)$.
By monotonicity of $x\mapsto\log(1+\sigma^{-2}x)$, each summand of
$\gamma_{t-1}$ in~\eqref{eq:infogain} is dominated by the corresponding
summand of $\hat\gamma_{t-1}$.
Measurability holds because $\tilde s_s^2(a_s)$, $u_s(a_s)$, and
$\bar\varepsilon_s$ are all $\cHist_s$-measurable.
The schedule property then follows term by term as in
\Cref{cor:beta-constructive}.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This makes the information-gain component of the confidence radius
observable \emph{only when $u_s(a_s)$ is itself certified}; it does
\emph{not} make the cumulative-linearization certificate $\bar F_t$ or the
centering certificate $\bar\psi_t$ observable.  \Cref{cor:gammahat} alone
therefore does not render the complete confidence radius operational.

\begin{remark}[Computability and dimension dependence of $\beta_t$]
Two caveats about $\beta_t$ deserve emphasis.
First, $\gamma_{t-1}$ is defined through the frozen-feature Gram
matrix $\bCbar_t$, whose per-sample Jacobians the matrix-free
implementation deliberately does not store; $\gamma_{t-1}$ is
therefore not directly computable in the stated memory model.
Second, the worst-case substitute
$\gamma_{t-1}\le d\log\bigl(1+(t-1)G^2/(d\lambda\sigma^2)\bigr)$
scales with the parameter count and renders the resulting bonus, and
the bound of \Cref{thm:regret}, vacuous at foundation-model scale
($d\sim 10^8$).
\Cref{cor:gammahat} addresses both issues with an observable surrogate
built from quantities the algorithm already computes; the resulting
guarantee is data-dependent and is meaningful exactly when the
realized information gain is small.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Action-scaled centering discrepancy]\label{lem:discrepancy}
Under \Cref{asm:optim},
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t - \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|\le
\bar\psi_t\,\bar s_t(a)$
for all $a\in\cA_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from~\eqref{eq:pred-stab}.
\end{proof}

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Remark (status of the centering certificate $\bar\psi_t$).}
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred} bounds $\psi_t$ in terms of the optimizer
residual $\zeta_t$ and the gradient-mismatch vector $M_t$; the regret
theorem uses the certificate $\bar\psi_t\ge\psi_t$ inside
$\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$, so it should be read as a transfer
result: the centering certificate must be verified empirically or bounded
by a separate optimization argument, or removed by the corrected-center
variant (\Cref{cor:corrected-center}).
\end{quote}
In the convex last-layer regime, under exact optimization,
$\btheta_t = \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$ and $\bar\psi_t=0$.
The full prediction error at $(\bx_t,a)$ is bounded by the triangle
inequality, with the centering discrepancy \emph{action-scaled}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pred-error}
  |\mu_{\btheta^*}(\bx_t,a) - \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)|
  \;\le\;
  (\beta_t+\bar\psi_t)\,\bar s_t(a)
  \;+\;
  \epslin(t)
  \;\le\;
  \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a)+\epslin(t),
\end{equation}
using \eqref{eq:conf-lin}, \Cref{lem:discrepancy}, the per-round
linearization remainder $|\rho_t|\le\epslin(t)$, and $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$.



\subsection{Cost of Approximate Curvature}

\begin{proposition}[Unrescaled current-parameter subset]\label{prop:window}
Let $\mathcal{S}_t\subseteq\{1,\dots,t{-}1\}$ and $0\le w_s\le1$, and define the
current-parameter operator
\[
  \hat\bC_t
  \;=\;
  \lambda\bI
  +\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t} w_s\,
  \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top ,
\]
using the same relinearized Jacobians $\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)=\nabla_{\btheta}
\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)$ that define $\bC_t$~\eqref{eq:curvature}.  Then
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\bC_t$, so \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} applies with
$\kappa_{+,t}=1$ and, in the exact-transfer case, $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$
(\Cref{lem:whitened}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\bC_t-\hat\bC_t=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}(1-w_s)\bg_t\bg_t^\top
+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\notin\mathcal{S}_t}\bg_t\bg_t^\top$, a sum of PSD outer
products (each $1-w_s\ge0$), hence $\bC_t-\hat\bC_t\succeq0$.  With
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\bC_t$ (so $\kappa_{+,t}=1$ in
\eqref{eq:approx-onesided}) and $\hat\bC_t\succeq\lambda\bI$, the hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} hold.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This covers an unrescaled current-parameter sliding window, an unrescaled
current-parameter subsample, and any current-parameter downweighted subset with
weights in $[0,1]$.  It does \emph{not} cover rescaled subsampling with weights
$(t{-}1)/n>1$ (\Cref{lem:subsample}), a stale periodic-refresh operator evaluated
at an older parameter, or an arbitrary randomized sketch (e.g.\ the eigenspace
Lanczos surrogate of \Cref{app:legacy-diagnostics}), for which a factor
$\kappa_{+,t}$ must be certified separately.

\begin{theorem}[Approximate curvature via one-sided transfer]\label{thm:spectral-distortion}
Adopt the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:regret} with the algorithmic curvature
$\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$, an approximate operator that is symmetric,
$\cHist_t$-measurable, and satisfies $\hat\bC_t\succeq\lambda\bI$.  Suppose
the two \emph{one-sided} bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-onesided}
  \hat\bC_t \;\preceq\; \kappa_{+,t}\,\bC_t,
  \qquad
  \bC_t \;\preceq\; (1+\bar\chi_t)^2\,\bCbar_t
\end{equation}
hold for every $t\le T$, where $\kappa_{+,t}\ge1$ is $\cHist_t$-measurable
and $\bar\chi_t\ge\chi_t$ is the whitened-drift certificate
of~\Cref{lem:whitened}.  Then, for every action $a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-transfer}
  \bar s_t^2(a)\;\le\;\kappa_{+,t}\,(1+\bar\chi_t)^2\,\hat s_t^2(a),
\end{equation}
so \Cref{asm:transfer} holds with the uniform factor
$u_t=\kappa_{+,t}(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ and \Cref{thm:regret} applies verbatim
with $\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$ and $s_{\Calg,t}=\hat s_t$:
\[
  R_T\;\le\;2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T
    \sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t^2\,u_t(a_t)\,\omega_t^2}\;+\;2E_T .
\]
No \emph{lower} spectral factor on $\hat\bC_t$ is required for optimism; the
cost of an excessively small, stale, or nonmonotone approximate operator is
charged through its realized width complexity $\Lamalg_T$ (and its variation
$\Valg_T$ inside $\Gamdyn_T$), not through a lower Loewner bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\kappa_{+,t}\bC_t$ with both sides SPD inverts to
$\hat\bC_t^{-1}\succeq\kappa_{+,t}^{-1}\bC_t^{-1}$, so
$\hat s_t^2(a)\ge\kappa_{+,t}^{-1}s_t^2(a)$; and~\eqref{eq:onesided-C} gives
$\bar s_t^2(a)\le(1+\bar\chi_t)^2 s_t^2(a)$.  Chaining,
$\bar s_t^2(a)\le(1+\bar\chi_t)^2\kappa_{+,t}\hat s_t^2(a)$, which
is~\eqref{eq:approx-transfer} with $s_{\Calg,t}=\hat s_t$.
\Cref{thm:regret} applies with $\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$.  The legacy two-sided
$\sqrt{\rho_+/\rho_-}$ inflation---which instead assumes a lower spectral
factor---is retained for comparison in \Cref{app:cor}; the two results
trade different assumptions (a lower spectral bound versus dynamic width
complexity), and neither numerically dominates the other in general.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Composing subsampling with feature drift.}
\Cref{lem:subsample} guarantees a sandwich for the \emph{relinearized}
$\bC_t$: $(1{-}\varepsilon)\bC_t\preceq\hat\bC_t\preceq(1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$.
Only the \emph{upper} half is needed here: $\hat\bC_t\preceq(1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$
gives $\kappa_{+,t}=1+\varepsilon$, and composing with the one-sided
whitened bound $\bC_t\preceq(1+\bar\chi_t)^2\bCbar_t$ (\Cref{lem:whitened})
yields $u_t=(1+\varepsilon)(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ in
\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}.  A union bound over $t\le T$ (replacing
$\delta'$ by $\delta'/T$ in \Cref{lem:subsample}) makes the subsampling
event time-uniform and contributes to $\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.

For an \emph{unrescaled current-parameter window} (or any current-parameter
downweighted subset with weights in $[0,1]$), \Cref{prop:window} gives the
\emph{deterministic} upper factor $\kappa_{+,t}=1$ with \emph{no} distributional
assumption, so \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} applies directly with
$u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$.  Only the \emph{stale periodic-refresh} operator
(\S\ref{sec:curvop}(iii)), which reuses Jacobians from an older parameter, lacks
a deterministic $\kappa_{+,t}$; there \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} provides a
guarantee only when a one-sided bound $\hat\bC_t\preceq\kappa_{+,t}\bC_t$ is
separately verified, which we evaluate empirically.

The new operator audit is reported in \Cref{sec:operator-cg-audit}; the earlier
constructed diagnostic is retained in \Cref{app:legacy-diagnostics}.

\paragraph{What remains open.}
The remaining certificate gaps---an externally certified cumulative
linearization bound $\bar F_t$, a certified centering bound $\bar\psi_t$
for the original nonlinear center, a certified transfer factor $u_t$ (for which
the whitened-drift certificate $\bar\chi_t$, with $\kappa_{+,t}$ for approximate
operators, is one sufficient construction), and control of the realized dynamic
complexity
$\Lamalg_T$ (which, via $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$, requires bounding both the
variation charge $\Valg_T$ and the endpoint $\log\det(\Calg_{T+1}/\lambda\bI)$)
for a genuinely adapting backbone---are stated as open problems in the
Limitations.



\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Neural and linear contextual bandits.}
LinUCB \citep{abbasi2011improved} and Thompson sampling
\citep{thompson1933likelihood,agrawal2013thompson} provide the analytical
backbone for contextual bandit exploration.  Neural extensions---NeuralUCB
\citep{zhou2020neural}, NeuralTS \citep{zhang2021neural}, neural-linear
models \citep{riquelme2018deep}---typically linearize the network (via NTK or
last-layer features) and maintain a tractable covariance over the resulting
feature space.  Our work keeps the linearization for analysis but replaces
the compressed curvature with a full implicit solve via CG.
Neural-linear designs that train a deep representation but explore only
in the last layer \citep{xu2022shallow} occupy the opposite design point.

\paragraph{Online Bayesian and EKF-style neural bandits.}
\citet{duranmartin2022efficient} apply extended Kalman filter (EKF)
updates for online Bayesian inference in neural bandits, avoiding
explicit covariance inversion by maintaining a factored posterior.
Their approach shares the goal of tractable full-parameter uncertainty,
but relies on a diagonal-plus-low-rank posterior approximation rather than
exact curvature--vector products.
CC-UCB differs by using CG with the full GGN operator and providing a
regret analysis that explicitly quantifies the CG truncation error.

\paragraph{Last-layer Laplace for large-scale bandits.}
Last-layer Laplace methods
\citep{ritter2018scalable,daxberger2021laplace} restrict the posterior
approximation to the final layer of a deep network, arguing that
full-parameter covariance is computationally intractable for large models.
While last-layer methods are practical and well-understood, they discard backbone curvature and its potential miscalibration under partial fine-tuning.
CC-UCB targets exactly this gap: accessing full-parameter curvature
without materializing the covariance matrix.

\paragraph{Sampling-based full-parameter exploration.}
Langevin Monte Carlo Thompson sampling (LMC-TS) \citep{xu2022langevin}
explores with approximate posterior samples over the full parameter
vector, obtained by noisy gradient steps on the regularized loss; like
CC-UCB it never materializes a covariance matrix, and it carries
regret guarantees in the linear setting.
The two routes pay for exploration differently: LMC-TS through the
mixing of a per-round sampling chain, CC-UCB through CG truncation and
spectral distortion of an explicit curvature metric, which yields
deterministic widths, an optimism-style analysis, and a fixed curvature operator
reused across separate per-action CG solves.
\paragraph{Efficient nonlinear UCB.}
\citet{kassraie2022neural} propose NN-UCB and NN-TS, neural contextual
bandit algorithms whose confidence widths are built from the neural
tangent kernel (NTK), and prove sublinear regret when the reward lies in
the induced RKHS.
Their uncertainty route differs from ours: they use the NTK Gram matrix
(an initialization-anchored kernel object), whereas CC-UCB uses the
GGN/Fisher curvature at the current parameter~$\btheta_t$, accessed
matrix-free via CG.
CC-UCB thus targets the regime where the practitioner has a specific
pretrained model and wants to use its curvature as-is, rather than
constructing a surrogate NTK-based estimator.

\paragraph{Spectral sketching for linear bandits.}
\citet{kuzborskij2019efficient} analyze linear bandits where the Gram
matrix is replaced by a Frequent Directions sketch, deriving regret
bounds that depend on the spectral approximation quality.
\citet{wen2024pitfalls} catalog failure modes of sketch-based
bandits, showing that naive sketching can break optimism.
Our spectral-distortion theorem (\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}) instantiates the
same conceptual lever---quantifying regret inflation via spectral
distortion---in the matrix-free GGN/CVP setting.
We do not claim novelty for the sandwich-to-regret reduction itself;
the contribution is adapting it to the CG/GGN context with explicit
CG truncation and feature-drift factors.

\paragraph{Laplace approximation in deep learning.}
The Laplace approximation \citep{mackay1992practical,ritter2018scalable}
provides a Gaussian posterior centered at a MAP estimate with covariance
given by the inverse curvature.  Scalable variants use diagonal, KFAC, or
subnetwork approximations.  CC-UCB sidesteps these compressions by accessing
the GGN curvature only through matrix--vector products.

\paragraph{GGN--vector products, Hessian spectral analysis, and influence
functions.}
Pearlmutter's algorithm \citep{pearlmutter1994fast} computes exact
Hessian--vector products; the analogous GGN--vector product has the same
Jacobian-transpose-times-Jacobian-times-vector structure
\citep{schraudolph2002fast}.
Recent work on Krylov methods for Hessian spectral analysis
\citep{granziol2026hessian} demonstrates related distributed HVP/Krylov
infrastructure at $>10^8$-parameter scale, but does not benchmark the exact
per-example GGN operator or end-to-end CC-UCB latency.
Influence functions
\citep{koh2017understanding} use inverse-curvature--vector products to
attribute predictions to training points; the CG-based implicit solve
we employ is the same computational primitive.

\paragraph{Optimal experimental design.}
Information-theoretic exploration objectives---such as maximizing
$\log\det(\bCbar_t)$ or minimizing the trace of~$\bCbar_t^{-1}$---connect
bandit exploration to the classical D-optimal and A-optimal design
criteria \citep{valko2013finite}.  CC-UCB's use of the full GGN/Fisher curvature
enables stochastic estimates of these quantities via Hutchinson-type trace
estimators \citep{ubaru2017fast}, without forming the matrix.



\paragraph{Filtration.}
We separate \emph{pre-action} randomness, revealed before the agent commits to
$a_t$, from \emph{post-reward} randomness, drawn only after $r_t$ is observed.
Let $\Omega_t$ collect all pre-action algorithmic randomness used at round~$t$:
curvature subsampling indices, randomized sketches, and stochastic solver seeds.
The randomized sketch or subsample that defines the curvature operator is drawn
into $\Omega_t$ and \emph{fixed before action selection}; it is not re-randomized
during any conjugate-gradient (CG) solve at round~$t$.  (Standard CG requires a
fixed symmetric positive definite matrix--vector oracle across its iterations and
does not apply to a stochastic oracle that changes between iterations.)
Let $U_{t+1}$ collect the \emph{post-reward} randomness used to form the next
parameter estimate $\btheta_{t+1}$ (minibatch order, dropout, perturbed warm
starts).
Define $\cF_0 = \sigma(\btheta_1)$ and, for $t\ge 1$, the recursively built
$\sigma$-algebra
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtration-F}
  \cF_t \;:=\;
  \cF_{t-1}\vee
  \sigma\!\bigl(\bx_t,\cA_t,\Omega_t,a_t,r_t,U_{t+1}\bigr),
\end{equation}
so that $\btheta_{t+1}$---computed from the data through round~$t$ together with
the post-reward randomness $U_{t+1}$---is $\cF_t$-measurable.
Define the \emph{pre-action history} $\sigma$-algebra and the pre-reward
$\sigma$-algebra
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtration}
  \cHist_t
  \;:=\;
  \cF_{t-1}\vee\sigma\!\bigl(\bx_t,\cA_t,\Omega_t\bigr),
  \qquad
  \cG_t \;:=\; \cHist_t\vee\sigma(a_t).
\end{equation}
At round~$t$ the context $\bx_t$, the finite action set $\cA_t$, and the
pre-action randomness $\Omega_t$ are revealed; the agent selects $a_t$; then the
reward $r_t=\mu^*(\bx_t,a_t)+\eta_t$ is observed; finally $U_{t+1}$ is drawn and
$\btheta_{t+1}$ is computed.
By construction of \Cref{alg:ccucb} the selection rule is a deterministic function
of $\cHist_t$-measurable quantities, so the chosen action $a_t$ is itself
$\cHist_t$-measurable and hence $\cG_t=\cHist_t$; we retain the symbol $\cG_t$
where the played action is used.
The following are all $\cHist_t$-measurable (i.e.\ determined before the reward
$r_t$): the candidate action set $\cA_t$ and every candidate feature
$\bg_t(\bx_t,a)$, $a\in\cA_t$; the chosen action $a_t$; the algorithmic
curvature operator~$\Calg_t$ (\Cref{sec:method}); the confidence schedule
$\bar\beta_t$; the centering certificate~$\bar\psi_t$
(\Cref{asm:optim}); the per-round CG tolerance~$\bar\varepsilon_t$ together with
any condition-number certificate used by the CG stopping rule; and the one-sided
optimism-transfer factor~$u_t(a)$ (\Cref{asm:transfer}).
Since $\btheta_t$ is $\cF_{t-1}\subseteq\cHist_t$-measurable, each candidate design
vector $\bg_t(\bx_t,a)$ is $\cHist_t$-measurable and the realized design vector
$\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)$ is $\cG_t$-measurable.
We assume $\{U_s\}_{s\ge1}$ is independent of the reward noise
$\{\eta_s\}_{s\ge1}$, so that revealing $U_{t+1}$ and forming $\btheta_{t+1}$
after $r_t$ preserves the conditional sub-Gaussian property of \Cref{asm:noise}
for the future noise $\{\eta_s\}_{s>t}$.
Every non-deterministic guarantee used by the algorithm or the analysis is
collected into a single \emph{certificate event} (\Cref{thm:regret}); a
certificate that is deterministic or almost sure contributes zero failure
probability.



\paragraph{Auxiliary linearized ridge estimator.}
For the analysis we define a \emph{linearized ridge estimator} based on
frozen features.  Set the predictable intercept
$b_s := \mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx_s,a_s) - \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\btheta_s$
and the pseudo-response $y_s := r_s - b_s$.
Under \Cref{asm:linear} and realizability ($\mu^*=\mu_{\btheta^*}$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pseudo-response}
  y_s
  \;=\;
  \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\btheta^*
  \;+\;
  \rho_s(\bx_s,a_s,\btheta^*)
  \;+\;
  \eta_s,
\end{equation}
where $|\rho_s|\le\epslin(s)$.
The linearized estimator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thetahat-lin}
  \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
  \;:=\;
  \argmin_{\btheta}\,\Biggl[\,
    \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
      \bigl(y_s - \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\btheta\bigr)^2
    \;+\;\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\btheta\|^2
  \Biggr]
  \;=\;
  \bCbar_t^{-1}\,\frac{1}{\sigma^2}
    \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)\,y_s.
\end{equation}
This is a standard linear ridge regression with predictable design
vectors~$\bg_s/\sigma$ and Gram matrix~$\bCbar_t$, so the
self-normalized martingale bound
\citep{abbasi2011improved} applies to it directly
(\Cref{lem:confidence}).



\paragraph{GGN versus Hessian (context only).}
The regret theorem uses the GGN/Fisher curvature as the exploration
metric.  It does not require the GGN to be an accurate Hessian
approximation.
For squared loss~\eqref{eq:erm} the full Hessian of the data-fit term is
\[
  \bH_t \;=\; \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
  \Bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top
  \;-\; \bigl(r_s - \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)
        \nabla^2\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)\Bigr]
  \;+\;\lambda\bI.
\]
The curvature matrix $\bC_t$ drops the residual-times-second-derivative
term, retaining only the outer-product part that is guaranteed PSD.
The dropped residual-times-second-derivative term
$D_t=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}(r_s{-}\mu_{\btheta_t})\nabla^2\mu_{\btheta_t}$
has operator norm at most
$(t{-}1)\epsilon_r H/\sigma^2$, where $\epsilon_r$ bounds the residuals and
$H$ the Hessian norms.
When this quantity is small relative to~$\lambda$, $\bC_t$ approximates
the full Hessian in the Loewner sense (a formal statement with proof is
given in \Cref{app:ggn-approx}).
This comparison is \emph{not} invoked in the regret analysis
(\Cref{thm:regret} uses $\bC_t$ directly as the exploration metric);
it serves only to contextualize $\bC_t$ as a proxy for the full
Hessian.  Outside the small-residual regime, the GGN is not an exact
Hessian approximation; it is the PSD Fisher/GGN metric used by the
algorithm.

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Remark (Fisher, empirical Fisher, and GGN).}
Three curvature objects appear in the literature and must be
distinguished.
The \emph{empirical Fisher} is the realized score outer product
$\sum_s\nabla_{\btheta}\log p(r_s\!\mid\!\bx_s,a_s;\btheta)\,
 \nabla_{\btheta}\log p(r_s\!\mid\!\bx_s,a_s;\btheta)^\top$;
it depends on observed rewards and is \emph{not} the operator used by
CC-UCB.
The \emph{true (model) Fisher} is the model expectation of the score
outer product,
$F(\btheta)=\mathbb{E}_{r\sim p(\cdot\mid\bx,a;\btheta)}
 [\nabla_{\btheta}\log p\;\nabla_{\btheta}\log p^\top]$.
The \emph{generalized Gauss--Newton} (GGN) drops the
residual-times-Hessian term from the full Hessian, retaining only the
outer-product Jacobian part that is guaranteed PSD
\citep{martens2020new,schraudolph2002fast}.
For Gaussian regression with fixed variance (the squared-loss setting
of this paper), the true Fisher and GGN are identical, and both
reduce to the outer-product curvature $\sigma^{-2}\sum_s\bg_s\bg_s^\top$
used in~\eqref{eq:curvature}.
More generally, for exponential-family negative log-likelihoods
parameterized by natural parameters $z_{\btheta}(\bx,a)$, the true
Fisher equals the GGN:
$J_{\btheta}^\top\nabla^2 A(z)\,J_{\btheta}$,
where $J_{\btheta}=\nabla_{\btheta}z_{\btheta}$ and
$\nabla^2 A$ is the Hessian of the log-partition function
\citep{martens2020new,pascanu2014revisiting}.
Bernoulli rewards yield scalar weight~$p(1{-}p)$;
categorical rewards yield $\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbf{p})-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^\top$.
See \Cref{app:expfam} for the extension.
\end{quote}

We access $\bC_t$ only through curvature--vector products
$\bv\mapsto\bC_t\,\bv$, computed via forward-mode Jacobian--vector
products composed with reverse-mode vector--Jacobian products
\citep{pearlmutter1994fast,schraudolph2002fast}.



\paragraph{Extension to exponential-family likelihoods.}%
The same matrix-free CG/CVP machinery extends algorithmically to
exponential-family likelihoods (e.g.\ Bernoulli or categorical rewards)
via the Fisher/GGN curvature $J_t^\top(\nabla^2 A)\,J_t$, but no regret
guarantee beyond Gaussian squared loss is claimed in this draft.
Details of the curvature definitions are in \Cref{app:expfam}.



\paragraph{Per-round CG tolerance~$\bar\varepsilon_t$.}
The algorithm uses a \emph{known} per-round tolerance
$\bar\varepsilon_t\in(0,1)$ in the UCB bonus (line~\ref{line:ucb}), not the
true per-action energy-norm errors $\varepsilon_{t,a}$ (which are unobservable
without the exact solves).  It suffices that
$\max_{a\in\cA_t}\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t$ at every round
(\eqref{eq:cg-tol-uniform}; zero-gradient actions have $\varepsilon_{t,a}=0$ by
convention and are trivially within tolerance); the bonus is then conservative.
Two strategies ensure this:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Adaptive residual stopping (recommended).}
  For each action, monitor the computable Euclidean relative residual
  $\|r_I\|/\|\bg_t(a)\|$ at each CG step (skipping actions with
  $\bg_t(a)=\mathbf0$).  Since
  $\varepsilon_{t,a} \le \sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}\cdot\|r_I\|/\|\bg_t(a)\|$
  (standard CG bound, with $\bar\kappa_t$ a certified upper bound on the
  condition number of $\Calg_t$), stopping that solve when
  $\|r_I\|/\|\bg_t(a)\| \le \bar\varepsilon_t/\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}$
  guarantees $\varepsilon_{t,a} \le \bar\varepsilon_t$, hence
  \eqref{eq:cg-tol-uniform}.
  This is conservative by the factor~$\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}$ but requires
  no access to $\Calg_t^{-1}$ and adapts automatically to the
  per-round condition number.
\item \emph{Fixed budget (requires a uniform condition-number bound).}
  If an a~priori bound
  $\bar\kappa_t \le \kappa_{\max}$ holds for \emph{all rounds}~$t\le T$,
  choose $I$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:cg-budget} at $\kappa_{\max}$ and a fixed
  target~$\bar\varepsilon_t\equiv\bar\varepsilon$.
  For a \emph{nonnegative weighted outer-product} operator
  $\Calg_t=\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s\,
  \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$ the condition number is bounded in
  closed form, $\bar\kappa_t\le 1+W_tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ with
  $W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s$; a fixed buffer of size~$n$ ($W_t=n$,
  unrescaled) makes this independent of~$T$.
  For the full-history operator ($W_t=t{-}1$), and for rescaled uniform
  subsampling with $w_s=(t{-}1)/n$ (so $W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s=t{-}1$),
  the bound grows with~$t$, so a fixed budget does \emph{not} guarantee
  $\max_a\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon$ uniformly; adaptive stopping or an
  increasing budget $I_t$ is required (see \Cref{app:cg}).
  For a \emph{general} SPD operator that is not a nonnegative weighted outer
  product (e.g.\ a randomized sketch), the closed-form bound
  $1+W_tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ does not apply, and a separately certified
  $\cHist_t$-measurable condition-number upper bound $\bar\kappa_t$ must be
  supplied.
\end{enumerate}
In the regret bound (\Cref{thm:regret}), the CG inflation enters through the
per-round factor $\alpha_t = \sqrt{(1{+}\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1{-}\bar\varepsilon_t)}$,
inside the sum of squares $\sum_t\alpha_t^2 u_t(a_t)\omega_t^2$; no horizon
maximum over $\bar\varepsilon_t$ is taken.

\paragraph{Action screening (practical heuristic).}
When the action set $\cA_t$ is large, computing the CG solve for every
action is prohibitive ($|\cA_t|\times I$ CG steps per round).
A practical heuristic is to screen the top-$K$ actions by mean prediction
$m_t(a)$ (forward passes only), then compute UCB widths for the $K$
candidates.  This reduces cost to $K\times I$ CG solves.
\emph{The regret guarantee in \Cref{thm:regret} analyzes the
full-enumeration case ($K=|\cA_t|$); screening can drop the optimal arm
and is not covered by the theory unless one adds an assumption that
$a_t^*\in\cA_t^K$ for all~$t$.}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Detailed CC-UCB implementation}\label{alg:ccucb-detailed}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Reward predictor $\mu_{\btheta}$, damping $\lambda>0$,
         noise proxy $\sigma$;
         predictable confidence schedule $\{\bar\beta_t\}$
         ($\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$; \Cref{cor:beta-constructive});
         centering certificate $\{\bar\psi_t\}$ (\Cref{asm:optim};
         $\bar\psi_t=0$ for the corrected center);
         algorithmic curvature mode $\Calg_t$ (exact full-history $\bC_t$
         or approximate $\hat\bC_t$) with a certified condition-number
         upper bound $\bar\kappa_t$;
         per-round CG tolerance $\{\bar\varepsilon_t\}$ (or budget $I$);
         one-sided transfer factor $u_t(a)\ge1$ (\Cref{asm:transfer})
         \hfill\COMMENT{exact case: $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$, \Cref{lem:whitened}}
\STATE Initialize displacement $\btheta_1=\mathbf 0$ (deployed model at pretrained $\btheta_0$), dataset $\mathcal{D}_0=\emptyset$
\FOR{$t = 1, 2, \ldots, T$}
  \STATE Observe context $\bx_t$ and action set $\cA_t$; draw pre-action
         randomness $\Omega_t$ \hfill\COMMENT{$\cHist_t$ of~\eqref{eq:filtration}}
  \STATE Form or receive a fixed $\cHist_t$-measurable SPD matrix--vector
         oracle $\bv\mapsto\Calg_t\bv$, $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$, held fixed
         across all CG solves this round \label{line:oracle-detailed}
         \hfill\COMMENT{exact/buffered special case
         $\Calg_t\bv=\lambda\bv
         +\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}\!w_s\,
         \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\bv]$, weights $w_s$ per
         \S\ref{sec:curvop}}
  \STATE Set $\omega_t \gets \bar\beta_t + \bar\psi_t$
  \FOR{each $a \in \cA_t$}
    \STATE $m_t(a) \gets \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t, a)$
        \hfill\COMMENT{mean prediction (or corrected center~\eqref{eq:corrected-center})}
    \STATE $\bg_t(a) \gets \nabla_{\btheta}\,\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t, a)$
        \hfill\COMMENT{gradient feature}
    \IF{$\bg_t(a)=\mathbf 0$}
      \STATE $\tilde{s}_t^2(a) \gets 0$ \hfill\COMMENT{skip CG; no residual ratio}
    \ELSE
      \STATE $\tilde\bu \gets \mathrm{CG}(\Calg_t,\;\bg_t(a))$; stop only once
           $\|r_I\|/\|\bg_t(a)\| \le \bar\varepsilon_t/\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}$,
           or use a certified budget $I_t$ from \eqref{eq:cg-budget}
        \hfill\COMMENT{ensures $\varepsilon_{t,a}\!\le\!\bar\varepsilon_t$} \label{line:cg-detailed}
      \STATE $\tilde{s}_t^2(a) \gets \bg_t(a)^\top \tilde\bu$
        \hfill\COMMENT{approx.\ predictive variance}
    \ENDIF
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE $a_t \gets \argmax_{a\in\cA_t}\Bigl[\,m_t(a)
    + \omega_t\,\sqrt{\dfrac{u_t(a)}{1-\bar\varepsilon_t}}\,
      \sqrt{\tilde{s}_t^2(a)}\,\Bigr]$
      \hfill\COMMENT{bonus~\eqref{eq:bonus}; ties broken by a fixed rule (e.g.\ lowest index) or by $\Omega_t$} \label{line:ucb-detailed}
  \STATE Play $a_t$, observe $r_t$
  \STATE $\mathcal{D}_t \gets \mathcal{D}_{t-1} \cup \{(\bx_t, a_t, r_t)\}$
  \STATE Update $\btheta_{t+1}$ via warm-started optimization on $\mathcal{D}_t$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

For the exact-curvature theorem, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ccucb} is run with
$\mathcal{S}_t=\{1,\ldots,t{-}1\}$, $w_s=1$, full action enumeration,
$\Calg_t=\bC_t$, and the transfer factor $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ from
\Cref{lem:whitened}.  For an approximate operator $\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$, use
$u_t=\kappa_{+,t}(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ from \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}.
The selection rule needs only the \emph{one-sided} upper transfer
factor~$u_t(a)$ for optimism; no lower spectral factor is required (the cost
of the operator is charged through the realized dynamic complexity
$\Lamalg_T$).  We stress that $u_t(a)$, $\bar\varepsilon_t$, $\bar\psi_t$,
and $\bar\beta_t$ are the \emph{analyzed, certified} schedules; in the
experiments (\S\ref{sec:experiments}) we additionally report results under
an oracle-tuned bonus scale~$\cbonus$, which is a diagnostic and not part of
the certified guarantee.
Windowed/refresh curvature and action screening are practical heuristics
unless the corresponding one-sided transfer factor or optimal-arm-survival
condition is separately verified.

\paragraph{Corrected-center remark.}
Replacing the center $m_t(a)$ by the corrected center
$m_t^{\mathrm{corr}}(a)$ of~\eqref{eq:corrected-center} sets $\bar\psi_t=0$
(so $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$) and removes the centering-stability assumption
exactly (\Cref{cor:corrected-center}), at the cost of frozen-feature access for
$\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$; this departs from the $O(d)$ additional
curvature-state-memory,
no-stored-feature design unless a frozen-feature oracle, replay mechanism, or
certified sketch is supplied (see the remark after
\Cref{cor:corrected-center}).

CC-UCB is the analyzed method throughout.



\paragraph{How to read the theorem.}
The theorem is a conditional transfer result for a general predictable SPD
curvature sequence.  It does not prove that nonlinear fine-tuning is
stable.  It says that once local linearization error ($E_T$), the centering
certificate ($\bar\psi_t$), and the one-sided optimism-transfer factor
($u_t$) are supplied, the regret is controlled by the realized dynamic
width-complexity $\Lamalg_T$ (or its potential bound $\Gamdyn_T$, or its
observable surrogate $\widehat{\Lamalg}_T$) and the per-round factors
$\alpha_t,u_t(a_t),\omega_t$ inside a single sum of squares.  It no longer
requires a horizon-uniform \emph{lower} feature-drift sandwich; only the
one-sided upper transfer~\eqref{eq:transfer} is used.  The sufficient
conditions in \Cref{lem:primitive-pred,lem:whitened} express $\bar\psi_t$ and
$u_t$ as primitive diagnostic targets in optimizer residuals and whitened
replayed-Jacobian drift (computable only with additional frozen-feature replay or a
separately validated estimator), but they remain restrictive; unrestricted
end-to-end fine-tuning is not covered unless these certificates, and the
control of $\Lamalg_T$/$\Valg_T$, are verified.

\paragraph{Discussion of terms.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Exploration term.}
Write $S_T:=\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t^2\,u_t(a_t)\,\omega_t^2$ for the sum of
squares of the per-round factors.  The exploration term is
$2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T\,S_T}$.  A sufficient condition
for sublinear regret is
\[
  \Lamalg_T\,S_T = o(T^2),
  \qquad
  E_T=o(T).
\]
Concretely this holds whenever the per-round factors are polylogarithmically
bounded ($\sup_t\alpha_t^2 u_t(a_t)\omega_t^2 = O(\mathrm{polylog}\,T)$, so
$S_T=O(T\,\mathrm{polylog}\,T)$) and the realized dynamic complexity is
small ($\Lamalg_T=O(\mathrm{polylog}\,T)$, e.g.\ via $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$
with both the variation charge $\Valg_T$ and the endpoint
$\log\det(\Calg_{T+1}/\lambda\bI)$ polylogarithmically bounded).
Note $\omega_t^2$ contains the cumulative squared linearization error
through $\bar F_t$ (via $\beta_t$) and the centering certificate
$\bar\psi_t$, so a small $\Lamalg_T$ alone is insufficient.
$\alpha_t$ is bounded whenever the CG tolerance $\bar\varepsilon_t$ is
bounded away from~$1$; in the exact-curvature case $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$
is bounded whenever the whitened-drift certificate $\bar\chi_t$ is bounded
(\Cref{lem:whitened}); the whitened $\ell_2$ accumulation
$\chi_t\le\|\Dmat_t\|_F$ makes this a near-stationarity condition on
replayed-Jacobian drift.  The unstable-curvature cost is isolated in
$\Lamalg_T$ (through $\Valg_T$), not hidden in a spectral constant.
The factor $\sqrt{\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda}$ arises from the width-sum bound of the
dynamic width-complexity lemma (\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential},
Eq.~\eqref{eq:width-sum-dyn}); when $G^2\le\lambda\sigma^2$ it is at most
$\sigma\sqrt2$.
\item \emph{Linearization remainder.}
The bound involves $E_T=\sum_t\epslin(t)$, not $T\epslin$.
Near-linear regimes also require the squared-error sum
$F_T=\sum_t\epslin(t)^2$ (through $\bar F_t$, hence $\omega_t$) to be
controlled.  $\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(1/t)$ gives $E_T=O(1)$;
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(t^{-1/4})$ gives $E_T=O(\sqrt{T})$.
The additive role of $E_T$ mirrors misspecified linear bandits, where a
uniformly bounded per-round model error commonly contributes an additive regret
term that scales linearly in the cumulative approximation error
\citep{ghosh2017misspecified,lattimore2020learning}; sublinear regret
therefore genuinely requires $\epslin(t)$ to decay, not merely to be
uniformly small.
\item \emph{Centering.}
The centering certificate enters \emph{multiplicatively}, through
$\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$ inside the sum of squares $S_T$, rather than as
a separate additive term.  It vanishes
($\bar\psi_t=0$) when $\btheta_t=\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$ (convex
last-layer models with exact optimization), and the corrected-center
variant (\Cref{cor:corrected-center}) removes it in general at the cost of
a frozen-feature solve.
\item \emph{Regret improvement over diagonal/last-layer surrogates.}
The bound itself does not prove that full curvature yields strictly lower
regret than diagonal or last-layer methods in any specific regime; it
provides an upper bound with explicit per-round factors.
\emph{Demonstrating regret improvement is an empirical hypothesis}
(\Cref{sec:experiments}); the theory shows that the regret cost of an
approximate curvature is captured by its transfer factor $u_t$ and its
realized dynamic complexity $\Lamalg_T$ (\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}),
a diagnostic rather than a comparative guarantee.
\item \emph{Scope.}
The bound is proved for Gaussian/squared-loss rewards.
Extending it to exponential-family likelihoods (Bernoulli, categorical)
would require a weighted frozen-feature analysis accounting for the
data-dependent Fisher weights~$\nabla^2 A(z)$ and is left as future work
(\Cref{app:expfam}).
\end{itemize}

\begin{corollary}[Sanity-check: frozen features recover linear UCB]\label{cor:frozen}
Suppose the backbone is frozen and the reward head is linear in its
parameters, the ridge objective is optimized exactly, and take the algorithmic curvature to be the sequential frozen
Gram matrix, $\Calg_t=\bCbar_t$.  Then:
$\epslin(t)=0$ for all~$t$ ($L=0$ in \Cref{asm:linear}), so $E_T=F_T=0$;
$\bar\psi_t=0$ (under exact optimization the MAP and linearized estimators coincide, so
\Cref{asm:optim} holds with equality), giving $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$;
$u_t(a)=1$ for all $a$ (indeed $\bar s_t^2(a)=s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$, so
\Cref{asm:transfer} holds with equality); and, since
$\bCbar_{t+1}=\bCbar_t+\sigma^{-2}\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)^\top$
by~\eqref{eq:bar-update}, the update is exactly the rank-one step, i.e.\
$\Calg_{t+1}=\Calg_t^{+}$, so $\Xi_t=0$, $\Valg_T=0$, and
$\Lamalg_T=\gamma_T$.  Then \Cref{thm:regret} gives
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\gamma_T\,
    \sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t^2\,\bar\beta_t^2}.
\]
If in addition $\bar\varepsilon_t\le\bar\varepsilon$ so that
$\alpha_t\le\alpha_I:=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon)/(1-\bar\varepsilon)}$, the
looser \Cref{cor:worstcase} recovers the familiar LinUCB form
$R_T\le 2\,\alpha_I\,\bar\beta_{\max,T}\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T}$,
where $\bar\beta_{\max,T}:=\max_{t\le T}\bar\beta_t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $\epslin(t)=0$, $\bar\psi_t=0$, $u_t(a)=1$, and
$\Lamalg_T=\gamma_T$, $\Valg_T=0$ into~\eqref{eq:regret-bound}; the frozen
Gram is monotone, so $\Xi_t=0$ and the dynamic potential collapses to the
information gain $\gamma_T$.  The LinUCB form is \Cref{cor:worstcase} with
$\alpha_t\le\alpha_I$, $u_t=1$, $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t\le\bar\beta_{\max,T}$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Interpretation and statistics.}
Primary regret results are generated by online policies executed from scratch.
Synthetic evaluators know the teacher only to compute pseudo-regret, optimism
failures, and post-hoc certificates; the action rule never receives it.  Hyperparameter
selection, where used, is performed on the declared tuning seeds before evaluation.
Common random numbers pair methods within an evaluation seed.  We report arithmetic
means, marginal 95\% Student-$t$ intervals, and paired Student-$t$ intervals for
method differences.  No multiplicity adjustment is applied, so the intervals are
descriptive.  An offline common-trajectory diagnostic uses one logged action and
checkpoint sequence for all operators and is never mixed with online regret.

\paragraph{Linear theorem-event audit.}
The context is $x\in\{-1,+1\}^8/\sqrt8$, there are five actions, and
$\phi(x,a)=[x,e_a,x\otimes e_a]$ has dimension 53 and norm $\sqrt3$.  The fixed
teacher contains shared, arm-intercept, and context--arm interaction blocks; exact
enumeration of all $2^8$ contexts confirms that multiple arms are optimal.  Noise
is $\mathcal N(0,0.25^2)$.  Evaluation uses seeds 100--119 after tuning on seeds
0--9.  The tuning grid is $\lambda\in\{0.5,1,2\}$ and

the recorded confidence and small-scale numerical checks.  Fresh

\section{Legacy Oracle-Selected Matched-Coverage Diagnostics}\label{app:legacy-diagnostics}
These secondary diagnostics are retrospective, oracle-selected, and interpolated;
no policy was run at an interpolated coefficient.  They use method-specific
damping, reuse the same seeds for selection and reporting, and give nominal
selection-unadjusted intervals.  Surviving records are incomplete, and the
comparison is noncausal and identifies no causal mechanism.

The constructed study used a $d=100$, ten-arm linear-Gaussian nuisance-context
stress test with a context-independent optimal arm, $T=1500$, and ten paired
seeds.  The Covertype study treated seven labels as arms but restricted
uncertainty to a 196-parameter last layer and used Gaussian squared-loss
curvature for binary rewards.  Its optimizer, preprocessing, trained-backbone

A $k$-step Lanczos run constructs a $k$-dimensional Krylov subspace.  After
orthogonalization and Rayleigh--Ritz projection, retained Ritz pairs define the
rank-$k$-plus-damping surrogate in \eqref{eq:lanczos-surrogate}; they need not be
the exact top-$k$ eigenpairs of $\bC_t$.  The displayed inverse is exact for that

\subsection*{Protocol details (bounded-feature event, coverage, grids, interpolation)}
\paragraph{Bounded-feature event.}
\Cref{asm:bounded} posits a deterministic gradient-feature bound~$G$, whereas the
constructed contexts are Gaussian and hence unbounded, so the theorem applies to
this instance only on a finite-horizon high-probability event.  For
$\bx_t\stackrel{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\mathcal{N}(\mathbf0,\Sigma)$, Gaussian Lipschitz
concentration of $\bx\mapsto\|\bx\|_2$ ($\sqrt{\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}}$-Lipschitz
scale, equivalently variance proxy $\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}$; mean at most
$\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}$) with a union bound over $t\le T$
gives, with probability at least $1-\delta_G$,
\[
  \max_{t\le T}\|\bx_t\|_2 \;\le\; \sqrt{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}
    + \sqrt{2\,\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(T/\delta_G)} .
\]
Since the model is linear, $\bg_t(a)=\boldsymbol\phi(\bx_t,a)=\bx_t+b\delta_a
\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{align}}$, so on this event
$\max_{t\le T,\,a}\|\bg_t(a)\|\le G_T(\delta_G)$ with
\[
  G_T(\delta_G)=\sqrt{\operatorname{tr}\Sigma}
    +\sqrt{2\,\|\Sigma\|_{\mathrm{op}}\log(T/\delta_G)}+b\max_a|\delta_a| .
\]
Application of \Cref{thm:regret} to this instance is thus conditional on this event,
and $\delta_G$ contributes to the cumulative certificate failure probability
$\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.



\paragraph{Protocol and matched-coverage metric.}
We use $d{=}100$, $\kappa{=}30$, $\vartheta\in\{0,7.5,15,45\}^\circ$, $T{=}1500$, an
exact solver, and $10$ paired seeds $\{0,\dots,9\}$.  Per method we sweep the
ridge damping~$\lambda$ (full: $\{10^2,\dots,3\times10^5\}$; diagonal/lanczos:
$\{1,10,30,100\}$) and the bonus scale~$\cbonus$ (full: $\{16,\dots,1024\}$;
diagonal: $\{0.25,\dots,80\}$; lanczos: $\{0.03125,\dots,80\}$).  Methods are
compared at matched \emph{top-two armwise oracle optimism coverage} on a fixed
held-out batch (held fixed across methods, seeds, and $\cbonus$ values).  The batch
comprises $1000$ context--arm pairs; for the coverage diagnostic we retain, per
context, the two arms with the largest \emph{true conditional mean}
$\mu^\star(\bx,a)=\boldsymbol\phi(\bx,a)^\top\btheta^\star$.  Writing
$\{(\bx_i,a_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}$ for the retained pairs and
$\mathcal{T}=\{250,500,\dots,1500\}$ for the snapshot ticks, coverage is the
empirical average
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cov-top2}
  \mathrm{cov}_{\mathrm{top2}}(\cbonus)
  =\frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}|\,N}\sum_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}}\sum_{i=1}^{N}
    \mathbf{1}\!\bigl\{\,\mu^\star(\bx_i,a_i)\le
      \mu_{\btheta_\tau}(\bx_i,a_i)+\cbonus\,\tilde s_\tau(a_i)\,\bigr\},
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the optimistic upper bound is compared against the true \emph{mean} reward
(never a noisy realized reward), averaged over the fixed batch and the ticks, then
treated per seed.  The available records do not establish whether ``$1000$
context--arm pairs'' denotes $1000$ total pairs or $1000$ contexts each contributing
two arms (i.e.\ whether $N=1000$ or $N=2000$); this is left as an author action
item.  This metric averages \emph{armwise} coverage indicators: it measures how
often the per-arm optimistic upper bound covers the true mean, and does \emph{not}
directly measure correct action selection, decision-gap coverage, the probability of
choosing the optimal arm, or deployable calibration.
For each target $q\in\{0.90,0.95\}$ we isotonically project (pool
adjacent violators) the coverage-versus-$\log_2\cbonus$ points to a nondecreasing
curve---monotonicity is \emph{imposed}, not guaranteed, since coverage need not be
monotone in~$\cbonus$ in an adaptive bandit---invert it by local linear
interpolation to the $\cbonus^\star$ meeting~$q$, read the piecewise-linearly
interpolated pseudo-regret there, and keep the~$\lambda$ minimizing per-method
matched-coverage regret.  The selected damping for full curvature is interior to the tested grid
(regret rises past it: at $\vartheta{=}7.5^\circ$,
$8.1\!\to\!12.2\!\to\!31.1$ as $\lambda$ grows), so the selection did not hit a
tested grid boundary; this does not rule out grid or selection artifacts.  We report paired full-minus-surrogate
matched-coverage pseudo-regret (\Cref{tab:legacy-matched-coverage}).  This is a retrospective
oracle diagnostic (it uses the true mean reward): for each method the ridge
damping~$\lambda$ and the $\cbonus^\star$ meeting the coverage target are selected
using the same $10$ seeds subsequently used for reporting, and the tabulated regret
is \emph{read off by piecewise-linear interpolation} at $\cbonus^\star$---an
\emph{interpolated response-surface estimate}, not the realized cumulative regret of
any single executed policy (no method is actually run at the interpolated
$\cbonus^\star$).  The available manuscript records do not establish whether
$\lambda$ and $\cbonus$ were selected per seed or from a pooled aggregate; the
reported values should therefore be interpreted as oracle-selected interpolated
summaries until the original run records are recovered.  Because the per-method
$\lambda/\cbonus$ selection, the isotonic projection, and the reported intervals all
reuse the same $10$ seeds, the intervals are \emph{nominal descriptive paired-t
intervals, unadjusted for model and hyperparameter selection} and are not
post-selection confidence intervals (\Cref{app:legacy-diagnostics} details the
estimator); formal post-selection inference would require independent tuning and
evaluation seeds, and the raw coverage--regret points before isotonic projection
are produced by the analysis code, which is not attached to this submission.

\begin{corollary}[Looser worst-case reduction]\label{cor:worstcase}
If $\alpha_t\le\alpha_{\max}$, $u_t(a_t)\le u_{\max}$, and
$\omega_t\le\omega_{\max}$ for all $t\le T$, then
$S_T\le\alpha_{\max}^2 u_{\max}\omega_{\max}^2\,T$
and
\[
  R_T \;\le\; 2\,\alpha_{\max}\,\omega_{\max}\,\sqrt{u_{\max}}\,
  \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\Lamalg_T}\;+\;2E_T .
\]
This $\sqrt{T\,\Lamalg_T}$ form recovers the familiar worst-case expression;
it is weakly looser than~\eqref{eq:regret-bound} (no tighter, with equality when
the per-round factors are constant) and is \emph{not} the main result.
\end{corollary}

Three formulas used by the legacy diagnostics are recorded here.  The constructed

\begin{equation}\label{eq:constructed-sigma}
  \Sigma(\vartheta)
  \;=\;
  \kappa\,\bh(\vartheta)\bh(\vartheta)^\top
  \;+\;
  \nu\,\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{align}}(\vartheta)\mathbf{e}_{\mathrm{align}}(\vartheta)^\top
  \;+\;
  \rho_{\mathrm{bulk}}\,\bigl(\bI-\mathbf{e}_1\mathbf{e}_1^\top
  -\mathbf{e}_2\mathbf{e}_2^\top\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the in-plane basis rotates with the single knob~$\vartheta$.

For the defined rank-$k$-plus-damping Lanczos--Ritz surrogate, the inverse is

\begin{equation}\label{eq:lanczos-surrogate}
  \hat\bC_t^{-1}\;=\;\bQ\Lambda^{-1}\bQ^\top

with $\bQ=[\bu_1,\dots,\bu_k]$ the Ritz vectors and $\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_i)$
the Ritz values (which include the damping~$\lambda$).  The surrogate itself
approximates $\bC_t$; its Ritz pairs need not be the exact top-$k$ eigenpairs, and
the legacy run did not certify a global one-sided Loewner factor.

The legacy Covertype label-coverage statistic is the empirical average

\section{Proof of \texorpdfstring{\Cref{thm:regret}}{the main regret theorem}}\label{app:proof}

We recall the key objects:
$\bg_s(\bx,a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx,a)$ (frozen features),
$\bCbar_t=\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$
(frozen-feature Gram matrix),
$\bar s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)}$,
the algorithmic curvature $\Calg_t$ with width
$s_{\Calg,t}(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)}$,
the linearized ridge estimator $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:thetahat-lin}), the combined width factor
$\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$, the per-round CG inflation
$\alpha_t=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$, and the
realized dynamic complexity $\Lamalg_T$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:lam-alg}).
Write $m_t(a)=\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$.

\paragraph{Step 1: Confidence, centering, and prediction-error bound.}
By \Cref{lem:confidence}, with probability $\ge 1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf-app}
  \bigl|\bg_t(a)^\top(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}-\btheta^*)\bigr|
  \;\le\; \bar s_t(a)\,\beta_t,
  \qquad\forall\,t,\,a,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_t$ is as in~\eqref{eq:beta}.
The self-normalized martingale inequality
\citep{abbasi2011improved} is applied to the $1$-sub-Gaussian sequence
$\xi_s=\eta_s/\sigma$ with the predictable design $\bg_s/\sigma$
and Gram matrix~$\bCbar_t$.
Each realized design vector~$\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$ is
$\cG_s$-measurable (where $\cG_s=\cHist_s\vee\sigma(a_s)$ is
the pre-reward sigma-algebra of~\eqref{eq:filtration}), and the sequential
update~\eqref{eq:bar-update} ensures the hypotheses
of the self-normalized bound are satisfied.
By \Cref{lem:discrepancy} (\Cref{asm:optim}),
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t{-}\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|\le
\bar\psi_t\,\bar s_t(a)$.
Combined with $|\rho_t|\le\epslin(t)$ and $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$, the
triangle inequality gives the prediction-error
bound~\eqref{eq:pred-error}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pred-app}
  |\mu_{\btheta^*}(\bx_t,a)-m_t(a)|
  \;\le\;
  (\beta_t+\bar\psi_t)\,\bar s_t(a) + \epslin(t)
  \;\le\;
  \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a) + \epslin(t),
  \qquad\forall\,a .
\end{equation}
(The corrected-center variant of \Cref{cor:corrected-center} reaches
\eqref{eq:pred-app} with $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$ and without
\Cref{asm:optim}.)

\paragraph{Step 2: Bonus sandwiching.}
By \Cref{lem:bonus} (which uses \Cref{lem:cg} with
$\max_{a\in\cA_t}\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t$ and the one-sided
transfer~\eqref{eq:transfer}), for all $a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bonus-app}
  \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a)
  \;\le\;
  w_t(a)
  \;\le\;
  \alpha_t\,\omega_t\,\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a).
\end{equation}
The lower bound and~\eqref{eq:pred-app} give
$\mu^*(\bx_t,a)\le m_t(a)+w_t(a)+\epslin(t)$ for all~$a$: the UCB covers
$\mu^*$ up to the additive $\epslin(t)$ model-mismatch term.

\paragraph{Step 3: Instantaneous regret.}
From the UCB rule $m_t(a_t)+w_t(a_t)\ge m_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)$, and using
$\mu^*(a_t^*)\le m_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)+\epslin(t)$ (Step~2 at $a_t^*$, valid
since $a_t^*\in\cA_t$),
\begin{align*}
  \mu^*(a_t^*)-\mu^*(a_t)
  &\;\le\; [m_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)+\epslin(t)] - \mu^*(a_t) \\
  &\;\le\; [m_t(a_t)+w_t(a_t)+\epslin(t)] - \mu^*(a_t)
    \quad\text{(UCB selection)} \\
  &\;=\; w_t(a_t) + [m_t(a_t)-\mu^*(a_t)] + \epslin(t) \\
  &\;\le\; w_t(a_t) + \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a_t) + 2\epslin(t)
    \quad\text{(\eqref{eq:pred-app} at $a_t$)} \\
  &\;\le\; 2\,w_t(a_t) + 2\epslin(t)
    \quad\text{(lower bound in~\eqref{eq:bonus-app})}.
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Step 4: Upper bonus bound.}
The upper bound in~\eqref{eq:bonus-app} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret-round}
  \mu^*(a_t^*)-\mu^*(a_t)
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_t\,\omega_t\,\sqrt{u_t(a_t)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a_t)
  \;+\; 2\epslin(t).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 5: Cauchy--Schwarz after summing.}
Summing~\eqref{eq:regret-round} over $t=1,\dots,T$ and applying
Cauchy--Schwarz to the sum (\emph{not} per round),
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a_t)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a_t) + 2E_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t^2\omega_t^2 u_t(a_t)}\,
  \sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^T s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)} + 2E_T .
\]

\paragraph{Step 6: Dynamic potential and collection.}
\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential} gives
$\sum_{t=1}^T s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\le(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T$.
Substituting,
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T\,
    \sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t^2\,u_t(a_t)\,\omega_t^2} \;+\; 2E_T,
\]
which is~\eqref{eq:regret-bound}.  The interpretable and observable
versions~\eqref{eq:regret-bound-dyn} follow from $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$
(\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential}) and $\Lamalg_T\le\widehat{\Lamalg}_T$
(\Cref{cor:lam-observable}).  The Cauchy--Schwarz step is applied only after
summing, so the time-varying factors $\alpha_t,u_t(a_t),\omega_t$ enter
through a sum of squares; no horizon maximum is taken before Cauchy--Schwarz, and
the usual $\sqrt{T}$ scaling is recovered when the per-round factors are
uniformly bounded (\Cref{cor:worstcase}).  The stated probability follows from a
union bound over the self-normalized confidence event
$\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}$ ($\ge 1-\delta$) and the certificate event
$\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$ ($\ge 1-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$), giving
$\ge 1-\delta-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.
\qed

\section{CG Convergence Analysis}\label{app:cg}

Fix a round~$t$ and an action~$a$ with $\bg:=\bg_t(a)\ne\mathbf0$.  After
$I_{t,a}$~iterations of CG on the SPD system $\Calg_t\bu=\bg$ (starting
from $\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf{0}$), the standard energy-norm bound gives
\[
  \varepsilon_{t,a}
  \;=\;
  \frac{\|\tilde\bu_{I_{t,a}} - \Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}
       {\|\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}
  \;\le\;
  2\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_t}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa_t}+1}\right)^{\!I_{t,a}},
\]
where $\kappa_t=\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)/\lambda_{\min}(\Calg_t)$
is the true condition number.  The bound is right-hand-side independent, so a
common iteration budget bounds $\max_a\varepsilon_{t,a}$.  This zero-start form is
what the fixed-budget certificate~\eqref{eq:cg-budget} uses.
For a \emph{warm start} $\tilde\bu_0\ne\mathbf0$, energy-norm minimization over the
shifted Krylov space instead gives
\[
  \frac{\|\tilde\bu_{I_{t,a}}-\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}{\|\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}
  \;\le\;
  2\,q^{\,I_{t,a}}\,
  \frac{\|\tilde\bu_0-\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}{\|\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}},
  \qquad q=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_t}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa_t}+1},
\]
i.e.\ the extra factor is the initial relative energy error $\|e_0\|_{\Calg_t}/
\|\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}$ (equal to one at $\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf0$, recovering the
zero-start bound).  Adaptive residual stopping (line~\ref{line:cg}) remains valid with a
warm start because it certifies the realized residual directly; but the fixed-budget
count~\eqref{eq:cg-budget} is valid \emph{as written} only for zero initialization,
and a fixed-budget warm-start certificate additionally requires a bound on that
initial relative energy error (the zero-start iteration count does not by itself
certify a warm-started solve).
Any certified upper bound $\bar\kappa_t\ge\kappa_t$ may replace $\kappa_t$; for a
nonnegative weighted outer-product operator,
$\lambda_{\min}(\Calg_t)\ge\lambda$ and
$\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)\le\lambda+W_tG^2/\sigma^2$
(where $W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s$) give
\[
\kappa_t\le\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)}{\lambda}
\le1+\frac{W_tG^2}{\lambda\sigma^2}=: \bar\kappa_t,
\]
whereas a general SPD operator
requires a separately certified $\bar\kappa_t$.

Two cases at value one must be distinguished.  The true condition number
$\kappa_t=1$ forces all eigenvalues equal, i.e.\ $\Calg_t=c_t\bI$ for some
scalar $c_t>0$, and one CG iteration from $\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf0$ is \emph{exact}
($\varepsilon_{t,a}=0$) for every $\bg\ne\mathbf0$; this does \emph{not} by itself
imply $c_t=\lambda$.  With the specific certificate used here,
$\bar\kappa_t=1$
additionally forces $\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)=\lambda$, which together with
$\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ gives $\Calg_t=\lambda\bI$ exactly.  In either reading
the single-step solve is exact, so we keep $\bar\kappa_t=1$ as a separate branch and
never evaluate the logarithmic budget below at its zero denominator.  For
$\bar\kappa_t>1$, setting the
right-hand side of the rate equal to $\epsCGbar$ and requiring
$\epsCGbar < 1$ (where $\epsCGbar$ denotes the round-$t$ target tolerance
$\bar\varepsilon_t$) gives the per-solve budget
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-budget}
  I_{\min}(\bar\kappa_t,\epsCGbar)
  \;=\;
  \left\lceil\frac{\log(2/\epsCGbar)}
  {\log\!\bigl((\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}+1)/(\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}-1)\bigr)}
  \right\rceil
  \;=\; O\!\bigl(\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}\,\log(1/\epsCGbar)\bigr),
\end{equation}
which, applied to every scored action, ensures
$\max_a\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\epsCGbar=\bar\varepsilon_t$.
For $W_t=n$ (unrescaled fixed buffer size),
$\bar\kappa_t\le 1+nG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ is bounded independently of~$T$,
so $I_{\min}$ is constant.
For full history, and for rescaled uniform subsampling with
$w_s=(t{-}1)/n$, we have $W_t=t{-}1$, so the condition-number \emph{bound}
$\bar\kappa_t=O(tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2))$ grows with~$t$, and the worst-case
certificate is then obtained with
$I_{\min}=\Theta\!\bigl(G\sqrt{t/(\lambda\sigma^2)}\,\log(1/\epsCGbar)\bigr)$
iterations per solve---i.e.\ $I=O(\sqrt{t}\,\log(1/\epsCGbar))$ suffices under this
bound (this is an upper-bound certificate, not a lower bound on the number of
iterations CG actually needs).
CG may converge faster than this worst-case certificate suggests under favorable
spectral clustering of~$\Calg_t$; the realized iteration counts would be measured empirically.

\section{Legacy Two-Sided Spectral-Distortion Corollary}\label{app:cor}

The main-text \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} uses only a one-sided upper
transfer factor.  For comparison we record the earlier two-sided result,
which instead assumes both an upper \emph{and} a lower spectral factor and
yields the familiar $\sqrt{\rho_+/\rho_-}$ inflation.  The two results trade
different assumptions---the corollary below needs a lower spectral bound;
the dynamic \Cref{thm:regret} needs the dynamic width complexity
$\Lamalg_T$ instead---and neither numerically dominates the other in
general.

\begin{corollary}[Two-sided spectral-distortion inflation (legacy)]\label{cor:twosided}
Adopt the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:regret} with $\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$, retaining the
\emph{original nonlinear center} (so $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$; in the
convex last-layer regime under exact optimization, $\bar\psi_t=0$ and
$\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$), and a uniform
CG tolerance $\bar\varepsilon_t\le\bar\varepsilon$ so that
$\alpha_t\le\alpha_I:=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon)/(1-\bar\varepsilon)}$.
Suppose the \emph{two-sided} sandwich
$\rho_{-,t}\bCbar_t\preceq\hat\bC_t\preceq\rho_{+,t}\bCbar_t$ holds for all
$t\le T$ with $0<\rho_{-,t}\le\rho_{+,t}<\infty$; set
$\rho_-=\min_t\rho_{-,t}$, $\rho_+=\max_t\rho_{+,t}$, and (to satisfy the
convention $u_t\ge1$ of \Cref{asm:transfer}) take the transfer factor
$u_t:=\max\{1,\rho_{+,t}\}\le\max\{1,\rho_+\}$.  Write
$\omega_{\max,T}:=\max_{t\le T}(\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t)$ and
$\rho_+^{\star}:=\max\{1,\rho_+\}$.  Then, on the
intersection of the confidence event $\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}$ ($\ge1-\delta$) and the
certificate event $\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$ (which now also contains the two-sided
sandwich event; $\ge1-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$),
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_I\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_-}\;\omega_{\max,T}
  \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T} \;+\; 2E_T
\]
with probability at least $1-\delta-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$, where $\gamma_T$ is
the frozen-feature information gain~\eqref{eq:infogain}.  Under the standard
normalization $\rho_+\ge1$ (achievable by rescaling the sandwich so its upper
factor is at least one), $\rho_+^{\star}=\rho_+$ and the inflation is the familiar
$\sqrt{\rho_+/\rho_-}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
This is a \emph{direct} argument through the monotone frozen Gram $\bCbar_t$; it
does \emph{not} substitute $\hat s_t$ for the dynamic width in
\Cref{cor:worstcase}.
\emph{Step 1 (variance sandwich).}  Inverting the two-sided sandwich gives
$\rho_{+,t}^{-1}\bCbar_t^{-1}\preceq\hat\bC_t^{-1}\preceq\rho_{-,t}^{-1}\bCbar_t^{-1}$,
so, evaluating the quadratic form at $\bg_t(a)$,
$\rho_{+,t}^{-1/2}\,\bar s_t(a)\le\hat s_t(a)\le\rho_{-,t}^{-1/2}\,\bar s_t(a)$.
The lower half gives $\bar s_t^2(a)\le\rho_{+,t}\hat s_t^2(a)\le u_t\hat s_t^2(a)$,
so \Cref{asm:transfer} holds with $u_t=\max\{1,\rho_{+,t}\}$.
\emph{Step 2 (per-round regret).}  With $s_{\Calg,t}=\hat s_t$,
\Cref{thm:regret} Steps~1--4 give
$\mathrm{regret}_t\le
2\,\alpha_t\,\omega_t\,\sqrt{u_t}\,\hat s_t(a_t)+2\epslin(t)$; using
$\hat s_t(a_t)\le\rho_{-,t}^{-1/2}\bar s_t(a_t)$,
$u_t=\max\{1,\rho_{+,t}\}\le\rho_+^{\star}$,
$\alpha_t\le\alpha_I$, and $\omega_t\le\omega_{\max,T}$,
\[
  \mathrm{regret}_t
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_I\,\omega_{\max,T}\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_{-,t}}\;\bar s_t(a_t)
  +2\epslin(t)
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_I\,\omega_{\max,T}\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_-}\;\bar s_t(a_t)
  +2\epslin(t).
\]
\emph{Step 3 (frozen-feature elliptic potential).}  Sum over $t$ and apply
Cauchy--Schwarz, then the classical elliptic-potential identity for the
\emph{monotone} Gram $\bCbar_t$ (\eqref{eq:bar-update}),
$\sum_{t}\bar s_t^2(a_t)\le(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\sum_t\log(1+\sigma^{-2}
\bar s_t^2(a_t))=(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\gamma_T$:
\[
  R_T\le 2\,\alpha_I\,\omega_{\max,T}\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_-}
  \sqrt{T\sum_t\bar s_t^2(a_t)}+2E_T
  \le 2\,\alpha_I\,\omega_{\max,T}\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_-}
  \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T}+2E_T.
\]
The probability statement is the union bound over $\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}$ and
$\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
The time-uniform requirement is essential: a curvature sandwich that holds
only at a single~$t$ does not suffice for a regret bound that sums over all
rounds.  For the subsampled GGN in \Cref{lem:subsample}, a per-round union bound
over $t\le T$ (allocating $\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}/T$ per round within the
certificate event $\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$) gives a time-uniform sandwich with the
sample size in~\eqref{eq:subsample-n} evaluated at the worst-case $m=T{-}1$.



\section{GGN Approximation Error}\label{app:ggn-approx}

\begin{lemma}[GGN approximation error]\label{lem:ggn-approx}
Suppose $\mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a)$ is twice differentiable in~$\btheta$.
Suppose $|r_s - \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)| \le \epsilon_r$ and
$\|\nabla^2 \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le H$
for all $s < t$.
Define $\zeta := (t{-}1)\,\epsilon_r\, H / (\sigma^2 \lambda)$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item $\|\bH_t - \bC_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le (t{-}1)\,\epsilon_r\, H / \sigma^2 = \zeta\lambda$.
\item If $\zeta < 1$, then
$(1{-}\zeta)\bC_t \preceq \bH_t \preceq (1{+}\zeta)\bC_t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The dropped term is
$D_t = \sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}(r_s{-}\mu_{\btheta_t})\nabla^2\mu_{\btheta_t}$.
By the triangle inequality,
$\|D_t\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le \sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\epsilon_r H
= \zeta\lambda$, proving~(i).
For~(ii): since $-\|D_t\|\bI \preceq -D_t \preceq \|D_t\|\bI$,
we have $\bC_t - \zeta\lambda\bI \preceq \bH_t \preceq \bC_t + \zeta\lambda\bI$.
Because $\bC_t \succeq \lambda\bI$, the lower bound gives
$\bC_t - \zeta\lambda\bI \succeq \bC_t - \zeta\bC_t = (1{-}\zeta)\bC_t$
(using $\zeta\lambda\bI \preceq \zeta\bC_t$), and similarly
$\bC_t + \zeta\lambda\bI \preceq \bC_t + \zeta\bC_t = (1{+}\zeta)\bC_t$.
\end{proof}

\section{Exponential-Family Curvature Definitions}\label{app:expfam}

For a generic negative log-likelihood $\ell\bigl(r,\,z_{\btheta}(\bx,a)\bigr)$
with natural parameter $z_{\btheta}\in\R^{k}$ (scalar for Bernoulli,
$k$-dimensional for categorical), the GGN/Fisher curvature matrices are
\begin{align}
  \bC_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
  &\;=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    J_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\,\nabla^2 A\bigl(z_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)\,
    J_t(\bx_s,a_s),
  \label{eq:curvature-exp}\\[4pt]
  \bCbar_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
  &\;=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    J_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\,\nabla^2 A\bigl(z_s(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)\,
    J_s(\bx_s,a_s),
  \label{eq:curvature-bar-exp}
\end{align}
where $J_t(\bx,a)=\nabla_{\btheta}z_{\btheta_t}(\bx,a)\in\R^{k\times d}$
is the Jacobian of the natural parameter and
$\nabla^2 A(z)$ is the output-space Hessian of the log-partition
function.
For Bernoulli rewards $\nabla^2 A(z)=p(1{-}p)$ (scalar);
for categorical rewards $\nabla^2 A(z)=\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbf{p})
-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^\top)$.
Setting $k=1$ and $\nabla^2 A=\sigma^{-2}$ recovers the
Gaussian curvature~\eqref{eq:curvature}--\eqref{eq:curvature-bar}.

\section{Broader Impact}\label{app:impact}
The motivating applications---recommendation and learned reward heads---carry
deployment risks.  Exploration deliberately selects uncertain actions, which can
expose users to under-vetted content; a miscalibrated reward head can amplify
feedback loops between the policy and its future data.  Deployment should combine
exploration with explicit safety constraints, action filters, offline evaluation,
and online monitoring.  This paper analyzes regret under stated certificates and
does not establish safe exploration.
