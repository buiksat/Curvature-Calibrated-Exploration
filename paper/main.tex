% --- abstract -------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Retraining a contextual-bandit model changes the gradient features used in
current curvature, while sequential confidence can be built from features
frozen when rewards were collected. We study the guarantees and limitations of
transporting this confidence to relinearized curvature. A conditional UCB
theorem separates metric transport, approximate operators, and solver widths,
and bounds regret through the frozen sequential information gain. For squared
loss, a corrected prediction center supplies reference confidence along
predictable parameter paths without requiring nonlinear optimizer convergence,
while retaining explicit approximation-error charges. The construction also
has a simple limitation: its certified width cannot be smaller than the direct
reference width on the same history, and exact actionwise transport reproduces
that width. This comparison does not order the realized regret of policies
with different histories. We examine these distinctions in a 29-parameter
scaled-tanh model, for which a relative-weight comparison also supplies a
horizon-independent endpoint certificate. A prespecified follow-up evaluates
four policies across three conditions with 50 paired seeds per condition. The
Hessian-transport policy has higher mean pseudo-regret than every comparator
in every condition; all nine pointwise paired confidence intervals exclude
zero. Its regret bounds are vacuous and dominated by path inflation, while the
reference policy's own bounds are also vacuous. Floating-point confidence and
optimism diagnostics pass for all evaluated policies, including an uncertified
control. Exploratory log analyses distinguish conditional validity,
certificate usefulness, and observed policy behavior in this implementation.
\end{abstract}
