% --- abstract -------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{abstract}
Curvature surrogates in differentiable-model bandits change exploration widths,
but greater fidelity to full curvature need not improve online decisions.  We use
the full generalized Gauss--Newton (GGN)/Fisher operator as a reference, computing
its widths with curvature--vector products and conjugate gradients (CG) without
materializing a $d\times d$ matrix.  Rewards have conditionally sub-Gaussian
noise; the fitted curvature is the Gaussian squared-loss quasi-likelihood.  In
the small-drift exact-current regime, a sharpened singular-value argument transfers
the analysis to the monotone frozen-feature potential.  Splitting its spectrum at
rank $r$ yields a full-dimensional conditional regret bound in terms of a
rank-$r$ logarithmic trace term and the residual spectral mass $\Delta_{T,r}$,
without assuming an
exact tangent subspace.  Exact rank is a special case; a supplied subspace also
permits an $r$-dimensional implementation.  A more general transfer theorem
separates linearization, centering, feature and operator transfer, CG error, and
realized width complexity.  Predictable Welford and residual statistics instantiate
its nonlinear certificates with $O(d)$ additional path state.  These are
conditional differentiable-model results, not guarantees for unrestricted neural
training.  Experiments are reference-operator and failure-mode studies rather than
a performance demonstration, and they show no uniform fidelity-based regret
ordering in the tested regimes.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Contextual bandits are a standard framework for sequential
decision-making under partial feedback, and the reward predictor may be a
differentiable parametric model or a pretrained representation composed with a
reward head \citep{zhou2020neural,riquelme2018deep}.  The central algorithmic
question is how to build an exploration bonus that faithfully reflects
epistemic uncertainty over such a model's predictions.  We analyze the
Gaussian/squared-loss curvature model with sub-Gaussian noise and uniformly
Lipschitz parameter gradients on a known certified region.  The closed bounds
also require small feature drift and a controlled spectral tail or an exact-rank
special case; unrestricted neural training is not covered.

In the \emph{white-box} setting---gradient and curvature--vector-product
access to the model---the canonical bonus is the GGN/Fisher-linearized
predictive variance
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance-intro}
  s_t^2(a) \;=\; \bg_t(a)^\top \bC_t^{-1}\,\bg_t(a),
\end{equation}
where $\bg_t(a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$ is the
gradient feature (Jacobian of the mean reward) and $\bC_t$ is the
damped Gauss--Newton (GGN) curvature at the current
estimate~$\btheta_t$.  Exact full-history GGN/Fisher widths are costly at
large $d$ or $t$: dense materialization uses $O(d^2)$ state, while matrix-free
CG replays history at every iteration.  NeuralUCB retains the full neural-gradient
Gram in its theoretical algorithm but diagonalizes it in experiments
\citep{zhou2020neural}; neural-linear and scalable Laplace methods instead use
last-layer or structured curvature approximations
\citep{riquelme2018deep,ritter2018scalable,daxberger2021laplace}.
Outside online bandits, implicit Jacobian/Fisher products and CG have already
been used to compute full-network linearized predictive variances without
forming the Gram matrix \citep{maddox2021fast}.  Each curvature surrogate
changes the \emph{spectrum} of~$\bC_t$ and hence the value
of~$s_t^2(a)$.  The spectral change does not by itself imply a change
in the selected action, and therefore in regret; whether it does is an
empirical question, which we make measurable and study directly.

Current-parameter relinearization asks a different scientific question from a
historical-gradient Gram.  The latter preserves the features that were
predictable when data were collected and therefore supports a rank-one
self-normalized confidence process.  The former re-evaluates every historical
example under the representation used for the next decision, but destroys that
monotone update.  Our analysis keeps the predictable frozen Gram for confidence
and quantifies when its widths transfer to the current full-history GGN.  This
separation is why feature drift, rather than curvature fidelity alone, appears
explicitly in the regret bound.

\textbf{We use full curvature as a reference operator in a falsification study.}
Common-trajectory diagnostics isolate surrogate-induced width distortion, while
independently executed policies test whether those distortions survive adaptive
feedback.  We do not attribute a uniform fidelity--regret ordering claim to prior
work; instead we use it as a deliberately strong diagnostic null hypothesis.  A
controlled reversal rejects that hypothesis, whereas agreement does not establish
superiority.  Because executed policies follow different histories,
their regret contrasts are descriptive rather than causal operator effects.  We adapt the
established matrix-free predictive-variance primitive to online contextual
exploration: curvature--vector products (CVPs), available through automatic
differentiation, let us
solve $\bC_t\,\bu = \bg_t(a)$ by conjugate gradients (CG)
\emph{without forming}~$\bC_t$: only $O(d)$ \emph{additional curvature-state}
vectors (a constant number of CG vectors) are maintained and no $d\times d$
curvature matrix or per-sample gradient vector is stored---though exact
full-history curvature--vector products still require retaining or replaying the
historical raw examples to recompute their Jacobian products, and model
parameters, optimizer state, activations, replay data, and any frozen-feature
parameter checkpoints are not counted in this $O(d)$ curvature state.  Because
the damped GGN is symmetric positive definite, CG converges in exact arithmetic.
All CG convergence and residual-to-energy statements below use exact arithmetic
unless a verified rounding-error enclosure is supplied.  Building on this, we propose \textbf{Curvature-Calibrated
UCB (CC-UCB)}, a bandit algorithm whose exploration bonus is the
predictive variance under the \emph{uncompressed} GGN/Fisher metric,
accessed entirely through implicit CG solves.

\textbf{The analysis tracks predictable certificate requirements for each
approximation source and constructs them for the nonlinear path terms.}  The Gaussian/squared-loss theorem
(\Cref{thm:regret}) handles a general predictable SPD curvature sequence with a
one-sided transfer factor, a per-round CG factor, and a realized width complexity
$\Lamalg_T$.  Its exact determinant identity accounts for endpoint growth and
determinant-destroying refreshes but does not itself bound either term.  More
importantly, the previously external
nonlinear inputs are computable: Welford parameter moments and observed collection
residuals upper-bound feature transfer and centering, while an analytic Taylor
schedule supplies the confidence radius (\Cref{lem:path-certificates,cor:operational}).
These schedules are conservative and need known smoothness constants and a certified
optimizer residual.  In the exact-current small-drift regime, the sharpened
two-sided sandwich closes regret through the frozen potential, and a rank-$r$
logarithmic-trace plus spectral-tail decomposition closes its information gain
without a known subspace
(\Cref{cor:exact-current-frozen-potential,cor:approx-rank-current-ggn}).  A
supplied exact-rank path is a computational special case, while a persistently
excited growing window gives a separate conditional regret and width-solve
sample-CVP accounting bound
(\Cref{cor:rank-near-linear,thm:growing-window}).  None of these results certifies
unrestricted fine-tuning.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\textbf{(1)} A full-dimensional approximate-rank information-gain closure,
yielding a conditional regret bound for exact current GGN under predictable small
drift, using a sharpened frozen/current sandwich and a residual spectral-mass
term; exact rank is a special case
(\Cref{cor:exact-current-frozen-potential,lem:spectral-tail-logdet,cor:approx-rank-current-ggn}).
\textbf{(2)} A general realized-width regret reduction with one-sided
feature/operator transfer and explicit centering, linearization, and CG terms
(\Cref{thm:regret,lem:whitened}).
\textbf{(3)} Predictable $O(d)$ additional-state nonlinear transfer and
centering certificates (\Cref{lem:path-certificates,cor:operational}).
\textbf{(4)} A conditional projected-Gram shortcut for a supplied tangent
subspace and a separate growing-window bound with its burn-in and certificate
terms exposed (\Cref{cor:rank-near-linear,thm:growing-window}).
\textbf{(5)} Matrix-free full-GGN/Fisher widths through residual-checked CG/CVP
solves, together with executed nonlinear, operator-mechanism, baseline, and
systems audits that separate policy outcomes from post-hoc diagnostics
(\Cref{sec:experiments,prop:offdiag-witness}).

\section{Problem Setup}\label{sec:problem}

We present the problem setup for the \emph{Gaussian/squared-loss}
instantiation, which is the setting analyzed in \Cref{thm:regret}.
An extension to exponential-family likelihoods is discussed in
\Cref{app:expfam}.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
At each round $t=1,\dots,T$ the learner observes a context
$\bx_t\in\mathcal{X}$ and a finite action set~$\cA_t$.  It selects
$a_t\in\cA_t$ and observes a stochastic reward
\begin{equation}\label{eq:reward}
  r_t \;=\; \mu^*(\bx_t, a_t) + \eta_t, \qquad
  \eta_t \text{ is } \sigma\text{-sub-Gaussian},
\end{equation}
where $\mu^*:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{A}\to\R$ is the unknown mean reward.
The goal is to minimize cumulative regret
$R_T = \sum_{t=1}^{T}\bigl[\mu^*(\bx_t,a_t^*) - \mu^*(\bx_t,a_t)\bigr]$,
where $a_t^* = \argmax_{a\in\cA_t}\mu^*(\bx_t,a)$.
We assume $|\cA_t|\le A_{\max}$ deterministically for all~$t$.

% [filtration construction -> app:deferred]
\paragraph{Reward model and parameterization convention.}
We fix a pretrained initialization $\btheta_0\in\R^d$ and parameterize the model
by the \emph{displacement} $\btheta\in\R^d$ from it: the deployed predictor is
$\mu_{\btheta_0+\btheta}(\bx,a)$, and we abbreviate
$\mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a):=\mu_{\btheta_0+\btheta}(\bx,a)$ throughout.  The initial
displacement is $\btheta_1=\mathbf 0$ (the deployed model starts at the pretrained
weights $\btheta_0$).  With this convention the ridge penalty below is a penalty
on the \emph{displacement from the pretrained weights}, not a zero-centered prior
on the raw weights, and the realizability radius $S$ bounds
$\|\btheta^\star\|=\|\btheta^\star_{\mathrm{orig}}-\btheta_0\|$, the distance of
the true parameter from the pretrained initialization.
Define the regularized loss at round~$t$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:erm}
  \cL_t(\btheta)
  \;:=\;
    \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
      \bigl(r_s - \mu_{\btheta}(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)^2
    \;+\; \frac{\lambda}{2}\|\btheta\|^2 .
\end{equation}
We write $\btheta_t$ for the parameter returned by the warm-started
optimizer on~$\cL_t$.
Let $\mathcal D_{t-1}:=\{(\bx_s,a_s,r_s):s<t\}$ and
$N_t:=|\mathcal D_{t-1}|=t-1$.  Equation~\eqref{eq:erm} is a
\emph{full-history} objective.  The curvature replay set
$\mathcal S_t\subseteq\{1,\ldots,t-1\}$ and its size
$m_t:=|\mathcal S_t|$ affect $\Calg_t$, but do not replace
$\mathcal D_{t-1}$ in~\eqref{eq:erm}.  Thus a generic full-batch evaluation of
$\nabla\cL_t$ replays $N_t$ observations even when $m_t\ll N_t$.
In convex settings (e.g.\ a frozen backbone with a linear head), under exact
optimization this coincides with the global minimizer; in nonconvex settings $\btheta_t$
may be a local minimizer or approximate stationary point.
The gap between $\btheta_t$ and an idealized global minimizer is absorbed
by the action-scaled centering certificate (\Cref{asm:optim}).

\paragraph{Gradient features and curvature matrices.}
Define the \emph{gradient feature} (Jacobian of the mean reward) at the
current parameter estimate:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gfeat}
  \bg_t(\bx,a) \;:=\; \nabla_{\btheta}\,\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx,a)
  \;\in\;\R^d.
\end{equation}
The algorithm uses the \emph{relinearized curvature matrix}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature}
  \bC_t
  \;=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\,\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^{\!\top},
\end{equation}
the \emph{damped GGN curvature} for the Gaussian/squared-loss model
at the current parameter~$\btheta_t$.
(For the Gaussian working model underlying this squared-loss quasi-likelihood,
the GGN coincides with the model Fisher information; see the remark below.)
While $\bC_t$ is the natural object for
computing predictive variances at $\btheta_t$, it
re-evaluates all past Jacobians at~$\btheta_t$ and therefore does
\emph{not} admit a sequential rank-one update---all summands change
when $\btheta_t$ changes.

For the regret analysis we introduce the
\emph{frozen-feature Gram matrix}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:curvature-bar}
  \bCbar_t
  \;:=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)\,\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^{\!\top},
\end{equation}
where each $\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx_s,a_s)$
is evaluated at the parameter~$\btheta_s$ that was current when the sample
was collected.  This matrix satisfies the recursive update
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bar-update}
  \bCbar_{t+1}=\bCbar_t+\sigma^{-2}\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)^\top,
\end{equation}
and, critically, each realized design vector
$\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$ is measurable with respect to the pre-reward
sigma-algebra $\cG_s=\cHist_s\vee\sigma(a_s)$ of~\eqref{eq:filtration},
which is the filtration used in the self-normalized bound.
Both the additive update~\eqref{eq:bar-update} and
this measurability are structural requirements of the
self-normalized martingale bound and the elliptic potential lemma invoked
in~\Cref{sec:theory}.
The algorithm computes UCB widths using the algorithmic curvature~$\Calg_t$
(via CG; the exact case is $\Calg_t=\bC_t$), but the confidence analysis is
conducted in terms of~$\bCbar_t$; the one-sided optimism-transfer factor
of \Cref{asm:transfer}, certified by \Cref{lem:whitened}, provides the
bridge.

\section{Method: Curvature-Calibrated Exploration}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{CC-UCB Algorithm}

For a predictable SPD oracle $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$, set
$\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$ for the original nonlinear center.
\Cref{alg:ccucb} is the analyzed full-enumeration policy.  The same fixed
curvature operator is reused across separate per-action CG solves; a solution
for one action is not reused as the solution for another action.
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Curvature-Calibrated UCB (CC-UCB)}\label{alg:ccucb}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Observe context $\bx_t$ and finite action set $\cA_t$.
\STATE Construct a fixed predictable SPD oracle $\bv\mapsto\Calg_t\bv$ with $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$.
\FOR{each $a\in\cA_t$}
  \STATE Compute $\widehat\mu_t(a)=\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$ and $\bg_t(a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$.
  \STATE If $\bg_t(a)\ne\mathbf0$, solve $\Calg_t\bu=\bg_t(a)$ separately by CG until the certified tolerance holds; otherwise set $\tilde\bu=\mathbf0$. \label{line:cg}
  \STATE Set $\tilde s_t^2(a)=\bg_t(a)^\top\tilde\bu$.
\ENDFOR
\STATE Select and play $a_t\in\argmax_{a\in\cA_t}\{\widehat\mu_t(a)+\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}\sqrt{\tilde s_t^2(a)}\}$. \label{line:ucb}
\STATE Observe $r_t$, append $(\bx_t,a_t,r_t)$, and update $\btheta_{t+1}$.
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The oracle is accessed only through matrix--vector products.  At each round the
agent computes,
for every candidate action~$a$, the gradient feature
$\bg_t(a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$ and the
\emph{curvature-calibrated variance proxy}, the generic algorithmic width,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance}
  s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)
  \;=\;
  \bg_t(a)^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\,\bg_t(a).
\end{equation}
Under the GGN/Fisher-linearized Gaussian approximation with covariance
$\Calg_t^{-1}$, the quantity $s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$ is the metric-dependent
predictive variance of the linearized reward at $(\bx_t,a)$.
The \emph{exact} full-history instantiation of CC-UCB takes $\Calg_t=\bC_t$, the
relinearized curvature~\eqref{eq:curvature}; weighted replay, subsampling,
sliding windows, periodic refreshes, and randomized sketches are special
implementations of $\Calg_t$ (\S\ref{sec:curvop}), covered by the theory only
when the corresponding one-sided transfer factor is certified.

Because $\Calg_t\succ 0$ by construction, conjugate gradients (CG) is guaranteed
to converge on the system $\Calg_t\bu=\bg_t(a)$, provided the operator is held
\emph{fixed} across all iterations of the solve (the randomized sketch/subsample
is drawn into $\Omega_t$ before action selection; \S\ref{sec:problem}).  For each
action with $\bg_t(a)\ne\mathbf0$ we run CG to the per-action relative
energy-norm error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-err-eta}
  \varepsilon_{t,a}
  \;:=\;
  \frac{\|\bu_{t,a}-\tilde\bu_{t,a}\|_{\Calg_t}}{\|\bu_{t,a}\|_{\Calg_t}},
  \qquad
  \bu_{t,a}:=\Calg_t^{-1}\bg_t(a),
\end{equation}
yielding the computable proxy $\tilde{s}_t^2(a)=\bg_t(a)^\top\tilde\bu_{t,a}$;
if $\bg_t(a)=\mathbf0$ we set $\tilde{s}_t^2(a)=0$ and skip the solve (leaving the
residual ratio $\|r_I\|/\|\bg\|$ undefined), and by convention put
$\varepsilon_{t,a}:=0$ in this case, so that $\varepsilon_{t,a}$ is defined for
every $a\in\cA_t$ and~\eqref{eq:cg-quad} holds trivially there.
\Cref{lem:cg} shows $\tilde{s}_t^2(a)$ approximates $s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$ within a
multiplicative $(1\pm\varepsilon_{t,a})$ factor.  To ensure the UCB bonus
\emph{upper-bounds} the confidence width $\omega_t\,\bar s_t(a)$ despite the
approximation, the algorithm inflates the width by
$\sqrt{u_t(a)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$ (line~\ref{line:ucb}), where
$\bar\varepsilon_t\in(0,1)$ is a \emph{per-round} known tolerance enforced
uniformly over all actions,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-tol-uniform}
  \max_{a\in\cA_t}\varepsilon_{t,a}
  \;\le\;\bar\varepsilon_t\;<\;1,
\end{equation}
and $u_t(a)\ge1$ is the one-sided transfer factor (\Cref{asm:transfer}).

\section{Theoretical Analysis}\label{sec:theory}

We state a regret bound for CC-UCB (\Cref{alg:ccucb}, with full action
enumeration $K{=}|\cA_t|$) under the Gaussian/squared-loss setup of
\Cref{sec:problem} and
\Cref{asm:noise,asm:bounded,asm:linear,asm:optim,asm:transfer}.
The analysis does not constitute a full exponential-family or generalized
linear bandit proof; the extension in \Cref{app:expfam} is a
curvature-definition extension only.
The analysis handles a \emph{general predictable SPD algorithmic curvature
sequence} $\Calg_t$~\eqref{eq:calg-props}.  Confidence is proved for the
frozen-feature Gram $\bCbar_t$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:curvature-bar}), which has the
sequential-update and predictability properties required by the
self-normalized martingale bound; the \emph{one-sided} optimism-transfer
factor~$u_t(a)$ (\Cref{asm:transfer}) bridges from $\bCbar_t$ to $\Calg_t$
in the exploration direction only (no lower drift sandwich); the CG
approximation enters through the per-round factor $\alpha_t$; and the
nonmonotonicity of $\Calg_t$ is retained in the realized width complexity.
Appendix~\Cref{sec:dynamic-potential} gives an exact determinant bookkeeping
identity, not a primitive sublinear bound.

The theorem is a modular transfer result.  The path statistics below instantiate
its nonlinear inputs from pre-action quantities; smallness still requires a
restricted parameter path and is not claimed for unrestricted fine-tuning.

\paragraph{Core conditions and links.}
Assume conditionally $\sigma$-sub-Gaussian noise, $\|\bg_t(\bx_s,a)\|\le G$,
realizability by $\|\btheta^*\|\le S$, and a local expansion with round-$t$
remainder at most $\epslin(t)$.  For the original center assume the
action-scaled discrepancy is at most $\bar\psi_t\bar s_t(a)$.  The operator
$\Calg_t$ is predictable, SPD, satisfies $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ and
$\Calg_1=\lambda\bI$, and remains fixed throughout every CG solve.  Define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-widths}
\begin{aligned}
 \bar s_t^2(a)&=\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a),&
 s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)&=\bg_t(a)^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bg_t(a),\\
 \tilde s_t^2(a)&=\bg_t(a)^\top\tilde\bu_t(a).&&
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The analysis requires only the one-sided transfer and uniform CG relations
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-transfer-cg}
\begin{aligned}
 \bar s_t^2(a)&\le u_t(a)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a),\\
 (1-\bar\varepsilon_t)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)&\le\tilde s_t^2(a)
 \le(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
for every candidate action, where $u_t(a)\ge1$, $\bar\varepsilon_t<1$, and
$\alpha_t=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$.  Sufficient
feature-drift and approximate-operator constructions are given in
\Cref{lem:whitened,thm:spectral-distortion}; no lower spectral transfer is
needed.

\paragraph{Dynamic width complexity.}
The realized quantity used below is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-lambda}
 \Lamalg_T:=\sum_{t=1}^T\log\!\left(1+\sigma^{-2}s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\right).
\end{equation}
The bounded-width cap gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main-dynamic}
 \sum_{t=1}^T s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)
 \le(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\Lamalg_T.
\end{equation}
For a general predictable nonmonotone SPD sequence, we do not derive a
primitive sublinear bound on $\Lamalg_T$.  Appendix
\Cref{sec:dynamic-potential} gives an exact determinant bookkeeping identity,
but its upper bound is informative only when both the terminal log determinant
and determinant-decreasing refresh charge are independently controlled, for
example under the rank and spectral-floor hypotheses of
\Cref{lem:rank-refresh}.

\subsection{Regret Bound}

Let $\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}$ denote the simultaneous self-normalized confidence
event and let $\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$ collect the predictable linearization,
centering, transfer, condition-number, CG, and randomized-operator certificates.
The appendix defines each component and its failure allocation; write their
total failure probability as $\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.

\begin{theorem}[Dynamic transfer regret bound for CC-UCB (Gaussian/squared-loss)]\label{thm:regret}
Under the conditions above and \Cref{asm:noise,asm:bounded,asm:linear,asm:optim,asm:transfer},
suppose $|\cA_t|\le A_{\max}$, the optimal action is present, and
\Cref{alg:ccucb} enumerates all actions using a predictable schedule
$\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$ and uniform per-round CG tolerance.  Let
$E_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\epslin(t)$, $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$,
$\alpha_t=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$, and
$S_T=\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t^2u_t(a_t)\omega_t^2$.  On
$\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}\cap\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret-bound}
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sqrt{(\sigma^2 + G^2/\lambda)\;\Lamalg_T\;S_T}
  \;+\; 2\,E_T,
\end{equation}
with probability at least $1-\delta-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.  Deterministic or
almost-sure certificates contribute zero to $\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.  For the
corrected center, the same statement holds without \Cref{asm:optim} after
replacing $\omega_t$ by $\bar\beta_t$.
\end{theorem}

In particular, the bound is sublinear whenever
$\Lamalg_T S_T=o(T^2)$ and $E_T=o(T)$.
The exact-curvature instantiation is $\Calg_t=\bC_t$ with
$u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ from \Cref{lem:whitened}; approximate operators are
covered through \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}.  The proof is in
\Cref{app:proof}.

\paragraph{Proof sketch.}
Self-normalized confidence plus the centering and linearization terms gives
$|\mu^*(a)-\mu_{\btheta_t}(a)|\le\omega_t\bar s_t(a)+\epslin(t)$.
The one-sided relation and CG lower bound make the computed bonus optimistic,
so instantaneous regret is at most $2w_t(a_t)+2\epslin(t)$.  The CG upper
bound gives $w_t(a_t)\le\alpha_t\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a_t)}s_{\Calg,t}(a_t)$.
Summing, applying Cauchy--Schwarz only once, and using \eqref{eq:main-dynamic}
yields \eqref{eq:regret-bound}; hence time-varying factors remain inside $S_T$
rather than being replaced by horizon maxima.  The corrected center replaces
$\omega_t$ by $\bar\beta_t$ and removes the centering certificate, but requires
exact or certified frozen-feature access (\Cref{cor:corrected-center}).

\begin{corollary}[Exact-current regret through the frozen potential]
\label{cor:exact-current-frozen-potential}
Adopt \Cref{thm:regret} with exact current curvature $\Calg_t=\bC_t$.
Suppose a predictable certificate satisfies
$\chi_t\le\bar\chi_t<1$ for every $t$, and use the valid transfer factor
$u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$.  Then, on the event of \Cref{thm:regret},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:exact-current-frozen-potential}
\begin{aligned}
 R_T&\le 2\Biggl\{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\gamma_T\\[-1mm]
 &\qquad\quad\times\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t^2\omega_t^2
 \left(\frac{1+\bar\chi_t}{1-\bar\chi_t}\right)^2\Biggr\}^{1/2}
 +2E_T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For the corrected center of \Cref{cor:corrected-center}, the same statement
holds without \Cref{asm:optim} after replacing $\omega_t$ by $\bar\beta_t$.
This removes the nonmonotone dynamic-width term only in the small-drift,
exact-current regime.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The sharpened sandwich in \Cref{cor:drift-sandwich} and Loewner inversion give
\[
 s_t(a)\le\frac{\bar s_t(a)}{1-\bar\chi_t}.
\]
The per-round proof of \Cref{thm:regret}, together with
$\sqrt{u_t}=1+\bar\chi_t$, therefore gives
\[
 \operatorname{regret}_t\le
 2\alpha_t\omega_t
 \frac{1+\bar\chi_t}{1-\bar\chi_t}\bar s_t(a_t)
 +2\epslin(t).
\]
Sum and apply Cauchy--Schwarz once.  The frozen rank-one update and the usual
elliptic-potential inequality give
$\sum_t\bar s_t^2(a_t)\le
(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\gamma_T$, proving the display.  The corrected-center
claim follows from the prediction identity in \Cref{cor:corrected-center}.
\end{proof}

For an integer $0\le r\le d$, define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-tail-main}
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta_{T,r}&:=\sum_{i>r}\nu_i,\qquad
 r_T:=\min\{r,T\},\\
 \Gamma^{\rm tail}_{T,r}
 &:=r_T\log\!\left(1+\frac{TG^2}
 {r_T\lambda\sigma^2}\right)+\frac{\Delta_{T,r}}{\lambda},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\nu_1\ge\cdots\ge\nu_d\ge0$ are the eigenvalues of
$\bCbar_{T+1}-\lambda\bI$ and the first term is defined to be zero when
$r_T=0$.  \Cref{lem:spectral-tail-logdet} proves
$\gamma_T\le\Gamma^{\rm tail}_{T,r}$.
Writing $\tau_T=\sum_i\nu_i$ and
$q_{T,r}=\min\{d-r,\max\{T-r,0\}\}$, the same lemma proves the sharper
realized split bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gamma-split-main}
 \Gamma^{\rm split}_{T,r}:=
 r_T\log\!\left(1+\frac{\tau_T-\Delta_{T,r}}{r_T\lambda}\right)
 +q_{T,r}\log\!\left(1+\frac{\Delta_{T,r}}{q_{T,r}\lambda}\right),
\end{equation}
where a term with zero multiplier is defined as zero.

\begin{corollary}[Approximate-rank exact-current GGN]
\label{cor:approx-rank-current-ggn}
Under \Cref{cor:exact-current-frozen-potential}, on the same confidence event
the following holds simultaneously for every $r\in\{0,\ldots,d\}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-rank-current-ggn}
\begin{aligned}
 R_T&\le 2\Biggl\{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,
 \Gamma^{\rm tail}_{T,r}\\[-1mm]
 &\qquad\quad\times\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t^2\omega_t^2
 \left(\frac{1+\bar\chi_t}{1-\bar\chi_t}\right)^2\Biggr\}^{1/2}
 +2E_T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The same display holds with $\Gamma^{\rm tail}_{T,r}$ replaced by the
smaller $\Gamma^{\rm split}_{T,r}$ from~\eqref{eq:gamma-split-main}.
This is a realized-complexity statement: the policy may retain the existing
observable $\hat\gamma_{t-1}$ schedule, but a terminal realized
$\Delta_{T,r}$ is not used in any action-time radius.  Because the deterministic
spectral inequality holds for every $r$, the terminal report may minimize
$\Gamma^{\rm tail}_{T,r}$ over $r$ a posteriori, provided that choice does not
alter any past action, radius, or other action-time quantity.

Operationally, if before round $t$ a supplied deterministic or
$\cHist_t$-measurable envelope $\bar\Delta_{t-1,r}$ upper-bounds the spectral
tail of $\bCbar_t-\lambda\bI$, the old predictable envelope below remains
valid:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 r_{t-1}&:=\min\{r,t-1\},\\
 \bar\Gamma^{\rm tail}_{t-1,r}
 &:=r_{t-1}\log\!\left(1+\frac{(t-1)G^2}
 {r_{t-1}\lambda\sigma^2}\right)
 +\frac{\bar\Delta_{t-1,r}}\lambda.
\end{aligned}
\]
again taking the first term as zero when $r_{t-1}=0$.
Replacing $\gamma_{t-1}$ in the constructive confidence radius by this
predictable upper bound is valid.  In that version,
\eqref{eq:approx-rank-current-ggn} holds with
$\Gamma^{\rm tail}_{T,r}$ replaced by any supplied terminal envelope
$\bar\Gamma^{\rm tail}_{T,r}$ and with $\omega_t$ formed from the resulting
radius.

A refined predictable alternative uses the observable frozen trace
$\tau_{t-1}=\operatorname{tr}(\bCbar_t-\lambda\bI)
=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\|\bg_s\|^2$.  With
$q_{t-1,r}=\min\{d-r,\max\{t-1-r,0\}\}$, set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:predictable-split-envelope}
 \bar\Gamma^{\rm split}_{t-1,r}:=
 r_{t-1}\log\!\left(1+\frac{\tau_{t-1}}{r_{t-1}\lambda}\right)
 +q_{t-1,r}\log\!\left(1+
 \frac{\bar\Delta_{t-1,r}}{q_{t-1,r}\lambda}\right),
\end{equation}
again omitting zero-multiplier terms.  The head term deliberately uses the
full trace: subtracting an \emph{upper} tail envelope could reduce the head
term and invalidate an upper bound.  Thus
$\bar\Gamma^{\rm split}_{t-1,r}\ge\gamma_{t-1}$ is a valid action-time
schedule, although it can be looser than the realized split expression.

No exact tangent subspace is assumed.  A small spectral tail can contain
decision-relevant directions and does not supply their basis, so
full-dimensional CG may still be necessary.  Exact rank is recovered when
$\Delta_{T,r}=0$; the supplied-subspace result
\Cref{cor:rank-near-linear} is then an additional computational special case.
The corrected-center version again replaces $\omega_t$ by $\bar\beta_t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Apply \Cref{lem:spectral-tail-logdet} to substitute either
$\gamma_T\le\Gamma^{\rm split}_{T,r}\le\Gamma^{\rm tail}_{T,r}$ in
\eqref{eq:exact-current-frozen-potential}.  The prefix version of the same
lemma makes either $\bar\Gamma^{\rm tail}_{t-1,r}$ or
$\bar\Gamma^{\rm split}_{t-1,r}$ a predictable upper bound on
$\gamma_{t-1}$, so \Cref{lem:confidence,cor:beta-constructive} justify the
operational radius.  No eigenspace is used by either argument.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Gap-dependent exact-current regret]
\label{cor:gap-dependent-exact-current}
Adopt \Cref{cor:exact-current-frozen-potential}.  Define the realized gap
$\Delta_t:=\mu^*(\bx_t,a_t^*)-\mu^*(\bx_t,a_t)$ and suppose every
suboptimal candidate action at every round has gap at least a deterministic
(or almost-sure) $\Delta_{\min}>0$.  This gap is an analysis condition and is
not used to tune the policy; if it holds only on a high-probability event, that
event must be added to the certificate failure allocation.  Suppose also
$\epslin(t)\le\Delta_{\min}/4$ for all $t$, and set
\[
 H_t^{\rm gap}:=\alpha_t\omega_t
 \frac{1+\bar\chi_t}{1-\bar\chi_t},\qquad
 H_{\max}^{\rm gap}:=\max_{t\le T}H_t^{\rm gap}.
\]
Then, on the event of \Cref{thm:regret},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gap-dependent-exact-current}
 R_T\le\frac{16(H_{\max}^{\rm gap})^2}{\Delta_{\min}}
 \left(\sigma^2+\frac{G^2}{\lambda}\right)\gamma_T.
\end{equation}
Consequently $\gamma_T$ in this display may be replaced by the exact-rank
bound $\Gamma_{T,r}$ of~\eqref{eq:rank-gamma-main}, the simple tail bound
$\Gamma^{\rm tail}_{T,r}$ of~\eqref{eq:gamma-tail-main}, or the refined
$\Gamma^{\rm split}_{T,r}$ of~\eqref{eq:gamma-split-main}, under the
corresponding premises.  The corrected-center version replaces $\omega_t$ by
$\bar\beta_t$ as usual.  This result is not a minimax-optimality claim: unlike
the gap-free result, it requires a uniform positive gap and a per-round
linearization error smaller than one quarter of that gap.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The per-round argument in
\Cref{cor:exact-current-frozen-potential} gives
\[
 \Delta_t\le2H_t^{\rm gap}\bar s_t(a_t)+2\epslin(t).
\]
On a suboptimal round, $\Delta_t\ge\Delta_{\min}$ and hence
$2\epslin(t)\le\Delta_t/2$.  Rearranging and using
$H_t^{\rm gap}\le H_{\max}^{\rm gap}$ gives
$\Delta_t\le4H_{\max}^{\rm gap}\bar s_t(a_t)$.  Squaring this inequality and using
$\Delta_t\ge\Delta_{\min}$ yields
\[
 \Delta_t\le\frac{\Delta_t^2}{\Delta_{\min}}
 \le\frac{16(H_{\max}^{\rm gap})^2}{\Delta_{\min}}\bar s_t^2(a_t).
\]
Optimal rounds contribute zero.  Sum over $t$ and apply
$\sum_t\bar s_t^2(a_t)\le
(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\gamma_T$ to prove the display; the three substitutions
follow from \Cref{lem:rank-logdet,lem:spectral-tail-logdet}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional growing-window bound under fixed-subspace excitation]\label{thm:growing-window}
Adopt the hypotheses, events, and valid predictable schedules of
\Cref{thm:regret}, let $m_t=\min\{t-1,\lceil t^q\rceil\}$ for $q\in(0,1]$,
and let $\Calg_t$ be the unrescaled current-parameter Gram over the last $m_t$
observations.  Thus \Cref{prop:window} gives $\kappa_{+,t}=1$ and
$u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$.  Suppose candidate gradients lie in a fixed subspace
$U$, and let $P_U$ be its orthogonal projector.  For a supplied burn-in index
$\tau_w\in\{1,\ldots,T+1\}$, assume the
predictable window-excitation condition
\[
 \Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI+\kappa_wm_tP_U
\]
holds for a known $\kappa_w>0$ and every $t\ge\tau_w$.  Then, on the same
event of probability at least $1-\delta-\delta_{\rm cert}$ as
\Cref{thm:regret},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growing-window-lambda-main}
\begin{aligned}
 \Lamalg_T&\le L_q(T;\tau_w),\\
 L_q(T;\tau_w)&:=(\tau_w-1)\log\!\left(1+
 \frac{G^2}{\lambda\sigma^2}\right)\\
 &\quad+\frac{G^2}{\sigma^2}\sum_{t=\tau_w}^T
 \frac1{\lambda+\kappa_wm_t},\\
 L_q(T;\tau_w)&=\begin{cases}O(\tau_w+T^{1-q}),&q<1,\\
 O(\tau_w+\log T),&q=1.
 \end{cases}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
For $\tau_w=1$, more explicitly,
$L_q(T;1)\le(G^2/\sigma^2)[\lambda^{-1}+\kappa_w^{-1}h_q(T)]$,
where, for $T\ge2$, $h_q(T)=1+((T-1)^{1-q}-1)/(1-q)$ for $q<1$ and
$h_1(T)=1+\log(T-1)$, while $h_q(1):=0$ in both cases.
Writing $r_U=\dim U$, the excitation premise can first hold nontrivially only
once $m_t\ge r_U$, because $\operatorname{rank}(\Calg_t-\lambda I)\le m_t$;
thus $\tau_w$ includes the required rank-$r_U$ burn-in.
If $H_T=\max_{t\le T}\alpha_t\sqrt{u_t(a_t)}\,\omega_t$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growing-window-regret-main}
 R_T\le2H_T\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)T L_q(T;\tau_w)}+2E_T.
\end{equation}
If $H_T=\widetilde O(1)$, $E_T=\widetilde O(T^{1-q/2})$, and the burn-in
obeys $\tau_w=\widetilde O(T^{1-q})$ for $q<1$ (or
$\tau_w=\widetilde O(1)$ for $q=1$), then
\eqref{eq:growing-window-regret-main} simplifies to
$R_T=\widetilde O(T^{1-q/2})$; otherwise its displayed $L_q$ and $E_T$ terms
remain.  This is conditional on the fixed subspace, excitation, and all
confidence, transfer, optimizer, and CG certificates above.
Certified worst-case \emph{width-solve} work is
$O(KT^{1+3q/2}\log(1/\bar\varepsilon))$ sample-CVPs when a fixed uniform
target $\bar\varepsilon\in(0,1)$ is enforced; this count excludes model
optimization, candidate-gradient computation, and certificate construction.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Before $\tau_w$, $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ bounds each logarithm in
$\Lamalg_T$ by $\log(1+G^2/(\lambda\sigma^2))$.  Thereafter, for
$g_t(a)\in U$, inversion of the excitation inequality gives
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)\le G^2/(\lambda+\kappa_wm_t)$; summing
$\log(1+x)\le x$ proves \eqref{eq:growing-window-lambda-main}.  Since
$S_T\le TH_T^2$, substitution in \Cref{thm:regret} proves
\eqref{eq:growing-window-regret-main}.  The condition-number bound
$\kappa(\Calg_t)\le1+m_tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ makes CG use
$O(\sqrt{m_t}\log(1/\bar\varepsilon))$ iterations, each replaying $m_t$
samples; summing $Km_t^{3/2}$ proves the cost claim.
\end{proof}
Under the hypotheses of \Cref{cor:rank-near-linear}, if the window policy uses
the predictable rank envelope $\Gamma_{t-1,r}$ instead of the realized
$\hat\gamma_{t-1}$ in its confidence radius, then
$H_T\le\alpha_I(1+x_T)\Omega^{\rm win}_{T,r}$, where
$\Omega^{\rm win}_{T,r}$ is the explicit $\Omega_{T,r}$ in
\Cref{cor:rank-near-linear} with $A_{\rm inf}=1$.  Hence
\eqref{eq:growing-window-regret-main} contains no uncontrolled information-gain
placeholder.  The excitation premise is restrictive and standard in persistently excited
identification; contextual diversity alone does not imply it.  For $\dim U=r>1$
one must choose $\tau_w$ after a rank-$r$ burn-in, as the formal early charge
records.  The predictable rank schedule in
\Cref{cor:rank-near-linear} makes $H_T$ polylogarithmic under the same bounded-path
conditions.  Illustrative achieved upper-bound exponents are reported in
\Cref{sec:systems}; they are not a Pareto frontier or a lower
bound.  When $U$ is supplied, the projected Gram in
\eqref{eq:projected-width} can instead be solved directly.
}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

CC-UCB supplies a matrix-free full-GGN/model-Fisher reference with explicit transfer,
CG, and width terms.  The strongest full-dimensional result combines predictable
small feature drift for exact current curvature with a rank-$r$ logarithmic-trace
plus spectral-tail bound on the frozen
potential; it does not require a known tangent subspace.  Exact rank recovers a
projected-Gram special case where full-dimensional CG is unnecessary.  Neither
result is an unrestricted neural-training guarantee.  Empirically, the executed
studies test width and action-order preservation rather than demonstrate policy
superiority: the tanh bound is vacuous, the legacy scaling grid exposes failed
envelopes, coverage and bonus matching still produce mixed operator comparisons,
the real-autodiff study measures isolated primitives rather than a training loop,
full curvature is not uniformly preferred, balanced MNIST remains at chance for
all local methods, and Covertype fails its baseline check.
The explicit corrected-center rate additionally requires a nondecreasing
predictable prefix information envelope and frozen-feature access.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Theory assumes Gaussian squared loss, enumeration, realizability, smoothness,
and, for the original center, an optimizer certificate; approximate operators
and screening need further certificates.  Controlling feature drift and the
spectral tail during unrestricted training remains open.  Float64 checks lack
enclosures, bounds are vacuous, the measured autodiff models stop at roughly
$10^7$ parameters on one accelerator, and adaptive comparisons are non-causal.

\section{Deferred main-text derivations}\label{app:deferred}
This appendix collects the formal statements, proofs, and derivations deferred
from the compact main text; all cross-referenced results are stated here.

For the sequential frozen-feature Gram, the usual information gain is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:infogain}
\gamma_T:=\log\frac{\det(\bCbar_{T+1})}{\det(\lambda\bI)}
=\sum_{t=1}^T\log\!\left(1+\sigma^{-2}\bar s_t^2(a_t)\right).
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Rank and statistical-dimension information closure]\label{lem:rank-logdet}
For $A\succeq0$, $r\ge\operatorname{rank}(A)$, and
$q_A=\|A\|_{\rm op}/\lambda$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-logdet}
\log\det(\bI+A/\lambda)\le
\min\!\left\{r\log\!\left(1+\frac{\operatorname{tr}A}{r\lambda}\right),
 k(q_A)d_{{\rm eff},\lambda}(A)\right\},
\end{equation}
where $d_{{\rm eff},\lambda}(A)=\operatorname{tr}[A(A+\lambda\bI)^{-1}]$,
$k(0)=1$, and $k(q)=(1+q)\log(1+q)/q$ for $q>0$; the rank term is defined
as zero when $r=0$ (which forces $A=0$).  Consequently, if
$\operatorname{rank}(\bCbar_{T+1}-\lambda\bI)\le r$, then
$\gamma_T\le\Gamma_{T,r}$ from \eqref{eq:rank-gamma-main}.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Writing $x_i=\lambda_i(A)/\lambda$, concavity of $\log(1+x)$ after padding
the spectrum with zeros gives the rank--trace term.  Also
\[
 \log(1+x)=\frac{(1+x)\log(1+x)}x\frac{x}{1+x}.
\]
The first factor is increasing because its derivative is
$(x-\log(1+x))/x^2\ge0$; bounding it at $q_A$ and summing proves the
statistical-dimension term.  For the frozen Gram,
$\operatorname{tr}(\bCbar_{T+1}-\lambda\bI)\le TG^2/\sigma^2$, which gives
\eqref{eq:rank-gamma-main} (with $r_T=\min\{r,T\}$).
\end{proof}
The second term is useful only with a predictable or deterministic envelope on
$d_{{\rm eff},\lambda}$; a realized value is post-hoc.  It is not the empirical
trace effective rank $\operatorname{tr}(A)/\|A\|_{\rm op}$, which does not in
general control log determinant.

\begin{lemma}[Spectral-tail information closure]\label{lem:spectral-tail-logdet}
Let
\[
 A_T:=\bCbar_{T+1}-\lambda\bI
 =\sigma^{-2}\sum_{t=1}^T\bg_t\bg_t^\top\succeq0
\]
have eigenvalues $\nu_1\ge\cdots\ge\nu_d\ge0$.  For an integer
$r\in\{0,\ldots,d\}$, let
$\Delta_{T,r}:=\sum_{i>r}\nu_i$, $\tau_T:=\operatorname{tr}(A_T)$,
$r_T:=\min\{r,T\}$, and
$q_{T,r}:=\min\{d-r,\max\{T-r,0\}\}$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-tail-split}
\begin{aligned}
 \gamma_T
 &\le r_T\log\!\left(1+\frac{\tau_T-\Delta_{T,r}}
 {r_T\lambda}\right)
 +q_{T,r}\log\!\left(1+\frac{\Delta_{T,r}}
 {q_{T,r}\lambda}\right)\\
 &=:\Gamma^{\rm split}_{T,r},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where either term is defined as zero when its multiplier is zero.  Moreover,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:spectral-tail-logdet}
 \Gamma^{\rm split}_{T,r}\le
 r_T\log\!\left(1+\frac{TG^2}{r_T\lambda\sigma^2}\right)
 +\frac{\Delta_{T,r}}\lambda,
\end{equation}
where the first term is defined as zero when $r_T=0$.  Thus $r=0$ gives
$\gamma_T\le\operatorname{tr}(A_T)/\lambda$, while
$r\ge\operatorname{rank}(A_T)$ gives $\Delta_{T,r}=0$ and recovers the
rank closure.  The statement also covers $T=0$, when both sides vanish.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Because $A_T$ is a sum of $T$ rank-one matrices,
$\operatorname{rank}(A_T)\le T$, and \Cref{asm:bounded} gives
$\operatorname{tr}(A_T)\le TG^2/\sigma^2$.  If $r_T>0$, concavity of
$x\mapsto\log(1+x/\lambda)$ on the leading eigenvalues gives
\[
 \sum_{i=1}^{r}\log(1+\nu_i/\lambda)
 =\sum_{i=1}^{r_T}\log(1+\nu_i/\lambda)
 \le r_T\log\!\left(1+
 \frac{\tau_T-\Delta_{T,r}}{r_T\lambda}\right).
\]
The equality uses zero eigenvalues when $r>T$.  For the tail,
at most $q_{T,r}$ eigenvalues can be nonzero: there are at most $d-r$ tail
coordinates and at most $T-r$ nonzero eigenvalues after the first $r$.
Concavity, with zero padding when necessary, therefore gives
\[
 \sum_{i>r}\log(1+\nu_i/\lambda)
 \le q_{T,r}\log\!\left(1+
 \frac{\Delta_{T,r}}{q_{T,r}\lambda}\right).
\]
If $q_{T,r}=0$, the rank bound forces $\Delta_{T,r}=0$ and the tail sum is
empty.  This proves~\eqref{eq:spectral-tail-split}; the $r_T=0$ case similarly
uses only the tail term.

For the second inequality, use
$\tau_T-\Delta_{T,r}\le\tau_T\le TG^2/\sigma^2$ in the head term and
$q\log(1+x/q)\le x$ with $x=\Delta_{T,r}/\lambda$ in the tail term.
This proves~\eqref{eq:spectral-tail-logdet} without evaluating a zero
denominator.  The stated edge cases follow immediately.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Tightness of the split spectral-tail expression]
\label{prop:spectral-tail-tightness}
Fix integers $r,q\ge1$, masses $H,\Delta\ge0$, ambient dimension
$d=r+q$, and $T\ge d$.  Let a PSD
$d\times d$ matrix have $r$ head eigenvalues equal to $H/r$, $q$ tail eigenvalues equal to
$\Delta/q$, and all remaining eigenvalues zero, with the head values ordered
no smaller than the tail values.  Splitting after $r$ makes
\eqref{eq:spectral-tail-split} an equality:
\[
 \log\det(\bI_d+A/\lambda)
 =r\log\!\left(1+\frac{H}{r\lambda}\right)
 +q\log\!\left(1+\frac{\Delta}{q\lambda}\right).
\]
If $\Delta/(q\lambda)\le1$, then the tail contribution obeys
\[
 q\log\!\left(1+\frac{\Delta}{q\lambda}\right)
 \ge\frac{\Delta}{2\lambda}.
\]
Thus dependence linear in the tail mass cannot in general be removed without
additional information about the tail rank or spectrum.  This is a tightness
statement for the information inequality, not a regret lower bound for the
nonlinear class.  The example is algebraic; to embed it in the bounded-feature
bandit class one additionally chooses factors satisfying
$H+\Delta\le TG^2/\sigma^2$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
The equality follows by substituting the two constant spectral blocks.  For
$x\in[0,1]$, $\log(1+x)\ge x/2$; apply this with
$x=\Delta/(q\lambda)$ and multiply by $q$.
\end{proof}

A deterministic or history-measurable prefix envelope
$\bar\Delta_{t,r}\ge\Delta_{t,r}$ may enter an online confidence schedule
through the prefix form of~\eqref{eq:spectral-tail-logdet}.  A second valid
schedule is~\eqref{eq:predictable-split-envelope}, which uses the observable
full trace for the head and the tail envelope only for the tail.  It does not
subtract an upper tail envelope from the trace.  In contrast, a
realized terminal $\Delta_{T,r}$ is an a posteriori path-complexity bound only.
Since the inequality is pathwise and simultaneous over the finite set of ranks,
the terminal report may minimize over $r$.  Neither the terminal tail nor that
post-hoc rank may choose an eigenspace, a radius, an action, or any other
action-time quantity.  If valid predictable envelopes are available for several
ranks, their pointwise minimum is itself a valid predictable information bound.
The existing observable $\hat\gamma_t$ schedule remains valid when only realized
terminal tails are reported.

The dynamic-potential and CG-observable consequences of \Cref{thm:regret} are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret-bound-dyn}
\begin{aligned}
R_T&\le2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\Gamdyn_T S_T}+2E_T,\\
R_T&\le2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\widehat{\Lamalg}_T S_T}+2E_T.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\begin{corollary}[Corrected-center variant, no centering certificate]\label{cor:corrected-center}
Replace the predictive center in \Cref{alg:ccucb} by
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-center}
\widehat\mu_t^{\mathrm{corr}}(a):=\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)
+\bg_t(a)^\top(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}-\btheta_t)
\end{equation}
and use $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$.  Under the hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:regret} except \Cref{asm:optim}, the bounds
\eqref{eq:regret-bound}--\eqref{eq:regret-bound-dyn} hold.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Local linearization gives
$\mu^*(\bx_t,a)-\widehat\mu_t^{\mathrm{corr}}(a)
=\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta^*-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})+\rho_t$.
\Cref{lem:confidence} bounds the first term by
$\beta_t\bar s_t(a)$ and $|\rho_t|\le\epslin(t)$, which is the first step
of the regret proof with $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$; all later steps are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Cost of the corrected center]
Exact evaluation requires a solve against $\bCbar_t$.  A constant number of
$O(d)$ CG vectors is insufficient by itself: one additionally needs stored
historical frozen gradients, raw replay data with the historical parameter
checkpoints needed to reconstruct them, a frozen-feature matrix--vector oracle,
an explicit low-dimensional representation, or a certified sketch.  Maintaining
$\bCbar_t$ explicitly costs $O(d^2)$ memory.  Approximate frozen-feature access
introduces an error that must be returned to the centering certificate unless
separately controlled.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Assumptions}\label{sec:assumptions}

\begin{assumption}[Sub-Gaussian noise]\label{asm:noise}
The noise $\eta_t$ is conditionally $\sigma$-sub-Gaussian given the
context and action:
$\mathbb{E}[\exp(s\eta_t)\mid\cG_t]\le\exp(s^2\sigma^2/2)$
for all~$s\in\R$, where
$\cG_t := \cHist_t\vee\sigma(a_t)$ is the pre-reward sigma-algebra
of~\eqref{eq:filtration}, available immediately after the action is
selected and before the reward is revealed.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Bounded gradient features]\label{asm:bounded}
For a constant $G>0$,
\[
  \sup_{t\le T}\;\sup_{s\le t}\;\sup_{a}
  \|\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a)\| \;\le\; G.
\]
This bounds both the current-round features
$\bg_t(\bx_t,a)$ and the replayed features
$\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)$ appearing in the curvature
matrix~\eqref{eq:curvature}, as well as the frozen features
$\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$ in~\eqref{eq:curvature-bar}.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Local linearization]\label{asm:linear}
There is a known convex parameter region $\Theta$ containing
$\btheta^*$ and every realized $\btheta_t$, on which
$\nabla_\theta\mu_\theta(\bx,a)$ is $L_\mu$-Lipschitz uniformly over
candidate $(\bx,a)$.  Thus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:linearization}
  \mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a) \;=\;
    \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx,a)
    + \bg_t(\bx,a)^\top(\btheta - \btheta_t)
    + \rho_t(\bx,a,\btheta),
\end{equation}
where the remainder satisfies
$|\rho_t(\bx,a,\btheta)| \le \tfrac{L_\mu}{2}\|\btheta-\btheta_t\|^2$
for $\btheta\in\Theta$.
Evaluating at $\btheta=\btheta^*$ gives a time-varying linearization error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:epslin-t}
  \epslin(t)
  \;:=\;
  \sup_{a\in\cA_t}|\rho_t(\bx_t,a,\btheta^*)|
  \;\le\;
  \frac{L_\mu}{2}\|\btheta^* - \btheta_t\|^2.
\end{equation}
For \Cref{thm:regret} to give a sublinear guarantee, we require the cumulative linearization
error $E_T:=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\epslin(t) = o(T)$; see \Cref{thm:regret}
for the precise dependence.  Concretely, $E_T=O(1)$ when
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(1/t)$ (since
$\epslin(t)=O(1/t^2)$ and $\sum t^{-2}<\infty$;
achievable in convex or strongly convex regimes),
and $E_T=O(\sqrt{T})$ when
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(t^{-1/4})$.
In practice this is enforced by restricting fine-tuning depth,
	using trust-region updates, or freezing the backbone (which renders the
	last-layer model convex or near-linear with small~$L_\mu$).
These rate examples are illustrative only.  The present paper does not
prove convergence of the nonconvex optimization path
$\{\btheta_t\}$ to~$\btheta^*$; a sublinear guarantee from this bound therefore
requires this term to be controlled by an external stability or convergence
argument.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Action-scaled centering certificate]\label{asm:optim}
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred} below proves the geometric bound
$\bigl|\bg_t(\bx_t,a)^\top(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})\bigr|
\le\bar s_t(a)\,\psi_t$ for every $a\in\cA_t$, where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:psi-def}
  \psi_t \;:=\; \frac{\zeta_t}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
  \;+\; \|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}},
\end{equation}
$\zeta_t\ge0$ is an $\cHist_t$-measurable optimizer gradient-residual bound
($\|\nabla\cL_t(\btheta_t)\|_2\le\zeta_t$) and $M_t$ is the gradient
mismatch vector defined there.
We assume there is an $\cHist_t$-measurable upper bound
$\bar\psi_t\ge\psi_t$ (the \emph{centering certificate}) such that,
for every action $a\in\cA_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pred-stab}
  \bigl|\bg_t(\bx_t,a)^\top
    \bigl(\btheta_t - \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}\bigr)\bigr|
  \;\le\;
  \bar\psi_t\,\bar s_t(a).
\end{equation}
The discrepancy is thus \emph{action-scaled}: it multiplies the
confidence width $\bar s_t(a)$ rather than entering additively.  The
certificate absorbs the same three sources as before---optimizer residual
$\zeta_t$, replayed-Jacobian drift inside $M_t$, and higher-order nonlinear
terms---but now inside a single per-round scalar $\bar\psi_t$.
In the convex-in-parameters regime (frozen backbone with a linear reward
head), under exact optimization $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}=\btheta_t$ and
$\bar\psi_t=0$.
The corrected-center variant of \Cref{cor:corrected-center} removes
$\bar\psi_t$ entirely, at the cost of an explicit frozen-feature solve.
\end{assumption}

\begin{remark}[Status of Assumption~\ref{asm:optim}]
\Cref{lem:path-certificates} supplies a predictable valid $\bar\psi_t$ from
the optimizer residual, Welford path moments, and observed residual energy.
We do not prove that this conservative quantity is small outside a restricted
path.  It enters the exploration term
through $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$
(\Cref{eq:omega-def}), not as an additive $O(T)$ term.
\end{remark}

The next lemma gives the primitive geometric bound underlying
\Cref{asm:optim}, expressed through the certifiable quantity $\psi_t$.

\begin{lemma}[Primitive upper bound for prediction discrepancy]\label{lem:primitive-pred}
Let $z_s=(\bx_s,a_s)$,
$\bg_s=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(z_s)$,
$\bg_{s,t}=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(z_s)$, and
$\delta_{s,t}=\bg_{s,t}-\bg_s$.  Define
\[
  \bar\mu_{s,t}
  :=
  \mu_{\btheta_s}(z_s)+\bg_s^\top(\btheta_t-\btheta_s),
  \qquad
  e_{s,t}:=\mu_{\btheta_t}(z_s)-\bar\mu_{s,t},
  \qquad
  \bar r_{s,t}:=\bar\mu_{s,t}-r_s .
\]
Let $\bar{\cL}_t$ be the frozen-feature linearized ridge objective whose
minimizer is $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$, and define
\[
  M_t
  :=
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}
  \sum_{s<t}
  \left[
    \bar r_{s,t}\delta_{s,t}
    +
    e_{s,t}\bg_s
    +
    e_{s,t}\delta_{s,t}
  \right].
\]
If the nonlinear optimizer satisfies
$\|\nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)\|_2\le\zeta_t$, then for every candidate
action $a\in\cA_t$,
\[
  \left|
  \bg_t(a)^\top
  (\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})
  \right|
  \;\le\;
  \bar s_t(a)
  \left(
    \frac{\zeta_t}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
    +
    \|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
  \right),
  \qquad
  \bar s_t(a):=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)} .
\]
The left-hand side is exactly the geometric bound
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|\le\bar s_t(a)\,\psi_t$
of \Cref{asm:optim} with $\psi_t=\zeta_t/\sqrt\lambda+\|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}$
(\Cref{eq:psi-def}).  Consequently, since
$\|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}\le\|M_t\|_2/\sqrt{\lambda}$, a valid centering
certificate is
\[
  \bar\psi_t
  \;:=\;
  \frac{\zeta_t+\|M_t\|_2}{\sqrt{\lambda}}
  \;\ge\;
  \psi_t .
\]
If, in addition, on a fixed event,
$\btheta\mapsto\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta}(z)$ is $L_g$-Lipschitz,
$|\bar r_{s,t}|\le B_{t,\delta}$ for all $s<t$, and
$\Delta_{s,t}:=\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|$, then
\[
  \|M_t\|_2
  \;\le\;
  \frac{1}{\sigma^2}
  \sum_{s<t}
  \left[
    B_{t,\delta} L_g\Delta_{s,t}
    +
    \frac{G L_g}{2}\Delta_{s,t}^2
    +
    \frac{L_g^2}{2}\Delta_{s,t}^3
  \right].
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The linearized ridge objective satisfies
$\nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})=0$ and, by its
quadratic form,
\[
  \nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)
  =
  \bCbar_t(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}).
\]
Thus
$\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
=\bCbar_t^{-1}\nabla\bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)$.
Using
$\mu_{\btheta_t}(z_s)-r_s=\bar r_{s,t}+e_{s,t}$ and
$\bg_{s,t}=\bg_s+\delta_{s,t}$, the gradients of the nonlinear loss
and the frozen-feature linearized loss satisfy
\[
  \nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)
  =
  \nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)+M_t,
  \qquad
  \text{so}\qquad
  \nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)=\nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)-M_t .
\]
Therefore, by Cauchy--Schwarz in the $\bCbar_t$-norm,
\[
  |\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|
  \le
  \bar s_t(a)
  \|\nabla \bar{\cL}_t(\btheta_t)\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
  \le
  \bar s_t(a)
  \left(
    \|\nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
    +
    \|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
  \right).
\]
Since $\bCbar_t\succeq\lambda\bI$,
$\|\nabla \cL_t(\btheta_t)\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}\le \zeta_t/\sqrt{\lambda}$,
which gives the geometric inequality
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t-\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|
\le\bar s_t(a)\bigl(\zeta_t/\sqrt{\lambda}+\|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}\bigr)
=\bar s_t(a)\,\psi_t$, i.e.\ the bound of \eqref{eq:psi-def}.
For the certificate, $\bCbar_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ also gives
$\|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}\le\|M_t\|_2/\sqrt{\lambda}$, so
$\psi_t\le(\zeta_t+\|M_t\|_2)/\sqrt{\lambda}=:\bar\psi_t\ge\psi_t$, the proposed
certificate.
For the final display, Lipschitzness gives
$\|\delta_{s,t}\|\le L_g\Delta_{s,t}$ and the Taylor remainder bound
$|e_{s,t}|\le (L_g/2)\Delta_{s,t}^2$; applying these together with
$\|\bg_s\|\le G$ (\Cref{asm:bounded}) and $|\bar r_{s,t}|\le B_{t,\delta}$
term by term in the definition of $M_t$ gives the claimed bound on $\|M_t\|_2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Bounded-output residual energy]
\label{lem:bounded-output-residual}
Fix a horizon $T\ge1$ and $\delta_R\in(0,1)$.  Suppose
$|\mu_{\btheta}(x,a)|\le B_\mu$ throughout the certified parameter region and
$|\mu^*(x,a)|\le B_\mu$.  Under \Cref{asm:noise}, with probability at least
$1-\delta_R$, simultaneously for every $t\le T+1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bounded-output-residual}
 R_t^{\rm col}\le
 \left[8B_\mu^2+4\sigma^2\log\!\left(\frac{2T}{\delta_R}\right)\right](t-1).
\end{equation}
This finite-horizon envelope is not claimed to be tight.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Conditional sub-Gaussianity gives
\[
 \Pr\!\left(|\eta_s|>
 \sigma\sqrt{2\log(2T/\delta_R)}\mid\cG_s\right)
 \le\delta_R/T.
\]
Taking expectations and a union bound over $s=1,\ldots,T$ yields an event of
probability at least $1-\delta_R$ on which this bound holds for every noise
term.  On that event,
$c_s=\mu_{\btheta_s}(x_s,a_s)-\mu^*(x_s,a_s)-\eta_s$ and
$(a+b)^2\le2a^2+2b^2$ give
\[
 c_s^2\le
 2(2B_\mu)^2+2\sigma^2\,2\log(2T/\delta_R)
 =8B_\mu^2+4\sigma^2\log(2T/\delta_R).
\]
Summing the first $t-1$ terms proves~\eqref{eq:bounded-output-residual}; at
$t=1$ both sides are zero.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[$O(d)$-state path certificates]\label{lem:path-certificates}
Suppose $\nabla_\theta\mu_\theta(z)$ is $L_g$-Lipschitz and bounded by $G$
on a convex region containing the realized iterates.  Before round $t$, let
$n_t=t-1$, let $\bar\btheta_{<t}$ and
$J_{<t}=\sum_{s<t}\|\btheta_s-\bar\btheta_{<t}\|^2$ summarize past
iterates, and let
$R_t^{\rm col}=\sum_{s<t}c_s^2$ with
$c_s=\mu_{\btheta_s}(z_s)-r_s$.  At $t=1$ use the initialization
$\bar\btheta_{<1}=\mathbf0$, $J_{<1}=Q_1=R_1^{\rm col}=0$.  Then, for all $t$,
\[
 Q_t:=J_{<t}+n_t\|\btheta_t-\bar\btheta_{<t}\|^2
 =\sum_{s<t}\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|^2
\]
and the following $\cHist_t$-measurable quantities are valid:
\begin{align}
 \bar\chi_t&=\frac{L_g}{\sigma\sqrt\lambda}\sqrt{Q_t}\ge\chi_t,\label{eq:path-chi}\\
 \bar M_t&=\frac1{\sigma^2}\left[
 L_g\sqrt{R_t^{\rm col}Q_t}+\frac{3GL_g}{2}Q_t
 +\frac{L_g^2}{2}Q_t^{3/2}\right]\ge\|M_t\|_2.\label{eq:path-M}
\end{align}
If $\|\btheta_s\|,\|\btheta_t\|\le R$, the final term in
\eqref{eq:path-M} may be replaced by $L_g^2RQ_t$.  If a nonnegative
$\cHist_t$-measurable optimizer certificate satisfies
$\|\nabla\cL_t(\btheta_t)\|\le\zeta_t$, then
\[
 \bar\psi_t=(\zeta_t+\bar M_t)/\sqrt\lambda\ge\psi_t.
\]
If prediction gradients are $L_\mu$-Lipschitz on a convex region containing
$\btheta_t$ and $\btheta^*$ and $\|\btheta^*\|\le S$, then
\[
 \bar\epsilon_t^{\rm lin}:=\frac{L_\mu}{2}(S+\|\btheta_t\|)^2
 \ge\epslin(t).
\]
Consequently
$\bar F_t=\sum_{s<t}(\bar\epsilon_s^{\rm lin})^2\ge F_t$ and
$\bar E_T=\sum_{t\le T}\bar\epsilon_t^{\rm lin}\ge E_T$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The parallel-axis identity follows after writing
$\btheta_t-\btheta_s=(\btheta_t-\bar\btheta_{<t})
+(\bar\btheta_{<t}-\btheta_s)$; the cross term sums to zero.
\Cref{lem:whitened} and
$\|\nabla\mu_{\btheta_t}(z_s)-\nabla\mu_{\btheta_s}(z_s)\|
\le L_g\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|$ give \eqref{eq:path-chi}.
Let $d_{s,t}=\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|$.  In
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred},
\[
 |\bar r_{s,t}|\le |c_s|+Gd_{s,t},\qquad
 \|\delta_{s,t}\|\le L_gd_{s,t},\qquad
 |e_{s,t}|\le(L_g/2)d_{s,t}^2.
\]
Thus each summand defining $M_t$ has norm at most
\[
 L_g|c_s|d_{s,t}+\frac{3GL_g}{2}d_{s,t}^2
 +\frac{L_g^2}{2}d_{s,t}^3.
\]
Cauchy--Schwarz gives
$\sum|c_s|d_{s,t}\le\sqrt{R_t^{\rm col}Q_t}$, while
$\sum d_{s,t}^2=Q_t$ and
$\sum d_{s,t}^3\le Q_t^{3/2}$, proving \eqref{eq:path-M}.
Under the trust region, $d_{s,t}\le2R$, so
$\sum d_{s,t}^3\le2RQ_t$.  The centering claim follows from
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred} and $\bCbar_t\succeq\lambda\bI$.
Finally Taylor's theorem and
$\|\btheta^*-\btheta_t\|\le S+\|\btheta_t\|$ prove the remainder,
$F_t$, and $E_T$ claims.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Filtration-order implementation.}
At round $t$, the summaries through $t-1$, $\btheta_t$, and the certificate
$\zeta_t$ are available before selection.  The learner computes
$Q_t,\bar\chi_t,u_t,\bar M_t,\bar\psi_t,\bar\epsilon_t^{\rm lin}$ and
$\bar\beta_t$, then solves every candidate system to the uniform tolerance
and selects $a_t$.  After observing $r_t$, it adds $c_t^2$ to
$R_{t+1}^{\rm col}$, adds $\btheta_t$ with the Welford update
\[
 d=\btheta_t-\bar\btheta_{<t},\quad
 \bar\btheta_{\le t}=\bar\btheta_{<t}+d/t,\quad
 J_{\le t}=J_{<t}+d^\top(\btheta_t-\bar\btheta_{\le t}),
\]
and updates $\bar F_{t+1}$ and $\hat\gamma_t$.  It then forms
$\btheta_{t+1}$ and certifies the nonnegative bound $\zeta_{t+1}$ before
the next action.  No current reward enters the current score.

In frozen linear-head models $Q_t$ may be nonzero while $L_g=L_\mu=0$;
then $\bar\chi_t=\bar M_t=\bar\epsilon_t^{\rm lin}=0$, and exact
optimization also gives $\bar\psi_t=0$.

\begin{corollary}[Bounded-path near-linear rate]\label{cor:near-linear}
Fix $T$ and suppose for every $t\le T$
\[
 \|\btheta_t\|,\|\btheta^*\|\le R,\quad
 L_\mu\le c_\mu/\sqrt W,\quad L_g\le c_g/\sqrt W,\quad
 \zeta_t\le\zeta_0/\sqrt W,\quad
 R_t^{\rm col}\le C_RtP_T ,
\]
where $P_T\ge1$ is an explicitly supplied residual-energy factor.  For exact
current-parameter curvature and a uniform CG tolerance
$\bar\varepsilon_t\le\bar\varepsilon<1$, set
\[
 x_T=\frac{2c_gR\sqrt{T-1}}{\sigma\sqrt{\lambda W}},\quad
 \rho_-=(1-x_T)^2,\qquad
 \rho_+=(1+x_T)^2.
\]
If $x_T<1$, then
$\rho_-\bCbar_t\preceq\bC_t\preceq\rho_+\bCbar_t$ for all $t\le T$.
Writing
\[
 \alpha_I=\sqrt{\frac{1+\bar\varepsilon}{1-\bar\varepsilon}},\qquad
 A_{\rm inf}=\alpha_I^2\frac{\rho_+}{\rho_-},
\]
the observable information schedule satisfies
$\gamma_T\le\hat\gamma_T\le A_{\rm inf}\gamma_T$, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:near-linear-regret-gamma}
R_T\le2\alpha_I\sqrt{\rho_+/\rho_-}\,\omega_{\max,T}^{\rm op}
 \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)T\gamma_T}+2\bar E_T
\end{equation}
holds with
\[\omega_{\max,T}^{\rm op}:=
\max_{t\le T}(\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t)\]
and
\begin{align*}
 \omega_{\max,T}^{\rm op}\le{}&
 \sqrt{A_{\rm inf}\gamma_T+2\log(1/\delta)}+\sqrt\lambda R
 +\frac{2c_\mu R^2\sqrt T}{\sigma\sqrt W}\\
 &+\frac{A_T}{\sqrt{\lambda W}}+\frac{B_T}{\sqrt\lambda W},\\
 A_T={}&\zeta_0+\sigma^{-2}\!\left[
 2c_gR\sqrt{C_RT(T-1)P_T}+6Gc_gR^2(T-1)\right],\\
 B_T={}&4c_g^2R^3(T-1)/\sigma^2 .
\end{align*}
In addition, \eqref{eq:near-linear-rates-main} holds and
\[
 \bar\psi_t\le\frac1{\sqrt\lambda}\!\left\{
 \frac{\zeta_0}{\sqrt W}+\frac1{\sigma^2}\!\left[
 \frac{2c_gR\sqrt{C_Rt(t-1)P_T}+6Gc_gR^2(t-1)}{\sqrt W}
 +\frac{4c_g^2R^3(t-1)}{W}\right]\right\}.
\]
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The trust region gives $Q_t\le4R^2(t-1)$; substitution in
\Cref{lem:path-certificates} proves all displayed path bounds.
\Cref{cor:drift-sandwich} gives the two-sided operator relation.
The CG sandwich and $u_t\le(1+x_T)^2=\rho_+$ imply that every logarithm in
$\hat\gamma_T$ is at most
$\log(1+A_{\rm inf}\sigma^{-2}\bar s_t^2(a_t))$.
For $A\ge1$, $\log(1+Ax)\le A\log(1+x)$, proving the information comparison.
Substitution into \Cref{cor:twosided} gives the regret display.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Corrected-center near-linear rate]
\label{cor:corrected-near-linear}
Fix $T$ and suppose, for every $t\le T$,
\[
 \|\btheta_t\|,\|\btheta^*\|\le R,\qquad
 L_\mu\le c_\mu/\sqrt W,\qquad L_g\le c_g/\sqrt W,
\]
where $W$ is an abstract near-linearity scale.  Use the corrected center,
exact current curvature, and a uniform CG tolerance
$\bar\varepsilon_t\le\bar\varepsilon<1$.  Fix
$r\in\{0,\ldots,d\}$ and suppose a predictable prefix information envelope
$\mathcal G_{n,r}\ge\gamma_n$ is supplied for $n=0,\ldots,T$.  Valid choices
are the rank envelope $\Gamma_{n,r}$ under a known rank bound, or the
spectral-tail envelope in \Cref{cor:approx-rank-current-ggn} with supplied
prefix tails $\bar\Delta_{n,r}$.  Define its predictable monotone closure
$\mathcal G^\uparrow_{n,r}:=\max_{0\le j\le n}\mathcal G_{j,r}$ and set
\begin{align*}
 x_T&:=\frac{2c_gR\sqrt{T-1}}{\sigma\sqrt{\lambda W}},&
 \alpha_I&:=\sqrt{\frac{1+\bar\varepsilon}{1-\bar\varepsilon}},\\
 \mathcal B_{T,r}^{\rm corr}
 &:=\sqrt{\mathcal G^\uparrow_{T,r}+2\log(1/\delta)}+\sqrt\lambda R
 +\frac{2c_\mu R^2\sqrt T}{\sigma\sqrt W}.&&
\end{align*}
If $x_T<1$, the predictable radius
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-near-linear-beta}
 \bar\beta_t^{\rm corr}:=
 \sqrt{\mathcal G^\uparrow_{t-1,r}+2\log(1/\delta)}+\sqrt\lambda R
 +\frac{2c_\mu R^2\sqrt{t-1}}{\sigma\sqrt W}
\end{equation}
is valid and the corrected-center policy satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:corrected-near-linear-regret}
\begin{aligned}
 R_T\le{}&2\alpha_I\frac{1+x_T}{1-x_T}
 \mathcal B_{T,r}^{\rm corr}
 \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)T\mathcal G^\uparrow_{T,r}}\\
 &+\frac{4c_\mu R^2T}{\sqrt W}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
In particular, bounded transfer is ensured by
$W>4c_g^2R^2(T-1)/(\lambda\sigma^2)=\Omega(T)$ up to problem constants,
and $E_T\le2c_\mu R^2T/\sqrt W$.  If
$\mathcal G^\uparrow_{T,r}=O(r\log T)$---for example, if
$\max_{n\le T}\bar\Delta_{n,r}=O(r\log T)$ at fixed $\lambda$---and
$W=\Omega(T)$ with
a sufficiently large constant to keep $x_T$ bounded away from one, then
\[
 R_T=O(r\sqrt T\log T)+O(\sqrt T)
\]
up to confidence and CG constants.

This corollary assumes neither an optimizer-residual rate nor an envelope on
$R_t^{\rm col}$.  Its cost is frozen-feature access: exact corrected centers
require an explicit $d\times d$ frozen Gram, stored frozen gradients, replay
with historical checkpoints, a supplied low-dimensional representation, or a
separately certified sketch.  The result therefore does not have $O(d)$ total
memory merely because current-curvature products are matrix-free.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The running maximum $\mathcal G^\uparrow_{n,r}$ is predictable and dominates
both $\mathcal G_{n,r}$ and every earlier prefix envelope.
The trust region gives $Q_t\le4R^2(t-1)$, so
\Cref{lem:path-certificates} yields
$\bar\chi_t\le2c_gR\sqrt{t-1}/(\sigma\sqrt{\lambda W})\le x_T$,
$\bar\epsilon_t^{\rm lin}\le2c_\mu R^2/\sqrt W$,
$\bar F_t\le4c_\mu^2R^4(t-1)/W$, and
$\bar E_T\le2c_\mu R^2T/\sqrt W$.  Hence
\eqref{eq:corrected-near-linear-beta} dominates \eqref{eq:beta}.
Apply the corrected-center form of
\Cref{cor:exact-current-frozen-potential}, bound
$\alpha_t\le\alpha_I$, $(1+\bar\chi_t)/(1-\bar\chi_t)\le
(1+x_T)/(1-x_T)$, and
$\bar\beta_t^{\rm corr}\le\mathcal B_{T,r}^{\rm corr}$, then use
$\gamma_T\le\mathcal G_{T,r}\le\mathcal G^\uparrow_{T,r}$.  Finally substitute
$2\bar E_T\le4c_\mu R^2T/\sqrt W$.  The stated orders follow directly.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Why $W$ is an abstract near-linearity scale]
The all-weights MLP used in the nonlinear diagnostics has action-$a$ mean
$v_a^\top\tanh(Ux+b)+c_a$ and no $W^{-1/2}$ forward normalization.  With
$\bar x=(x,1)$, hidden vector $q_i=(U_i,b_i)$, and $z_i=q_i^\top\bar x$, the
Hessian block on $(q_i,v_{a,i})$ is
\[
 \begin{bmatrix}
  v_{a,i}\tanh''(z_i)\bar x\bar x^\top&
  \tanh'(z_i)\bar x\\
  \tanh'(z_i)\bar x^\top&0
 \end{bmatrix}.
\]
At $z_i=0$ and $v_{a,i}=0$ its operator norm is $\|\bar x\|$, independent of
hidden width.  Thus the executed all-weights parameterization does not imply
$L_\mu,L_g=O(W^{-1/2})$.  Such a conclusion would require a different
explicitly normalized parameterization and coordinate constraints, which
would also change the ridge penalty and GGN geometry.  We therefore do not
identify $W$ with generic neural-network width or derive optimizer-residual
scaling from it.
\end{remark}

\begin{corollary}[Conditional projected-Gram rate for a supplied subspace]\label{cor:rank-near-linear}
Under \Cref{cor:near-linear}, additionally suppose a subspace
$U\subseteq\R^d$ with $\dim U\le r$ is fixed and supplied before interaction,
and every candidate or replayed tangent feature through round $T$ lies in $U$.
Hence $\operatorname{rank}(\bCbar_t-\lambda\bI)\le r$ for every
$t\le T+1$.  Let $r_U=\dim U$, let $Q_U\in\R^{d\times r_U}$ have
orthonormal columns spanning $U$, and define
\[
 \mathbf z_{t,s}=Q_U^\top\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s),\qquad
 \mathbf z_t(a)=Q_U^\top\bg_t(\bx_t,a).
\]
Then the current-GGN width reduces exactly to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:projected-width}
 \bg_t(a)^\top\bC_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)
 =\mathbf z_t(a)^\top\!\left(\lambda I_{r_U}
 +\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\mathbf z_{t,s}\mathbf z_{t,s}^\top
 \right)^{-1}\!\mathbf z_t(a),
\end{equation}
with the analogous identity for $\bCbar_t$ using
$Q_U^\top\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$.  Thus a supplied basis permits an exact dense
$r_U\times r_U$ Gram with $O(r_U^2)$ curvature state.  Given tangent
coordinates, forming the current Gram costs $O((t-1)r_U^2)$, factorization
$O(r_U^3)$, and each width query $O(r_U^2)$; current-parameter replay, model
training, and gradient generation can still be $d$-dimensional.
Full-dimensional CG is not needed in this corollary's covered regime.

Define $\Gamma_{T,r}$ by \eqref{eq:rank-gamma-main}.  Then
\eqref{eq:near-linear-regret-main} holds with
\begin{align*}
\Omega_{T,r}:={}&
 \sqrt{A_{\rm inf}\Gamma_{T,r}+2\log(1/\delta)}+\sqrt\lambda R
 +\frac{2c_\mu R^2\sqrt T}{\sigma\sqrt W}
 +\frac{A_T}{\sqrt{\lambda W}}+\frac{B_T}{\sqrt\lambda W},\\
A_T={}&\zeta_0+\sigma^{-2}\!\left[
 2c_gR\sqrt{C_RT(T-1)P_T}+6Gc_gR^2(T-1)\right],\\
B_T={}&4c_g^2R^3(T-1)/\sigma^2,
\end{align*}
and $A_{\rm inf},\rho_\pm$ are exactly those in \Cref{cor:near-linear}.
In particular, for fixed $r$ and fixed problem constants, if
$W\ge C T^2P_T$ for a constant $C$ satisfying the explicit thresholds in
\eqref{eq:width-thresholds}, then
\[
 R_T=O(r\sqrt T\log T)+O(T/\sqrt W).
\]
This rate is conditional on the ex ante subspace $U$ and every bounded-path,
near-linearity, residual, optimizer, transfer, and solver premise of
\Cref{cor:near-linear}.  It neither learns $U$ nor covers unrestricted
representation learning or end-to-end neural-network training; it is an
effective projected-feature closure, not a full-dimensional computational
advantage for CC-UCB.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Extend $Q_U$ to an orthogonal basis of $\R^d$.  In that basis $\bC_t$ is block
diagonal with the $r_U\times r_U$ matrix in \eqref{eq:projected-width} and
$\lambda I_{d-r_U}$; the query has zero coordinates in the second block.  This
proves \eqref{eq:projected-width}, and the same argument proves the frozen-Gram
identity.  \Cref{lem:rank-logdet} gives $\gamma_T\le\Gamma_{T,r}$.  Every occurrence of
$\gamma_T$ in \Cref{cor:near-linear} is monotone, so direct substitution gives
the display.  Under the stated near-linearity scaling, $x_T$, the centering terms, and
the linearization terms are bounded, while
$\Gamma_{T,r}=O(r\log T)$.  Hence
$\Omega_{T,r}=O(\sqrt{r\log T}+1)$ and the leading product is
$O(r\sqrt T\log T)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Linear-subclass lower bound]
The exact-rank class contains a standard $r$-dimensional stochastic linear
bandit.  Take a constant context, a known orthonormal basis $Q_U$, the finite
action set $z(a)\in\{\pm1/\sqrt r\}^r$, linear mean
$\mu_\vartheta(a)=z(a)^\top\vartheta$, and Gaussian noise.  Choosing the usual
hypercube parameter family with coordinates of order $\sqrt{r/T}$ keeps the
parameter norm bounded when $T\gtrsim r^2$.  Here $L_\mu=L_g=E_T=0$,
$\bC_t=\bCbar_t$, and all features lie in $U$.  The stochastic linear-bandit
minimax expected-regret lower bound is $\Omega(r\sqrt T)$ in this regime
\citep{dani2008stochastic}.  Hence the leading $r\sqrt T$ dependence of the
exact-rank upper bound is sharp up to logarithms on this linear subclass.  This
does not prove a lower bound for the nonlinear or spectral-tail classes, nor a
computational lower bound.
\end{remark}

For explicit targets
$\bar x_{\rm drift}\in(0,1)$ and $\Psi,b_F,\eta_E>0$, the sufficient conditions
\begin{equation}\label{eq:width-thresholds}
\begin{aligned}
W&\ge\frac{4c_g^2R^2(T-1)}{\lambda\sigma^2\bar x_{\rm drift}^2},&
W&\ge\frac{4c_\mu^2R^4T}{\sigma^2b_F^2},&
W&\ge\frac{4c_\mu^2R^4}{\eta_E^2},\\
W&\ge\max\!\left\{\frac{4A_T^2}{\lambda\Psi^2},
\frac{2B_T}{\sqrt\lambda\Psi}\right\}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
respectively ensure $x_T\le\bar x_{\rm drift}<1$ (and therefore
$\rho_-\ge(1-\bar x_{\rm drift})^2$ and
$\rho_+\le(1+\bar x_{\rm drift})^2$),
$\sqrt{\bar F_{T+1}}/\sigma\le b_F$,
$\bar E_T/T\le\eta_E$, and $\bar\psi_T\le\Psi$.  Thus the centering
requirement has leading sufficient scaling $W=\Omega(T^2P_T)$; these
conditions do not bound $\gamma_T$.

\begin{corollary}[Nonlinear tanh-link Gaussian bandit]\label{cor:tanh-link}
For
$\mu_\theta(x,a)=\tanh(\phi(x,a)^\top\theta)$ with
$\|\phi(x,a)\|\le B_\phi$, one may take
\[
 G=B_\phi,\qquad
 L_\mu=L_g=\frac{4}{3\sqrt3}B_\phi^2.
\]
On any certified trust region, \Cref{cor:operational} therefore gives an
executable nonlinear Gaussian-reward CC-UCB policy using only the online path
statistics, a pre-action optimizer-residual certificate, and the stated
operator/CG certificates.
Under realizability, both the model and teacher outputs have $B_\mu=1$.
Hence \Cref{lem:bounded-output-residual} removes the supplied residual-energy
factor in \Cref{cor:near-linear}: on its event one may take
\[
 C_R=1,\qquad
 P_T=8+4\sigma^2\log(2T/\delta_R),
\]
and allocate $\delta_R$ to the certificate failure budget.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$\nabla_\theta\mu=\operatorname{sech}^2(\phi^\top\theta)\phi$, so its norm is
at most $B_\phi$.  The Hessian is
$-2\tanh(z)\operatorname{sech}^2(z)\phi\phi^\top$.
For $y=|\tanh z|$, $2y(1-y^2)$ is maximized at $y=1/\sqrt3$ with value
$4/(3\sqrt3)$, proving both Lipschitz constants.  Apply
\Cref{lem:path-certificates,cor:operational}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Scaled-tanh relative-link rate]
\label{cor:scaled-tanh-relative}
Consider the explicit zero-offset scalar-link family
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-tanh-model}
 \mu_{\btheta,W}(z)=\sqrt W\,
 \tanh\!\left(\frac{\phi(z)^\top\btheta}{\sqrt W}\right),
 \qquad \|\phi(z)\|\le B_\phi,\qquad
 \|\btheta_t\|,\|\btheta^*\|\le R.
\end{equation}
This is a scaled generalized-linear family in the displacement coordinates,
not a claim that generic neural-network width provides the scale $W$.
Let
\[
 c_h:=\frac{4B_\phi^2}{3\sqrt3},\qquad
 \rho_W:=\exp\!\left(\frac{4B_\phi R}{\sqrt W}\right)-1.
\]
Then valid global constants on the certified region are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-tanh-constants}
 G=B_\phi,\qquad L_\mu=L_g=\frac{c_h}{\sqrt W},qquad
 |\mu_{\btheta,W}(z)|\le B_\phi R,qquad \rho_t\le\rho_W.
\end{equation}
The stated exponential relative-drift certificate obeys $\rho_W<1$ exactly
when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-tanh-rho-threshold}
 W>\left(\frac{4B_\phi R}{\log2}\right)^2.
\end{equation}

Fix a uniform CG certificate
$\bar\varepsilon_t\le\bar\varepsilon<1$, let
$\alpha_I=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon)/(1-\bar\varepsilon)}$, and suppose
$\zeta_t\le\zeta_0/\sqrt W$ and
$R_t^{\rm col}\le C_RtP_T$ for $P_T\ge1$.  Fix a rank index $r$ and supply a
predictable prefix information envelope $\mathcal G_{n,r}\ge\gamma_n$; write
$\mathcal G^\uparrow_{n,r}=\max_{j\le n}\mathcal G_{j,r}$.  The envelope may
be the exact-rank bound or either valid predictable spectral-tail schedule in
\Cref{cor:approx-rank-current-ggn}.  Define
\begin{align}
 \Psi^{\rm rel}_{T,W}:={}&\frac{\zeta_0}{\sqrt{\lambda W}}
 +\frac1\sigma\Biggl\{\rho_W
 \left[\sqrt{C_RTP_T}+2B_\phi R\sqrt{T-1}\right]
 \nonumber\\[-1mm]
 &\hspace{29mm}
 +\frac{2(1+\rho_W)c_hR^2\sqrt{T-1}}{\sqrt W}\Biggr\},
 \label{eq:scaled-tanh-relative-psi}\\
 \mathcal B^{\rm rel}_{T,r}:={}&
 \sqrt{\mathcal G^\uparrow_{T,r}+2\log(1/\delta)}+\sqrt\lambda R
 +\frac{2c_hR^2\sqrt T}{\sigma\sqrt W}
 +\Psi^{\rm rel}_{T,W}.
 \label{eq:scaled-tanh-relative-radius}
\end{align}
If $\rho_W<1$, exact current curvature with the original nonlinear center and
the relative certificate of \Cref{lem:relative-link-drift} satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-tanh-relative-regret}
\begin{aligned}
 R_T\le{}&2\alpha_I\frac{1+\rho_W}{1-\rho_W}
 \mathcal B^{\rm rel}_{T,r}
 \sqrt{\left(\sigma^2+\frac{B_\phi^2}{\lambda}\right)
 T\mathcal G^\uparrow_{T,r}}
 +\frac{4c_hR^2T}{\sqrt W}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

More explicitly, choose a fixed target $\rho_0\in(0,1)$ and suppose
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-tanh-linear-width}
 W\ge CTP_T,\qquad
 C\ge C_{\rho_0}:=
 \left(\frac{4B_\phi R}{\log(1+\rho_0)}\right)^2.
\end{equation}
Then $\rho_W\le\rho_0$, and the mean-value theorem gives the displayed
uniform certificate
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-tanh-psi-uniform}
\begin{aligned}
 \Psi^{\rm rel}_{T,W}\le{}&\frac{\zeta_0}{\sqrt{\lambda W}}
 +\frac1\sigma\Biggl[
 4(1+\rho_0)B_\phi R\sqrt{\frac{C_R}{C}}\\[-1mm]
 &\quad+\frac{8(1+\rho_0)B_\phi^2R^2
 +2(1+\rho_0)c_hR^2}{\sqrt{CP_T}}\Biggr].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Furthermore
$\bar F_{T+1}\le4c_h^2R^4T/W$ and
$\bar E_T\le2c_hR^2T/\sqrt W$.  Hence, if
$\mathcal G^\uparrow_{T,r}=O(r\log T)$ with fixed problem, confidence, and CG
constants,
\[
 R_T=O(r\sqrt T\log T)+O(T/\sqrt W).
\]
At $W=\Theta(TP_T)$ this is
$O(r\sqrt T\log T+\sqrt{T/P_T})$, and therefore
$O(r\sqrt T\log T+\sqrt T)$ up to the displayed residual factor.

Under realizability, \Cref{lem:bounded-output-residual} supplies the premise
with probability at least $1-\delta_R$ by taking
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scaled-tanh-residual-factor}
 C_R=1,\qquad
 P_T=\max\!\left\{1,
 8B_\phi^2R^2+4\sigma^2\log(2T/\delta_R)\right\}.
\end{equation}
Thus this fully analytic residual choice makes the sufficient width
$W=\Omega(T\log(T/\delta_R))$ at fixed problem constants.  Its event is
added to the certificate failure allocation.  No claim is made that the
residual or width constants are tight.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
For $h_W(q)=\sqrt W\tanh(q/\sqrt W)$,
$h_W'(q)=\operatorname{sech}^2(q/\sqrt W)$ and
\[
 h_W''(q)=-\frac{2}{\sqrt W}\tanh(q/\sqrt W)
 \operatorname{sech}^2(q/\sqrt W).
\]
The maximum of $2y(1-y^2)$ on $y\in[0,1]$ is $4/(3\sqrt3)$, proving the
gradient and Hessian bounds in~\eqref{eq:scaled-tanh-constants}; also
$\sqrt W|\tanh(q/\sqrt W)|\le|q|\le B_\phi R$.
Moreover
\[
 \left|\frac{d}{dq}\log h_W'(q)\right|
 =\frac{2}{\sqrt W}|\tanh(q/\sqrt W)|\le\frac2{\sqrt W}.
\]
Since $|q_{s,t}-q_s|\le B_\phi\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|\le2B_\phi R$,
$|\log c_{s,t}|\le4B_\phi R/\sqrt W$.  Hence
$|c_{s,t}-1|\le e^{4B_\phi R/\sqrt W}-1=\rho_W$, and solving
$e^{4B_\phi R/\sqrt W}-1<1$ gives
\eqref{eq:scaled-tanh-rho-threshold} for this exponential certificate.

The trust region gives $Q_t\le4R^2(t-1)$.  Substitution in
\eqref{eq:relative-link-psi} proves
$\bar\psi_t^{\rm rel}\le\Psi^{\rm rel}_{T,W}$.  Taylor's theorem gives
$\bar\epsilon_t^{\rm lin}\le2c_hR^2/\sqrt W$, and hence the stated bounds on
$\bar F_{T+1},\bar E_T$ and the confidence radius
\eqref{eq:scaled-tanh-relative-radius}.  Apply
\Cref{cor:exact-current-frozen-potential}, bound the time-varying factors by
$\alpha_I$, $\rho_W$, and $\mathcal B^{\rm rel}_{T,r}$, and use
$\gamma_T\le\mathcal G^\uparrow_{T,r}$ to obtain
\eqref{eq:scaled-tanh-relative-regret}.

Under~\eqref{eq:scaled-tanh-linear-width}, the exponential mean-value bound
$e^x-1\le(1+\rho_0)x$ for $0\le x\le\log(1+\rho_0)$ gives
$\rho_W\le4(1+\rho_0)B_\phi R/\sqrt W$.  Substitute this and
$W\ge CTP_T$ in~\eqref{eq:scaled-tanh-relative-psi} to prove
\eqref{eq:scaled-tanh-psi-uniform}; the rate follows.  Finally
\Cref{lem:bounded-output-residual} with $B_\mu=B_\phi R$ proves
\eqref{eq:scaled-tanh-residual-factor}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Predictable algorithmic curvature under certified transfer.}
The analysis is conducted for a \emph{generic predictable SPD algorithmic
curvature sequence} $\{\Calg_t\}_{t\ge1}$ satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:calg-props}
  \Calg_t\ \text{is symmetric},\qquad
  \Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI\succ 0,\qquad
  \Calg_t\ \text{is}\ \cHist_t\text{-measurable}.
\end{equation}
This is the operator whose inverse the algorithm actually applies (via CG)
to form widths.  The exact full-history result is the special case
$\Calg_t=\bC_t$ (relinearized curvature~\eqref{eq:curvature}); approximate,
subsampled, windowed, refreshed, or sketched operators $\hat\bC_t$ are also
represented by $\Calg_t$, but are covered only when the required transfer
certificate below is available.  Write the algorithmic width
\begin{equation}\label{eq:scalg-def}
  s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)\;:=\;\bg_t(a)^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bg_t(a).
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[One-sided optimism transfer]\label{asm:transfer}
For every round $t\le T$ there is an $\cHist_t$-measurable factor
$u_t(a)\ge 1$ such that, for every candidate action $a\in\cA_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:transfer}
  \bar s_t^2(a)\;\le\;u_t(a)\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a).
\end{equation}
\end{assumption}

This is the \emph{only} spectral relation the main theorem needs, and it is
one-sided: it dominates the predictable frozen-feature width $\bar s_t(a)$
(the object of the self-normalized confidence bound) by an inflated
algorithmic width.  No \emph{lower} Loewner factor on $\Calg_t$ is required
for optimism.  The factor may be action-dependent; when a uniform per-round
$u_t$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:transfer} for every action we write $u_t$ and
state the action-dependent theorem with $u_t(a)$.
For the exact-curvature instantiation $\Calg_t=\bC_t$, the whitened
relative-drift lemma below certifies~\eqref{eq:transfer} with the uniform
factor $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$; for an approximate operator, the composition
of \Cref{lem:whitened} with a one-sided spectral bound
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\kappa_{+,t}\bC_t$ gives
$u_t=\kappa_{+,t}(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ (\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}).

The following lemma supplies the exact-curvature transfer factor through a
\emph{whitened, relative} feature-drift bound: the drift is measured in the
$\bCbar_t^{-1}$ metric and accumulated in $\ell_2$, and the resulting
one-sided bound needs \emph{no} smallness condition.

\begin{lemma}[Whitened relative feature-drift bound]\label{lem:whitened}
Let $\bg_s := \nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx_s,a_s)$,
$\bg_{s,t}:=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)$,
$\delta_{s,t}:=\bg_{s,t}-\bg_s$, and
$\Delta_{s,t}:=\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|$.
Using the symmetric (principal) square root $\bCbar_t^{1/2}$, define the
$d\times(t{-}1)$ whitened column matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whitened-cols}
  \Gmat_t := \Bigl[\,\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bg_s/\sigma\,\Bigr]_{s<t},
  \qquad
  \Dmat_t := \Bigl[\,\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\delta_{s,t}/\sigma\,\Bigr]_{s<t},
  \qquad
  \chi_t := \|\Dmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}} .
\end{equation}
Writing $n_t=t-1$, also define the $(d+n_t)\times d$ stacked matrices
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stacked-whitened-designs}
 \Amat_t:=\begin{bmatrix}
  \sqrt\lambda\,\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\\[1mm] \Gmat_t^\top
 \end{bmatrix},\qquad
 \Bmat_t:=\begin{bmatrix}
  \sqrt\lambda\,\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\\[1mm] (\Gmat_t+\Dmat_t)^\top
 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
At $t=1$ the lower blocks have zero rows.  All square roots and inverse
square roots in this statement are principal.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.6em]
\item $\Gmat_t\Gmat_t^\top = \bI - \lambda\,\bCbar_t^{-1} \preceq \bI$,
  hence $\|\Gmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le 1$.
\item $\bCbar_t^{-1/2}(\bC_t-\bCbar_t)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}
  = \Gmat_t\Dmat_t^\top + \Dmat_t\Gmat_t^\top + \Dmat_t\Dmat_t^\top$,
  so $\bigl\|\bCbar_t^{-1/2}(\bC_t-\bCbar_t)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bigr\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  \le 2\chi_t + \chi_t^2$.
\item The stacked designs obey
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:stacked-identities}
   \Amat_t^\top\Amat_t=\bI_d,\qquad
   \Bmat_t^\top\Bmat_t=
   \bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bC_t\bCbar_t^{-1/2},\qquad
   \|\Bmat_t-\Amat_t\|_{\rm op}=\chi_t.
  \end{equation}
\item \textbf{(One-sided, no smallness condition.)} For every $\chi_t\ge0$,
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:onesided-C}
    \bC_t \;\preceq\; (1+\chi_t)^2\,\bCbar_t,
    \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
    \bar s_t^2(a)\;\le\;(1+\chi_t)^2\,s_t^2(a)\ \ \forall a.
  \end{equation}
  A predictable certificate $\bar\chi_t\ge\chi_t$ therefore satisfies
  \Cref{asm:transfer} in the exact case $\Calg_t=\bC_t$ with the uniform
  factor $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$.
\item \textbf{(Whitened $\ell_2$ primitive bound.)}
  \begin{equation}\label{eq:chi-primitive}
    \chi_t \;\le\; \|\Dmat_t\|_F
    = \Biggl[\,\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\|\delta_{s,t}\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}^2\,\Biggr]^{1/2}
    \;\le\;
    \frac{L_g}{\sigma\sqrt{\lambda}}
    \Biggl[\,\sum_{s<t}\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|^2\,\Biggr]^{1/2},
  \end{equation}
  the last step under the $L_g$-Lipschitz-Jacobian assumption
  $\|\delta_{s,t}\|\le L_g\Delta_{s,t}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
\emph{(a)}
$\Gmat_t\Gmat_t^\top
=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bg_s\bg_s^\top\bCbar_t^{-1/2}
=\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bigl(\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bg_s\bg_s^\top\bigr)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}
=\bCbar_t^{-1/2}(\bCbar_t-\lambda\bI)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}
=\bI-\lambda\bCbar_t^{-1}$.
Since $\lambda\bCbar_t^{-1}\succeq0$, $\Gmat_t\Gmat_t^\top\preceq\bI$, and
$\|\Gmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2=\|\Gmat_t\Gmat_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1$.

\emph{(b)}
From $\bg_{s,t}=\bg_s+\delta_{s,t}$,
$\bg_{s,t}\bg_{s,t}^\top-\bg_s\bg_s^\top
=\bg_s\delta_{s,t}^\top+\delta_{s,t}\bg_s^\top+\delta_{s,t}\delta_{s,t}^\top$.
Conjugating each summand by $\bCbar_t^{-1/2}$ and weighting by
$\sigma^{-2}$, the three groups sum exactly to
$\Gmat_t\Dmat_t^\top$, $\Dmat_t\Gmat_t^\top$, and $\Dmat_t\Dmat_t^\top$.
By the triangle inequality and $\|\Gmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le1$,
$\|\Gmat_t\Dmat_t^\top+\Dmat_t\Gmat_t^\top+\Dmat_t\Dmat_t^\top\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le 2\|\Gmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\Dmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}+\|\Dmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2
\le 2\chi_t+\chi_t^2$.

\emph{(c)}
By part~(a),
$\Amat_t^\top\Amat_t=\lambda\bCbar_t^{-1}
+\Gmat_t\Gmat_t^\top=\bI_d$.  Expanding the current replay Gram gives
\[
 \Bmat_t^\top\Bmat_t
 =\lambda\bCbar_t^{-1}+(\Gmat_t+\Dmat_t)(\Gmat_t+\Dmat_t)^\top
 =\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bC_t\bCbar_t^{-1/2}.
\]
The two stacks differ only in their bottom block, hence
$\|\Bmat_t-\Amat_t\|_{\rm op}=\|\Dmat_t^\top\|_{\rm op}=\chi_t$.
These identities also hold at $t=1$, with empty bottom blocks and $\chi_1=0$.

\emph{(d)}
The matrix $W_t:=\bCbar_t^{-1/2}(\bC_t-\bCbar_t)\bCbar_t^{-1/2}$ is
symmetric with $\|W_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le2\chi_t+\chi_t^2$, hence
$W_t\preceq(2\chi_t+\chi_t^2)\bI$.  Congruence by $\bCbar_t^{1/2}$ gives
$\bC_t-\bCbar_t\preceq(2\chi_t+\chi_t^2)\bCbar_t$, i.e.
$\bC_t\preceq(1+\chi_t)^2\bCbar_t$; no bound on $\chi_t$ is used.
Both $\bC_t,\bCbar_t\succ0$, so inversion reverses the order:
$\bC_t^{-1}\succeq(1+\chi_t)^{-2}\bCbar_t^{-1}$.  The quadratic form at
$\bg_t(a)$ gives $s_t^2(a)\ge(1+\chi_t)^{-2}\bar s_t^2(a)$, i.e.
$\bar s_t^2(a)\le(1+\chi_t)^2 s_t^2(a)$.  Since $x\mapsto(1+x)^2$ is
increasing on $[0,\infty)$, replacing $\chi_t$ by any $\bar\chi_t\ge\chi_t$
preserves the inequality, giving $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$.

\emph{(e)}
$\chi_t=\|\Dmat_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le\|\Dmat_t\|_F$, and
$\|\Dmat_t\|_F^2=\sum_{s<t}\|\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\delta_{s,t}/\sigma\|^2
=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\delta_{s,t}^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\delta_{s,t}
=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\|\delta_{s,t}\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}^2$.
Since $\bCbar_t^{-1}\preceq\lambda^{-1}\bI$,
$\|\delta_{s,t}\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}^2\le\|\delta_{s,t}\|^2/\lambda
\le L_g^2\Delta_{s,t}^2/\lambda$; summing and taking square roots
gives~\eqref{eq:chi-primitive}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Relative scalar-link drift and centering]
\label{lem:relative-link-drift}
Suppose the prediction model has the fixed-feature scalar-link form
$\mu_{\btheta}(z)=h(\phi(z)^\top\btheta)$, where
$\phi(z)\in\R^d$, $h$ is differentiable, and $h'(q)$ is nonzero throughout
the certified scalar region.  For $s<t$, write $\phi_s=\phi(z_s)$ and define
\[
 q_s=\phi_s^\top\btheta_s,\qquad
 q_{s,t}=\phi_s^\top\btheta_t,\qquad
 c_{s,t}=\frac{h'(q_{s,t})}{h'(q_s)},\qquad
 \rho_t=\max_{s<t}|c_{s,t}-1|,
\]
with $\rho_1=0$.  Here $\phi_s,q_s$, and $h'(q_s)$ are fixed before reward
$r_s$, while $\btheta_t$ and every ratio above are $\cHist_t$-measurable
before action $a_t$ is selected.  Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\alph*),leftmargin=1.6em]
\item The scalar-link gradients obey
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-link-gradients}
 \bg_s=h'(q_s)\phi_s,\qquad
 \bg_{s,t}=h'(q_{s,t})\phi_s=c_{s,t}\bg_s,\qquad
 \delta_{s,t}=(c_{s,t}-1)\bg_s.
\end{equation}
Writing $\mathbf c_t=(c_{1,t},\ldots,c_{t-1,t})^\top\in\R^{t-1}$, the
$d\times(t-1)$ matrices of \Cref{lem:whitened} satisfy
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-link-D}
 \Dmat_t=\Gmat_t\operatorname{Diag}(\mathbf c_t-\mathbf{1}),\qquad
 \chi_t\le\|\Gmat_t\|_{\rm op}\rho_t\le\rho_t.
\end{equation}
At $t=1$ the matrices have zero columns and the same identities hold under the
empty-matrix convention.

\item Exact current curvature therefore satisfies, without a smallness
condition,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-link-transfer}
 \bC_t\preceq(1+\rho_t)^2\bCbar_t.
\end{equation}
If $\rho_t<1$, it also satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-link-sandwich}
 (1-\rho_t)^2\bCbar_t\preceq\bC_t
 \preceq(1+\rho_t)^2\bCbar_t.
\end{equation}
Any predictable upper certificate $\bar\rho_t\ge\rho_t$ may replace $\rho_t$
in the one-sided result, and may replace it in both sides when
$\bar\rho_t<1$.

\item Reuse $\bar r_{s,t},e_{s,t}$, and $M_t$ from
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred}, and define
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-link-v}
 v_{s,t}:=\bar r_{s,t}(c_{s,t}-1)+c_{s,t}e_{s,t},\qquad
 \bv_t=(v_{1,t},\ldots,v_{t-1,t})^\top\in\R^{t-1}.
\end{equation}
Then the centering mismatch has the exact structured form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-link-M}
 M_t=\frac1{\sigma^2}\sum_{s<t}v_{s,t}\bg_s,\qquad
 \|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}\le\frac{\|\bv_t\|_2}{\sigma}.
\end{equation}

\item If $\|\btheta_s\|,\|\btheta_t\|\le R$, the gradients are bounded by
$G$ and are $L_g$-Lipschitz on the radius-$R$ region, then
\begin{align}
 \|\bar{\mathbf r}_t\|_2
 &\le\sqrt{R_t^{\rm col}}+G\sqrt{Q_t},
 &\bar{\mathbf r}_t&=(\bar r_{1,t},\ldots,\bar r_{t-1,t})^\top,
 \label{eq:relative-link-rbar}\\
 \|\mathbf e_t\|_2
 &\le L_gR\sqrt{Q_t},
 &\mathbf e_t&=(e_{1,t},\ldots,e_{t-1,t})^\top,
 \label{eq:relative-link-e}\\
 \|\bv_t\|_2
 &\le \rho_t\!\left(\sqrt{R_t^{\rm col}}+G\sqrt{Q_t}\right)
 +(1+\rho_t)L_gR\sqrt{Q_t}.
 \label{eq:relative-link-v-bound}
\end{align}
Consequently a valid original-center certificate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:relative-link-psi}
 \bar\psi_t^{\rm rel}:=\frac{\zeta_t}{\sqrt\lambda}
 +\frac{\rho_t(\sqrt{R_t^{\rm col}}+G\sqrt{Q_t})
 +(1+\rho_t)L_gR\sqrt{Q_t}}{\sigma}.
\end{equation}
The right side remains valid after replacing $\rho_t$ by a predictable upper
bound $\bar\rho_t$.
\end{enumerate}
The exact $\rho_t$ requires replaying $\phi_s$ and retaining at least the
collection scalar $q_s$ (or $h'(q_s)$) for every sample.  Computing the exact
$v_{s,t}$ additionally uses the stored reward and the same scalar-link replay;
$q_s$ suffices because both $\bar r_{s,t}$ and $e_{s,t}$ can be reconstructed
from $q_s,q_{s,t},h(q_s)$, and $h'(q_s)$.  Thus the structured replay uses
$O(t)$ scalar metadata in addition to raw-example replay.  The Welford
quantities $Q_t,R_t^{\rm col}$ remain $O(d)$ path-summary state, but neither
implementation is $O(d)$ total memory.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Equation~\eqref{eq:relative-link-gradients} is the chain rule.  Applying it to
each column of~\eqref{eq:whitened-cols} gives
\eqref{eq:relative-link-D}.  Since the operator norm of the diagonal matrix is
$\rho_t$ and $\|\Gmat_t\|_{\rm op}\le1$ by \Cref{lem:whitened},
$\chi_t\le\rho_t$.  Equations~\eqref{eq:relative-link-transfer} and
\eqref{eq:relative-link-sandwich} then follow respectively from the one-sided
part of \Cref{lem:whitened} and \Cref{cor:drift-sandwich}.

Substitute $\delta_{s,t}=(c_{s,t}-1)\bg_s$ in the definition of $M_t$:
\[
 \bar r_{s,t}\delta_{s,t}+e_{s,t}\bg_s+e_{s,t}\delta_{s,t}
 =\{\bar r_{s,t}(c_{s,t}-1)+c_{s,t}e_{s,t}\}\bg_s.
\]
This proves the first identity in~\eqref{eq:relative-link-M}.  Since
$M_t=\sigma^{-1}\Gmat_t\bv_t$ after left multiplication by
$\bCbar_t^{-1/2}$,
\[
 \|M_t\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
 =\sigma^{-1}\|\Gmat_t\bv_t\|_2
 \le\sigma^{-1}\|\bv_t\|_2,
\]
which proves the second identity and uses equivalently
$\Gmat_t^\top\Gmat_t\preceq I_{t-1}$.

Finally let $d_{s,t}=\|\btheta_t-\btheta_s\|$.  The collection residual
$c_s=\mu_{\btheta_s}(z_s)-r_s$ gives
$\bar r_{s,t}=c_s+\bg_s^\top(\btheta_t-\btheta_s)$.  The triangle and
Cauchy--Schwarz inequalities therefore yield
$\|\bar{\mathbf r}_t\|_2\le\sqrt{R_t^{\rm col}}+G\sqrt{Q_t}$.
Taylor's theorem gives $|e_{s,t}|\le(L_g/2)d_{s,t}^2$; because both parameters
lie in the radius-$R$ region, $d_{s,t}\le2R$, hence
$|e_{s,t}|\le L_gR d_{s,t}$ and
$\|\mathbf e_t\|_2\le L_gR\sqrt{Q_t}$.  Applying the triangle inequality to
$\bv_t=\operatorname{Diag}(\mathbf c_t-\mathbf{1})\bar{\mathbf r}_t
+\operatorname{Diag}(\mathbf c_t)\mathbf e_t$, using
$\|\mathbf c_t-\mathbf{1}\|_\infty=\rho_t$ and
$\|\mathbf c_t\|_\infty\le1+\rho_t$, proves
\eqref{eq:relative-link-v-bound}.  Combine this with
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred} and~\eqref{eq:relative-link-M} to obtain
\eqref{eq:relative-link-psi}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Sharp two-sided feature-drift sandwich]\label{cor:drift-sandwich}
If $\chi_t<1$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:drift-sandwich}
  (1-\chi_t)^2\,\bCbar_t
  \;\preceq\;
  \bC_t
  \;\preceq\;
  (1+\chi_t)^2\,\bCbar_t .
\end{equation}
Consequently, if a predictable certificate satisfies
$\chi_t\le\bar\chi_t<1$, then
$(1-\bar\chi_t)^2\bCbar_t\preceq\bC_t
\preceq(1+\bar\chi_t)^2\bCbar_t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Because $\Amat_t^\top\Amat_t=\bI_d$, all $d$ singular values of the
$(d+n_t)\times d$ matrix $\Amat_t$ equal one.  Singular-value perturbation
and~\eqref{eq:stacked-identities} give, for $j=1,\ldots,d$,
\[
 |\sigma_j(\Bmat_t)-1|
 \le\|\Bmat_t-\Amat_t\|_{\rm op}=\chi_t.
\]
For $\chi_t<1$, every singular value of $\Bmat_t$ therefore lies in
$[1-\chi_t,1+\chi_t]$.  Squaring the singular values and using
$\Bmat_t^\top\Bmat_t=\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bC_t\bCbar_t^{-1/2}$ yields
$(1-\chi_t)^2\bI\preceq
\bCbar_t^{-1/2}\bC_t\bCbar_t^{-1/2}
\preceq(1+\chi_t)^2\bI$.  Congruence by the principal
$\bCbar_t^{1/2}$ proves~\eqref{eq:drift-sandwich}.
If $0\le\chi_t\le\bar\chi_t<1$, then
$(1-\bar\chi_t)^2\le(1-\chi_t)^2$ and
$(1+\chi_t)^2\le(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$, proving the certificate version.
\end{proof}

The main theorem uses only the one-sided bound~\eqref{eq:onesided-C}, which
holds for \emph{any} $\chi_t\ge0$; the smallness condition
$\chi_t<1$ is needed only for the lower half of the two-sided
sandwich~\eqref{eq:drift-sandwich}, which in turn is used by the explicit
near-linear corollary and by any optional analysis that
explicitly assumes a two-sided sandwich.  An unrescaled current-parameter window is
handled by \Cref{prop:window} through a one-sided relation and does not require this
condition.
The binding quantity is the \emph{whitened $\ell_2$ accumulation}
$\chi_t\le\|\Dmat_t\|_F$ of replayed-Jacobian drift, measured in the
$\bCbar_t^{-1}$ metric---a relative, whitened $\ell_2$ bound that can be sharper
than an $\ell_1$ sum normalized only by $\lambda$ when the drift occurs in
well-observed directions (it credits directions the design has already
explored), though it does not dominate uniformly.  Together,
\Cref{lem:primitive-pred,lem:whitened} identify exact whitened diagnostic
targets.  The operational upper bounds in \Cref{lem:path-certificates} replace
those targets by the predictable path statistics $Q_t$ and $R_t$, the certified
optimizer residual $\zeta_t$, and analytic smoothness constants.  Thus
$\bar\psi_t$ and $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ require only $O(d)$ additional state;
the exact whitened quantities remain post-hoc diagnostics used to measure the
conservatism of these certificates.

Positive definiteness of both~$\bC_t$ and~$\bCbar_t$ follows from
$\lambda>0$ and the outer-product form; it is not an independent assumption.
All theoretical results hold under these explicit assumptions; we do
\emph{not} claim a regret guarantee for the full nonlinear model outside
the local-linearization regime.



\subsection{Novelty Relative to Prior Neural Bandits}\label{sec:novelty}

NeuralUCB/NeuralTS use historical-time full-gradient Grams in their stated
algorithms, while NeuralLinear and last-layer Laplace restrict uncertainty to a
learned representation or final layer
\citep{zhou2020neural,zhang2021neural,riquelme2018deep,kristiadi2020being}.
Matrix-free full-network predictive-variance solves also predate this work
\citep{maddox2021fast}.  CC-UCB's narrower adaptation is to replay every
historical Jacobian at the current parameter, separate the predictable frozen
confidence Gram from that algorithmic operator, and expose centering, drift,
transfer, and residual-checked CG terms.  We claim neither the CG/CVP primitive
nor a prior assertion of uniform full-metric superiority as new.

The one-sided whitened result in \Cref{lem:whitened} is the main technical
step: optimism transfer needs no lower spectral comparison.  The determinant
formula is bookkeeping rather than a standalone rate, and the supplied-$U$
closure is exactly the projected Gram in \eqref{eq:projected-width}, not a
concrete neural-training theorem.  \Cref{sec:related} compares NTK, regression,
EKF/LO-FI, structured Laplace, and sketching alternatives.



\subsection{Computable Curvature Operator}\label{sec:curvop}

Applying $\bC_t\bv = \lambda\bv + \sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\bv\bigr]$
\emph{naively} requires one curvature--vector product (CVP) per historical
sample per CG step---cost $O(It)$ CVPs per round.
Each CVP evaluates one Jacobian--vector product (forward mode) followed by
one vector--Jacobian product (reverse mode) through the network, at roughly
the cost of one gradient evaluation (one JVP plus one VJP)\footnote{Implementations based on double-backward Hessian--vector
products cost closer to two gradient-equivalents per CVP; the constant
does not affect the $O(\cdot)$ accounting.}; we do not store
per-sample gradient features~$\bg_s$, only a constant number of $O(d)$ CG vectors
for CG, with CVPs computed on the fly.
We describe three strategies that limit the per-step cost to $|\mathcal{S}_t|$
CVPs:

\paragraph{(i) Subsampled GGN (worst-case spectral baseline).}
At each round draw a uniform subsample $\mathcal{S}_t\subset\{1,\dots,t{-}1\}$
of size~$n$ (without replacement) and set $w_s=(t{-}1)/n$ to obtain the
rescaled operator.
The following lemma is a worst-case uniform spectral guarantee.

\begin{lemma}[Worst-case uniform spectral guarantee for subsampled GGN]\label{lem:subsample}
Fix round~$t$ and set $m=t{-}1$.
Let $Y_s = \sigma^{-2}\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$ for
$s=1,\dots,m$, so that $\bC_t = \lambda\bI + V$ with $V=\sum_{s=1}^m Y_s$.
Each $Y_s$ is PSD with $\|Y_s\|_{\mathrm{op}}\le G^2/\sigma^2 =: B$.
For $\delta\in(0,1)$ and $1\le n\le m$, draw $\mathcal{S}_t$ of size~$n$
uniformly without replacement from
$\{1,\dots,m\}$ and define $\hat V = \tfrac{m}{n}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}Y_s$
and $\hat\bC_t = \lambda\bI + \hat V$.
Write $L:=\log(2d/\delta)$.

\emph{(i) Deviation bound.}
With probability $\ge 1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subsample-dev}
  \|\hat V - V\|_{\mathrm{op}}
  \;\le\;
  mB\sqrt{\frac{2L}{n}}
  \;+\;
  \frac{4\,mB\,L}{3\,n}.
\end{equation}

\emph{(ii) Sufficient sample size.}
For any $\varepsilon\in(0,1)$, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:subsample-n}
  n \;\ge\; 8L\,\biggl[\,
    \frac{m^2\,B^2}{\varepsilon^2\,\lambda^2}
    \;+\;
    \frac{m\,B}{3\,\varepsilon\,\lambda}
  \biggr],
\end{equation}
then $\Pr[\|\hat V - V\|_{\mathrm{op}} > \varepsilon\lambda] \le \delta$
and consequently
$(1{-}\varepsilon)\bC_t\preceq\hat\bC_t\preceq(1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The $\{Y_s\}_{s=1}^m$ are fixed PSD self-adjoint matrices (deterministic
conditional on the history $\cF_{t-1}$ and the current
parameter~$\btheta_t$).
The displayed proof is for \emph{with-replacement} (i.i.d.) sampling.
The without-replacement version follows from the domination argument of
\citet{gross2010note}, building on Hoeffding's reduction
\citep{hoeffding1963probability}: the matrix moment-generating function
of a sum sampled without replacement from a fixed finite collection of
self-adjoint matrices is dominated by that of the corresponding
i.i.d.\ sum, so every tail bound derived from the matrix
Laplace-transform method, including the matrix Bernstein inequality of
\citet[Thm.~6.1.1]{tropp2015introduction}, carries over with the same
constants.
In our setting the $\{Y_s\}$ are \emph{fixed} PSD matrices
(deterministic conditional on $\cF_{t-1}$ and~$\btheta_t$) and only
the index set $\mathcal{S}_t$ is random, so the domination argument
applies directly.
We therefore state and prove the bound for i.i.d.\ draws
$s_1,\dots,s_n$ uniform on~$\{1,\dots,m\}$; the
without-replacement guarantee is at least as strong.
Define the centered random matrices
$Z_k = (m/n)Y_{s_k} - V/n$ (mean zero, independent).
The range satisfies
$\|Z_k\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le (m/n)B + (1/n)\|V\| \le 2mB/n =: R$
(using $\|V\|\le mB$).
For the variance parameter, since $Y_s\preceq B\bI$,
\[
  \mathbb{E}[Z_k Z_k^\top]
  \;\preceq\; \tfrac{m^2}{n^2}\mathbb{E}[Y_{s_1}^2]
  \;=\; \tfrac{m}{n^2}\sum_{s=1}^m Y_s^2
  \;\preceq\; \tfrac{m}{n^2}B\,V
  \;\preceq\; \tfrac{m^2B^2}{n^2}\bI,
\]
so $v :=
\bigl\|\sum_{k=1}^n\mathbb{E}[Z_kZ_k^\top]\bigr\|\le m^2B^2/n$.
By the matrix Bernstein inequality
\citep[Thm.~6.1.1]{tropp2015introduction},
$\Pr[\|\hat V{-}V\| \ge t]
\le 2d\exp\!\bigl(-t^2/(2v + 2Rt/3)\bigr)$.
Choosing
$t_0=\sqrt{2vL}+\tfrac{2}{3}RL$
makes the matrix-Bernstein tail probability at most~$\delta$.  Substituting
$v\le m^2B^2/n$ and $R=2mB/n$ gives
$t_0\le mB\sqrt{2L/n}+\tfrac{4mBL}{3n}$,
yielding~\eqref{eq:subsample-dev}.
For~\eqref{eq:subsample-n}, require each summand
in~\eqref{eq:subsample-dev} to be $\le\varepsilon\lambda/2$:
$mB\sqrt{2L/n}\le\varepsilon\lambda/2$ gives
$n\ge 8m^2B^2L/(\varepsilon^2\lambda^2)$, and
$4mBL/(3n)\le\varepsilon\lambda/2$ gives
$n\ge 8mBL/(3\varepsilon\lambda)$.
Summing the two conditions
yields~\eqref{eq:subsample-n}; this is a conservative sufficient
condition (using $a+b$ rather than $\max\{a,b\}$), not a sharp threshold.
The multiplicative sandwich follows:
$\|\hat V{-}V\|\le\varepsilon\lambda$ gives
$\bC_t - \varepsilon\lambda\bI \preceq \hat\bC_t \preceq
\bC_t + \varepsilon\lambda\bI$.
Since $\bC_t\succeq\lambda\bI$, we have
$\varepsilon\lambda\bI \preceq \varepsilon\bC_t$, so
$\bC_t - \varepsilon\lambda\bI \succeq (1{-}\varepsilon)\bC_t$ and
$\bC_t + \varepsilon\lambda\bI \preceq (1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$.
\end{proof}

With $B=G^2/\sigma^2$, the right-hand side of the sufficient
condition~\eqref{eq:subsample-n} is
$O\!\bigl(L\cdot[m^2G^4/(\varepsilon^2\lambda^2\sigma^4)
  + mG^2/(\varepsilon\lambda\sigma^2)]\bigr)$.
The first (variance) term dominates unless
$\varepsilon\lambda\sigma^2\gtrsim mG^2$, so this worst-case sufficient threshold has
a \emph{quadratic leading dependence} on~$m=t{-}1$ (it is a sufficient upper bound on
the required sample size, not an exact or necessary sample complexity).
The condition~\eqref{eq:subsample-n} is conservative and sufficient, not
necessary, with worst-case leading dependence quadratic in $m$.  If its
threshold exceeds the population size $m=t{-}1$, it provides no nontrivial
subsampling guarantee for $n<m$.  Taking $n=m$ recovers the full population
exactly for the separate reason that every summand is included.

\emph{This is a worst-case baseline guarantee, too conservative for
practical use at large~$t$.}
In particular, the bound above does not by itself justify computational
savings at large $t$; it should be read as a worst-case spectral
guarantee rather than as a practical scaling result.
Subsampling reduces computation ($n\ll m$) only when $m$ is moderate or
$\varepsilon\lambda$ is large relative to~$mB$; for large~$t$ the full
history may be needed under the uniform Bernstein bound.
Under additional spectral structure, leverage-score sampling or Frequent
Directions sketches may yield useful approximations with smaller state.
For linear bandits, \citet{kuzborskij2019efficient} give regret bounds in terms
of the Frequent Directions spectral-tail error.  Translating such structure
into a uniform GGN sandwich and a concrete buffer or sketch size is left open.
The Bernstein guarantee is not the mechanism by which we expect
practical savings at scale.  Practical savings require small buffers
treated heuristically, spectral decay/effective dimension,
ridge-leverage sampling, Frequent Directions, or empirical verification
of the spectral sandwich.  We leave structure-aware sampling and empirical
spectral certification as future work.
To obtain a spectral sandwich that holds simultaneously for all
$t\le T$, union-bound over $t$ (replacing $\delta$ by $\delta/T$).
\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} then exposes the one-sided transfer factor and
realized width complexity as operator-dependent terms along the realized path;
it does not identify a standalone causal regret inflation.

\paragraph{(ii) Windowed curvature (unrescaled, current parameter).}
Maintain a sliding window $\mathcal{S}_t\subseteq\{1,\dots,t{-}1\}$ of the most
recent~$n$ interactions with unit weights ($w_s{=}1$), \emph{re-evaluating the
Jacobians at the current parameter}~$\btheta_t$.  This is a nonnegative-weighted
\emph{subset} of the same current-parameter outer products, so
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\bC_t$ (\Cref{prop:window}); \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} then
applies with the \emph{deterministic} approximate-to-full factor $\kappa_{+,t}{=}1$
and the full-to-frozen factor $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$---no two-sided sandwich is
required, though the overall guarantee remains conditional on that transfer
certificate.  The cost of the
smaller operator is not hidden: it surfaces as larger realized widths
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)$ and hence a larger dynamic width complexity~$\Lamalg_T$.  The
same holds for any current-parameter downweighted subset with $w_s\in[0,1]$
(including an unrescaled subsample).  A \emph{stale} window that reuses Jacobians
from an older parameter is \emph{not} covered; see~(iii).

\paragraph{(iii) Periodic refresh (heuristic; stale operator).}
Recompute the curvature operator every~$M$ rounds on a cached buffer and reuse it
for~$M$ consecutive actions.  Because the reused operator evaluates Jacobians at
an \emph{older} parameter, it is no longer a subset of the current-parameter
outer products, so the $\hat\bC_t\preceq\bC_t$ argument of \Cref{prop:window} does
not apply and the stale operator can both over- and under-estimate $\bC_t$.  This
remains a heuristic: \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} covers it only when a
one-sided factor $\kappa_{+,t}$ (an explicit bound
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\kappa_{+,t}\bC_t$) is separately verified, which we evaluate
empirically.  The rescaled subsample of~(i), with weights $w_s=(t{-}1)/n>1$, is
likewise not covered by \Cref{prop:window}; it instead uses the two-sided matrix
Bernstein bound of \Cref{lem:subsample}.

\subsection{Computational Cost Model}\label{sec:systems}

\paragraph{Primitive assumptions.}
CC-UCB requires a single computational primitive: the
\emph{curvature--vector product} (CVP) $\bv\mapsto\bC_t\bv$, which
decomposes into per-sample operations
$\bv\mapsto\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\bv\bigr]$.
Each such operation consists of:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item a Jacobian--vector product (JVP) $\bv\mapsto\bg_t^\top\bv$
  (forward-mode AD, cost $\approx 1\times$ forward pass);
\item a vector--Jacobian product (VJP) $\alpha\mapsto\alpha\,\bg_t$
  (reverse-mode AD, cost $\approx 1\times$ backward pass).
\end{enumerate}
Sharded autodiff can distribute JVP/VJP evaluation, but its communication
pattern and sample parallelism are implementation-dependent and are not
benchmarked here.
For $m_t:=|\mathcal S_t|$, one matrix-free oracle application consists of
$m_t$ sample-CVPs plus $O(d)$ damping and accumulation work.  Thus
$I_{t,a}$ CG recurrences for action $a$ use $I_{t,a}m_t$ sample-CVPs; an
explicit final check of the original-system residual
$\bg_t(a)-\Calg_t\tilde\bu$ uses another $m_t$.  The sequential Krylov state
is $O(d)$ ($O(Kd)$ if right-hand sides are batched), excluding model and
optimizer state, activations, and raw replay.  Caching an explicit
$m_t\times d$ Jacobian matrix instead uses $O(m_td)$ additional state and is
not the on-the-fly JVP/VJP implementation analyzed by this operation count.

Krylov methods for Hessian spectral analysis have been run on networks with
$>10^8$ parameters under distributed training \citep{granziol2026hessian}, which
indicates that curvature--vector primitives of this general form are implementable
at scale.  We do \emph{not} claim foundation-model feasibility from this: that
work times Hessian--vector products, not the exact per-example GGN JVP/VJP used
here.  Our real-\texttt{torch.func} study reaches roughly $10^7$ parameters and
$m=512$ on one accelerator; it does not measure distributed execution or model
training.
Warm-starting CG from the previous round's solution can reduce the
iteration count in practice.

\subsection{An Exact Log-Determinant Bookkeeping Identity}\label{sec:dynamic-potential}

The regret bound is driven not by $\gamma_T$ but by the realized
\emph{width complexity} of the widths the algorithm actually uses,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lam-alg}
  \Lamalg_T \;:=\; \sum_{t=1}^T
  \log\!\bigl(1+\sigma^{-2}\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\bigr).
\end{equation}
Unlike $\bCbar_t$, the algorithmic curvature $\Calg_t$ need \emph{not} be
monotone in~$t$: relinearization, subsampling, windowing, and periodic
refresh can all \emph{shrink} it, so the elementary determinant
telescoping of~\eqref{eq:infogain} no longer applies.  The following lemma is an
exact pathwise bookkeeping identity for a general predictable SPD sequence.  It
is not a standalone rate result: its upper bound is informative only when the
terminal log determinant and determinant-decreasing refresh charge are
independently controlled, as in \Cref{lem:rank-refresh}.

\begin{lemma}[Dynamic log-determinant potential]\label{lem:dynamic-potential}
Let $\{\Calg_t\}_{t\ge1}$ satisfy~\eqref{eq:calg-props} with
$\Calg_1=\lambda\bI$.  For the played feature
$\bh_t:=\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)$ define the rank-one update
$\Calg_t^{+}:=\Calg_t+\sigma^{-2}\bh_t\bh_t^\top$ and, using the symmetric
(principal) root $(\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}$, the normalized perturbation
\begin{equation}\label{eq:xi-def}
  \Xi_t \;:=\; (\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}\,(\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^{+})\,
  (\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2},
\end{equation}
so that $\bI+\Xi_t=(\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}\Calg_{t+1}(\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}$ is SPD;
equivalently, every eigenvalue of $\Xi_t$ exceeds $-1$ and every eigenvalue of
$\bI+\Xi_t$ is positive (with no smallness condition on the jump
$\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^{+}$).  Fix any \emph{end-of-round-$T$} terminal operator
$\Calg_{T+1}$ constructed only from data and randomness available through
round~$T$ ($\cF_T$-measurable, hence not a function of $\bx_{T+1}$, $\cA_{T+1}$,
or future rewards; e.g.\ the canonical rank-one update $\Calg_{T+1}=\Calg_T^{+}$,
or the operator the algorithm commits for round $T{+}1$).  The identity below is
then an a~posteriori statement about the completed sequence
$\Calg_1,\dots,\Calg_{T+1}$; the quantities $\Xi_T$, $\Valg_T$, and $\Gamdyn_T$
are defined relative to this choice, and the bound $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$ holds
for \emph{every} such valid choice.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lam-identity}
  \Lamalg_T
  \;=\;
  \log\frac{\det(\Calg_{T+1})}{\det(\lambda\bI)}
  \;-\;
  \sum_{t=1}^T \log\det(\bI+\Xi_t).
\end{equation}
With the determinant-destroying variation charge and the dynamic potential
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vdyn-def}
  \Valg_T := \sum_{t=1}^T \bigl[-\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)\bigr]_+,
  \qquad
  \Gamdyn_T := \log\frac{\det(\Calg_{T+1})}{\det(\lambda\bI)} + \Valg_T,
\end{equation}
we have $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$, and, using
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\le G^2/\lambda$ and $\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:width-sum-dyn}
  \sum_{t=1}^T s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)
  \;\le\;
  (\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T
  \;\le\;
  (\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Gamdyn_T .
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By the matrix determinant lemma applied to
$\Calg_t^{+}=\Calg_t+\sigma^{-2}\bh_t\bh_t^\top$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:detratio-plus}
  \frac{\det(\Calg_t^{+})}{\det(\Calg_t)}
  = 1+\sigma^{-2}\bh_t^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bh_t
  = 1+\sigma^{-2}s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t).
\end{equation}
Writing $B_t:=(\Calg_t^{+})^{-1/2}$, we have
$\bI+\Xi_t=B_t\Calg_t^{+}B_t+B_t(\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^{+})B_t
=B_t\Calg_{t+1}B_t$, a congruence of the SPD matrix $\Calg_{t+1}$ by the
invertible symmetric $B_t$, hence SPD (in particular its eigenvalues exceed
$-1$ with no smallness condition on the jump $\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^{+}$), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:detratio-xi}
  \frac{\det(\Calg_{t+1})}{\det(\Calg_t^{+})}
  = \det\!\bigl(B_t^{-1}(\bI+\Xi_t)B_t^{-1}\bigr)\big/\det(\Calg_t^{+})
  = \det(\bI+\Xi_t),
\end{equation}
using $\det(\Calg_{t+1})=\det(\Calg_t^{+})\det(\bI+\Xi_t)$.
Taking logs of~\eqref{eq:detratio-plus}--\eqref{eq:detratio-xi} and adding,
\[
  \log\!\bigl(1+\sigma^{-2}s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\bigr)
  = \log\det(\Calg_{t+1}) - \log\det(\Calg_t) - \log\det(\bI+\Xi_t).
\]
Summing over $t=1,\dots,T$ telescopes the first difference to
$\log\det(\Calg_{T+1})-\log\det(\Calg_1)$; with $\Calg_1=\lambda\bI$ this is
$\log[\det(\Calg_{T+1})/\det(\lambda\bI)]$, proving~\eqref{eq:lam-identity}.
Since $-\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)\le[-\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)]_+$ term by term,
$-\sum_t\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)\le\Valg_T$, so $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$.
For~\eqref{eq:width-sum-dyn}: $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ gives
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)=\bh_t^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bh_t\le\|\bh_t\|^2/\lambda\le
G^2/\lambda=:c_{\max}\sigma^2$.  With $x_t:=\sigma^{-2}s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\in
[0,c_{\max}]$ and $\log(1+x)\ge x/(1+x)\ge x/(1+c_{\max})$,
$\Lamalg_T\ge(1+c_{\max})^{-1}\sum_t x_t$, i.e.
$\sum_t s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\le(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\Lamalg_T$; the second
inequality is $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation and scope of the dynamic potential]
Identity~\eqref{eq:lam-identity} is pathwise.  $\Valg_T$ charges only changes
that reduce determinant relative to the rank-one update; determinant-increasing
changes contribute zero.  If $\Calg_1\ne\lambda\bI$, the denominator becomes
$\det(\Calg_1)$.  Neither $\Valg_T$ nor the endpoint term is generally small.
Bounding both is sufficient, but not necessary because the exact identity may
cancel them.  Thus the identity does not imply sublinear regret; outside
\Cref{cor:rank-near-linear,thm:growing-window}, controlling $\Lamalg_T$ for an
adapting backbone remains open.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Rank-sensitive refresh bound]\label{lem:rank-refresh}
Let $\mathcal R=\{t:\Calg_{t+1}\ne\Calg_t^+\}$.  Suppose that for every
$t\in\mathcal R$, the negative part $(\Xi_t)_-=(-\Xi_t)_+$ has rank at
most $r_t$ and
$\lambda_{\min}(\bI+\Xi_t)\ge1-\nu_t$ for $0\le\nu_t<1$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rank-refresh}
 \Valg_T\le\sum_{t\in\mathcal R}r_t\log\frac1{1-\nu_t}.
\end{equation}
If additionally
$\Calg_{T+1}=\lambda\bI+A$, where $A\succeq0$,
$\operatorname{rank}(A)\le r_{\rm end}$, and
$\operatorname{tr}(A)\le W_{\rm end}G^2/\sigma^2$ for a specified total
endpoint weight $W_{\rm end}\ge0$, then, for $r_{\rm end}>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:endpoint-rank}
 \Lamalg_T\le r_{\rm end}\log\!\left(
 1+\frac{W_{\rm end}G^2}{r_{\rm end}\lambda\sigma^2}\right)
 +\sum_{t\in\mathcal R}r_t\log\frac1{1-\nu_t}.
\end{equation}
For $r_{\rm end}=0$, the first term is defined as zero.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Let $\xi_{t,i}$ be the eigenvalues of $\Xi_t$.  At most $r_t$ are
negative, each is at least $-\nu_t$, and every nonnegative eigenvalue makes
a nonpositive contribution to $-\sum_i\log(1+\xi_{t,i})$.  Hence
$[-\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)]_+\le r_t\log(1/(1-\nu_t))$, proving
\eqref{eq:rank-refresh}.  List the nonzero eigenvalues of $A$ and pad with
zeros to obtain $a_1,\ldots,a_{r_{\rm end}}$.  Concavity gives
\[
 \sum_i\log(1+a_i/\lambda)
 \le r_{\rm end}\log\!\left(1+
 \frac{\sum_i a_i}{r_{\rm end}\lambda}\right),
\]
and the trace premise plus \Cref{lem:dynamic-potential} proves
\eqref{eq:endpoint-rank}.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Consequences.}
Sequential Gram updates have $\Xi_t=0$.  If canonical rank-one updates are
used outside $J_T$ refresh rounds, and each refresh has
$r_t\le r$, $\nu_t\le\nu<1$, then
$\Valg_T\le J_Tr\log(1/(1-\nu))$; a geometric schedule is therefore
polylogarithmic only when these rank and spectral-floor bounds hold.
For a replacement
$\Calg_{t+1}-\Calg_t^+=A_t^{\rm new}-A_t^{\rm old}$ with both terms PSD
and $\operatorname{rank}(A_t^{\rm old})\le r$, Sylvester inertia gives
$r_t\le r$, but a spectral floor is still required.

For a fixed-feature window of length $m$, after warm-up each deletion has
rank one.  Writing $N_{\rm drop}=\max\{0,T-m\}$, Sherman--Morrison gives the
following exact charge for each $t>m$.  Let
$v_t^{\rm drop}=\sigma^{-1}\bg_{t-m}(\bx_{t-m},a_{t-m})$ and let
$D_t=\Calg_t^+-v_t^{\rm drop}(v_t^{\rm drop})^\top=\Calg_{t+1}$ be the
remaining-window matrix.  Then
$-\log\det(\bI+\Xi_t)=
\log[1+(v_t^{\rm drop})^\top D_t^{-1}v_t^{\rm drop}]$, and therefore
\[
 \Valg_T\le N_{\rm drop}\log\!\left(1+\frac{G^2}{\lambda\sigma^2}\right).
\]
This can be linear in $T$.  A relinearized window need not be a rank-one
replacement.  Likewise, merely holding a stale operator fixed does not make
non-refresh transitions canonical rank-one updates, so geometric refresh timing
alone does not imply a logarithmic charge.

\begin{corollary}[Observable CG upper bound on the width complexity]\label{cor:lam-observable}
If the played-action CG solve satisfies $\varepsilon_{t,a_t}\le\bar\varepsilon_t<1$
so that $\tilde s_t^2(a_t)\ge(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)$
(\Cref{lem:cg}), then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:lam-observable}
  \Lamalg_T
  \;\le\;
  \widehat{\Lamalg}_T
  \;:=\;
  \sum_{t=1}^T
  \log\!\Bigl(1+\frac{\tilde s_t^2(a_t)}{\sigma^2\,(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\le\tilde s_t^2(a_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)$ and
$x\mapsto\log(1+\sigma^{-2}x)$ is increasing, so each summand
of~$\Lamalg_T$ is dominated term by term.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This gives an observable upper bound on the dynamic width-complexity term,
conditional on the certified CG tolerances; $\Valg_T$ itself is \emph{not}
observable in general (it requires determinant estimation of the curvature
sequence).



\subsection{CG Approximation Lemma}

\begin{lemma}[CG multiplicative accuracy]\label{lem:cg}
Fix a round~$t$ and an action~$a$ with right-hand side
$\bg:=\bg_t(a)\ne\mathbf0$.  Let $\bu = \Calg_t^{-1}\bg$ and let $\tilde\bu$ be
the iterate after $I_{t,a}$ steps of CG on the SPD system $\Calg_t\bu=\bg$
starting from $\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf{0}$, with $\Calg_t$ held \emph{fixed} across
all iterations.  The relative energy-norm error is the per-action quantity
$\varepsilon_{t,a}$ of~\eqref{eq:cg-err-eta}, and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-rate}
  \varepsilon_{t,a}
  \;=\;
  \frac{\|\bu-\tilde\bu\|_{\Calg_t}}{\|\bu\|_{\Calg_t}}
  \;\le\;
  2\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}-1}{\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}+1}\right)^{\!I_{t,a}},
\end{equation}
where
$\kappa_t:=\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)/\lambda_{\min}(\Calg_t)$ is the true
condition number and $\bar\kappa_t\ge\kappa_t$ is a certified
$\cHist_t$-measurable upper bound.  The convergence rate in~\eqref{eq:cg-rate} is right-hand-side
independent, so it holds for every action with the same $\bar\kappa_t$.
For a \emph{nonnegative weighted outer-product} operator
$\Calg_t=\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s\,
\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$ ($w_s\ge0$, so
$\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI\succ0$ and CG is well-posed),
\[
\kappa_t\le\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)}{\lambda}
\le1+\frac{W_tG^2}{\lambda\sigma^2}=: \bar\kappa_t,
\]
with $W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s$ and
$\|\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\|\le G$ from \Cref{asm:bounded}; here $W_t=n$ for unrescaled
windowed/refresh buffers, giving $\bar\kappa_t$ and $\alpha_t$ independent
of~$T$, and $W_t=t{-}1$ for full history and for rescaled uniform subsampling
with $w_s=(t{-}1)/n$.  For a general SPD operator that is not such an outer
product, $\bar\kappa_t$ must be certified separately.
Write $\tilde{s}_t^2(a) = \bg^\top\tilde\bu$ and
$s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)=\bg^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bg$.

\textbf{Assume $\varepsilon_{t,a} < 1$}
(see \Cref{app:cg} for the minimum~$I_{t,a}$ ensuring this as a function
of~$\bar\kappa_t$;
\Cref{alg:ccucb} enforces a known \emph{per-round} tolerance~$\bar\varepsilon_t$
uniformly over the scored actions, $\max_a\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t<1$,
via adaptive residual stopping or a fixed budget under a certified
condition-number bound; see \S\ref{sec:method}).
Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-quad}
  (1-\varepsilon_{t,a})\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)
  \;\le\;
  \tilde{s}_t^2(a)
  \;\le\;
  (1+\varepsilon_{t,a})\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a).
\end{equation}
For $\bg_t(a)=\mathbf0$ the algorithm sets
$\tilde{s}_t^2(a)=0=s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$ and~\eqref{eq:cg-quad} holds trivially.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The CG minimax property gives
$\|e_I\|_{\Calg_t}/\|e_0\|_{\Calg_t}
\le\min_{p\in\mathcal P_I,\,p(0)=1}
\max_{x\in[\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}]}|p(x)|$.
Choosing the scaled Chebyshev polynomial bounds this maximum by
$2[(\sqrt{\kappa_t}-1)/(\sqrt{\kappa_t}+1)]^I$; this ratio is increasing in
$\kappa_t$, so $\bar\kappa_t\ge\kappa_t$ proves~\eqref{eq:cg-rate}.
$\tilde{s}_t^2(a) - s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)
= \bg^\top(\tilde\bu-\bu)
= (\Calg_t\bu)^\top(\tilde\bu-\bu)
= \bu^\top\Calg_t(\tilde\bu-\bu)$.
By Cauchy--Schwarz in the $\Calg_t$-norm,
$|\bu^\top\Calg_t(\tilde\bu-\bu)|
\le\|\bu\|_{\Calg_t}\|\tilde\bu-\bu\|_{\Calg_t}
\le\varepsilon_{t,a}\|\bu\|_{\Calg_t}^2 = \varepsilon_{t,a}\,s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$.
For the outer-product special case the condition-number bound follows from
$\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)\le\lambda+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t} w_s\|\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\|^2
\le\lambda+W_tG^2/\sigma^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Fixed-preconditioner CG accuracy]\label{lem:pcg}
Fix round $t$ and let $P_t\in\R^{d\times d}$ be an
$\cHist_t$-measurable SPD preconditioner, held fixed and applied as the same
exact linear map throughout every iteration of a solve.  For
$\bg=\bg_t(a)\ne0$, define the symmetric transformed system
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pcg-transformed-system}
 \widehat C_t\by=\widehat\bg,\qquad
 \widehat C_t=P_t^{-1/2}\Calg_tP_t^{-1/2},\qquad
 \widehat\bg=P_t^{-1/2}\bg,
\end{equation}
using principal square roots.  If CG starts from zero on this system and
$\tilde\by_I$ is its $I$th iterate for $I\ge1$, set
$\bu=\Calg_t^{-1}\bg$, $\by=P_t^{1/2}\bu$, and
$\tilde\bu_I=P_t^{-1/2}\tilde\by_I$.  Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pcg-energy-identity}
 \frac{\|\bu-\tilde\bu_I\|_{\Calg_t}}{\|\bu\|_{\Calg_t}}
 =\frac{\|\by-\tilde\by_I\|_{\widehat C_t}}
 {\|\by\|_{\widehat C_t}}
 \le2\left(\frac{\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t^P}-1}
 {\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t^P}+1}\right)^I,
\end{equation}
where
$\bar\kappa_t^P\ge\kappa(\widehat C_t)$ is a certified predictable bound.
In particular, positive $\cHist_t$-measurable certified quantities
$\ell_t,L_t$ satisfying the spectral equivalence
$\ell_tP_t\preceq\Calg_t\preceq L_tP_t$ with
$0<\ell_t\le L_t$ give
$\bar\kappa_t^P=L_t/\ell_t$.

Writing the left side of~\eqref{eq:pcg-energy-identity} as
$\varepsilon_{t,a}^P$, the approximate width
$\tilde s_{P,t}^2(a)=\bg^\top\tilde\bu_I$ satisfies, whenever
$\varepsilon_{t,a}^P<1$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pcg-width-sandwich}
 (1-\varepsilon_{t,a}^P)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)
 \le\tilde s_{P,t}^2(a)
 \le(1+\varepsilon_{t,a}^P)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a).
\end{equation}

There is also a directly recomputable residual certificate.  For
$\br_I=\bg-\Calg_t\tilde\bu_I$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pcg-residual-certificate}
 \varepsilon_{t,a}^P
 \le\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t^P}
 \left(\frac{\br_I^\top P_t^{-1}\br_I}
 {\bg^\top P_t^{-1}\bg}\right)^{1/2}.
\end{equation}
Thus stopping only after the preconditioned residual ratio is at most
$\bar\varepsilon_t/\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t^P}$ certifies
$\varepsilon_{t,a}^P\le\bar\varepsilon_t$.  For $\bg=0$, both widths are
defined as zero.  Taking $P_t=I$ recovers ordinary CG.  The lemma makes no
claim that a particular preconditioner improves the condition number; that
requires a proved or measured spectral-equivalence bound.  Iteration-varying
or inexact nonlinear preconditioners are outside this fixed transformed-CG
argument.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The substitutions $\bu=P_t^{-1/2}\by$ and
$\tilde\bu_I=P_t^{-1/2}\tilde\by_I$ give
\[
 \|\bu-\tilde\bu_I\|_{\Calg_t}^2
 =\|\by-\tilde\by_I\|_{\widehat C_t}^2,
 \qquad
 \|\bu\|_{\Calg_t}^2=\|\by\|_{\widehat C_t}^2.
\]
The standard zero-start CG Chebyshev bound on the SPD matrix $\widehat C_t$
proves~\eqref{eq:pcg-energy-identity}.  Congruence of
$\ell_tP_t\preceq\Calg_t\preceq L_tP_t$ by $P_t^{-1/2}$ puts the spectrum of
$\widehat C_t$ in $[\ell_t,L_t]$.

Also
$\bg^\top(\tilde\bu_I-\bu)=
\bu^\top\Calg_t(\tilde\bu_I-\bu)$, so Cauchy--Schwarz in the
$\Calg_t$ energy norm proves~\eqref{eq:pcg-width-sandwich}, exactly as in
\Cref{lem:cg}.  Finally the transformed residual is
$\widehat\br_I=P_t^{-1/2}\br_I$.  If
$m_t=\lambda_{\min}(\widehat C_t)$ and
$M_t^P=\lambda_{\max}(\widehat C_t)$, then
\[
 \|\by-\tilde\by_I\|_{\widehat C_t}
 \le\frac{\|\widehat\br_I\|_2}{\sqrt{m_t}},
 \qquad
 \|\by\|_{\widehat C_t}
 \ge\frac{\|\widehat\bg\|_2}{\sqrt{M_t^P}}.
\]
Their ratio is at most
$\sqrt{\kappa(\widehat C_t)}
\|\widehat\br_I\|_2/\|\widehat\bg\|_2$, which yields
\eqref{eq:pcg-residual-certificate} after using the certified upper bound.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{The algorithmic bonus.}
With the \emph{per-round} certified tolerance
$\bar\varepsilon_t\ge\max_a\varepsilon_{t,a}$ and the one-sided transfer factor
$u_t(a)\ge1$ of \Cref{asm:transfer}, \Cref{alg:ccucb} plays the bonus
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bonus}
  w_t(a)
  \;:=\;
  \omega_t\,
  \sqrt{\frac{u_t(a)}{1-\bar\varepsilon_t}}\,
  \sqrt{\tilde{s}_t^2(a)},
\end{equation}
where $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$ is the predictable confidence upper bound,
$\bar\psi_t$ the centering certificate (\Cref{asm:optim}), and
\begin{equation}\label{eq:omega-def}
  \omega_t := \bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t
\end{equation}
is the combined confidence-plus-centering width factor.  Define the
\emph{per-round} CG inflation factor
$\alpha_t:=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$.

\begin{lemma}[Bonus sandwiching]\label{lem:bonus}
Under \Cref{asm:transfer} and $\max_{a\in\cA_t}\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t<1$,
the bonus~\eqref{eq:bonus} satisfies, for every $a\in\cA_t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bonus-sandwich}
  \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a)
  \;\le\;
  w_t(a)
  \;\le\;
  \alpha_t\,\omega_t\,\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $\bg_t(a)=\mathbf0$ all three terms vanish and the bound is trivial.
Otherwise \Cref{lem:cg} with $\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t$ gives
$(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)\le\tilde s_t^2(a)\le
(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$.  The lower bound and~$\omega_t\ge0$
give $\sqrt{\tilde s_t^2(a)}\ge\sqrt{1-\bar\varepsilon_t}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)$,
so $w_t(a)\ge\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)$; then~\eqref{eq:transfer}
gives $\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)\ge\bar s_t(a)$, i.e.\
$w_t(a)\ge\omega_t\bar s_t(a)$.  The upper bound gives
$\sqrt{\tilde s_t^2(a)}\le\sqrt{1+\bar\varepsilon_t}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)$, whence
$w_t(a)\le\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}\sqrt{1+\bar\varepsilon_t}\,
s_{\Calg,t}(a)=\alpha_t\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a)$.
Both bounds are per-round; no horizon maximum is taken before summing.
\end{proof}



\subsection{Confidence and Discrepancy Lemmas}

\begin{lemma}[Confidence for the linearized ridge estimator]%
\label{lem:confidence}
Suppose \Cref{asm:noise,asm:bounded,asm:linear} hold and
$\mu^*=\mu_{\btheta^*}$ with $\|\btheta^*\|\le S$.
Define the linearized ridge estimator~$\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$
by~\eqref{eq:thetahat-lin}, the frozen-feature variance
$\bar s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)}$, and
the cumulative squared linearization error
$F_t := \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\epslin(s)^2$.
Then with probability at least $1-\delta$, for all $t\ge 1$ and all
$a\in\cA_t$ simultaneously,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf-lin}
  \bigl|\bg_t(a)^\top
    \bigl(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}-\btheta^*\bigr)\bigr|
  \;\le\;
  \bar s_t(a)\,\beta_t,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:beta}
  \beta_t
  \;:=\;
  \sqrt{\gamma_{t-1} + 2\log(1/\delta)}
  \;+\; \sqrt{\lambda}\,S
  \;+\; \frac{1}{\sigma}\sqrt{F_t}.
\end{equation}
(Here $\gamma_{t-1}=\log\det(\bCbar_t)/\det(\lambda\bI)$ is the
information gain through round $t{-}1$.)

The quantity $\beta_t$ in~\eqref{eq:beta} is a theoretical radius.
The algorithm may use any predictable upper bound
$\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$; all results below remain valid with
$\bar\beta_t$ in place of~$\beta_t$.
This is necessary because $\beta_t$ depends on the unknown cumulative
linearization term~$F_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
From~\eqref{eq:pseudo-response},
$y_s = \bg_s^\top\btheta^* + \rho_s + \eta_s$ with
$|\rho_s|\le\epslin(s)$.
The closed form of $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$ gives
\[
  \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}} - \btheta^*
  = -\lambda\bCbar_t^{-1}\btheta^*
    + \bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s<t}\bg_s\rho_s
    + \bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s<t}\bg_s\eta_s.
\]
Taking the inner product with~$\bg_t(a)$ and bounding in absolute value:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item\emph{Prior bias.}\;
$|\lambda\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\btheta^*|
\le \lambda\,\bar s_t(a)\,\|\btheta^*\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
\le \bar s_t(a)\sqrt{\lambda}\,S$.
(Cauchy--Schwarz in the $\bCbar_t$-norm;
$\lambda\|\btheta^*\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
\le \lambda\|\btheta^*\|/\sqrt{\lambda_{\min}(\bCbar_t)}
\le \sqrt{\lambda}\,S$
since $\bCbar_t\succeq\lambda\bI$.)
\item\emph{Linearization bias.}\;
Define $\Phi=[\bg_1/\sigma,\dots,\bg_{t-1}/\sigma]$ (the $d\times(t{-}1)$
design matrix) and $\hat\rho=(\rho_1,\dots,\rho_{t-1})^\top$.
Then $\sigma^{-2}\sum\bg_s\rho_s = \sigma^{-1}\Phi\hat\rho$.
Since $\bCbar_t \succeq \Phi\Phi^\top$, we have
$\Phi^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\Phi \preceq \bI$, so
$\|\Phi\hat\rho\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
= \|\hat\rho\|_{\Phi^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\Phi}
\le \|\hat\rho\| = \sqrt{F_t}$.
Therefore
$|\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\sigma^{-1}\Phi\hat\rho|
\le \bar s_t(a)\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{F_t}$.
\item\emph{Noise.}\;
\[
  \left\|
  \sum_{s<t}\frac{\bg_s}{\sigma}\frac{\eta_s}{\sigma}
  \right\|_{\bCbar_t^{-1}}
  \le
  \sqrt{\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta)}
\]
holds for all $t\ge 1$ with probability at least $1-\delta$ by the
time-uniform vector self-normalized martingale bound
\citep{abbasi2011improved}.  Here $x_s=\bg_s/\sigma$ is predictable
with respect to the pre-reward sigma-algebra $\cG_s$, and
$\xi_s=\eta_s/\sigma$ is conditionally $1$-sub-Gaussian given
$\cG_s$.  For any candidate action $a$, Cauchy--Schwarz in the
$\bCbar_t$-norm gives
\[
  \left|
  \bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}
  \sum_{s<t}\frac{\bg_s\eta_s}{\sigma^2}
  \right|
  \le
  \bar s_t(a)\sqrt{\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta)} .
\]

\end{enumerate}
Summing gives~\eqref{eq:conf-lin}--\eqref{eq:beta}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Predictable confidence schedule]\label{cor:beta-constructive}
Suppose a nonnegative $\cHist_t$-measurable sequence
$\{\bar F_t\}_{t\ge 1}$
satisfies $F_t \le \bar F_t$ for all~$t$.
Then \Cref{alg:ccucb} may set
\[
  \bar\beta_t \;:=\;
  \sqrt{\gamma_{t-1} + 2\log(1/\delta)}
  \;+\; \sqrt{\lambda}\,S
  \;+\; \sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\bar F_t},
\]
which is predictable and satisfies $\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$\bar F_t \ge F_t$ term-by-term implies each summand in~\eqref{eq:beta}
is dominated, so $\bar\beta_t \ge \beta_t$.
Predictability holds because $\gamma_{t-1}$, $S$, $\bar F_t$, $\lambda$,
$\sigma$, and $\delta$ are all determined before round~$t$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
\Cref{lem:path-certificates} supplies the computable choice
$\bar F_t=\sum_{s<t}(\bar\epsilon_s^{\rm lin})^2$; a tighter externally
certified predictable sequence may replace it.

\begin{corollary}[Observable information-gain surrogate via the one-sided transfer factor]\label{cor:gammahat}
Run \Cref{alg:ccucb} with a predictable algorithmic curvature
sequence~$\Calg_s$, per-round CG tolerance with
$\varepsilon_{s,a_s}\le\bar\varepsilon_s<1$ for the played action, and an
$\cHist_s$-measurable one-sided optimism-transfer factor $u_s(a)\ge1$ satisfying
$\bar s_s^2(a)\le u_s(a)\,s_{\Calg,s}^2(a)$ (\Cref{asm:transfer}).
Suppose also that the nonnegative predictable sequence $\bar F_t$ satisfies
$\bar F_t\ge F_t$.
Define the observable information-gain surrogate
\[
  \hat\gamma_{t-1} \;:=\;
  \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\log\!\Bigl(1+\sigma^{-2}\,
  \frac{u_s(a_s)}{1-\bar\varepsilon_s}\,
  \tilde s_s^2(a_s)\Bigr),
\]
where $\tilde s_s^2(a_s)=\bg_s(a_s)^\top\tilde\bu_s$ is the CG variance
proxy computed at round~$s$ for the played action.
Then $\hat\gamma_{t-1}$ is $\cHist_t$-measurable and
$\hat\gamma_{t-1}\ge\gamma_{t-1}$, so
\[
  \hat\beta_t \;:=\;
  \sqrt{\hat\gamma_{t-1}+2\log(1/\delta)}
  \;+\;\sqrt{\lambda}\,S
  \;+\;\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\bar F_t}
\]
is a predictable schedule with $\hat\beta_t\ge\beta_t$.  Played-action CG
accuracy is sufficient for this information comparison.  If the same tolerance
also holds uniformly for every candidate action before selection, then
\Cref{thm:regret} holds with $\bar\beta_t=\hat\beta_t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The transfer factor gives
$\bar s_s^2(a_s)\le u_s(a_s)\,s_{\Calg,s}^2(a_s)$, and \Cref{lem:cg}
with $\varepsilon_{s,a_s}\le\bar\varepsilon_s$ gives
$s_{\Calg,s}^2(a_s)\le\tilde s_s^2(a_s)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_s)$;
hence
$\bar s_s^2(a_s)\le u_s(a_s)\,\tilde s_s^2(a_s)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_s)$.
By monotonicity of $x\mapsto\log(1+\sigma^{-2}x)$, each summand of
$\gamma_{t-1}$ in~\eqref{eq:infogain} is dominated by the corresponding
summand of $\hat\gamma_{t-1}$.
Measurability holds because $\tilde s_s^2(a_s)$, $u_s(a_s)$, and
$\bar\varepsilon_s$ are all $\cHist_s$-measurable.
The schedule property then follows term by term as in
\Cref{cor:beta-constructive}.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This makes the information-gain component observable when $u_s(a_s)$ is
certified.  Together with \Cref{lem:path-certificates}, it renders the full
confidence and centering schedules operational; it does not make them tight.

\begin{remark}[Computability and dimension dependence of $\beta_t$]
Two caveats about $\beta_t$ deserve emphasis.
First, $\gamma_{t-1}$ is defined through the frozen-feature Gram
matrix $\bCbar_t$, whose per-sample Jacobians the matrix-free
implementation deliberately does not store; $\gamma_{t-1}$ is
therefore not directly computable in the stated memory model.
Second, the worst-case substitute
$\gamma_{t-1}\le d\log\bigl(1+(t-1)G^2/(d\lambda\sigma^2)\bigr)$
does not exploit low realized information gain and can be order $t$ when
$d\gg t$, rendering the resulting bonus and theorem bound vacuous.
\Cref{cor:gammahat} addresses both issues with an observable surrogate
built from quantities the algorithm already computes; the resulting
guarantee is data-dependent, and its tightness still depends on the realized
surrogate together with the other theorem factors.
\end{remark}

\begin{lemma}[Action-scaled centering discrepancy]\label{lem:discrepancy}
Under \Cref{asm:optim},
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t - \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|\le
\bar\psi_t\,\bar s_t(a)$
for all $a\in\cA_t$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Immediate from~\eqref{eq:pred-stab}.
\end{proof}

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Remark (status of the centering certificate $\bar\psi_t$).}
\Cref{lem:path-certificates} computes a predictable
$\bar\psi_t\ge\psi_t$ from the certified optimizer residual, Welford path
moments, observed collection residuals, and analytic smoothness constants.
The schedule is operational but can be loose; the corrected-center variant
(\Cref{cor:corrected-center}) removes it at the cost of frozen-feature access.
\end{quote}
In the convex last-layer regime, under exact optimization,
$\btheta_t = \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$ and $\bar\psi_t=0$.
The full prediction error at $(\bx_t,a)$ is bounded by the triangle
inequality, with the centering discrepancy \emph{action-scaled}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pred-error}
  |\mu_{\btheta^*}(\bx_t,a) - \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)|
  \;\le\;
  (\beta_t+\bar\psi_t)\,\bar s_t(a)
  \;+\;
  \epslin(t)
  \;\le\;
  \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a)+\epslin(t),
\end{equation}
using \eqref{eq:conf-lin}, \Cref{lem:discrepancy}, the per-round
linearization remainder $|\rho_t|\le\epslin(t)$, and $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$.



\subsection{Cost of Approximate Curvature}

\begin{proposition}[Unrescaled current-parameter subset]\label{prop:window}
Let $\mathcal{S}_t\subseteq\{1,\dots,t{-}1\}$ and $0\le w_s\le1$, and define the
current-parameter operator
\[
  \hat\bC_t
  \;=\;
  \lambda\bI
  +\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t} w_s\,
  \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top ,
\]
using the same relinearized Jacobians $\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)=\nabla_{\btheta}
\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)$ that define $\bC_t$~\eqref{eq:curvature}.  Then
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\bC_t$, so \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} applies with
$\kappa_{+,t}=1$ and, in the exact-transfer case, $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$
(\Cref{lem:whitened}).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\bC_t-\hat\bC_t=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}(1-w_s)\bg_t\bg_t^\top
+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\notin\mathcal{S}_t}\bg_t\bg_t^\top$, a sum of PSD outer
products (each $1-w_s\ge0$), hence $\bC_t-\hat\bC_t\succeq0$.  With
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\bC_t$ (so $\kappa_{+,t}=1$ in
\eqref{eq:approx-onesided}) and $\hat\bC_t\succeq\lambda\bI$, the hypotheses of
\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} hold.
\end{proof}

\noindent
This covers an unrescaled current-parameter sliding window, an unrescaled
current-parameter subsample, and any current-parameter downweighted subset with
weights in $[0,1]$.  It does \emph{not} cover rescaled subsampling with weights
$(t{-}1)/n>1$ (\Cref{lem:subsample}), a stale periodic-refresh operator evaluated
at an older parameter, or an arbitrary randomized sketch (e.g.\ an eigenspace
Lanczos surrogate), for which a factor
$\kappa_{+,t}$ must be certified separately.

\begin{theorem}[Approximate curvature via one-sided transfer]\label{thm:spectral-distortion}
Adopt the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:regret} with the algorithmic curvature
$\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$, an approximate operator that is symmetric,
$\cHist_t$-measurable, and satisfies $\hat\bC_t\succeq\lambda\bI$.  Suppose
the two \emph{one-sided} bounds
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-onesided}
  \hat\bC_t \;\preceq\; \kappa_{+,t}\,\bC_t,
  \qquad
  \bC_t \;\preceq\; (1+\bar\chi_t)^2\,\bCbar_t
\end{equation}
hold for every $t\le T$, where $\kappa_{+,t}\ge1$ is $\cHist_t$-measurable
and $\bar\chi_t\ge\chi_t$ is the whitened-drift certificate
of~\Cref{lem:whitened}.  Then, for every action $a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:approx-transfer}
  \bar s_t^2(a)\;\le\;\kappa_{+,t}\,(1+\bar\chi_t)^2\,\hat s_t^2(a),
\end{equation}
so \Cref{asm:transfer} holds with the uniform factor
$u_t=\kappa_{+,t}(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ and \Cref{thm:regret} applies verbatim
with $\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$ and $s_{\Calg,t}=\hat s_t$:
\[
  R_T\;\le\;2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T
    \sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t^2\,u_t(a_t)\,\omega_t^2}\;+\;2E_T .
\]
No \emph{lower} spectral factor on $\hat\bC_t$ is required for optimism; the
cost of an excessively small, stale, or nonmonotone approximate operator is
charged through its realized width complexity $\Lamalg_T$ (and its variation
$\Valg_T$ inside $\Gamdyn_T$), not through a lower Loewner bound.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$\hat\bC_t\preceq\kappa_{+,t}\bC_t$ with both sides SPD inverts to
$\hat\bC_t^{-1}\succeq\kappa_{+,t}^{-1}\bC_t^{-1}$, so
$\hat s_t^2(a)\ge\kappa_{+,t}^{-1}s_t^2(a)$; and~\eqref{eq:onesided-C} gives
$\bar s_t^2(a)\le(1+\bar\chi_t)^2 s_t^2(a)$.  Chaining,
$\bar s_t^2(a)\le(1+\bar\chi_t)^2\kappa_{+,t}\hat s_t^2(a)$, which
is~\eqref{eq:approx-transfer} with $s_{\Calg,t}=\hat s_t$.
\Cref{thm:regret} applies with $\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$.  The two-sided
$\sqrt{\rho_+/\rho_-}$ inflation---which instead assumes a lower spectral
factor---is retained for comparison in \Cref{app:cor}; the two results
trade different assumptions (a lower spectral bound versus dynamic width
complexity), and neither numerically dominates the other in general.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[Roundwise scalar-width invariance]\label{prop:scalar-invariance}
If two width functions satisfy $\hat s_t(a)=c_ts_t(a)$ for every candidate
action, where $c_t>0$ is action independent, then replacing a scalar bonus
coefficient $b_t$ by $b_t/c_t$ gives identical UCB scores and therefore the
same selected action under the same tie rule.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\widehat\mu_t(a)+(b_t/c_t)\hat s_t(a)=\widehat\mu_t(a)+b_ts_t(a)$ for every $a$.
\end{proof}
This is a roundwise statement.  If two policies also have identical centers,
histories, optimization, and coupled exogenous randomness, if $c_t$ is
predictable, and if proportionality holds at every subsequent common history,
induction gives identical actions and rewards after the coefficient adjustment.
Absent those conditions the proposition makes no regret claim.  Accordingly,
regret differences can reflect anisotropic width distortion or a difference in
damping, center, adaptive path, optimization, calibration, or solve accuracy.

\begin{proposition}[Off-diagonal linear-Gram witness]\label{prop:offdiag-witness}
Let $\sigma=1$, $\bu=(1,1)/\sqrt2$, $\bv=(1,-1)/\sqrt2$, the action set be
$\{\bu,\bv\}$, and $\btheta^*=\epsilon\bu+\Delta\bv$ with
$\Delta>\epsilon>0$.  Rewards are noiseless, the ridge is $\lambda\bI$, all
policies use the same full-ridge mean center, and ties select $\bu$.  If
\begin{equation}\label{eq:offdiag-bonus-range}
\max\!\left\{\sqrt{\lambda(\epsilon^2+\Delta^2)},
 \epsilon\sqrt\lambda\!\left(1+\sqrt{\frac\lambda{\lambda+1}}\right)\right\}
 < b\le\left(\Delta-\frac\epsilon{\lambda+1}\right)\sqrt{\lambda+1},
\end{equation}
then full-Gram UCB plays $\bu$ once and $\bv$ thereafter, so
$R_T^{\rm full}=\Delta-\epsilon$.  The raw diagonal policy and the policy
inflated by the uniform analytic factor
$\kappa_n=(\lambda+n/2)/\lambda$ play $\bu$ forever and have
$R_T^{\rm diag}=T(\Delta-\epsilon)$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
After $n$ pulls of $\bu$ and no pull of $\bv$,
\[
 C_n=\lambda\bI+n\bu\bu^\top,\qquad
 \hat\btheta_n=\frac{n\epsilon}{\lambda+n}\bu .
\]
Thus the full scores are
$n\epsilon/(\lambda+n)+b/\sqrt{\lambda+n}$ and $b/\sqrt\lambda$.
The strict lower inequality in \eqref{eq:offdiag-bonus-range} makes the second
score larger at $n=1$.  The confidence lower bound also implies
$b>\Delta\sqrt\lambda$; after any number of $\bv$ pulls its score is strictly
larger than $\Delta$, whereas the upper inequality makes the fixed $\bu$ score
at most $\Delta$.  Hence there is no return.  In contrast,
$D_n=\operatorname{diag}(C_n)=(\lambda+n/2)\bI$, so both diagonal widths are
equal and the center gives $\bu$ a strict advantage after round one.  Moreover
$D_n\preceq\kappa_nC_n$ analytically, but multiplying both diagonal widths by
$\sqrt{\kappa_n}$ leaves them equal; uniform certified inflation therefore does
not change the diagonal action.  For example,
$(\lambda,\epsilon,\Delta,b)=(1,0.1,1,1.1)$ satisfies the interval.
\end{proof}
This is a two-dimensional linear-Gram existence witness.  For a linear predictor
with Gaussian squared loss, this Gram is exactly the GGN/Fisher operator, so the
construction isolates a GGN-relevant geometric mechanism.  It is neither a
nonlinear-network result nor a uniform dominance result.  A dense action-dependent
factor recovers the full widths, but it uses the reference geometry that the
diagonal approximation was meant to avoid.

\paragraph{Composing subsampling with feature drift.}
\Cref{lem:subsample} guarantees a sandwich for the \emph{relinearized}
$\bC_t$: $(1{-}\varepsilon)\bC_t\preceq\hat\bC_t\preceq(1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$.
Only the \emph{upper} half is needed here: $\hat\bC_t\preceq(1{+}\varepsilon)\bC_t$
gives $\kappa_{+,t}=1+\varepsilon$, and composing with the one-sided
whitened bound $\bC_t\preceq(1+\bar\chi_t)^2\bCbar_t$ (\Cref{lem:whitened})
yields $u_t=(1+\varepsilon)(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ in
\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}.  A union bound over $t\le T$ (replacing
$\delta'$ by $\delta'/T$ in \Cref{lem:subsample}) makes the subsampling
event time-uniform and contributes to $\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.

For an \emph{unrescaled current-parameter window} (or any current-parameter
downweighted subset with weights in $[0,1]$), \Cref{prop:window} gives the
\emph{deterministic} upper factor $\kappa_{+,t}=1$ with \emph{no} distributional
assumption, so \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} applies directly with
$u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$.  Only the \emph{stale periodic-refresh} operator
(\S\ref{sec:curvop}(iii)), which reuses Jacobians from an older parameter, lacks
a deterministic $\kappa_{+,t}$; there \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} provides a
guarantee only when a one-sided bound $\hat\bC_t\preceq\kappa_{+,t}\bC_t$ is
separately verified, which we evaluate empirically.

\paragraph{What remains open.}
The path schedules close the computability gap, but their constants can be
extremely loose.  The approximate-rank result replaces exact rank by a realized
or predictably bounded spectral tail, but does not show that this tail or feature
drift remains small during unrestricted training.  Useful control of these two
quantities for a genuinely adapting backbone, tighter smoothness/residual
certificates, and verified numerical enclosures for floating-point solver and
spectral calculations remain open.



\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Neural and linear contextual bandits.}
LinUCB \citep{abbasi2011improved} and Thompson sampling
\citep{thompson1933likelihood,agrawal2013thompson} provide the analytical
backbone for contextual bandit exploration.  Neural extensions---NeuralUCB
\citep{zhou2020neural}, NeuralTS \citep{zhang2021neural}, neural-linear
models \citep{riquelme2018deep}---use different neural feature covariances.
NeuralUCB and NeuralTS accumulate full neural-gradient Grams in their stated
algorithms (and diagonalize them in experiments), while NeuralLinear maintains
Bayesian linear uncertainty over learned last-layer features.  CC-UCB instead
re-evaluates every replayed Jacobian at the current parameter and accesses the
resulting full operator implicitly.
Neural-linear designs that train a deep representation but explore only
in the last layer \citep{xu2022shallow} occupy the opposite design point.
Recent alternatives sharpen different parts of this landscape.
\citet{salgia2023provably} analyze overparameterized smooth-activation networks
through non-asymptotic network--NTK approximation and give an efficient
sublinear-regret algorithm.  \citet{deb2024contextual} instead reduce contextual
bandits to online neural regression, using perturbed predictions to obtain their
regression guarantees.  \citet{bui2025variance} combine a learned deep
representation with a variance-aware linear-UCB exploration layer.  These are,
respectively, an NTK analysis, a regression-oracle route, and a learned
last-layer geometry; none analyzes the dynamically relinearized full GGN and its
CG error.  Our low-rank/excitation result is correspondingly narrower than
generic neural regression, but controls that current-parameter operator.

\paragraph{Online Bayesian and EKF-style neural bandits.}
\citet{duranmartin2022efficient} apply extended Kalman filter (EKF)
updates in a learned or random low-dimensional affine parameter subspace.
They maintain a dense or diagonal covariance in that subspace, inducing a
low-rank covariance over parameters spanning all network layers, with memory
independent of the number of rounds.  This differs from applying exact
curvature--vector products in the full parameter space.
More directly, \citet{chang2023lowrank} maintain a diagonal-plus-low-rank
approximation to the full-parameter posterior precision through recursive EKF
and online-SVD updates, and use it for Thompson sampling in an MNIST contextual
bandit.  Their LO-FI method is a one-pass compressed-posterior approach with
fixed-rank cost linear in parameter count and no replay of past data.  CC-UCB
instead relinearizes the full replay history at the current parameter and
accesses the uncompressed GGN through residual-checked CG.  Their bandit study
is empirical; our conditional UCB analysis explicitly accounts for CG
truncation, but does not establish a practical advantage over LO-FI.

\paragraph{Last-layer and structured Laplace approximations.}
Last-layer Laplace fixes the backbone and restricts the approximate posterior
to the final layer \citep{kristiadi2020being}, omitting backbone-weight
uncertainty by construction.  This is only one scalable option: all-layer
block-diagonal Kronecker curvature has also been used
\citep{ritter2018scalable}, and \citet{daxberger2021laplace} systematize
all-weight, subnetwork, and last-layer variants with full, low-rank, KFAC, or
diagonal curvature.  CC-UCB uses the uncompressed current-parameter GGN as its
reference operator without materializing the covariance matrix.

\paragraph{Sampling-based full-parameter exploration.}
Langevin Monte Carlo Thompson sampling (LMC-TS) \citep{xu2022langevin}
explores with approximate samples of a loss-defined parameter posterior,
obtained by noisy gradient steps on the regularized loss; like CC-UCB it never
materializes a covariance matrix.  Its regret guarantee is for linear
contextual bandits; its neural-bandit evidence is empirical.
The two routes pay for exploration differently: LMC-TS through the
mixing of a per-round sampling chain, CC-UCB through CG truncation and
spectral distortion of an explicit curvature metric, which yields
deterministic widths, an optimism-style analysis, and a fixed curvature operator
reused across separate per-action CG solves.
\paragraph{Efficient nonlinear UCB.}
\citet{kassraie2022neural} analyze NTK-UCB and propose NN-UCB and its
convolutional variant CNN-UCB.  NN-UCB combines a trained-network mean with
confidence widths from the empirical NTK feature map at initialization.
They prove sublinear regret for the SupNN-UCB variant under NTK-RKHS,
sufficient-exploration, and network-width assumptions.  Their fixed-feature
uncertainty differs from CC-UCB's replayed GGN/Fisher curvature at the current
parameter~$\btheta_t$, accessed matrix-free via CG.
CC-UCB thus targets the regime where the practitioner has a specific
pretrained model and wants to use its curvature as-is, rather than
constructing a surrogate NTK-based estimator.

\paragraph{Spectral sketching for linear bandits.}
\citet{kuzborskij2019efficient} analyze linear bandits where the Gram
matrix is replaced by a Frequent Directions sketch, deriving regret
bounds that depend on the spectral approximation quality.
\citet{wen2026revisiting} show that fixed-size sketch regret bounds can
degenerate to the trivial linear rate under heavy spectral tails, and propose
an adaptive dyadic block sketch.
Our spectral-distortion theorem (\Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}) instantiates the
same conceptual lever in the matrix-free GGN/CVP setting by exposing transfer
and realized-width terms along the realized trajectory.
We do not claim novelty for the sandwich-to-regret reduction itself;
the contribution is adapting it to the CG/GGN context with explicit
CG truncation and feature-drift factors.

\paragraph{Laplace approximation in deep learning.}
The Laplace approximation \citep{mackay1992practical,ritter2018scalable}
provides a Gaussian posterior centered at a MAP estimate with covariance
given by the inverse curvature.  Scalable variants use diagonal, KFAC
\citep{martens2015optimizing}, or subnetwork approximations
\citep{daxberger2021laplace}.  Matrix-free full-Jacobian/Fisher predictive
variance using implicit products and CG predates this work
\citep{maddox2021fast}; CC-UCB adapts that computational route to an online,
current-relinearized GGN operator and supplies bandit-specific transfer and
solver-error accounting.

\paragraph{GGN--vector products, Hessian spectral analysis, and influence
functions.}
Pearlmutter's algorithm \citep{pearlmutter1994fast} computes exact
Hessian--vector products; the analogous GGN--vector product has the same
Jacobian-transpose-times-Jacobian-times-vector structure
\citep{schraudolph2002fast}.
Recent work on Krylov methods for Hessian spectral analysis
\citep{granziol2026hessian} demonstrates related distributed HVP/Krylov
infrastructure at $>10^8$-parameter scale, but does not benchmark the exact
per-example GGN operator or end-to-end CC-UCB latency.
Influence functions
\citep{koh2017understanding} use inverse-curvature--vector products to
attribute predictions to training points; the CG-based implicit solve
we employ is the same computational primitive.

\paragraph{Optimal experimental design.}
Information-theoretic exploration objectives---such as maximizing
$\log\det(\bCbar_t)$ or minimizing the trace of~$\bCbar_t^{-1}$---connect
bandit exploration to the classical D-optimal and A-optimal design
criteria \citep{valko2013finite}.  CC-UCB's use of the full GGN/Fisher curvature
enables stochastic estimates of these quantities via Hutchinson-type trace
estimators \citep{ubaru2017fast}, without forming the matrix.



\paragraph{Filtration.}
We separate \emph{pre-action} randomness, revealed before the agent commits to
$a_t$, from \emph{post-reward} randomness, drawn only after $r_t$ is observed.
Let $\Omega_t$ collect all pre-action algorithmic randomness used at round~$t$:
curvature subsampling indices, randomized sketches, and stochastic solver seeds.
The randomized sketch or subsample that defines the curvature operator is drawn
into $\Omega_t$ and \emph{fixed before action selection}; it is not re-randomized
during any conjugate-gradient (CG) solve at round~$t$.  (Standard CG requires a
fixed symmetric positive definite matrix--vector oracle across its iterations and
does not apply to a stochastic oracle that changes between iterations.)
Let $U_{t+1}$ collect the \emph{post-reward} randomness used to form the next
parameter estimate $\btheta_{t+1}$ (minibatch order, dropout, perturbed warm
starts).
Define $\cF_0 = \sigma(\btheta_1)$ and, for $t\ge 1$, the recursively built
$\sigma$-algebra
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtration-F}
  \cF_t \;:=\;
  \cF_{t-1}\vee
  \sigma\!\bigl(\bx_t,\cA_t,\Omega_t,a_t,r_t,U_{t+1}\bigr),
\end{equation}
so that $\btheta_{t+1}$---computed from the data through round~$t$ together with
the post-reward randomness $U_{t+1}$---is $\cF_t$-measurable.
Define the \emph{pre-action history} $\sigma$-algebra and the pre-reward
$\sigma$-algebra
\begin{equation}\label{eq:filtration}
  \cH_t^-\;:=\;\cF_{t-1}\vee\sigma\!\bigl(\bx_t,\cA_t\bigr),
  \qquad
  \cHist_t\;:=\;\cH_t^-\vee\sigma(\Omega_t),
  \qquad
  \cG_t \;:=\; \cHist_t\vee\sigma(a_t).
\end{equation}
At round~$t$ the context $\bx_t$, the finite action set $\cA_t$, and the
pre-action randomness $\Omega_t$ are revealed; the agent selects $a_t$; then the
reward $r_t=\mu^*(\bx_t,a_t)+\eta_t$ is observed; finally $U_{t+1}$ is drawn and
$\btheta_{t+1}$ is computed.
$\cH_t^-$ is the history immediately before the randomized certificate draw;
after that draw, its realized operator and schedules are $\cHist_t$-measurable.
By construction of \Cref{alg:ccucb} the selection rule is a deterministic function
of $\cHist_t$-measurable quantities, so the chosen action $a_t$ is itself
$\cHist_t$-measurable and hence $\cG_t=\cHist_t$; we retain the symbol $\cG_t$
where the played action is used.
The following are all $\cHist_t$-measurable (i.e.\ determined before the reward
$r_t$): the candidate action set $\cA_t$ and every candidate feature
$\bg_t(\bx_t,a)$, $a\in\cA_t$; the chosen action $a_t$; the algorithmic
curvature operator~$\Calg_t$ (\Cref{sec:method}); the confidence schedule
$\bar\beta_t$; the centering certificate~$\bar\psi_t$
(\Cref{asm:optim}); the per-round CG tolerance~$\bar\varepsilon_t$ together with
any condition-number certificate used by the CG stopping rule; and the one-sided
optimism-transfer factor~$u_t(a)$ (\Cref{asm:transfer}).
Since $\btheta_t$ is $\cF_{t-1}\subseteq\cHist_t$-measurable, each candidate design
vector $\bg_t(\bx_t,a)$ is $\cHist_t$-measurable and the realized design vector
$\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)$ is $\cG_t$-measurable.
We assume $\{U_s\}_{s\ge1}$ is independent of the reward noise
$\{\eta_s\}_{s\ge1}$, so that revealing $U_{t+1}$ and forming $\btheta_{t+1}$
after $r_t$ preserves the conditional sub-Gaussian property of \Cref{asm:noise}
for the future noise $\{\eta_s\}_{s>t}$.
Index every randomized certificate source (operator sketch, transfer bound,
condition bound, or other randomized oracle) by $j\in\mathcal J$.  For its
round-$t$ validity event $\cE_{j,t}$ choose in advance
$\delta_{j,t}\ge0$ such that its stated conditional guarantee gives
$\Pr(\cE_{j,t}^c\mid\cH_t^-)\le\delta_{j,t}$ and
\[
 \sum_{j\in\mathcal J}\sum_{t=1}^T\delta_{j,t}
 \le\delta_{\rm cert},\qquad
 \cE_{\rm cert}:=\bigcap_{j\in\mathcal J}\bigcap_{t=1}^T\cE_{j,t}.
\]
The tower property and union bound yield
$\Pr(\cE_{\rm cert})\ge1-\delta_{\rm cert}$.  Deterministic or almost-sure
certificates use $\delta_{j,t}=0$.  The self-normalized confidence event is
separate and receives probability $\delta$.
When \Cref{lem:bounded-output-residual} supplies the residual-energy envelope,
include its time-uniform event $\cE_R$ as one certificate source and reserve
$\delta_R$ inside $\delta_{\rm cert}$ (equivalently, require
$\delta_R+\sum_{j,t}\delta_{j,t}\le\delta_{\rm cert}$ for the remaining
sources).  The residual event is never counted again in the self-normalized
failure probability $\delta$.



\paragraph{Auxiliary linearized ridge estimator.}
For the analysis we define a \emph{linearized ridge estimator} based on
frozen features.  Set the predictable intercept
$b_s := \mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx_s,a_s) - \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\btheta_s$
and the pseudo-response $y_s := r_s - b_s$.
Under \Cref{asm:linear} and realizability ($\mu^*=\mu_{\btheta^*}$),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pseudo-response}
  y_s
  \;=\;
  \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\btheta^*
  \;+\;
  \rho_s(\bx_s,a_s,\btheta^*)
  \;+\;
  \eta_s,
\end{equation}
where $|\rho_s|\le\epslin(s)$.
The linearized estimator is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thetahat-lin}
  \hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}
  \;:=\;
  \argmin_{\btheta}\,\Biggl[\,
    \frac{1}{2\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
      \bigl(y_s - \bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\btheta\bigr)^2
    \;+\;\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\btheta\|^2
  \Biggr]
  \;=\;
  \bCbar_t^{-1}\,\frac{1}{\sigma^2}
    \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)\,y_s.
\end{equation}
This is a standard linear ridge regression with predictable design
vectors~$\bg_s/\sigma$ and Gram matrix~$\bCbar_t$, so the
self-normalized martingale bound
\citep{abbasi2011improved} applies to it directly
(\Cref{lem:confidence}).



\paragraph{GGN versus Hessian (context only).}
The regret theorem uses the GGN/Fisher curvature as the exploration
metric.  It does not require the GGN to be an accurate Hessian
approximation.
For squared loss~\eqref{eq:erm} the full Hessian of the data-fit term is
\[
  \bH_t \;=\; \frac{1}{\sigma^2}\sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
  \Bigl[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top
  \;-\; \bigl(r_s - \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)
        \nabla^2\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)\Bigr]
  \;+\;\lambda\bI.
\]
The curvature matrix $\bC_t$ drops the residual-times-second-derivative
term, retaining only the outer-product part that is guaranteed PSD.
The dropped residual-times-second-derivative term
$D_t=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}(r_s{-}\mu_{\btheta_t})\nabla^2\mu_{\btheta_t}$
has operator norm at most
$(t{-}1)\epsilon_r H/\sigma^2$, where $\epsilon_r$ bounds the residuals and
$H$ the Hessian norms.
When this quantity is small relative to~$\lambda$, $\bC_t$ approximates
the full Hessian in the Loewner sense (a formal statement with proof is
given in \Cref{app:ggn-approx}).
This comparison is \emph{not} invoked in the regret analysis
(\Cref{thm:regret} uses $\bC_t$ directly as the exploration metric);
it serves only to contextualize $\bC_t$ as a proxy for the full
Hessian.  Outside the small-residual regime, the GGN is not an exact
Hessian approximation; it is the PSD Fisher/GGN metric used by the
algorithm.

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Remark (Fisher, empirical Fisher, and GGN).}
Three curvature objects appear in the literature and must be
distinguished.
The \emph{empirical Fisher} is the realized score outer product
$\sum_s\nabla_{\btheta}\log p(r_s\!\mid\!\bx_s,a_s;\btheta)\,
 \nabla_{\btheta}\log p(r_s\!\mid\!\bx_s,a_s;\btheta)^\top$;
it depends on observed rewards and is \emph{not} the operator used by
CC-UCB.
The \emph{true (model) Fisher} is the model expectation of the score
outer product,
$F(\btheta)=\mathbb{E}_{r\sim p(\cdot\mid\bx,a;\btheta)}
 [\nabla_{\btheta}\log p\;\nabla_{\btheta}\log p^\top]$.
The \emph{generalized Gauss--Newton} (GGN) drops the
residual-times-Hessian term from the full Hessian, retaining only the
outer-product Jacobian part that is guaranteed PSD
\citep{martens2020new,schraudolph2002fast}.
For the fixed-variance Gaussian working model underlying the squared-loss
quasi-likelihood used here, the model Fisher and GGN are identical, and both
reduce to the outer-product curvature $\sigma^{-2}\sum_s\bg_s\bg_s^\top$
used in~\eqref{eq:curvature}.
More generally, for exponential-family negative log-likelihoods
parameterized by natural parameters $z_{\btheta}(\bx,a)$, the true
Fisher equals the GGN:
$J_{\btheta}^\top\nabla^2 A(z)\,J_{\btheta}$,
where $J_{\btheta}=\nabla_{\btheta}z_{\btheta}$ and
$\nabla^2 A$ is the Hessian of the log-partition function
\citep{martens2020new,pascanu2014revisiting}.
Bernoulli rewards yield scalar weight~$p(1{-}p)$;
categorical rewards yield $\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbf{p})-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^\top$.
See \Cref{app:expfam} for the extension.
\end{quote}

We access $\bC_t$ only through curvature--vector products
$\bv\mapsto\bC_t\,\bv$, computed via forward-mode Jacobian--vector
products composed with reverse-mode vector--Jacobian products
\citep{pearlmutter1994fast,schraudolph2002fast}.



\paragraph{Extension to exponential-family likelihoods.}%
The same matrix-free CG/CVP machinery extends algorithmically to
exponential-family likelihoods (e.g.\ Bernoulli or categorical rewards)
via the Fisher/GGN curvature $J_t^\top(\nabla^2 A)\,J_t$, but no regret
guarantee beyond Gaussian squared loss is claimed in this draft.
Details of the curvature definitions are in \Cref{app:expfam}.



\paragraph{Per-round CG tolerance~$\bar\varepsilon_t$.}
The algorithm uses a \emph{known} per-round tolerance
$\bar\varepsilon_t\in(0,1)$ in the UCB bonus (line~\ref{line:ucb}), not the
true per-action energy-norm errors $\varepsilon_{t,a}$ (which are unobservable
without the exact solves).  It suffices that
$\max_{a\in\cA_t}\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t$ at every round
(\eqref{eq:cg-tol-uniform}; zero-gradient actions have $\varepsilon_{t,a}=0$ by
convention and are trivially within tolerance); the bonus is then conservative.
Two strategies ensure this:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Adaptive residual stopping (recommended).}
  In exact arithmetic, for each action monitor the computable Euclidean relative residual
  $\|r_I\|/\|\bg_t(a)\|$ at each CG step (skipping actions with
  $\bg_t(a)=\mathbf0$).  Since
  $\varepsilon_{t,a} \le \sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}\cdot\|r_I\|/\|\bg_t(a)\|$
  (standard CG bound, with $\bar\kappa_t$ a certified upper bound on the
  condition number of $\Calg_t$), stopping that solve when
  $\|r_I\|/\|\bg_t(a)\| \le \bar\varepsilon_t/\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}$
  guarantees $\varepsilon_{t,a} \le \bar\varepsilon_t$, hence
  \eqref{eq:cg-tol-uniform}.
  This is conservative by the factor~$\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}$ but requires
  no access to $\Calg_t^{-1}$ and adapts automatically to the
  per-round condition number.  A floating-point implementation needs a
  verified residual enclosure to inherit this certification literally.
\item \emph{Fixed budget (requires a uniform condition-number bound).}
  If an a~priori bound
  $\bar\kappa_t \le \kappa_{\max}$ holds for \emph{all rounds}~$t\le T$,
  choose $I$ via Eq.~\eqref{eq:cg-budget} at $\kappa_{\max}$ and a fixed
  target~$\bar\varepsilon_t\equiv\bar\varepsilon$.
  For a \emph{nonnegative weighted outer-product} operator
  $\Calg_t=\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s\,
  \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$ the condition number is bounded in
  closed form, $\bar\kappa_t\le 1+W_tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ with
  $W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s$; a fixed buffer of size~$n$ ($W_t=n$,
  unrescaled) makes this independent of~$T$.
  For the full-history operator ($W_t=t{-}1$), and for rescaled uniform
  subsampling with $w_s=(t{-}1)/n$ (so $W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s=t{-}1$),
  the bound grows with~$t$.  For a known finite horizon, a fixed budget at
  $\kappa_{\max}=1+(T-1)G^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ is valid but scales as
  $O(\sqrt T\log(1/\bar\varepsilon))$; a horizon-independent fixed budget is
  not uniform, so adaptive stopping or an increasing $I_t$ is preferable
  (see \Cref{app:cg}).
  For a \emph{general} SPD operator that is not a nonnegative weighted outer
  product (e.g.\ a randomized sketch), the closed-form bound
  $1+W_tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ does not apply, and a separately certified
  $\cHist_t$-measurable condition-number upper bound $\bar\kappa_t$ must be
  supplied.
\end{enumerate}
In the regret bound (\Cref{thm:regret}), the CG inflation enters through the
per-round factor $\alpha_t = \sqrt{(1{+}\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1{-}\bar\varepsilon_t)}$,
inside the sum of squares $\sum_t\alpha_t^2 u_t(a_t)\omega_t^2$; no horizon
maximum over $\bar\varepsilon_t$ is taken.

\paragraph{Action screening (practical heuristic).}
When the action set $\cA_t$ is large, computing the CG solve for every
action is prohibitive ($|\cA_t|\times I$ CG steps per round).
A practical heuristic is to screen the top-$K$ actions by mean prediction
$\widehat\mu_t(a)$ (forward passes only), then compute UCB widths for the $K$
candidates.  This reduces cost to $K\times I$ CG solves.
\emph{The regret guarantee in \Cref{thm:regret} analyzes the
full-enumeration case ($K=|\cA_t|$); screening can drop the optimal arm
and is not covered by the theory unless one adds an assumption that
$a_t^*\in\cA_t^K$ for all~$t$.}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Operational CC-UCB with path certificates}\label{alg:ccucb-detailed}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Reward predictor $\mu_{\btheta}$, damping $\lambda>0$,
         noise proxy $\sigma$, confidence level $\delta$, radius $S$,
         analytic constants $G,L_g,L_\mu$;
         algorithmic curvature mode $\Calg_t$ (exact full-history $\bC_t$
         or approximate $\hat\bC_t$) with certified one-sided factor
         $\kappa_{+,t}$ and condition upper bound $\bar\kappa_t$;
         per-round CG tolerance $\bar\varepsilon_t<1$
\STATE Initialize $\btheta_1$, certified $\zeta_1\ge\|\nabla\cL_1(\btheta_1)\|$,
       $n\gets0$, $\bar\btheta\gets\mathbf0$, $J\gets0$,
       $R^{\rm col}\gets0$, $\bar F\gets0$, $\hat\gamma\gets0$, and
       $\mathcal D_0\gets\emptyset$
\FOR{$t = 1, 2, \ldots, T$}
  \STATE Observe context $\bx_t$ and action set $\cA_t$; draw pre-action
         randomness $\Omega_t$ \hfill\COMMENT{$\cHist_t$ of~\eqref{eq:filtration}}
  \STATE $Q_t\gets J+n\|\btheta_t-\bar\btheta\|^2$;
         $\bar\chi_t\gets L_g\sqrt{Q_t}/(\sigma\sqrt\lambda)$;
         $u_t\gets\kappa_{+,t}(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$
  \STATE $\bar M_t\gets\sigma^{-2}[L_g\sqrt{R^{\rm col}Q_t}
         +(3GL_g/2)Q_t+(L_g^2/2)Q_t^{3/2}]$;
         $\bar\psi_t\gets(\zeta_t+\bar M_t)/\sqrt\lambda$
         \hfill\COMMENT{in a radius-$R$ region use $L_g^2RQ_t$ for the last term}
  \STATE $\bar\epsilon_t^{\rm lin}\gets(L_\mu/2)(S+\|\btheta_t\|)^2$;
         $\bar\beta_t\gets\sqrt{\hat\gamma+2\log(1/\delta)}
         +\sqrt\lambda S+\sigma^{-1}\sqrt{\bar F}$;
         $\omega_t\gets\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$
         \hfill\COMMENT{corrected center uses $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$}
  \STATE Form or receive a fixed $\cHist_t$-measurable SPD matrix--vector
         oracle $\bv\mapsto\Calg_t\bv$, $\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$, held fixed
         across all CG solves this round \label{line:oracle-detailed}
         \hfill\COMMENT{exact/buffered special case
         $\Calg_t\bv=\lambda\bv
         +\sigma^{-2}\!\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}\!w_s\,
         \bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)[\bg_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\bv]$, weights $w_s$ per
         \S\ref{sec:curvop}}
  \FOR{each $a \in \cA_t$}
    \STATE $\widehat\mu_t(a) \gets \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t, a)$
        \hfill\COMMENT{mean prediction (or corrected center~\eqref{eq:corrected-center})}
    \STATE $\bg_t(a) \gets \nabla_{\btheta}\,\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t, a)$
        \hfill\COMMENT{gradient feature}
    \IF{$\bg_t(a)=\mathbf 0$}
      \STATE $\tilde{s}_t^2(a) \gets 0$ \hfill\COMMENT{skip CG; no residual ratio}
    \ELSE
      \STATE $\tilde\bu \gets \mathrm{CG}(\Calg_t,\;\bg_t(a))$; stop only once
           $\|r_I\|/\|\bg_t(a)\| \le \bar\varepsilon_t/\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}$,
           or use a certified budget $I_t$ from \eqref{eq:cg-budget}
        \hfill\COMMENT{recompute the original residual in floating point;
        a point check is not an enclosure} \label{line:cg-detailed}
      \STATE $\tilde{s}_t^2(a) \gets \bg_t(a)^\top \tilde\bu$
        \hfill\COMMENT{approx.\ predictive variance}
    \ENDIF
  \ENDFOR
  \STATE $a_t \gets \argmax_{a\in\cA_t}\Bigl[\,\widehat\mu_t(a)
    + \omega_t\,\sqrt{\dfrac{u_t(a)}{1-\bar\varepsilon_t}}\,
      \sqrt{\tilde{s}_t^2(a)}\,\Bigr]$
      \hfill\COMMENT{bonus~\eqref{eq:bonus}; ties broken by a fixed rule (e.g.\ lowest index) or by $\Omega_t$} \label{line:ucb-detailed}
  \STATE Play $a_t$, observe $r_t$
  \STATE $\mathcal{D}_t \gets \mathcal{D}_{t-1} \cup \{(\bx_t, a_t, r_t)\}$
  \STATE $c_t\gets\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a_t)-r_t$;
         $R^{\rm col}\gets R^{\rm col}+c_t^2$;
         $\bar F\gets\bar F+(\bar\epsilon_t^{\rm lin})^2$;
         $\hat\gamma\gets\hat\gamma+log[1+\sigma^{-2}
         u_t\tilde s_t^2(a_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)]$
  \STATE $n\gets n+1$; $d\gets\btheta_t-\bar\btheta$;
         $\bar\btheta\gets\bar\btheta+d/n$;
         $J\gets J+d^\top(\btheta_t-\bar\btheta)$
         \hfill\COMMENT{vector Welford update}
  \STATE Run $J^{\rm opt}_{t+1}$ warm-started optimizer steps on the
         \emph{full} data set $\mathcal D_t$ to obtain $\btheta_{t+1}$,
         projecting to the certified region if required
  \STATE Certify
         $\zeta_{t+1}\ge\|\nabla\cL_{t+1}(\btheta_{t+1})\|$; unless the
         terminal optimizer evaluation supplies this quantity, this requires
         one additional full-data gradient evaluation
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

For the exact-curvature theorem, Algorithm~\ref{alg:ccucb} is run with
$\mathcal{S}_t=\{1,\ldots,t{-}1\}$, $w_s=1$, full action enumeration,
$\Calg_t=\bC_t$, and the transfer factor $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ from
\Cref{lem:whitened}.  For an approximate operator $\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$, use
$u_t=\kappa_{+,t}(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$ from \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion}.
The selection rule needs only the \emph{one-sided} upper transfer
factor~$u_t(a)$ for optimism; no lower spectral factor is required (the cost
of the operator is charged through the realized dynamic complexity
$\Lamalg_T$).  Algorithm~\ref{alg:ccucb-detailed} computes $u_t$,
$\bar\psi_t$, and $\bar\beta_t$ from pre-action state.  The
operator-specific inputs are the proved factor $\kappa_{+,t}$ and condition
upper bound $\bar\kappa_t$; exact full and unrescaled-subset operators use
$\kappa_{+,t}=1$.
Windowed/refresh curvature and action screening are practical heuristics
unless the corresponding one-sided transfer factor or optimal-arm-survival
condition is separately verified.

\paragraph{Corrected-center remark.}
Replacing the center $\widehat\mu_t(a)$ by the corrected center
$\widehat\mu_t^{\mathrm{corr}}(a)$ of~\eqref{eq:corrected-center} sets $\bar\psi_t=0$
(so $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$) and removes the centering-stability assumption
exactly (\Cref{cor:corrected-center}), at the cost of frozen-feature access for
$\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$; this departs from the $O(d)$ additional
curvature-state-memory,
no-stored-feature design unless a frozen-feature oracle, replay mechanism, or
certified sketch is supplied (see the remark after
\Cref{cor:corrected-center}).

CC-UCB is the analyzed method throughout.



\paragraph{How to read the theorem.}
The theorem is a transfer result for a general predictable SPD curvature
sequence.  \Cref{lem:path-certificates,cor:operational} instantiate its
nonlinear confidence, centering, and feature-transfer inputs from $O(d)$ path
state, observed collection residuals, analytic smoothness constants, and a
certified optimizer residual.  These schedules are executable but can be very
loose.  A sublinear guarantee from this bound still requires control of the realized dynamic width
complexity and a restricted parameter path; unrestricted end-to-end fine-tuning
is not covered.  No horizon-uniform lower transfer is required by
\Cref{thm:regret}; the lower sandwich appears only in the explicit near-linear
corollary.

\paragraph{Discussion of terms.}
\begin{itemize}[nosep,leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \emph{Exploration term.}
Write $S_T:=\sum_{t=1}^T\alpha_t^2\,u_t(a_t)\,\omega_t^2$ for the sum of
squares of the per-round factors.  The exploration term is
$2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T\,S_T}$.  A sufficient condition
for sublinear regret is
\[
  \Lamalg_T\,S_T = o(T^2),
  \qquad
  E_T=o(T).
\]
Concretely this holds whenever the per-round factors are polylogarithmically
bounded ($\sup_t\alpha_t^2 u_t(a_t)\omega_t^2 = O(\mathrm{polylog}\,T)$, so
$S_T=O(T\,\mathrm{polylog}\,T)$) and the realized dynamic complexity is
small ($\Lamalg_T=O(\mathrm{polylog}\,T)$, e.g.\ via $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$
with both the variation charge $\Valg_T$ and the endpoint
$\log\det(\Calg_{T+1}/\lambda\bI)$ polylogarithmically bounded).
Note $\omega_t^2$ contains the cumulative squared linearization error
through $\bar F_t$ (via $\beta_t$) and the centering certificate
$\bar\psi_t$, so a small $\Lamalg_T$ alone is insufficient.
$\alpha_t$ is bounded whenever the CG tolerance $\bar\varepsilon_t$ is
bounded away from~$1$; in the exact-curvature case $u_t=(1+\bar\chi_t)^2$
is bounded whenever the whitened-drift certificate $\bar\chi_t$ is bounded
(\Cref{lem:whitened}); the whitened $\ell_2$ accumulation
$\chi_t\le\|\Dmat_t\|_F$ makes this a near-stationarity condition on
replayed-Jacobian drift.  The unstable-curvature cost is isolated in
$\Lamalg_T$ (through $\Valg_T$), not hidden in a spectral constant.
The factor $\sqrt{\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda}$ arises from the width-sum bound of the
dynamic width-complexity lemma (\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential},
Eq.~\eqref{eq:width-sum-dyn}); when $G^2\le\lambda\sigma^2$ it is at most
$\sigma\sqrt2$.
\item \emph{Linearization remainder.}
The bound involves $E_T=\sum_t\epslin(t)$, not $T\epslin$.
Near-linear regimes also require the squared-error sum
$F_T=\sum_t\epslin(t)^2$ (through $\bar F_t$, hence $\omega_t$) to be
controlled.  $\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(1/t)$ gives $E_T=O(1)$;
$\|\btheta^*{-}\btheta_t\|=O(t^{-1/4})$ gives $E_T=O(\sqrt{T})$.
The additive role of $E_T$ mirrors misspecified linear bandits, where a
uniformly bounded per-round model error commonly contributes an additive regret
term that scales linearly in the cumulative approximation error
\citep{ghosh2017misspecified,lattimore2020learning}.  The displayed bound
requires $E_T/T\to0$; a nonvanishing uniform per-round error bound alone yields
an $O(T)$ additive term.
\item \emph{Centering.}
The centering certificate enters \emph{multiplicatively}, through
$\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$ inside the sum of squares $S_T$, rather than as
a separate additive term.  It vanishes
($\bar\psi_t=0$) when $\btheta_t=\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$ (convex
last-layer models with exact optimization), and the corrected-center
variant (\Cref{cor:corrected-center}) removes it in general at the cost of
a frozen-feature solve.
\item \emph{Regret improvement over diagonal/last-layer surrogates.}
The bound itself does not prove that full curvature yields strictly lower
regret than diagonal or last-layer methods in any specific regime; it
provides an upper bound with explicit per-round factors.
\emph{Demonstrating regret improvement is an empirical hypothesis}
(\Cref{sec:experiments}).  The theorem exposes the transfer factor $u_t$ and
realized width complexity $\Lamalg_T$ as operator-dependent terms along the
realized trajectory; it does not identify a causal regret cost because the
adaptive path and other factors may also change.
\item \emph{Scope.}
The bound is proved for Gaussian/squared-loss rewards.
Extending it to exponential-family likelihoods (Bernoulli, categorical)
would require a weighted frozen-feature analysis accounting for the
data-dependent Fisher weights~$\nabla^2 A(z)$ and is left as future work
(\Cref{app:expfam}).
\end{itemize}

\begin{corollary}[Sanity-check: frozen features recover linear UCB]\label{cor:frozen}
Suppose the backbone is frozen and the reward head is linear in its
parameters, the ridge objective is optimized exactly, and take the algorithmic curvature to be the sequential frozen
Gram matrix, $\Calg_t=\bCbar_t$.  Then:
$\epslin(t)=0$ for all~$t$ ($L=0$ in \Cref{asm:linear}), so $E_T=F_T=0$;
$\bar\psi_t=0$ (under exact optimization the MAP and linearized estimators coincide, so
\Cref{asm:optim} holds with equality), giving $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$;
$u_t(a)=1$ for all $a$ (indeed $\bar s_t^2(a)=s_{\Calg,t}^2(a)$, so
\Cref{asm:transfer} holds with equality); and, since
$\bCbar_{t+1}=\bCbar_t+\sigma^{-2}\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)\bg_t(\bx_t,a_t)^\top$
by~\eqref{eq:bar-update}, the update is exactly the rank-one step, i.e.\
$\Calg_{t+1}=\Calg_t^{+}$, so $\Xi_t=0$, $\Valg_T=0$, and
$\Lamalg_T=\gamma_T$.  Then \Cref{thm:regret} gives
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\gamma_T\,
    \sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t^2\,\bar\beta_t^2}.
\]
If in addition $\bar\varepsilon_t\le\bar\varepsilon$ so that
$\alpha_t\le\alpha_I:=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon)/(1-\bar\varepsilon)}$, the
looser \Cref{cor:worstcase} recovers the familiar LinUCB form
$R_T\le 2\,\alpha_I\,\bar\beta_{\max,T}\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T}$,
where $\bar\beta_{\max,T}:=\max_{t\le T}\bar\beta_t$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $\epslin(t)=0$, $\bar\psi_t=0$, $u_t(a)=1$, and
$\Lamalg_T=\gamma_T$, $\Valg_T=0$ into~\eqref{eq:regret-bound}; the frozen
Gram is monotone, so $\Xi_t=0$ and the dynamic potential collapses to the
information gain $\gamma_T$.  The LinUCB form is \Cref{cor:worstcase} with
$\alpha_t\le\alpha_I$, $u_t=1$, $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t\le\bar\beta_{\max,T}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Looser worst-case reduction]\label{cor:worstcase}
If $\alpha_t\le\alpha_{\max}$, $u_t(a_t)\le u_{\max}$, and
$\omega_t\le\omega_{\max}$ for all $t\le T$, then
$S_T\le\alpha_{\max}^2 u_{\max}\omega_{\max}^2\,T$
and
\[
  R_T \;\le\; 2\,\alpha_{\max}\,\omega_{\max}\,\sqrt{u_{\max}}\,
  \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\Lamalg_T}\;+\;2E_T .
\]
This $\sqrt{T\,\Lamalg_T}$ form recovers the familiar worst-case expression;
it is weakly looser than~\eqref{eq:regret-bound} (no tighter, with equality when
the per-round factors are constant) and is \emph{not} the main result.
\end{corollary}

\section{Proof of \texorpdfstring{\Cref{thm:regret}}{the main regret theorem}}\label{app:proof}

We recall the key objects:
$\bg_s(\bx,a)=\nabla_{\btheta}\mu_{\btheta_s}(\bx,a)$ (frozen features),
$\bCbar_t=\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top$
(frozen-feature Gram matrix),
$\bar s_t(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\bCbar_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)}$,
the algorithmic curvature $\Calg_t$ with width
$s_{\Calg,t}(a)=\sqrt{\bg_t(a)^\top\Calg_t^{-1}\bg_t(a)}$,
the linearized ridge estimator $\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:thetahat-lin}), the combined width factor
$\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$, the per-round CG inflation
$\alpha_t=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon_t)/(1-\bar\varepsilon_t)}$, and the
realized dynamic complexity $\Lamalg_T$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:lam-alg}).
Write $\widehat\mu_t(a)=\mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_t,a)$.

\paragraph{Step 1: Confidence, centering, and prediction-error bound.}
By \Cref{lem:confidence}, with probability $\ge 1-\delta$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conf-app}
  \bigl|\bg_t(a)^\top(\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}}-\btheta^*)\bigr|
  \;\le\; \bar s_t(a)\,\beta_t,
  \qquad\forall\,t,\,a,
\end{equation}
where $\beta_t$ is as in~\eqref{eq:beta}.
The self-normalized martingale inequality
\citep{abbasi2011improved} is applied to the $1$-sub-Gaussian sequence
$\xi_s=\eta_s/\sigma$ with the predictable design $\bg_s/\sigma$
and Gram matrix~$\bCbar_t$.
Each realized design vector~$\bg_s(\bx_s,a_s)$ is
$\cG_s$-measurable (where $\cG_s=\cHist_s\vee\sigma(a_s)$ is
the pre-reward sigma-algebra of~\eqref{eq:filtration}), and the sequential
update~\eqref{eq:bar-update} ensures the hypotheses
of the self-normalized bound are satisfied.
By \Cref{lem:discrepancy} (\Cref{asm:optim}),
$|\bg_t(a)^\top(\btheta_t{-}\hat\btheta_t^{\mathrm{lin}})|\le
\bar\psi_t\,\bar s_t(a)$.
Combined with $|\rho_t|\le\epslin(t)$ and $\bar\beta_t\ge\beta_t$, the
triangle inequality gives the prediction-error
bound~\eqref{eq:pred-error}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pred-app}
  |\mu_{\btheta^*}(\bx_t,a)-\widehat\mu_t(a)|
  \;\le\;
  (\beta_t+\bar\psi_t)\,\bar s_t(a) + \epslin(t)
  \;\le\;
  \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a) + \epslin(t),
  \qquad\forall\,a .
\end{equation}
(The corrected-center variant of \Cref{cor:corrected-center} reaches
\eqref{eq:pred-app} with $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$ and without
\Cref{asm:optim}.)

\paragraph{Step 2: Bonus sandwiching.}
By \Cref{lem:bonus} (which uses \Cref{lem:cg} with
$\max_{a\in\cA_t}\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\bar\varepsilon_t$ and the one-sided
transfer~\eqref{eq:transfer}), for all $a$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bonus-app}
  \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a)
  \;\le\;
  w_t(a)
  \;\le\;
  \alpha_t\,\omega_t\,\sqrt{u_t(a)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a).
\end{equation}
The lower bound and~\eqref{eq:pred-app} give
$\mu^*(\bx_t,a)\le \widehat\mu_t(a)+w_t(a)+\epslin(t)$ for all~$a$: the UCB covers
$\mu^*$ up to the additive $\epslin(t)$ model-mismatch term.

\paragraph{Step 3: Instantaneous regret.}
From the UCB rule $\widehat\mu_t(a_t)+w_t(a_t)\ge \widehat\mu_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)$, and using
$\mu^*(a_t^*)\le \widehat\mu_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)+\epslin(t)$ (Step~2 at $a_t^*$, valid
since $a_t^*\in\cA_t$),
\begin{align*}
  \mu^*(a_t^*)-\mu^*(a_t)
  &\;\le\; [\widehat\mu_t(a_t^*)+w_t(a_t^*)+\epslin(t)] - \mu^*(a_t) \\
  &\;\le\; [\widehat\mu_t(a_t)+w_t(a_t)+\epslin(t)] - \mu^*(a_t)
    \quad\text{(UCB selection)} \\
  &\;=\; w_t(a_t) + [\widehat\mu_t(a_t)-\mu^*(a_t)] + \epslin(t) \\
  &\;\le\; w_t(a_t) + \omega_t\,\bar s_t(a_t) + 2\epslin(t)
    \quad\text{(\eqref{eq:pred-app} at $a_t$)} \\
  &\;\le\; 2\,w_t(a_t) + 2\epslin(t)
    \quad\text{(lower bound in~\eqref{eq:bonus-app})}.
\end{align*}

\paragraph{Step 4: Upper bonus bound.}
The upper bound in~\eqref{eq:bonus-app} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret-round}
  \mu^*(a_t^*)-\mu^*(a_t)
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_t\,\omega_t\,\sqrt{u_t(a_t)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a_t)
  \;+\; 2\epslin(t).
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Step 5: Cauchy--Schwarz after summing.}
Summing~\eqref{eq:regret-round} over $t=1,\dots,T$ and applying
Cauchy--Schwarz to the sum (\emph{not} per round),
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t\omega_t\sqrt{u_t(a_t)}\,s_{\Calg,t}(a_t) + 2E_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t^2\omega_t^2 u_t(a_t)}\,
  \sqrt{\sum_{t=1}^T s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)} + 2E_T .
\]

\paragraph{Step 6: Dynamic potential and collection.}
\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential} gives
$\sum_{t=1}^T s_{\Calg,t}^2(a_t)\le(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T$.
Substituting,
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\Lamalg_T\,
    \sum_{t=1}^T \alpha_t^2\,u_t(a_t)\,\omega_t^2} \;+\; 2E_T,
\]
which is~\eqref{eq:regret-bound}.  The interpretable and observable
versions~\eqref{eq:regret-bound-dyn} follow from $\Lamalg_T\le\Gamdyn_T$
(\Cref{lem:dynamic-potential}) and $\Lamalg_T\le\widehat{\Lamalg}_T$
(\Cref{cor:lam-observable}).  The Cauchy--Schwarz step is applied only after
summing, so the time-varying factors $\alpha_t,u_t(a_t),\omega_t$ enter
through a sum of squares; no horizon maximum is taken before Cauchy--Schwarz, and
the usual $\sqrt{T}$ scaling is recovered when the per-round factors are
uniformly bounded (\Cref{cor:worstcase}).  The stated probability follows from a
union bound over the self-normalized confidence event
$\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}$ ($\ge 1-\delta$) and the certificate event
$\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$ ($\ge 1-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$), giving
$\ge 1-\delta-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$.
\qed

\section{CG Convergence Analysis}\label{app:cg}

Fix a round~$t$ and an action~$a$ with $\bg:=\bg_t(a)\ne\mathbf0$.  After
$I_{t,a}$~iterations of CG on the SPD system $\Calg_t\bu=\bg$ (starting
from $\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf{0}$), the standard energy-norm bound gives
\[
  \varepsilon_{t,a}
  \;=\;
  \frac{\|\tilde\bu_{I_{t,a}} - \Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}
       {\|\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}
  \;\le\;
  2\!\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_t}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa_t}+1}\right)^{\!I_{t,a}},
\]
where $\kappa_t=\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)/\lambda_{\min}(\Calg_t)$
is the true condition number.  The bound is right-hand-side independent, so a
common iteration budget bounds $\max_a\varepsilon_{t,a}$.  This zero-start form is
what the fixed-budget certificate~\eqref{eq:cg-budget} uses.
For a \emph{warm start} $\tilde\bu_0\ne\mathbf0$, energy-norm minimization over the
shifted Krylov space instead gives
\[
  \frac{\|\tilde\bu_{I_{t,a}}-\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}{\|\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}
  \;\le\;
  2\,q^{\,I_{t,a}}\,
  \frac{\|\tilde\bu_0-\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}}{\|\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}},
  \qquad q=\frac{\sqrt{\kappa_t}-1}{\sqrt{\kappa_t}+1},
\]
i.e.\ the extra factor is the initial relative energy error $\|e_0\|_{\Calg_t}/
\|\Calg_t^{-1}\bg\|_{\Calg_t}$ (equal to one at $\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf0$, recovering the
  zero-start bound).  Under the same certified condition-number bound, adaptive
residual stopping (line~\ref{line:cg}) remains valid with a warm start because it
certifies the realized residual directly; but the fixed-budget
count~\eqref{eq:cg-budget} is valid \emph{as written} only for zero initialization,
and a fixed-budget warm-start certificate additionally requires a bound on that
initial relative energy error (the zero-start iteration count does not by itself
certify a warm-started solve).
Any certified upper bound $\bar\kappa_t\ge\kappa_t$ may replace $\kappa_t$; for a
nonnegative weighted outer-product operator,
$\lambda_{\min}(\Calg_t)\ge\lambda$ and
$\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)\le\lambda+W_tG^2/\sigma^2$
(where $W_t=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_t}w_s$) give
\[
\kappa_t\le\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)}{\lambda}
\le1+\frac{W_tG^2}{\lambda\sigma^2}=: \bar\kappa_t,
\]
whereas a general SPD operator
requires a separately certified $\bar\kappa_t$.

Two cases at value one must be distinguished.  The true condition number
$\kappa_t=1$ forces all eigenvalues equal, i.e.\ $\Calg_t=c_t\bI$ for some
scalar $c_t>0$, and one CG iteration from $\tilde\bu_0=\mathbf0$ is \emph{exact}
($\varepsilon_{t,a}=0$) for every $\bg\ne\mathbf0$; this does \emph{not} by itself
imply $c_t=\lambda$.  With the specific certificate used here,
$\bar\kappa_t=1$
additionally forces $\lambda_{\max}(\Calg_t)=\lambda$, which together with
$\Calg_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ gives $\Calg_t=\lambda\bI$ exactly.  In either reading
the single-step solve is exact, so we keep $\bar\kappa_t=1$ as a separate branch and
never evaluate the logarithmic budget below at its zero denominator.  For
$\bar\kappa_t>1$, setting the
right-hand side of the rate equal to $\epsCGbar$ and requiring
$\epsCGbar < 1$ (where $\epsCGbar$ denotes the round-$t$ target tolerance
$\bar\varepsilon_t$) gives the per-solve budget
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cg-budget}
  I_{\min}(\bar\kappa_t,\epsCGbar)
  \;=\;
  \left\lceil\frac{\log(2/\epsCGbar)}
  {\log\!\bigl((\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}+1)/(\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}-1)\bigr)}
  \right\rceil
  \;=\; O\!\bigl(\sqrt{\bar\kappa_t}\,\log(1/\epsCGbar)\bigr),
\end{equation}
which, applied to every scored action, ensures
$\max_a\varepsilon_{t,a}\le\epsCGbar=\bar\varepsilon_t$.
For $W_t=n$ (unrescaled fixed buffer size),
$\bar\kappa_t\le 1+nG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2)$ is bounded independently of~$T$,
so $I_{\min}$ is constant.
For full history, and for rescaled uniform subsampling with
$w_s=(t{-}1)/n$, we have $W_t=t{-}1$, so the condition-number \emph{bound}
$\bar\kappa_t=O(tG^2/(\lambda\sigma^2))$ grows with~$t$, and the worst-case
certificate is then obtained with
$I_{\min}=\Theta\!\bigl(G\sqrt{t/(\lambda\sigma^2)}\,\log(1/\epsCGbar)\bigr)$
iterations per solve---i.e.\ $I=O(\sqrt{t}\,\log(1/\epsCGbar))$ suffices under this
bound (this is an upper-bound certificate, not a lower bound on the number of
iterations CG actually needs).
CG may converge faster than this worst-case certificate suggests under favorable
spectral clustering of~$\Calg_t$; the realized iteration counts would be measured empirically.

\section{Two-Sided Spectral-Distortion Corollary}\label{app:cor}

The main-text \Cref{thm:spectral-distortion} uses only a one-sided upper
transfer factor.  For comparison we record the earlier two-sided result,
which instead assumes both an upper \emph{and} a lower spectral factor and
yields the familiar $\sqrt{\rho_+/\rho_-}$ inflation.  The two results trade
different assumptions---the corollary below needs a lower spectral bound;
the dynamic \Cref{thm:regret} needs the dynamic width complexity
$\Lamalg_T$ instead---and neither numerically dominates the other in
general.

\begin{corollary}[Two-sided spectral-distortion inflation]\label{cor:twosided}
Adopt the hypotheses of \Cref{thm:regret} with $\Calg_t=\hat\bC_t$, retaining the
\emph{original nonlinear center} (so $\omega_t=\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t$; in the
convex last-layer regime under exact optimization, $\bar\psi_t=0$ and
$\omega_t=\bar\beta_t$), and a uniform
CG tolerance $\bar\varepsilon_t\le\bar\varepsilon$ so that
$\alpha_t\le\alpha_I:=\sqrt{(1+\bar\varepsilon)/(1-\bar\varepsilon)}$.
Suppose predictable factors $\rho_{-,t},\rho_{+,t}$ are certified before
action selection and the \emph{two-sided} sandwich
$\rho_{-,t}\bCbar_t\preceq\hat\bC_t\preceq\rho_{+,t}\bCbar_t$ holds for all
$t\le T$ with $0<\rho_{-,t}\le\rho_{+,t}<\infty$; set
$\rho_-=\min_t\rho_{-,t}$, $\rho_+=\max_t\rho_{+,t}$, and (to satisfy the
convention $u_t\ge1$ of \Cref{asm:transfer}) take the transfer factor
$u_t:=\max\{1,\rho_{+,t}\}\le\max\{1,\rho_+\}$.  Write
$\omega_{\max,T}:=\max_{t\le T}(\bar\beta_t+\bar\psi_t)$ and
$\rho_+^{\star}:=\max\{1,\rho_+\}$.  Then, on the
intersection of the confidence event $\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}$ ($\ge1-\delta$) and the
certificate event $\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$ (which now also contains the two-sided
sandwich event; $\ge1-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$),
\[
  R_T
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_I\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_-}\;\omega_{\max,T}
  \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T} \;+\; 2E_T
\]
with probability at least $1-\delta-\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}$, where $\gamma_T$ is
the frozen-feature information gain~\eqref{eq:infogain}.  Under the standard
normalization $\rho_+\ge1$ (achievable by rescaling the sandwich so its upper
factor is at least one), $\rho_+^{\star}=\rho_+$ and the inflation is the familiar
$\sqrt{\rho_+/\rho_-}$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
This is a \emph{direct} argument through the monotone frozen Gram $\bCbar_t$; it
does \emph{not} substitute $\hat s_t$ for the dynamic width in
\Cref{cor:worstcase}.
\emph{Step 1 (variance sandwich).}  Inverting the two-sided sandwich gives
$\rho_{+,t}^{-1}\bCbar_t^{-1}\preceq\hat\bC_t^{-1}\preceq\rho_{-,t}^{-1}\bCbar_t^{-1}$,
so, evaluating the quadratic form at $\bg_t(a)$,
$\rho_{+,t}^{-1/2}\,\bar s_t(a)\le\hat s_t(a)\le\rho_{-,t}^{-1/2}\,\bar s_t(a)$.
The lower half gives $\bar s_t^2(a)\le\rho_{+,t}\hat s_t^2(a)\le u_t\hat s_t^2(a)$,
so \Cref{asm:transfer} holds with $u_t=\max\{1,\rho_{+,t}\}$.
\emph{Step 2 (per-round regret).}  With $s_{\Calg,t}=\hat s_t$,
\Cref{thm:regret} Steps~1--4 give
$\mathrm{regret}_t\le
2\,\alpha_t\,\omega_t\,\sqrt{u_t}\,\hat s_t(a_t)+2\epslin(t)$; using
$\hat s_t(a_t)\le\rho_{-,t}^{-1/2}\bar s_t(a_t)$,
$u_t=\max\{1,\rho_{+,t}\}\le\rho_+^{\star}$,
$\alpha_t\le\alpha_I$, and $\omega_t\le\omega_{\max,T}$,
\[
  \mathrm{regret}_t
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_I\,\omega_{\max,T}\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_{-,t}}\;\bar s_t(a_t)
  +2\epslin(t)
  \;\le\;
  2\,\alpha_I\,\omega_{\max,T}\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_-}\;\bar s_t(a_t)
  +2\epslin(t).
\]
\emph{Step 3 (frozen-feature elliptic potential).}  Sum over $t$ and apply
Cauchy--Schwarz, then the classical elliptic-potential identity for the
\emph{monotone} Gram $\bCbar_t$ (\eqref{eq:bar-update}),
$\sum_{t}\bar s_t^2(a_t)\le(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\sum_t\log(1+\sigma^{-2}
\bar s_t^2(a_t))=(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,\gamma_T$:
\[
  R_T\le 2\,\alpha_I\,\omega_{\max,T}\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_-}
  \sqrt{T\sum_t\bar s_t^2(a_t)}+2E_T
  \le 2\,\alpha_I\,\omega_{\max,T}\,\sqrt{\rho_+^{\star}/\rho_-}
  \sqrt{(\sigma^2+G^2/\lambda)\,T\,\gamma_T}+2E_T.
\]
The probability statement is the union bound over $\cE_{\mathrm{conf}}$ and
$\cE_{\mathrm{cert}}$.
\end{proof}

\noindent
For \Cref{lem:subsample}, allocate $\delta_{\mathrm{cert}}/T$ per round and use
the worst-case $m=T{-}1$ in \eqref{eq:subsample-n} to obtain the required
time-uniform sandwich.



\section{GGN Approximation Error}\label{app:ggn-approx}

\begin{lemma}[GGN approximation error]\label{lem:ggn-approx}
Suppose $\mu_{\btheta}(\bx,a)$ is twice differentiable in~$\btheta$.
Suppose $|r_s - \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)| \le \epsilon_r$ and
$\|\nabla^2 \mu_{\btheta_t}(\bx_s,a_s)\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le H$
for all $s < t$.
Define $\zeta := (t{-}1)\,\epsilon_r\, H / (\sigma^2 \lambda)$.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,label=(\roman*)]
\item $\|\bH_t - \bC_t\|_{\mathrm{op}}
\le (t{-}1)\,\epsilon_r\, H / \sigma^2 = \zeta\lambda$.
\item If $\zeta < 1$, then
$(1{-}\zeta)\bC_t \preceq \bH_t \preceq (1{+}\zeta)\bC_t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For $D_t=\sigma^{-2}\sum_{s<t}(r_s-\mu_{\btheta_t})
\nabla^2\mu_{\btheta_t}$, the triangle inequality gives
$\|D_t\|_{\rm op}\le\zeta\lambda$, proving~(i).  Combining
$-\|D_t\|\bI\preceq-D_t\preceq\|D_t\|\bI$ with
$\bC_t\succeq\lambda\bI$ proves~(ii).
\end{proof}

\section{Exponential-Family Curvature Definitions}\label{app:expfam}

For a regular exponential-family negative log-likelihood with natural parameter
$z_{\btheta}(\bx,a)\in\R^{k}$ (scalar for Bernoulli, $k$-dimensional for
categorical) and log-partition function $A$, the GGN/Fisher curvature matrices are
\begin{align}
  \bC_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
  &\;=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    J_t(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\,\nabla^2 A\bigl(z_t(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)\,
    J_t(\bx_s,a_s),
  \label{eq:curvature-exp}\\[4pt]
  \bCbar_t^{\mathrm{exp}}
  &\;=\;
  \lambda\,\bI
  \;+\;
  \sum_{s=1}^{t-1}
    J_s(\bx_s,a_s)^\top\,\nabla^2 A\bigl(z_s(\bx_s,a_s)\bigr)\,
    J_s(\bx_s,a_s),
  \label{eq:curvature-bar-exp}
\end{align}
where $J_t(\bx,a)=\nabla_{\btheta}z_{\btheta_t}(\bx,a)\in\R^{k\times d}$
is the Jacobian of the natural parameter and
$\nabla^2 A(z)$ is the output-space Hessian of the log-partition
function.
For Bernoulli rewards $\nabla^2 A(z)=p(1{-}p)$ (scalar);
for categorical rewards $\nabla^2 A(z)=\mathrm{Diag}(\mathbf{p})
-\mathbf{p}\mathbf{p}^\top)$.
Setting $k=1$ and $\nabla^2 A=\sigma^{-2}$ recovers the
Gaussian curvature~\eqref{eq:curvature}--\eqref{eq:curvature-bar}.
