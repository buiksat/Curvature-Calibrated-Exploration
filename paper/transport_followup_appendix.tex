\paragraph{Reading the bound scale.}
The logarithm of a mean bound-to-cap ratio differs from the mean of the
logarithms and from checks of individual bounds. Subtracting the path
inflation from the Hessian-path bound does not reconstruct another policy's
bound or outcome.

\paragraph{Taylor envelope.}
Every first-study policy uses the Taylor envelope at the current parameter
norm, $\epslin=\tfrac12(c_h/\sqrt W)(R+\|\btheta_t\|_2)^2$ with
$c_h=4/(3\sqrt3)$ and $R=B_\phi=1$, both for the error allowance $b_t$ and for
the historical terms of $\beta_t$. At $\|\btheta_t\|_2=R$ it equals the
constant $2c_hR^2/\sqrt W$ of \Cref{cor:global-near-linear-new}: 0.385, 1.540
and 3.079 at $W=16,1,0.25$. The per-run mean envelope is 98.3\%--98.5\% of
that constant (median over seeds, for every policy and condition).

\subsection{Prespecified protocol, uncertainty, and provenance}

\paragraph{Environment and data splits.}
The full study fixes teacher seed 1729, development seeds 30000--30001,
tuning seeds 30100--30109, and evaluation seeds 30200--30249.
Contexts are normalized Rademacher vectors.  The feature map concatenates the
context, action indicator, and their tensor product, then normalizes it to
unit norm; its dimension is $4+5+20=29$.  The teacher has unit norm and a fixed
regular-simplex construction in the interaction block.  The learner starts at
zero, uses training ridge one, and receives only the selected action's reward.
Rewards are Gaussian with scaled-tanh mean, $\|\phi(z)\|_2\le1$, projected
$\|\btheta\|_2\le1$, noise standard deviation $0.25$, metric ridge one and
$\delta=0.05$. Dense float64 Cholesky solves implement exact-current operators;
action ties use the smallest index.
The potential-noise table couples methods without revealing unselected rewards.
The 24 development trajectories cover three conditions and four policies at
both development seeds.

\paragraph{Selecting a shared optimizer.}
We select the optimizer by prediction accuracy, using histories generated
independently of the study policies' evaluation decisions.  The 270 tuning
trajectories cover nine optimizer
candidates, three conditions, and ten tuning seeds, using uniform-random
actions.  The nine candidates combine learning rates
$\{3\times10^{-5},10^{-4},3\times10^{-4}\}$ with $\{1,5,20\}$ full-batch steps.
All were eligible.  Candidate 008, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$ and 20 steps,
minimized equally weighted all-action prediction MSE over the 30 tuning
seed--condition cells, with selected MSE $0.00392155$.
Evaluation consists of $3\times4\times50=600$ further trajectories.  The 294
development and tuning trajectories are not evaluation replicates.

\paragraph{Numerical checks before evaluation.}
A documented development-only numerical refinement froze Gauss--Legendre
quadrature order 2048 before tuning and evaluation.  The full development gate
compared orders 1024 and 2048 on all 24 declared trajectories; a separate
development check also compared 2048 and 4096.  No failed development check was
reclassified as a passing evaluation.

\paragraph{What the intervals measure.}
Pairing compares policies exposed to the same underlying context and
potential-noise stream.
The paired-bootstrap intervals resample the 50 evaluation seeds within a
condition, preserving the pairing between policies, with 10,000 resamples and
the recorded deterministic bootstrap seed. Rounds within a trajectory are
dependent, and seeds shared across conditions do not make all runs one
independent population. The condition labels in the configuration are
encodings of $W$, not measured endpoint distances.

The main-text table gives the terminal numerical values; its retained per-seed
inputs support independent bootstrap reconstruction.

\paragraph{Descriptive benchmarks.}
These benchmarks provide descriptive reference performance levels.
The online context-free benchmark samples each action once, then selects the
smallest-index action with the largest empirical mean of its own selected
rewards, ignoring context.  The best-fixed-action benchmark instead selects in
hindsight the action with the largest total true mean along the realized
context stream.  Both use the same potential-outcome streams as the study policies.
They do not estimate the best contextual performance or bound the benefit of
using context, and they do not show that policies match in their actions, use
context, or are equivalent.

\paragraph{What the retained evidence can reproduce.}
The portable full-study export records the configuration, selected optimizer,
development/tuning summaries, per-seed terminal outcomes and drift summaries.
Its independent verifier reconstructs terminal means and paired intervals.
The original numerical tables were generated by
\texttt{tools/make\_transport\_followup\_paper.py};
\texttt{transport\_followup\_provenance.json} records that generation.
\Cref{tab:transport-followup-outcomes} is rendered separately by
\texttt{tools/make\_tmlr\_outcomes.py}, with its own
\texttt{transport\_outcomes\_provenance.json}.

The explanatory and diagnostic charts were originally generated by
\texttt{tools/make\_transport\_explanatory\_figures.py} and
\texttt{tools/make\_transport\_table\_charts.py}; their original input/output
digests remain in \texttt{transport\_explanatory\_provenance.json} and
\texttt{transport\_table\_provenance.json}.
The conceptual figures
illustrate the certificate interfaces and analytic example.

\subsection{Exploratory recommendations on fixed histories}

This analysis asks what different scores recommend when given the same
information.  For each logged state, we evaluate all four original score
rules using that state's matrices, centers, and radii, without changing its
history.
\Cref{fig:transport-followup-same-history} includes all 12 combinations of
condition and behavior policy.  Each point averages one-step pseudo-regret
over 50,000 dependent states; the black-ringed points equal the actual mean cumulative
pseudo-regret divided by 1000.  The other points are recommendations on
states visited by the row policy.    The analysis was specified after the
full-study outcomes were observed.

\paragraph{When the width bonus determines the action.}
A large bonus can make the widest action win even when its predicted reward
is lower.  A sufficient check is that its bonus advantage exceeds the largest
possible difference between prediction centers on the recorded state.
Write the primary score on a recorded state as
$m_t(a)+B_t s_t(a)+b_t$, where $b_t$ is action independent and
$B_t=\beta_t^{\rm corr}\exp(\bar D_t^Q/2)$.
Let $s_{t,(1)}\ge s_{t,(2)}$ be the largest two current widths.
The strict condition
\[
 B_t\bigl(s_{t,(1)}-s_{t,(2)}\bigr)
 >\max_a m_t(a)-\min_a m_t(a)
\]
is sufficient for the unique largest-width action to maximize the score.
Width-argmax agreement alone is weaker: prediction centers might favor the
same action.  The exact tie rule is the smallest action index.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to score rounding.}
We distinguish recomputing the final score from recomputing the quantities
that enter it.  This matters when two actions have nearly equal scores.
The numerical scan reconstructs the recorded selected scores exactly and
finds no nonfinite coefficients or scores and no zero widths.  A separate
250-digit decimal calculation evaluates the final score arithmetic from the
exact stored binary64 centers, widths, offsets, and \emph{already rounded}
coefficient $B_t$.  It changes the primary recommendation at 16 states:
five, five, and six in the respective conditions.  Across all behavior
histories there are 65 changes among 600,000 states.  All occur at rounds
2--10, with score differences below 0.629 of a binary64 score ULP
(the spacing of representable numbers at the score's magnitude).
The decimal calculation does not recompute upstream solves, exponentials or
fitted parameters, the downstream effects of these early ranking changes are
unmeasured, and equal scores, including the initially zero centers, do not by
themselves indicate corrupted values.

\subsection{Exploratory relative-certificate audit}

The endpoint constructions compare the current matrix directly with the
reference matrix.  This audit checks their implementation and measures the
resulting widths and recommendations on recorded states.

\paragraph{Which factors are compared?}
For unit feature and parameter bounds, \Cref{cor:tanh-relative-weights} gives
the uniform endpoint bound $D_0=4\log\cosh(1/\sqrt W)$, approximately
$0.124$, $1.735$, and $5.300$ at the three conditions.  The category-specific
sandwich $c_{-,t}\bVbar_t\preceq\bV_t\preceq c_{+,t}\bVbar_t$ gives a
symmetric coefficient $\exp(D_{{\rm cat},t}/2)$, where
$D_{{\rm cat},t}=\max\{-\log c_{-,t},\log c_{+,t}\}$, or the separate outward
coefficient $\sqrt{c_{+,t}}$ of \Cref{cor:relative-endpoint-ucb}.
The latter retains the separate return factor $c_{-,t}^{-1/2}$ in the
cumulative bound.

\paragraph{Replay and numerical checks.}
The post-outcome audit replays the original representation optimizer with
fixed logged actions and rewards for all 600 trajectories; it never replaces
a behavior action or generates a new observation.  It reconstructs
collection-time category weights and compares the resulting sandwich,
effective widths, and recommendations at the stored states.  Matrix, center,
and endpoint reconstruction are checked at rounds 1, 2, 10, 100, 500, and 1000;
the original float64 tolerances are retained.  All declared checks
passed; the largest residual divided by its original tolerance was below
$0.005$. Float64 solves, eigenvalues, quadrature, replay residuals and source
hashes are not rigorous numerical enclosures.  The per-seed variation in the preceding figure was reproduced by this
separate fixed-action replay.

\paragraph{How to read the width and regret comparisons.}
The upper panel of \Cref{fig:transport-followup-relative-audit} compares each
effective width with its reference width before averaging.  The lower panel
asks what immediate expected reward is lost by the recommendations on those
same states.

\paragraph{What the timing records leave out.}
Fixed-state kernel timings are retained in the audit artifact.  They were collected under parallel analysis load.
Fixed-state timings under parallel load exclude matrix construction, confidence
estimation and the policy loop, so do not establish end-to-end speedup.  The category construction requires additional
accumulated collection weights and still uses matrix solves and the frozen
confidence construction.

\subsection{Protocol of the second study}
\label{app:transport-v3}

\paragraph{Seeds and phases.}
Development seeds 41000--41009, tuning seeds 41100--41109 and evaluation
seeds 41200--41249 are disjoint from the first study's. Development runs fixed
the design-cell scale, the margin and the numerical checks; development and
tuning runs are not evaluation replicates. The design cell uses $c=1$ in
$W=cT$, the smallest $c$ in $\{1,10,100\}$ for which every development frozen
bound was at most 0.9 of the cap; no record of this rule predates the first
development output. The margin $\Delta=3.0$ is a quarter of the
development mean Hessian-path-minus-frozen difference, 12.0, and was fixed before
evaluation. Evaluation runs were not repeated.

\paragraph{Which scales keep the a priori frozen bound below the cap.}
Let $W=cT$ with $W\ge1$, so that the regret cap is $2T$. In
\Cref{prop:frozen-width-ucb}, bound $\gamma_{t-1}\le\Gamma_{T,d}$ by
\eqref{eq:finite-d-gamma-new} with $G=B_\phi$, the historical and current
Taylor envelopes by $2c_hR^2/\sqrt W$, the misspecification envelopes by zero,
and $\sum_t\beta_t^2$ by $T$ times the largest $\beta_t^2$. With $B_\phi=1$,
the resulting a priori frozen bound divided by the regret cap is
\[
 \Bigl[\sqrt{\Gamma_{T,d}+2\log(1/\delta)}+\sqrt\lambda R\Bigr]
 \sqrt{\frac{(\sigma^2+1/\lambda)\Gamma_{T,d}}{T}}
 +\frac{1}{\sqrt c}\Bigl[\frac{2c_hR^2}{\sigma}
 \sqrt{\frac{(\sigma^2+1/\lambda)\Gamma_{T,d}}{T}}
 +\frac{2c_hR^2}{\sqrt T}\Bigr]
 =:A+\frac{B}{\sqrt c}.
\]
At the constants of \Cref{sec:transport-v3}, $A=0.556$ and $B=0.374$. The
ratio therefore decreases in $c$, equals 0.93 in the design cell, and is below
one exactly when $c>(B/(1-A))^2=0.709$. When $W<1$ the cap $2T\sqrt W$ is
smaller than $2T$ while the bound is unchanged, so the ratio exceeds $A+B/\sqrt
c$ and is above one there as well. As $c$ grows the ratio decreases to $A$.

\paragraph{Optimizer.}
Each corrected-center policy fits $\btheta_t$ by projected full-batch gradient
descent on $\sum_{s<t}(r_s-\mu_{\btheta}(z_s))^2/(2\sigma^2)+\|\btheta\|_2^2/2$
from $\btheta_1=0$: a fixed number of steps per round, then one projection
onto $\|\btheta\|_2\le1$. Sixty tuning runs with uniform-random actions in the
design cell compared learning rates $\{3\times10^{-6},10^{-5},3\times10^{-5}\}$
and $\{5,20\}$ steps by all-action prediction MSE after a 10\% burn-in,
averaged over the ten tuning seeds. All six candidates were eligible; learning
rate $3\times10^{-6}$ with 5 steps had the smallest mean MSE,
$4.062\times10^{-5}$, and the six means were within 0.2\% of each other. The
same optimizer is used in every cell.

\paragraph{Analysis.}
The unit of analysis is the evaluation seed, and every comparison is paired by
seed. The design checks are: one-sided paired $t$-tests of policy minus
context-free benchmark, with Holm's procedure over the six policies at
familywise level $0.05$; two one-sided tests with $\Delta=3.0$ for endpoint
minus frozen, naive minus frozen and endpoint minus naive, using the
two-sided 90\% paired-$t$ interval and holding only if all three pairs pass;
and the frozen bound below the cap on every frozen run. The primary family is
UCB-GLM minus frozen and Hessian-path minus frozen, each a two-sided paired $t$-test,
with Holm's procedure over the two at familywise level $0.05$. All six
policies have lower mean regret than the context-free benchmark in the design
cell, with every $p\le1.2\times10^{-108}$ in the first family. Every reported
interval is a 95\% percentile bootstrap interval from 10,000 resamples of
seed-paired differences with a fixed generator seed; intervals are
descriptive, and decisions use the $t$-based procedures.

\paragraph{Matrices and numerical checks.}
The current matrix is computed from category counts,
$\bV_t=\lambda\bI+\sigma^{-2}\sum_cn_{t,c}\operatorname{sech}^4
(\phi_c^\top\btheta_t/\sqrt W)\phi_c\phi_c^\top$, and $Q_t$ from a running mean
and scatter of $\btheta_1,\ldots,\btheta_{t-1}$, which avoids the cancellation
of raw running sums at small $W$. At rounds 1, 10, 100, 1,000, 10,000 and
20,000 each corrected-center run rebuilds $\bV_t$, the frozen matrix and $Q_t$
by dense per-observation replay; the tolerance per check is
$4096\,\varepsilon_{\rm mach}\,d\max(1,m)$, with $m$ the largest absolute entry
of the two compared objects. UCB-GLM solves its score equation by Newton's
method with Armijo backtracking on the convex objective
$\sum_c[n_cW\log\cosh(\phi_c^\top\theta/\sqrt W)-S_c\phi_c^\top\theta]$, where
$S_c$ is the reward sum of category $c$, warm-started and with at most 50
iterations. A replay excess, a Newton nonconvergence, an unreached random-phase
stopping rule or an exception would have been recorded and the run retained;
none occurred.
