\section{Full-study protocol and exploratory diagnostics}
\label{app:transport-followup}

This appendix explains how the study was run and what its later diagnostics
can tell us.  The protocol and policy comparisons were specified before
evaluation.  The analyses on recorded histories came afterward: they hold
the learner's information fixed to examine scores, rather than rerun an
adaptive policy after changing its action.

\subsection{Prespecified protocol, uncertainty, and provenance}

\paragraph{Environment and data splits.}
The full study fixes teacher seed 1729, development seeds 30000--30001,
tuning seeds 30100--30109, and evaluation seeds 30200--30249.
Contexts are normalized Rademacher vectors.  The feature map concatenates the
context, action indicator, and their tensor product, then normalizes it to
unit norm; its dimension is $4+5+20=29$.  The teacher has unit norm and a fixed
regular-simplex construction in the interaction block.  The learner starts at
zero, uses training ridge one, and receives only the selected action's reward.
The potential-noise table couples methods without revealing unselected rewards.
The 24 development trajectories cover three conditions and four policies at
both development seeds.

\paragraph{Selecting a shared optimizer.}
We select the optimizer by prediction accuracy, using histories generated
independently of the study policies' evaluation decisions.  The 270 tuning
trajectories cover nine optimizer
candidates, three conditions, and ten tuning seeds, using uniform-random
actions.  The nine candidates combine learning rates
$\{3\times10^{-5},10^{-4},3\times10^{-4}\}$ with $\{1,5,20\}$ full-batch steps.
All were eligible.  Candidate 008, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$ and 20 steps,
minimized equally weighted all-action prediction MSE over the 30 tuning
seed--condition cells, with selected MSE $0.00392155$.
Evaluation consists of $3\times4\times50=600$ further trajectories.  The 294
development and tuning trajectories are not evaluation replicates.

\paragraph{Numerical checks before evaluation.}
A documented development-only numerical refinement froze Gauss--Legendre
quadrature order 2048 before tuning and evaluation.  The full development gate
compared orders 1024 and 2048 on all 24 declared trajectories; a separate
development check also compared 2048 and 4096.  No failed development check was
reclassified as a passing evaluation.  Quadrature checks, dense eigenvalue
comparisons, and confidence/optimism checks remain floating-point diagnostics.
They do not supply rigorous numerical enclosures for theorem assumptions.

\paragraph{What the intervals measure.}
Pairing compares policies exposed to the same underlying context and
potential-noise stream.  It does not force them to select the same actions.
The paired-bootstrap intervals resample the 50 evaluation seeds within a
condition, preserving the pairing between policies, with 10,000 resamples and
the recorded deterministic bootstrap seed.  The nine intervals in
\Cref{fig:transport-followup-outcomes} are comparisonwise rather than
multiplicity-adjusted.  Shared seeds across conditions do not make the 600
trajectories independent observations from one common population.  The teacher,
conditions, and shared optimizer are fixed in this uncertainty calculation.

The numerical values underlying the outcome chart at $T=1000$, with
50 paired evaluation seeds per condition, remain in the supplementary
reproducibility files. All nine intervals are pointwise 95\% paired bootstrap
intervals; no simultaneous coverage is asserted.

\paragraph{Descriptive benchmarks.}
These benchmarks provide reference performance levels for the same reward
streams.  They neither estimate the best achievable contextual performance
nor serve as equivalence tests between policies.
The online context-free benchmark samples each action once, then selects the
smallest-index action with the largest empirical mean of its own selected
rewards, ignoring context.  The best-fixed-action benchmark instead selects in
hindsight the action with the largest total true mean along the realized
context stream.  Both use the same potential-outcome streams as the study
policies, but neither follows a study policy's selected-action history.

\paragraph{What the retained evidence can reproduce.}
The committed full-study export records the exact configuration, selected
optimizer, source inventory, development/tuning summaries, per-seed terminal
outcomes, drift summaries, and compact aggregate.  Its independent verifier
recomputes the final-horizon means and paired intervals from the per-seed
values.  The later mechanism analysis scans the preserved raw trajectories and
records their hash inventory.  The supplementary numerical tables are generated directly
from these machine artifacts by
\texttt{tools/make\_transport\_followup\_paper.py}; the accompanying
\texttt{transport\_followup\_provenance.json} binds input and output SHA-256
digests.  This provenance is separate from the original near-linear study,
whose raw trajectories were unavailable for the revision.

The explanatory charts in
\Cref{fig:transport-followup-outcomes,fig:transport-followup-bound-scale}
are generated from the same fixed summaries by
\texttt{tools/make\_transport\_explanatory\_figures.py}.
Their CSV sources retain the plotted values, and
\texttt{transport\_explanatory\_provenance.json} records the input and output
digests.  The logarithmic bound chart transforms the mean RHS divided by the
condition's deterministic cap; it does not average trajectory-level logarithms.
The additional diagnostic charts are generated from the reviewed numerical
tables by \texttt{tools/make\_transport\_table\_charts.py}.
They retain the published table precision; the original tables remain in the
supplement for numerical lookup.  Their input and output digests are recorded
in \texttt{transport\_table\_provenance.json}.
The conceptual figures illustrate the stated certificate interfaces and
analytic example, independently of these empirical summaries.

\subsection{Exploratory recommendations on fixed histories}

This analysis asks what different scores recommend when given the same
information.  For each logged state, we evaluate all four original score
rules using that state's matrices, centers, and radii, without changing its
history.
\Cref{fig:transport-followup-same-history} includes all 12 combinations of
condition and behavior policy.  Each point averages one-step pseudo-regret
over 50,000 dependent states; the black-ringed points equal the actual mean cumulative
pseudo-regret divided by 1000.  The other points are recommendations on
states visited by the row policy.  They describe a recommendation at a
recorded state, not the subsequent trajectory of an alternative policy.
Taking a different action would generally change future observations,
parameters, matrices, and decisions.  The analysis was specified after the
full-study outcomes were observed.

\paragraph{When the width bonus determines the action.}
A large bonus can make the widest action win even when its predicted reward
is lower.  A sufficient check is that its bonus advantage exceeds the largest
possible difference between prediction centers on the recorded state.
Write the primary score on a recorded state as
$m_t(a)+B_t s_t(a)+b_t$, where $b_t$ is action independent and
$B_t=\beta_t^{\rm corr}\exp(\bar D_t^Q/2)$.
Let $s_{t,(1)}\ge s_{t,(2)}$ be the largest two current widths.
The strict condition
\[
 B_t\bigl(s_{t,(1)}-s_{t,(2)}\bigr)
 >\max_a m_t(a)-\min_a m_t(a)
\]
is sufficient for the unique largest-width action to maximize the score.
Width-argmax agreement alone is weaker: prediction centers might favor the
same action.  The exact tie rule is the smallest action index.

\paragraph{Sensitivity to score rounding.}
We distinguish recomputing the final score from recomputing the quantities
that enter it.  This matters when two actions have nearly equal scores.
The numerical scan reconstructs the recorded selected scores exactly and
finds no nonfinite coefficients or scores and no zero widths.  A separate
250-digit decimal calculation evaluates the final score arithmetic from the
exact stored binary64 centers, widths, offsets, and \emph{already rounded}
coefficient $B_t$.  It changes the primary recommendation at 16 states:
five, five, and six in the respective conditions.  Across all behavior
histories there are 65 changes among 600,000 states.  All occur at rounds
2--10, with score differences below 0.629 of a binary64 score ULP
(the spacing of representable numbers at the score's magnitude).
The decimal calculation does not recompute upstream matrix solves,
exponentials, coefficients, or fitted parameters.  An early changed action
could affect subsequent adaptive history; that effect has not been measured.
Likewise, equality between the full score vector and its bonus-plus-offset
part in binary64 arithmetic includes initially zero centers and is not, by
itself, evidence that the selected action was corrupted.

\paragraph{Variation across seeds and history-generating policies.}
\Cref{fig:transport-followup-mechanism-variation} reports per-seed variation
instead of treating the pooled rounds as independent.  The row label tells
us which policy generated the history; the score being diagnosed is always
Hessian's.  Thus only Hessian rows describe the behavior policy's own score.
The other rows ask what Hessian would recommend on states visited by endpoint,
frozen reference, or naive current.  They do not diagnose width dominance in
those policies' own scores.
Each fraction has 1000 rounds in its denominator, and the
reported median and range summarize 50 seeds.  These descriptive summaries
are not confidence intervals.

\subsection{Exploratory relative-certificate audit}

The endpoint constructions compare the current matrix directly with the
reference matrix.  This audit checks their implementation and measures the
resulting widths and recommendations on recorded states.  It does not test
the behavior of a new adaptive policy.

\paragraph{Which factors are compared?}
For unit feature and parameter bounds, \Cref{cor:tanh-relative-weights} gives
the uniform endpoint bound $D_0=4\log\cosh(1/\sqrt W)$, approximately
$0.124$, $1.735$, and $5.300$ at the three conditions.  The category-specific
sandwich $c_{-,t}\bVbar_t\preceq\bV_t\preceq c_{+,t}\bVbar_t$ gives a
symmetric coefficient $\exp(D_{{\rm cat},t}/2)$, where
$D_{{\rm cat},t}=\max\{-\log c_{-,t},\log c_{+,t}\}$, or the separate outward
coefficient $\sqrt{c_{+,t}}$ of \Cref{cor:relative-endpoint-ucb}.
The latter retains the separate return factor $c_{-,t}^{-1/2}$ in the
cumulative bound.  Neither category construction is an upper bound on the
length of the original interpolated factor path.

\paragraph{Replay and numerical checks.}
The post-outcome audit replays the original representation optimizer with
fixed logged actions and rewards for all 600 trajectories; it never replaces
a behavior action or generates a new observation.  It reconstructs
collection-time category weights and compares the resulting sandwich,
effective widths, and recommendations at the stored states.  Matrix, center,
and endpoint reconstruction are checked at rounds 1, 2, 10, 100, 500, and 1000;
the original float64 tolerances are retained.  These computations check an
implementation of the mathematical construction, without converting
floating-point residuals into verified certificates.  All declared checks
passed; the largest residual divided by its original tolerance was below
$0.005$.  The per-seed variation in the preceding figure was reproduced by this
separate fixed-action replay.

\paragraph{How to read the width and regret comparisons.}
The upper panel of \Cref{fig:transport-followup-relative-audit} compares each
effective width with its reference width before averaging.  The lower panel
asks what immediate expected reward is lost by the recommendations on those
same states.  It does not accumulate the outcomes of a policy that follows
those recommendations over time.

\paragraph{What the timing records leave out.}
Fixed-state kernel timings are retained in the audit artifact.  They were
collected under parallel analysis load and exclude matrix construction,
confidence estimation, and the policy loop, so they do not establish
end-to-end speedup.  The category construction requires additional
accumulated collection weights and still uses matrix solves and the frozen
confidence construction.
