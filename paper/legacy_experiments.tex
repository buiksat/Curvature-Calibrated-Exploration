\section{Legacy empirical evidence and scope}
\label{app:legacy-evidence}

The repository contains several studies designed for the earlier
optimizer-centered, one-sided dynamic theorem.  They are retained as scientific
record, not as empirical validation of \Cref{thm:transported-ucb}.  None executes
the headline exponential transported score while recording a predictable
$\bar D_t$, a certified two-sided operator pair $\kappa_{-,t},\kappa_{+,t}$,
all-domain solver upper validity, played-action sharpness, and the resulting new
regret bound.  Most detailed figures, comparison tables, and protocol
chronologies have therefore been removed from the manuscript.  The two compact
tables below and the remaining generated files retain their original provenance.

\paragraph{Corrected-center and local-transport traces.}
The scaled-tanh and bounded-tanh studies record corrected centers, frozen
information gain, path statistic $Q_t$, exact-current widths, and dense or CG
solver diagnostics.  They used scalar-link or additive local scores rather than
the exponential score in \eqref{eq:transported-score-new}.  Their reported
PASS/FAIL fields refer only to the legacy theorem event.  In particular,
optimizer and small-drift fields removed from the new headline theorem cannot be
used to declare the revised event failed.  The tracked scaled-tanh aggregate is
insufficient for a revised corrected-center analysis because the required
per-round payload is stored in an external raw bundle.  Restoring that bundle
would permit a post-hoc audit of the local-small-drift premises and of whether
the executed score matches the revised local score.  It would not establish that
compatibility by itself.  The headline exponential score requires a new policy
execution.

\paragraph{Exact-linear and solver sanity checks.}
In the fixed-feature linear studies, the dense exact-current and full-CG rows
exercise the specialization $\bar D_t=0$.  The dense row checks the LinUCB
reduction; the full-CG row checks original-operator residual logic and float64
all-action width agreement against dense solves.  These are algebra and
implementation diagnostics, not verified numerical enclosures.  They do not
test nontrivial metric transport.  Approximate-operator
rows record upper comparisons or point generalized eigenvalues, not a reportable
positive lower certificate $\kappa_{-,t}$.

\paragraph{Nonlinear path and operator mechanisms.}
The nonlinear drift audit directly checks the corrected-center identity and
records replay-path statistics, but its schedules fail and its scores predate
the revised theorem.  Off-diagonal and operator-ablation studies provide
diagnostic examples in which operator approximation error, width distortion,
action disagreement, and regret do not share a total ordering.  They motivate
separating the three metrics in \Cref{sec:abstract-transport}; they are not
theorem instantiations.  The spectral-tail study's
\texttt{rank\_truncation} control explicitly sets discarded-coordinate widths
to zero.  That control cannot be represented by a finite SPD operator satisfying
\eqref{eq:operator-sandwich-new}.

\paragraph{Autodiff feasibility.}
The real-\texttt{torch.func} study demonstrates JVP/VJP application of a GGN
primitive and records residual, operator-call, time, and memory diagnostics.
It measures isolated linear-algebra primitives rather than a transported-UCB
policy.  The larger model has no dense reference oracle.  The study therefore
supports implementation feasibility only.

\paragraph{Negative and incomplete benchmarks.}
The balanced synthetic comparison does not establish a full-curvature advantage.
The MNIST study remains near its context-free baseline, and Covertype fails its
contextual baseline gate.  None of these three benchmarks executes the corrected
transported score or records the revised certificates.  The Wheel study
terminated during tuning and
has no reportable evaluation.  These outcomes remain available in the artifact
record, but detailed benchmark plots and tables do not contribute to the present
theory-first paper.
