\section{A controlled instantiation of transported confidence}
\label{sec:transport-instantiation}

The experiment asks four questions about one controlled instantiation.  Do the
declared algebra and confidence diagnostics pass?  How conservative is the
analytic path certificate relative to endpoint and quadrature diagnostics?  Is
the resulting regret bound informative relative to realized regret and the
deterministic cap?  How do the four policy rules behave under shared exogenous
streams?  The experiment does not test approximate operators, CG, real data, or
large-model scalability.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We use the finite-dimensional scaled-tanh Gaussian specialization of
\Cref{cor:global-near-linear-new}.  Contexts have dimension $4$, there are five
actions, and the normalized context--action feature map has dimension $29$.
The frozen and current matrices are formed densely, all widths use float64
Cholesky solves, and finite enumeration uses the smallest-index tie rule.  Thus
the intended theorem interface has
\[
  \alpha_t=1,\qquad
  \kappa_{-,t}=\kappa_{+,t}=1,\qquad
  \xi_t=0.
\]
These equalities describe the exact operator, solve, and oracle abstractions.
The recorded floating-point checks are diagnostics, not verified numerical
certificates.

The locked grid contains horizons $T\in\{250,500,1000\}$, 50 evaluation seeds,
and target labels $D_{\rm target}\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$.  Each $(T,D_{\rm
target})$ cell is a separately executed model condition because both quantities
enter the rule for $W$.  The target label is neither realized transport distance
nor generic network width, so trends across targets are not a dose response to
certificate inflation.  The four policies share contexts and potential-noise tables.
The primary \emph{transport Hessian} policy uses $\bar D_t^Q$ in the score.  The
\emph{transport endpoint} policy replaces it by the dense endpoint Thompson
distance and is only a diagnostic oracle.  The \emph{frozen reference} policy
uses the frozen width directly.  The \emph{naive current} policy omits transport
and is intentionally uncertified.  Each policy has its own selected history and
representation path.  Ten disjoint tuning seeds selected learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$ and 20 full-batch steps followed by projection each round by
preregistered all-action prediction MSE; evaluation seeds never entered that
selection.
The environment was not filtered after generation.  Within each $T=1000$
target cell, the 50-seed means round to optimal-action entropy $1.579$, optimality
gap $0.123$, best-fixed-action regret $180.4$, and online context-free
mean-only regret $185.4$.  All five actions are optimal somewhere in every
evaluation stream.  These diagnostics were reported after the grid was fixed;
they were not used to select seeds.

\paragraph{Validity audit.}
For the primary policy, every one of the same 50 base seeds in each of the 12
horizon--target cells retained simultaneous reference confidence and
transported optimism.  The cellwise exact 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval is
$[92.9\%,100\%]$.  Reusing base seeds across cells supports paired comparisons
within a condition, not a pooled sample of 600 independent runs.  The strict aggregate accepted all 2400 policy
trajectories with no deterministic algebra or certificate-check failure, and
the primary policy had no bound violation on the joint confidence event
(\Cref{tab:transport-instantiation-validity}).  This finite-sample observation
does not prove or empirically validate the theorem.  In particular, ordinary
float64 Cholesky, eigenvalue, and quadrature checks are not verified
enclosures.

\paragraph{Certificate tightness.}
At $T=1000$, the median of each trajectory's maximum realized $\bar D_t^Q$
ranges from $0.0486$ to $0.384$ across target cells.  The corresponding median
of trajectory-maximum endpoint distances ranges from $1.31\times10^{-7}$ to
$8.74\times10^{-6}$; both summaries have 50 trajectory-level observations per
cell.  At the 100 declared checkpoints per trajectory, pooling the 5,000
eligible seed--checkpoint ratios gives a median selected factor-path length only
$1.20$--$1.22$ times the endpoint distance, while $\bar D_t^Q$ is
$2.51\times10^5$--$1.88\times10^6$ times the selected-path diagnostic.  The
large ratio accompanies extremely small realized metric motion.  The reported
median $e^{\bar D_t^Q/2}$, by contrast, pools all 50,000 seed--round values in a
cell and ranges from $1.02$ to $1.18$; it is not a terminal or run-maximum
summary.

\paragraph{Bound nonvacuity.}
The cumulative guarantee is vacuous in every reported cell.  The median of the
per-trajectory terminal sharp-RHS/regret ratios is $126.7$--$155.7$ over the
full grid and $126.7$--$135.0$ at $T=1000$.  No run has zero regret.
Since $|\mu^*(z)|\le\|\phi(z)\|\|\btheta^\circ\|\le1$, cumulative
pseudo-regret has the deterministic cap $2T$.  In every cell the theorem bound
also exceeds this cap: the median right-hand sides are
$3{,}989$--$12{,}607$, versus caps of $500$--$2{,}000$.  The appendix reports
the complete tightness table and bound decomposition.

\paragraph{Policy outcomes.}
At $T=1000$, mean regret for the operational Hessian-certificate policy rises
from $81.0$ to $94.0$ across the four target conditions
(\Cref{tab:transport-instantiation-performance}).  The dense endpoint, frozen
reference, and naive-current policies have lower sample means in every
condition.  These are matched-stream total-policy comparisons: the policies
share contexts and potential outcomes, but their actions, fitted parameters,
centers, radii, and widths may diverge after the first disagreement.  The
endpoint comparison is consistent with conservatism in the analytic envelope,
but it does not isolate a fixed-history mechanism and does not provide a
scalable policy certificate.  The naive score's observed optimism does not make
it theoretically valid.  Since $D_{\rm target}$ also changes $W$, the
cross-condition pattern is not a causal dose response.
