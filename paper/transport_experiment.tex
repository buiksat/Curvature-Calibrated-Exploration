\section{A controlled instantiation of transported confidence}
\label{sec:transport-instantiation}

We execute the corrected-center score in the finite-dimensional scaled-tanh
Gaussian specialization of \Cref{cor:global-near-linear-new}.  Contexts have dimension
$4$, there are five actions, and the normalized context--action feature map has
dimension $29$.  We form the frozen and current matrices densely and use
float64 Cholesky solves.  Finite enumeration uses the smallest-index tie rule.
Thus the intended theorem interface has
\[
  \alpha_t=1,\qquad
  \kappa_{-,t}=\kappa_{+,t}=1,\qquad
  \xi_t=0.
\]
These equalities describe the exact operator, solve, and oracle abstractions.
The recorded floating-point checks are diagnostics, not verified numerical
certificates.

The locked grid contains horizons $T\in\{250,500,1000\}$, 50 evaluation seeds,
and target labels $D_{\rm target}\in\{0.25,0.5,1,2\}$.  Each label sets the
abstract near-linearity scale $W$ through the worst-case trust-region formula
in \Cref{cor:global-near-linear-new}; it is not the realized path length and is
not network width.  Four policies share contexts and potential-noise tables.
The primary \emph{transport Hessian} policy uses $\bar D_t^Q$ in the score.  The
\emph{transport endpoint} policy replaces it by the dense endpoint Thompson
distance and is only a diagnostic oracle.  The \emph{frozen reference} policy
uses the frozen width directly.  The \emph{naive current} policy omits transport
and is intentionally uncertified.  Each policy has its own selected history and
representation path.  Ten disjoint tuning seeds selected learning rate
$3\times10^{-4}$ and 20 full-batch steps followed by projection each round by
preregistered all-action prediction MSE; evaluation seeds never entered that
selection.
The environment was not filtered after generation.  At $T=1000$, all five
actions were optimal somewhere in every evaluation stream, mean optimal-action
entropy was $1.579$, and the mean optimality gap was $0.123$.  Mean regret was
$180.4$ for the best fixed action and $185.4$ for the online context-free
mean-only baseline.  These diagnostics were reported, not used to select seeds.

\paragraph{Validity audit.}
For the primary policy, every one of the 50 runs in each of the 12
horizon--target cells retained simultaneous reference confidence and
transported optimism.  The exact 95\% Clopper--Pearson interval is
$[92.9\%,100\%]$ in every cell.  The strict aggregate accepted all 2400 policy
trajectories with no deterministic algebra or certificate-check failure, and
the primary policy had no bound violation on the joint confidence event
(\Cref{tab:transport-instantiation-validity}).  This finite-sample observation
does not prove or empirically validate the theorem.  In particular, ordinary
float64 Cholesky, eigenvalue, and quadrature checks are not verified
enclosures.

\paragraph{Certificate tightness and bound nonvacuity.}
At $T=1000$, the median maximum realized $\bar D_t^Q$ ranges from $0.0486$ to
$0.384$, while the median maximum endpoint distance ranges from
$1.31\times10^{-7}$ to $8.74\times10^{-6}$.  At quadrature checkpoints, the
median selected factor-path length is only $1.20$--$1.22$ times the endpoint
distance, whereas $\bar D_t^Q$ is $2.51\times10^5$--$1.88\times10^6$ times the
selected-path diagnostic.  The large ratios arise because the realized metric
motion is extremely small; the corresponding median $e^{\bar D_t^Q/2}$ is
$1.02$--$1.18$.  The cumulative guarantee is nevertheless vacuous here.  The
median sharp right-hand side is $126.7$--$155.7$ times realized regret over all
reported cells and $126.7$--$135.0$ times at $T=1000$.  No run has zero regret.
Since $|\mu^*(z)|\le\|\phi(z)\|\|\btheta^\circ\|\le1$, cumulative
pseudo-regret has the deterministic cap $2T$.  In every cell the theorem bound
also exceeds this cap: the median right-hand sides are
$3{,}989$--$12{,}607$, versus caps of $500$--$2{,}000$.  The appendix reports
the complete tightness table and bound decomposition.

\paragraph{Policy outcomes.}
At $T=1000$, mean regret for the operational Hessian-certificate policy rises
from $81.0$ to $94.0$ across the four target conditions
(\Cref{tab:transport-instantiation-performance}).  The dense endpoint, frozen
reference, and naive-current policies have lower descriptive regret in every
condition.  The endpoint result isolates conservatism in the analytic path
envelope but does not provide a scalable policy certificate.  The naive score's
observed optimism does not make it theoretically valid.  These comparisons do
not show a causal or uniform advantage for full curvature; they show that the
operational certificate can change actions and increase regret here.
