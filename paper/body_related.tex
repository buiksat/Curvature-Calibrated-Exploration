\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

The main distinction is how a method constructs the uncertainty used to choose
an action.  Some methods retain features computed when data arrive; others
fix a network linearization, learn a last-layer representation, or update an
approximate posterior.  We study a different choice: recomputing every past
observation's Jacobian at the current parameter, then relating that current
curvature to confidence built from collection-time features.

\paragraph{Confidence from collection-time or fixed features.}
Linear UCB \citep{abbasi2011improved} and Thompson sampling
\citep{thompson1933likelihood,agrawal2013thompson} provide the statistical basis
for many neural-bandit methods.  In their stated algorithms, NeuralUCB
\citep{zhou2020neural} and NeuralTS \citep{zhang2021neural} add a gradient
feature to the covariance when its observation arrives.  Later network updates
do not trigger a replay of all historical gradients in that covariance.

Other methods separate the learned prediction from a fixed reference for
uncertainty.  \citet{kassraie2022neural} use initialization-time features for a
kernelized NTK reference alongside a trained-network mean.  Their SupNN-UCB
variant has a sublinear-regret guarantee under NTK-RKHS,
sufficient-exploration, and network-width assumptions.
\citet{salgia2023provably} controls adaptivity through grouped histories and a
confidence argument relating a finite network to the NTK.
\citet{deb2024contextual} instead study online neural regression and
contextual-bandit reductions.  These guarantees address their respective
feature and data constructions.  Our question is what additional certificates
justify using a metric that replays the entire history at each current
parameter.

\paragraph{Uncertainty in learned last-layer features.}
NeuralLinear maintains Bayesian linear uncertainty in learned final-layer
features \citep{riquelme2018deep}.  The methods of \citet{xu2022shallow} and
\citet{bui2025variance} use UCB-style linear exploration over learned deep
representations.  Their linear uncertainty models concern those representations;
recomputing last-layer features is different from constructing a GGN over all
network parameters.  Our theorem specifies certificates for the latter
transport.  It does not give a general analysis of neural-linear representation
learning.

\paragraph{Uncertainty from recursively updated posteriors.}
\citet{duranmartin2022efficient} use extended Kalman filter (EKF) updates in a
learned or random low-dimensional affine parameter subspace.  A dense or
diagonal covariance in that subspace induces a low-rank covariance over
parameters spanning all network layers.  Memory does not grow with the number
of rounds.  This reduces the space in which uncertainty is represented, rather
than applying exact curvature--vector products in the full parameter space.

LO-FI \citep{chang2023lowrank} instead maintains a diagonal-plus-low-rank
approximation to the full-parameter posterior precision.  Recursive EKF and
online-SVD updates compress the posterior in one pass, without replaying past
data; at fixed rank, cost is linear in the parameter count.  The authors use
this approximation for Thompson sampling in an MNIST contextual bandit.
Our transported-UCB theorem relates current replayed curvature to a separate
frozen-feature confidence metric, with explicit factors for approximate
operators and solver widths.  Our dense audit evaluates neither compressed
operators nor CG and establishes no practical advantage over LO-FI.

\paragraph{Fixed linearizations, Laplace approximations, and sampling.}
\citet{maddox2021fast} linearize once around a trained network and use implicit
Jacobian/Fisher products with CG for predictive inference.  These implicit
solves are closely related to those that could compute our widths, but the
reference linearization remains fixed.  A Laplace approximation places a local
Gaussian posterior around a MAP solution, using inverse curvature as its
covariance \citep{mackay1992practical}.  Last-layer Laplace fixes the backbone
and therefore omits uncertainty in its weights \citep{kristiadi2020being}.
\citet{daxberger2021laplace} systematize all-weight, subnetwork, and last-layer
variants with full, low-rank, Kronecker-factored, or diagonal curvature.
Structured all-layer variants use Kronecker-factored curvature
\citep{ritter2018scalable,martens2015optimizing}.  These constructions motivate
ways to represent predictive uncertainty.  The issue here is sequential
validity: whether a current relinearized operator can justify a UCB score when
it differs from the predictable confidence Gram.

Langevin Monte Carlo Thompson sampling (LMC-TS) \citep{xu2022langevin} avoids
materializing a covariance matrix.  Noisy gradient steps on a regularized loss
produce approximate samples from a loss-defined parameter posterior.  Its
regret guarantee concerns linear contextual bandits; its neural-bandit evidence
is empirical.  The sampling approximation must be controlled at each round.
Our theorem has different requirements: certificates for metric transport,
operator distortion, and the width used in the score.  The dense audit does
not compare computational cost or empirical performance with LMC-TS.

\paragraph{The statistical confidence model.}
\citet[Appendix~E]{lattimore2020learning} analyze LinUCB with round-dependent
feature sets and an explicit bonus for bounded misspecification.  This is a
close connection to the corrected center used here.  Our pseudo-responses
in \Cref{eq:pseudo-response-new} turn the nonlinear observations into
predictable linear regression with a Taylor remainder and misspecification
error.  Adding the current affine offset gives the corrected prediction center.
Its confidence argument combines standard self-normalized regression bounds
with deterministic bias bounds.  We do not claim a new generic method for
misspecified linear confidence.  The further question is how that reference
confidence can support current replayed curvature and the numerical width
actually used for selection.

\paragraph{The geometric and operator comparisons.}
The metric geometry is established.  \citet{thompson1963certain} introduced
the part metric now called the Thompson metric on ordered cones, and
\citet{nussbaum1994finsler} developed its Finsler structure and applications.
On the positive-definite cone, the tangent norm is
$\|V^{-1/2}\dot V V^{-1/2}\|_{\rm op}$.  We use the resulting comparison
between a path and its endpoints to connect frozen bandit confidence, current
replayed GGN/Fisher curvature, approximate operators, and certified solver
widths.  The geometry and the path-to-endpoint comparison themselves are not
contributions of this paper.

Spectral approximations also have an established role in bandit regret bounds.
\citet{kuzborskij2019efficient} replace a linear-bandit Gram matrix with a
Frequent Directions sketch and derive bounds in terms of its spectral
approximation quality.  They already note that SOFUL may incur linear regret
when the spectrum has heavy tails.  Our two-sided operator comparison in
\Cref{thm:transported-ucb} plays a similar role: it relates the applied operator
to exact current curvature.  A separate path certificate relates that
curvature to the frozen-feature confidence metric, and a solver certificate
controls the computed width.  We claim no novelty for converting a spectral
sandwich into a regret bound, and we do not evaluate a sketched operator.

\paragraph{Computing widths without storing a matrix.}
Pearlmutter's algorithm \citep{pearlmutter1994fast} computes exact
Hessian--vector products.  The analogous GGN--vector product has the
Jacobian-transpose-times-Jacobian-times-vector structure
\citep{schraudolph2002fast}.  Influence functions use inverse-curvature--vector
products to attribute predictions to training points
\citep{koh2017understanding}.  A CG implementation of our solver interface
would use the same implicit-solve primitive.  The theorem permits certified
operator and solve approximations, but the experiment uses dense current
metrics and direct Cholesky solves.  It makes no matrix-free scalability claim.

\paragraph{Weighted Grams and experimental design.}
Weighted least-squares Grams appear in the experimental-design analysis of
\citet{lattimore2020learning}.  In our scaled-tanh specialization, feature
directions are fixed and relinearization changes their positive weights.
Comparing those weights term by term gives elementary positive-semidefinite bounds
(\Cref{cor:tanh-relative-weights}), which we use as an endpoint certificate.
The reward model remains fixed during this comparison; a nonstationary reward
model would require a separate statistical analysis.

Bandit exploration also connects to classical D-optimal and A-optimal design
through objectives such as maximizing $\log\det(\bVbar_t)$ or minimizing
the trace of $\bVbar_t^{-1}$ \citep{valko2013finite}.  Stochastic trace and
log-determinant estimators offer related matrix-free tools
\citep{ubaru2017fast}.  Here the frozen log-determinant enters the regret
analysis.  The audited policy chooses actions by its UCB score and does not
implement those estimators.

For current full-parameter GGN/Fisher scores, the conditional theorem requires
transport from collection-time confidence, bounds on operator distortion, and
control of solver error.  Whether this choice is computationally preferable to
fixed NTK features, grouped histories, regression reductions, learned last-layer
representations, or approximate posterior methods is not established here.
