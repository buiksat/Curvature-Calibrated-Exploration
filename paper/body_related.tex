\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Collection-time and fixed-feature confidence.}
Linear UCB and Thompson sampling provide the statistical basis for many
neural-bandit methods \citep{abbasi2011improved,thompson1933likelihood,agrawal2013thompson}.
NeuralUCB and NeuralTS add arriving gradient features to their covariance
without replaying historical gradients after retraining
\citep{zhou2020neural,zhang2021neural}. Initialization-time NTK uncertainty
\citep{kassraie2022neural}, grouped histories \citep{salgia2023provably} and
online-regression reductions \citep{deb2024contextual} use different confidence
constructions. Learned last-layer uncertainty
\citep{riquelme2018deep,xu2022shallow,bui2025variance} concerns learned linear
representations rather than a GGN over all network parameters.

\paragraph{Generalized linear and logistic bandits.}
Scaled tanh is a single-index model with a known increasing link, the form
studied by generalized linear bandits. \citet{li2017provably} analyze
UCB-GLM and SupCB-GLM under conditionally sub-Gaussian noise, a link with
$\inf\dot\mu=\kappa>0$ near the true parameter, bounded first and second link
derivatives, i.i.d.\ contexts with a nondegenerate design, and an estimator
that solves the GLM score equation; their bounds scale as
$\tilde O(d\sqrt T/\kappa)$ and $\tilde O(\sqrt{dT\log K}/\kappa)$. For the
logistic link with Bernoulli rewards, \citet{faury2020improved} remove
$\kappa$ from the leading $\sqrt T$ term using a Bernstein-like inequality for
self-normalized martingales, and \citet{abeille2021instance} obtain a leading
term $d\sqrt{T/\kappa_\star}$ with a matching local lower bound. None of these
results applies as stated to our setting: the logistic analyses assume
Bernoulli rewards, and \citet{li2017provably} state rewards in $[0,1]$. Our
analysis also differs in its object: it allows an arbitrary predictable
parameter fitted with squared loss and corrects the prediction with a frozen
linear estimate, rather than analyzing a likelihood estimator.

\paragraph{Posterior and linearization methods.}
\citet{duranmartin2022efficient} use recursive EKF updates in a learned or random
low-dimensional affine parameter subspace. LO-FI maintains a
 diagonal-plus-low-rank full-parameter precision through EKF/online-SVD updates
without replaying old data, with linear parameter-count cost at fixed rank
\citep{chang2023lowrank}. Fixed trained-network linearizations use implicit
Jacobian/Fisher products and CG \citep{maddox2021fast}. Laplace approximations
use local inverse curvature around a MAP solution \citep{mackay1992practical},
with last-layer \citep{kristiadi2020being}, all-weight/subnetwork/structured
variants \citep{daxberger2021laplace,ritter2018scalable,martens2015optimizing}.
LMC-TS instead uses noisy loss-gradient steps to approximate posterior samples
without materializing covariance \citep{xu2022langevin}. Our theorem connects
replayed current curvature to a separate collection-time confidence metric.

\paragraph{Statistical and geometric connections.}
The bounded-misspecification LinUCB analysis of
\citet[Appendix~E]{lattimore2020learning} is closely related to the corrected
center: pseudo-responses yield predictable linear regression with Taylor and
misspecification errors, and the current affine offset restores the prediction.
The Thompson metric \citep{thompson1963certain} and its Finsler structure
\citep{nussbaum1994finsler} give the normalized SPD path comparison.
Frequent Directions bandit sketches already use spectral approximation quality
in regret guarantees \citep{kuzborskij2019efficient}; here an additional metric
comparison connects that applied operator to frozen confidence.

\paragraph{Implicit computation and fixed directions.}
Hessian--vector products \citep{pearlmutter1994fast}, GGN products
\citep{schraudolph2002fast} and inverse-curvature products
\citep{koh2017understanding} provide the primitives for implicit width solves.
Weighted Grams also appear in experimental design
\citep{lattimore2020learning}; termwise scaled-tanh weight comparisons give our
endpoint certificates. Frozen log-determinant growth connects to D-optimal and
A-optimal exploration \citep{valko2013finite}; stochastic trace/log-determinant
estimators offer related implicit tools \citep{ubaru2017fast}.
