\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

Confidence built from collection-time features can justify a score based on
current relinearized curvature, but only through an explicit chain of
certificates.  The metric path controls frozen-to-current drift, the two-sided
operator comparison controls the curvature actually applied, and the solver
interface controls the numerical width used for selection.  The lower operator
factor is what returns the played width to the frozen sequential potential.  If
that factor is unavailable, the one-sided dynamic result remains valid but does
not supply the same closed rate.

For squared loss, the corrected center makes this chain independent of
nonlinear optimizer convergence.  The representation may follow any
predictable path inside the certified region, while historical error, current
bias, transport, operator distortion, solver accuracy, and approximate score
maximization remain explicit charges.  The construction reduces to the usual
LinUCB determinant-potential argument for frozen linear features and exact
solves.  Under the assumptions of \Cref{cor:global-near-linear-new}, for fixed
$d$ and fixed problem constants, taking $W=\Omega(T)$ with a sufficiently large
problem-dependent constant yields
$R_T=O(d\sqrt T\log T)+O(\sqrt T)$ without requiring $D_t<1$.  Here $W$ is an
abstract smoothness scale, not generic network width.

The controlled scaled-tanh audit separates formal validity from practical
usefulness.  All 600 transport-Hessian trajectories retained simultaneous
reference confidence and transported optimism, and the locked grid recorded no
deterministic audit failure.  Yet the analytic path envelope was orders of
magnitude longer than the selected-path diagnostic, every reported theorem
bound was vacuous relative to the deterministic cap, and the three comparison
policies had lower sample mean regret at the primary horizon.  The result is a
negative practical finding about this certificate and environment, not evidence
for a uniform ordering of curvature methods.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The abstract theorem assumes a standard-Borel action space, a graph-measurable
random action correspondence, finite measurable mean-reward and score suprema,
a measurable approximate selector and universal events, a finite pre-reward
path envelope, and a two-sided operator comparison.  The squared-loss
specialization requires a fixed ex-ante reference model, a prefix-measurable
representation path, and supplied pre-reward linearization and misspecification
envelopes.  Constant misspecification can yield linear regret.
The corrected center also needs access to the frozen-feature ridge estimator.
The factor-path geometry does not by itself prove an exponential-family
confidence theorem.  We do not claim unrestricted neural-network training or
generic fine-tuning guarantees, verified floating-point certification, uniform
regret improvement from full curvature, sublinear regret under fixed
nonvanishing misspecification, two-sided guarantees for arbitrary diagonal,
low-rank, stale, or sketched operators, empirical validation of the transported
theorem, or end-to-end scalability of the full policy.  Ordinary floating-point
residual checks are diagnostics rather than verified numerical certificates.
The new experiment is a controlled execution and audit, not empirical proof or
validation of the theorem.  Its endpoint method is a dense diagnostic comparator,
and its intentionally naive current-width control is uncertified even where its
observed optimism rate is high.
