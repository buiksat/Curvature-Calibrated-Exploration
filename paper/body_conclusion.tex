\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A frozen ridge correction supplies reference confidence along predictable
nonlinear parameter paths without optimizer convergence, and confidence
transport connects that band to current curvature through separate metric,
operator and solver certificates. The reference-width floor and sharp
symmetric factors characterize this conversion, and fixed-direction scaled tanh
permits tighter relative-weight endpoint certificates.

Four takeaways follow. First, on a fixed history transport cannot certify a
width below the reference width while it preserves the full reference band
(\Cref{prop:reference-width-floor}). It can make a current-curvature score
valid, but it cannot make the certified width narrower. Second, for scaled tanh
with $W\ge cT$ the uniform endpoint factor is $1+O(1/(cT))$
(\Cref{cor:tanh-endpoint-regime}), so the endpoint-transported bound keeps the
frozen bound's form. In the experiments, Hessian-path transport had higher mean
regret than the frozen-reference policy in every condition of both studies.
Third, in the curved cells of the second study, removing the Taylor-error
allowances lowered mean regret far more than switching among the reference,
endpoint and naive current widths changed it
(\Cref{tab:transport-v3-descriptive}). Fourth, no cell of either study has both
substantial curvature and a non-vacuous frozen bound. A useful next comparison
needs stronger confidence information or a lower complete computational cost,
including the frozen estimator, and a specific adaptive-policy hypothesis with
a separately fixed protocol and fresh evaluation data.
