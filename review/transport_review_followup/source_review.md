## A. Executive assessment

**What the paper does.** It studies the mismatch that arises when a contextual-bandit policy retrains a nonlinear model: statistical confidence is naturally supported by *frozen* collection-time gradient features, but the score uses the *current* relinearized GGN/Fisher metric, which changes every replayed Jacobian. The paper (i) keeps four objects distinct — frozen Gram `V̄_t`, exact current metric `V_t`, the applied SPD operator `C_t`, and the numerical solver width `ŝ_t` — (ii) proves a conditional transported-UCB regret theorem composing a Thompson–Finsler path certificate, a two-sided operator sandwich, and a solver certificate (`transport_theory.tex:174–212`), (iii) gives a corrected-prediction-center squared-loss confidence corollary that needs no optimizer-convergence assumption (`transport_theory.tex:483–494`), and (iv) reports a locked 29-parameter scaled-tanh audit whose headline findings are deliberately negative: the analytic path certificate is conservative by 5–6 orders of magnitude, every numerical regret bound is vacuous, and all three comparison policies beat the certified transport policy in sample mean at T=1000 (`transport_experiment.tex:88–132`).

**Strengths.**
1. **Unusual scientific honesty.** The abstract, introduction, experiment section, and conclusion tell the same story, including the negative empirical half. Claim scoping ("validity vs. usefulness", "diagnostics, not verified certificates", "not a causal dose response") is exemplary and rare.
2. **The mathematics I checked is correct.** I verified the main theorem's proof chain, the log-SPD path lemma, the factor-path lemma, the Hessian-path corollary, the corrected-center identity and radius, the solver interval certificate, and the CG energy certificate line by line (details in §D). The measurability assumptions are stated as substantive assumptions rather than swept under standard-Borel boilerplate (`transport_theory.tex:11–35`).
3. **Evidence hygiene is strong.** The committed aggregate (`results/derived/transport_instantiation/full_aggregate.json`, 77 MB, SHA recorded in each generated table) reproduces the manuscript's numbers at every point I spot-checked: mean regret 81.05 at (T=1000, D=0.25), sharp-RHS/regret median 126.73, `D_Q/d_Th` median 2.24×10⁶, selected optimizer lr 3×10⁻⁴ / 20 steps, Clopper–Pearson [92.9, 100] for 50/50.

**Most important weaknesses.**
1. **Modest theoretical depth relative to length.** The headline theorem is a careful but elementary composition of a Thompson-metric sandwich, an operator sandwich, Cauchy–Schwarz, and the standard elliptic potential. The paper admits this (`body_intro.tex:84–85`), but a 68-page document (16 pages of body plus a very large partially-legacy appendix) is heavy packaging for it.
2. **A structural tension is left implicit.** Whenever the analytic certificate is informative (`D̄_t = O(1)` via `W = Ω(T)`), the frozen and current metrics are provably within constant factors, so the transported score cannot materially outperform the frozen-width policy; the experiment confirms exactly this. The paper's most interesting scientific lesson — *the certified transport is useful only where it is unnecessary, under this certificate family* — is visible in the data but never crystallized as a statement.
3. **Vacuity attribution is incomplete.** The reported vacuity ratios (median 126.7–155.7) are dominated by the base confidence radius, while transport inflation is only `e^{D̄/2} ∈ [1.02, 1.18]` median. Much of the vacuity is generic to optimism bounds at T≤1000 and not specific to transport; the headline framing ("validity does not deliver a useful bound") should be decomposed quantitatively.
4. **Related-work drift from a previous draft.** `body_related.tex` repeatedly discusses "CC-UCB" with "residual-checked CG" as if it were this paper's method (lines 66–70, 90–97, 101–105, 130, 140), yet CC-UCB is only defined in the legacy appendix, the CG path is explicitly *not* evaluated, and `appendix_twosided.tex:11` still calls `thm:spectral-distortion` a "main-text" theorem although it now lives in the legacy appendix.
5. **Reproducibility is partial by the authors' own record:** raw per-run trajectories are untracked, so the paired bootstrap cannot be recomputed from committed material (`availability.tex:31–38`), and the branch records a SciPy pin inconsistency and two source-digest divergences (`submissions/tmlr_2026/IMPLEMENTATION_REVIEW.md:28–38`).

**Readiness.** Close to submittable for TMLR on the correctness and claims-support axes. Before submission I would fix the related-work drift (P1), add the vacuity decomposition and the "informative-certificate ⇒ frozen-suffices" remark (P1), prune or stage the legacy appendix (P2), and repair bibliography issues (P1). No P0 finding.

## D. Detailed scientific and experimental review

### D.1 Mathematics (checked line by line unless noted)

- **Filtrations/pre-reward protocol.** The split into `H_t⁻` (prefix + context + action set), `Ω_t` (pre-reward randomization), `H_t = H_t⁻ ∨ σ(Ω_t)` is coherent; "predictable" is reserved for `H_t⁻` and the noise MGF condition (`transport_theory.tex:430–433`) is imposed conditionally on `H_t`, i.e., after action selection and before the reward — exactly what the self-normalized argument needs. The paper correctly flags that standard-Borel structure plus graph measurability does **not** yield measurable suprema/selectors and assumes them (`transport_theory.tex:28–35`); the finite-enumeration corollary then discharges these assumptions in the only case the experiment uses. This is a restrictive-but-valid assumption set, honestly labeled.
- **Main theorem.** The optimism chain (frozen → path → operator → solver upper) and the return chain at the played action (sharpness → lower operator → path) are both correct; the asymmetric use of all-domain upper validity vs. played-action-only sharpness is a genuinely nice interface design. The elliptic-potential step uses `x ≤ (1+M)log(1+x)` on `0 ≤ x ≤ M` with `M = G²/(λσ²)` — valid since `s̄_t² ≤ G²/λ`. Union-bound probability accounting is correct and independence-free (`transport_proofs.tex:80–81`).
- **Path certificates.** `lem:log-spd-path` is proved cleanly via scalar quadratic forms (no matrix square-root differentiation). The sharpness example (`transport_theory.tex:306–329`) genuinely shows each one-way `e^{D/2}` factor is attained, and the text correctly limits the claim to metric factors, not regret lower bounds. `cor:hessian-path-new` is correct; note the bound uses Frobenius-over-rows and `V(τ) ⪰ λI`, which are precisely the slack sources the audit later measures at 10⁵–10⁶. The paper gestures at the sharper relative form (`transport_theory.tex:387–390`) but never quantifies how much slack each relaxation contributes — see experiment E3 below.
- **Corrected center.** The pseudo-response identity `y_s = q_sᵀθ° + η_s + e_s` and the `‖Φe/σ‖_{V̄⁻¹} ≤ ‖e‖₂/σ` step are correct; the `1/σ` (not `1/σ²`) scaling remark (`transport_theory.tex:498–499`) is a helpful correctness guard. The claim of "no optimizer convergence assumption" is accurate: the only role of `θ_t` is predictability and membership in the certified region. The cost — frozen-feature access for the ridge estimator — is stated (`body_intro.tex:70–72`), though a concrete per-round arithmetic/memory accounting of the audited implementation (category sufficient statistics over 80 context–action categories; see `experiments/transport_instantiation.py:1–13`) belongs in the paper, not only in the code docstring.
- **Rates and nonvacuity.** `Γ_{T,d}` closure and both corollaries check out. `cor:global-near-linear-new` is valid but the regime is strong: `W = Ω(T)` makes the model `O(1/√T)`-close to linear, which is what drives weakness F5. The paper says "not an unrestricted neural-training theorem" — correct and appreciated — but should also state the converse implication for the *frozen* policy explicitly.
- **Solver certificates.** Both the residual interval (algebraic identity verified) and the CG energy certificate (verified) are correct in exact arithmetic; the repeated disclaimer that float64 checks are diagnostics, not enclosures (`transport_proofs.tex:377–381`), is exactly right. The randomized-certificate failure-budget accounting (`transport_proofs.tex:388–400`) is sensible; I read it but did not verify its proof completion past line 400.
- **Not verified.** The ~2550-line `appendix_deferred.tex` (spectral-tail closures, dynamic-width theory, `thm:spectral-distortion`), `legacy_dynamic.tex` beyond its opening, `appendix_rates.tex`, `appendix_protocol.tex`, and the one-sided proof appendix. These are legacy-scoped and not load-bearing for the headline claims, but they are cross-referenced from the body (F1/F2) and would be inside the review surface at TMLR.

### D.2 Experiments and implementation

- **Protocol fidelity.** `experiments/transport_instantiation.py:1440–1670` implements exactly the stated per-round order: form frozen/current metrics and corrected centers → certificates (`D_Q`, endpoint Thompson distance, checkpointed quadrature path length) → scores per policy → argmax with smallest-index ties → regret → audits; reward usage and representation update occur post-selection. Shared exogenous streams are real: context and potential-noise child seeds are method-independent (`:1480–1500`), and the config (`experiments/configs/transport_instantiation.yaml`) pins seeds, tolerances (`C_tol·ε·…`), audit list, bootstrap resamples (10⁴, deterministic seed from the config digest — confirmed in `aggregate_transport_instantiation.py:532–563, 752–785, 902`), and the preregistered tuning grid. The selected optimizer (lr 3×10⁻⁴, 20 steps) matches the manuscript and the aggregate's `selected_optimizer`.
- **Statistics.** Per-cell n=50 with exact Clopper–Pearson [92.9, 100] is correctly computed and correctly *not* pooled into "600 independent runs" (`transport_experiment.tex:55–56`). Paired bootstrap deltas in the performance table are consistent (e.g., endpoint and naive-current rows identical at D=0.5 and 2, which is exactly what `e^{d_Th/2} ≈ 1` predicts — a nice internal consistency check the paper could point out). Denominator-tolerance exclusions are counted (`n₀`, 53 of 50,000 at one cell) rather than silently dropped.
- **Interpretation of negatives.** Careful and correct: matched-stream total-policy comparisons, no causal dose-response claims (W confounded with `D_target`), endpoint policy labeled a dense diagnostic oracle. My only complaint is F3: without a same-environment frozen-policy bound ratio, "every reported bound is vacuous" reads as an indictment of transport when it is substantially an indictment of `β_t√(γ_T)` at T≤1000.
- **Provenance.** Generated tables embed the aggregate SHA (`tables/transport_instantiation_performance.tex:1`), which matches the independent hash in `IMPLEMENTATION_REVIEW.md:14–15`. The aggregate's `git_revision` (`0cd6264c…`) predates the snapshot commit — expected for a locked artifact, but the paper/repo should state this lineage once. Raw trajectories are absent (limitation, not error). Dependency inconsistencies (SciPy pin vs. wheel; host-specific Buck) are recorded by the authors and should be resolved before release.
- **Tests.** Extensive test files exist (`experiments/tests/test_transport_instantiation*.py`, plus a very large `test_trust_boundaries.py` on the realistic-transport side). I inspected their presence and skimmed names only; I did not execute them, and I treat README/handoff test reports as historical records.

### D.3 Positioning

The four-object separation (frozen confidence geometry vs. current curvature vs. applied operator vs. solver width) is, to my knowledge, a genuinely clean framing not present in NeuralUCB/NeuralTS/NTK-bandit analyses, and the paper correctly disclaims novelty for Thompson geometry, CG, and the UCB reduction. Gaps: (a) the misspecification term `ε_mis` producing linear regret parallels the misspecified linear-bandit literature (e.g., Lattimore–Szepesvári-style `ε√d·T` results) — worth a citation (**needs external verification**); (b) parameter-drift control via `Q_t` is reminiscent of weighted/nonstationary linear bandits (Russac et al.-type) — a sentence distinguishing representation drift from reward drift would help (**needs external verification**); (c) F1/F4 above.

### Revised abstract

> When a contextual-bandit policy retrains its model, statistical confidence naturally rests on the gradient features that were frozen when each reward was collected, while scores are computed with the current relinearized Gauss–Newton or Fisher metric, which changes every replayed Jacobian at once. We ask when the first can justify the second. Four objects stay distinct throughout: the frozen confidence metric, the exact current metric, the SPD operator the algorithm actually applies, and the numerical width a solver returns. A Thompson–Finsler path certificate transports frozen confidence to current curvature; a two-sided operator comparison handles the applied operator; a solver certificate handles numerical widths. These compose into a conditional upper-confidence-bound regret theorem that closes through frozen sequential information gain and permits measurable approximate selection on standard-Borel action spaces. For squared loss, a corrected prediction center yields the reference-confidence event for any predictable parameter path in the certified region, with no optimizer-convergence assumption; historical and current approximation errors remain explicit. A locked, controlled 29-parameter scaled-tanh audit — a dense exact-solve study, not a benchmark — records simultaneous float64 confidence and optimism in all 600 primary runs with no deterministic audit failure. The same audit reports the negative half: the analytic path certificate is conservative by five to six orders of magnitude against the endpoint Thompson distance, every numerical regret bound is vacuous relative to a deterministic cap, and all three comparison policies attain lower sample-mean pseudo-regret at the primary horizon. A valid transport certificate therefore does not by itself yield a useful bound or better regret; we report the conditions under which the theorem holds rather than a curvature advantage.

(~255 words; preserves all theorem conditions, numbers, and negative findings; shortens and de-jargonizes the current ~330-word abstract at `submissions/tmlr_2026/main.tex:79–108`.)

## Qualifications to the earlier findings

Three findings from sections C–D need sharper epistemic boundaries.

**On F5 ("informative certificate ⇒ frozen suffices").** What the mathematics supports is only this: if $\bar D_t \le D = O(1)$, then the frozen-width policy admits a regret *upper bound* of the same order as the transported policy's, up to an $e^{D}$ factor, because the sandwich bounds the two width families by constant factors. That is a statement about provable guarantees under this certificate family. It is **not** a proof that transport cannot improve realized regret — upper bounds of the same order do not order actual performance — and it is not an impossibility result for sharper certificates (the relative form in `lem:factor-spd-path` could in principle certify small $\bar D_t$ in regimes where the ridge-only bound cannot). The proposed remark should be worded as a bound-comparison proposition, and the experimental ordering at $T=1000$ should remain what the paper already calls it: an observation specific to this environment, certificate, and horizon.

**On F3 (vacuity attribution).** My attribution of vacuity "mostly" to the base confidence radius rests on marginal median inflation factors ($e^{\bar D_t^Q/2}$ pooled medians of 1.02–1.18) read from the aggregate. Marginal medians do not determine the joint decomposition of the cumulative RHS: inflation enters multiplicatively inside a sum whose terms covary with widths, and run-maximum $\bar D_t^Q$ values (median up to 0.384) imply terminal inflation factors up to roughly $e^{0.19}\approx1.21$ on the largest-width late rounds. The decomposition (action item F.6/E1) is still to be performed; until then the claim should be stated as a hypothesis suggested by the medians, as in edit 3 above, not as a finding. It remains true, and unconditional, that the reported RHS exceeds the deterministic $2T$ cap in every cell, which the paper already states.

**On F4 (bibliography).** I could not browse. The inability to verify `granziol2026hessian` (arXiv 2602.00816) and `wen2026revisiting` from this session, and the `pascanu2014revisiting` key/year mismatch, are flags for author verification, not demonstrations that any reference is fabricated or wrong; key/year mismatches are common clerical artifacts.

Finally, a general qualification on coverage: where I wrote that proofs were "checked line by line," that applies to the proofs I actually read in full (listed in §G); it does not extend to the unread remainder of `transport_proofs.tex` or to any legacy appendix, and the experiment verification establishes agreement between the manuscript and the locked aggregate, not reproduction of the underlying trajectories.
