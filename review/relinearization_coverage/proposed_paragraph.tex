\paragraph{A realizable trajectory on which the naive width fails.}
Remark~\ref{rem:naive-width-band-counterexample} is an abstract band instance.
A controlled scaled-tanh learner shows the same failure on a realizable
trajectory. Take $\mu_\theta(\phi)=h(\phi\theta)$ with
$h(u)=\sqrt W\tanh(u/\sqrt W)$, $W=6.25$, actions $\phi\in\{-1,+1\}$, $R=1$,
teacher $\theta^\star=-R$, $\lambda=1$, $\sigma=0.25$ and $\delta=0.05$. The
learner holds $\theta_t=R$ for $n=8192$ rounds of frozen-reference UCB with the
corrected center of \Cref{cor:corrected-center-new}, then takes one projected
batch gradient step of the squared loss, which moves $\theta$ to within $0.004$
of zero, and scores round $n+1$ before observing another reward. The Taylor
allowances use the exact supremum of the remainder over the radius-$R$ ball,
$\max_{v\in\{-A,A,-x,x\}}|h(v)-h(x)-h'(x)(v-x)|$ with $x=\phi^\top\theta$ and
$A=R\|\phi\|_2$, which is sharper than the envelope used in
Section~\ref{sec:transport-followup}. At the post-update query the current
width is $0.856$ of the reference width, the corrected center is off by
$0.262$--$0.277$, the naive half-width is $0.253$ and the reference half-width
is $0.287$. In 256 held-out seeds, run under a protocol fixed before
evaluation, the naive-current band failed simultaneous coverage in all 256
seeds and the frozen-reference and endpoint-transported bands in none
(one-sided Clopper--Pearson bounds at level $0.05/3$: naive failure probability
at least $0.984$, frozen and endpoint failure probabilities at most $0.016$).
The covering intervals span $0.30$ of the feasible mean range $2h(R)$.

This is a constructed example: it shows that the failure can occur on a
realizable trajectory, not how often it occurs under ordinary training. Three
further restrictions apply. In one dimension with $\bV_t\succeq\bVbar_t$,
endpoint transport reproduces the reference width exactly, so the example does
not distinguish the two. With the envelope of
Section~\ref{sec:transport-followup}, the naive half-width is $0.784$ and no
seed fails. Both actions receive equal bonuses within each construction, so
all three scores select the same actions; every naive failure occurs at round
$n+1$ and violates the upper bound only at the suboptimal action, and the
example says nothing about regret. In the six-dimensional model of
Section~\ref{sec:transport-v3} at $W=100$ with the same sharper envelope, the
three policies run on their own histories over 64 seeds had mean regrets
within $0.7$ of one another (paired 95\% bootstrap intervals for naive and
endpoint minus frozen include zero), no coverage failure, and current and
reference widths within $0.25\%$; that cell has a non-vacuous frozen bound but
negligible curvature.
